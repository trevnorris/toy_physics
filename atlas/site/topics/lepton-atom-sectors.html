Lepton and atom reduced sectors

Atom and lepton constructions currently live in reduced-sector and conditional corridors, with several pieces still awaiting formal papers.

paper pending 20 selected graph nodes · 8 paper-pending source entries

Physical Picture

These sectors explain how the existing 4D action can be read in reduced atomic and lepton-like regimes. They should not be presented as completed full moving-throat PDE theorems.

The atom side starts from the controlled zero-mode Maxwell and Coulomb branch. Hydrogenic structure is therefore a reduced brane-effective consequence, with q_eff setting the observed charge strength.

Finite-mouth and P22 atomic effects are a later response corridor. They are plausible shape corrections, but they are not yet a solved full-defect atomic PDE theorem.

The lepton mass ledger is also reduced. It organizes internal support energy and a rest-energy partition, while the absolute branch scale remains open.

The lepton half-integer route is more conditional. It uses a same-charge mixed-sector Berry rotor and needs selective subbundle, central holonomy, mixed recirculation, and autonomous standing-wave closure before it can be upgraded.

Mathematical Core

The useful public split is reduced-sector consequence, conditional theorem, and open spin/discretizer gate. That split makes the pending work visible without hiding the structured progress.

The atomic reduction imports the zero-mode Maxwell equation and the thickness-controlled q_eff normalization from the charge sector.

The mass partition equation records the reduced isolated-defect ledger. It should be read as an internal support-energy result inside the declared closure, not as a completed derivation of every lepton parameter.

The half-integer equation records the conditional Berry quantizer. Its status depends on the same mixed-sector and autonomous-closure gates named on the page.

The mixed recirculation gate remains important because the Berry route cannot erase A_w, F_muw, current-source, or fluxoid plumbing and still claim a full particle-sector theorem.
