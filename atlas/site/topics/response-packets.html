Branch response packets and target-blind export

The model needs branch data exported before comparison to PN or anomaly targets; response packets must be target-blind and downstream of the physical branch.

paper pending 19 selected graph nodes · 10 paper-pending source entries

Physical Picture

A response packet is the output contract between a solved moving-throat branch and a downstream PN, anomaly, or continuation comparison.

The packet is not a physical object in its own right. It is compressed readout data downstream of the throat mouth, interior support, mixed sector, outgoing port, and material closure.

Packet A carries the conservative and outgoing grouped target data. It is where K, M, B_n, Z_n, N_n, D0, C, P0, N2, and N4 become branch-exported quantities rather than fitted comparison constants.

Packet B carries orbit-lock and weak-axisymmetric data when that tangent is relevant, especially for the 5PN continuation and g-2 style common quotient language.

The target-blind rule is the whole point. The branch must be selected, solved, frozen, and exported before the target surface is evaluated.

Mathematical Core

The weak-form branch extractor is the planned interface between solved profiles and invariant packet data. It prepares Galerkin or weak-form extraction of response moments, grouped projectors, residuals, and stability gates.

Packet A includes the one-pole and full-bundle target surface, plus the P0 quadrupole normalization inherited from the 2.5PN and 4PN tail story.

Packet B includes the weak-axisymmetric prefactor slope and orbit-lock tangent. It should be read as a branch-output condition, not as evidence that the actual weak-axisymmetric branch has already been solved.

The open exporter decomposes into an executable branch solver, source and port normalization, N2/N4 outgoing moment-shape control, and weak-axisymmetric orbit-lock where needed.

The readout firewall matters because D0, C, P0, N2, and N4 are audit coordinates. They summarize projected branch behavior, but they do not replace the physical throat or support system.

Status

This page is still notes-backed. The equations on it are useful target surfaces and readout definitions, but the actual target-blind physical branch exporter remains open.

Treat Packet A and Packet B as future-paper interfaces until the moving-throat branch solver exports them from a frozen branch.
