A smooth one-dimensional segment of the conservative carrier on [0,1/2].
The complete complex field at the feed is exp(-it). Both ends have fixed
densities, 1 and 81/100, hence fixed scalar pressures P=(4/5)n^5. The
number current is positive toward the outlet. Unit area, zero transverse
velocity, no gauge, confinement or conversion terms. These illustrative
numbers specify boundary controls for a test of what pressure fixes.

Writing psi=z(w)exp(-it) gives z''=2(|z|^8-1)z. SciPy solves the
two real field equations by collocation. Wolfram writes z=a exp(i theta)
and shoots with two unknowns, initial amplitude slope and conserved
current j, satisfying a''=j^2/a^3+2(a^8-1)a and theta'=j/a^2.
The independently specified outlet conditions are a=9/10 and theta equal
to the chosen phase. Neither solver takes an imposed current as data.

### E.5 Receiving-region modeling (how 4D expansion creates back-pressure)

Back-pressure is not a boundary condition; it’s a **result** of:

* EOS pressure from (U(\rho)),
* geometric spreading in 4D,
* and any confining potentials in the receiving region.

To make this real, ensure:

1. the confinement beyond the throat actually permits bulk occupancy (the gate truly opens access),
2. the receiving region is large enough and/or has absorbers so that matter can spread or exit rather than reflect,
3. you measure back-pressure via a defined observable (e.g., brane-projected enthalpy near (\Gamma) relative to far-field baseline).

A useful operational diagnostic is:
[
\Delta h_{\rm back}(t) \equiv \overline{h}_{\rm near\,mouth}(t)-\overline{h}_{\rm far}(t)
]
where (h(\rho)) is the enthalpy implied by your EOS and the overlines denote averages in specified regions.
