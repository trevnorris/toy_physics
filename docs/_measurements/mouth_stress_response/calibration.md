# Can two rest densities determine the mouth's stress response?

**The present preparations do not supply the calibration.** In the radius-one feeding slab the zero-sideways-flow state and the two weak-flow states share feed number density 1, inward number rate 0.25 and a final plane at the mouth. The field at that plane gives the feeding-side trace. The normal boundary condition supplies its feeding-side slope. A ghost plane built from that condition represents the numerical derivative on that side. The archive contains no independently determined receiving-side slope. The open-sleeve solver has a finite receiver and an adjustable bath density. Its entrance is a prescribed bath value at the lower boundary of a different domain, and a single bath-density parameter sets both ends. Its default equation-of-state coefficient also differs from the feed patch. The two archived finest-grid runs vary the prescribed inward rate (0 and 0.125) at a common bath density of 1. Joining this receiver to the brane face would require an independently justified, common matching law and consistent preparation. The three frozen stationary Stage-1 backgrounds checked previously have zero spatial material current. **What the calibration would measure.** In the fixed spherical geometry let $J$ be the receiving-minus-feeding slope of log density along the common normal. Suppose the physical response is local and has the same differentiable law $J=B(r,n)$ for both rest and weak-flow preparations. At each fixed mouth radius coordinate $r$, change the feed density $f$ on the same draining branch. Measure the actual local mouth density and both normal slopes. Wherever the local density changes to first order, the chain rule gives

$$
B_n(r,n_0)=\frac{\partial_f J}{\partial_f n},\qquad J_1(r)=B_n(r,n_0)\,n_1(r).
$$

Here $n_1$ and $J_1$ are the coefficients of the companion-facing density and slope-jump dipoles per unit incident speed. The ratio uses **local** mouth density in the denominator. The physical geometry, law and other independent arguments must stay fixed, or their responses must be determined separately. Density dipoles from the archived prescribed mouth law would apply to a physical mouth only if its response is independently matched. The weighted integral and rim term for the carrier load are given in the [force calculation](force.md).

Two finite rest readings give a secant. For example two smooth laws can agree at local density offsets $-h,0,h$ and differ in derivative at zero by $-\gamma h^2$. A halved spacing checks whether the secant has settled, subject to measurement precision. Regions where density fails to change sufficiently remain uncalibrated wherever they contribute to the force. Other physical rim loads still require their own response or a direct total-force measurement. The physical total sideways force per unit incident speed remains open.
