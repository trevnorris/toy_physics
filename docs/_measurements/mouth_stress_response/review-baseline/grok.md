**How two rest densities change identifiability.** Suppose the physical jump law is local and differentiable, \(J=B(r,n)\), with geometry and other arguments held fixed. Changing the feed density \(f\) on the same draining branch and measuring local mouth density together with both normal slopes gives, wherever density changes to first order,
\[
B_n(r,n_0)=\frac{\partial_f J}{\partial_f n},\qquad J_1(r)=B_n(r,n_0)\,n_1(r).
\]
That ratio converts the measured density dipole into the missing slope-jump dipole and thereby closes the weighted integral in the signed radial formula. Two finite rest readings replace the derivative by a secant. Smooth laws that agree at offsets \(-h,0,h\) can still differ in center slope by \(-\gamma h^2\), so the secant remains biased until the spacing is halved and the change falls inside measurement precision. Regions where density fails to move stay uncalibrated wherever they contribute to the force. Other rim loads still need their own response or a direct total-force measurement.
