### Item 3: momentum received, distinct from the rate of stored momentum

`momentumReceived` is a three-vector made from the integrated `f_i` and the two **rest**
momentum-flux surface pieces. `momentumRate` prints the full regional rate, and
`momentumSurfaceMotion` prints the motion pieces separately. The shared twelve surface-motion
components are bound one by one. Py's latter payload additionally contains two `Volume(0)`
bulk-reference entries per component, which WL's payload omits; those values/keys remain
unpaired, as do their six extra Py dimension tags. The corresponding separately emitted
rate pieces are compared elsewhere. No equality of received momentum and `dP/dt` is imposed,
and no moving-frame reinterpretation replaces the supplied `x`-coordinate object. Momentum
received is not a passive factor or the force a drain exerts on another body. (Retrieve
M01–M03/M06–M09/M12–M13.)


**Requirement at the opening.** For the same radius-selected inward flow, a mouth law must give the net sideways reaction to independently varying the incident brane flow and the center velocity. At the opening take the difference of the two **total** momentum currents, each including the material crossing relative to the moving cut and its transmitted stress. Add independent rim forces and account once for momentum stored in or exchanged with material outside the translating body. Equal mass rates can still carry different tangential momenta. Their carried difference vanishes on the stationary, equal-current, single-carrier branch with a common differentiable tangential phase. An independent total mechanical reaction in each preparation would measure the combined effect. A wall-slip coefficient alone leaves both whole-mouth responses open.

def aperture_center_x(radius, mu, weights):
    """Volume centroid of the aperture, integrating radial rays to the rim."""
    radius, mu, weights = (np.asarray(v, dtype=float) for v in (radius, mu, weights))
    if (radius.ndim != 1 or mu.shape != radius.shape or weights.shape != radius.shape
            or not np.all(np.isfinite([radius, mu, weights])) or np.any(radius <= 0)
            or np.any(abs(mu) > 1) or np.any(weights <= 0)):
        raise ValueError("Invalid aperture radii or angular quadrature")
    # The radial integrals for volume and its first moment are R^3/3 and R^4/4.
    return 0.75 * np.dot(weights * mu, radius**4) / np.dot(weights, radius**3)


def spherical_rim_probe(radius, mu, force):
    """Signed normal load per rim area at the initial spherical mouth.

    External work is force times variation of aperture_center_x. At a sphere
    the variation of the centroid is 3/(4*pi) integral mu*delta_R dOmega.
    """
    mu = np.asarray(mu, dtype=float)
    if (not np.isfinite(radius) or radius <= 0 or not np.isfinite(force)
            or not np.all(np.isfinite(mu)) or np.any(abs(mu) > 1)):
        raise ValueError("Expected a positive radius and finite force and direction cosines")
    return 3 * force * mu / (4 * np.pi * radius**2)

## What passes on if the carrier phase is continuous at the mouth?

For a flat stationary cut, orient the same normal $N$ from the feeding side $(-)$ to the receiving side $(+)$. Take a single neutral carrier with the same constituent mass on both sides, positive number density, and no number stored or transported along the cut. The normal number current $j$ then agrees pointwise. The carried part of the sideways drive on the interface is $mj(v_x^- - v_x^+)$. A common differentiable phase trace makes the tangential velocities equal because $mv_x=\hbar\partial_x\theta$. All of this carried sideways momentum passes on, even if the intake redistributes across the opening. On a smooth cut with no singular interaction the normal density slope agrees as well, so the carrier gives zero localized force there. This statement concerns a cut through that carrier. Forces on a physical rim and any separately coupled fields still require their own terms. [Carrier transmission conditions](drain_driven_initial_acceleration.md#matching-the-core-to-the-receiving-bulk-at-0pn) · [Carrier momentum tensor](../software/stage1_solver/reports/pathA_21c_force_from_noether_stress_tensor.md#L22)

A thin physical coupling can transmit the same material current and exert a force. As a conditional example take a real surface energy density $e(\mathbf x-X\hat x,n)$ tied to the center, with no other surface force. Set $c=\hbar^2/(2m)$ and $[b]=b^+-b^-$. Variation of the two-sided carrier energy gives $c[\partial_N\psi]=e_n\psi$, hence $[\partial_N\log n]=2e_n/c$. The difference of the two mixed density stresses is $n\,d_x e_n$, where $d_x$ differentiates both the profile and the density trace. It equals $d_x(ne_n-e)+(\partial_x e)_n$. For a coupling with smooth compact support the first term integrates to zero over the aperture. The net force on the translated coupling is therefore $-\partial_X\int e\,d^3x$ at fixed field. Its mechanical support force in a stationary state has the opposite sign. [Two-sided variation](drain_driven_initial_acceleration.md#matching-a-conversion-layer-to-the-sleeve-and-receiving-bulk-at-0pn) · [Symbolic identity](_measurements/mouth_transmission_work.txt)

Take $e=\lambda c g_X(\sqrt n-1)^2$ and write $u=\sqrt n$. The benchmark specifies $\partial_N u^-=-g_X(u-1)$. The surface law then gives $\partial_N u^+=(\lambda-1)g_X(u-1)$. Two local matches illustrate what is missing. With $\lambda=0$ the smooth continuation passes on the entire carrier load. With $\lambda=1$ the receiving amplitude slope is zero and the interface feels the restoring force. Both allow exactly the same feeding trace and inward current locally. Neither constructs a global receiving solution or determines the physical surface law.

On the radius-1 periodic benchmark, the $\lambda=1$ interaction gave an odd force per unit incident speed of $7.4812472343\times10^{-6}$ at $|U|=0.01$ and $7.48163888963\times10^{-6}$ at $|U|=0.02$. This is about $0.0030\%$ of the incoming momentum per unit speed, $0.25$. On the saved unequal pair at separation 8 the same conditional force is the previously tabulated restoring contribution, $+9.36032381319\times10^{-9}$ and $-1.94720351749\times10^{-8}$. These numbers use prescribed benchmark intake laws. The physical 0PN choice still requires a tangential momentum-transfer relation or a determined stress difference at the opening. On the common-phase, single-carrier branch any additional force must enter through stress or a separate coupling. The model lists the physical surface functional as open.
