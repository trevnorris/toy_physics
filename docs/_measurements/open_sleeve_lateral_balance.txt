Sideways response of the neutral carrier in the open sleeve. Working result.
Repository base 8e9808153a745ac4ed8ac6d99f1d5e328ea3d96c. No new commit or independent physics review.
Compare prescribed axial number rates zero and 0.125 at the same outlet condition.
Lengths and materials use open_sleeve_current.txt. Positive w runs into the receiver.
Each coefficient is a first derivative with respect to imposed frame acceleration A.
The force unit divided by the acceleration unit has units of mass. The external
acceleration is a control. No bare throat mass or free-motion equation enters.

Solve the angular l=1 carrier equation in the instantaneous comoving frame, with
potential m A r cos(theta). This is a stationary family in an accelerating rig.
With psi0 the saved carrier and eta cos(theta) its perturbation per unit A, linearize
[(kinetic + 5K|psi|^8/4 - mu) psi + m A x psi]=0. The finite-volume angular kinetic
term integrates 2/r^2 over each radial shell. Hold the bath amplitudes and feed phase
fixed in that frame. On the far bath the phase perturbation per unit A is either zero
or (m/hbar) tau x. Use tau=1 solver time unit for an explicit boundary sensitivity.
The latter specifies an external relative drift as well as the frame acceleration.
Neither is a selected physical receiver law. The scalar intake has zero first
variation for both dipoles. All stresses use the neutral carrier convention
Pi_ij=m n v_i v_j + K n^5 delta_ij - hbar^2 n partial_i partial_j ln(n)/(4m).

H=ln n0, B=2 Re(eta/psi0), G=(hbar/m) Im(eta/psi0) and phi=(hbar/m) arg(psi0).
B and G multiply cos(theta). At a flat cut the angular means for advection and
stress use the equations audited in sleeve_outlet_dipole.txt. At the reflecting
side, H_r=B_r=0 and advection through it vanishes. Per unit side area the means
are 5 K n^5 B/3 for pressure and -hbar^2 n(B H_rr+B_rr-2B/r^2)/(12m) for
quantum stress. The latter includes the variation of the Cartesian normal direction
in the Hessian expressed in spherical coordinates. Constant-density end baths give
B=0 and zero isotropic lateral traction there. The mixed quantum flux integrated
over a bath face reduces to -4 pi hbar^2 n r^2 B_w/(12m) at the outer rim.
The readout uses a second-order one-sided axial derivative and a reflecting radial
extrapolation there. Results at 64 and 128 cells disclose its mesh sensitivity.
The exact convective end flux derivative with these prescribed bath phases is
m times the measured axial number rate times tau. Feed has tau=0. The baseline
carrier is axisymmetric so integrated lateral momentum fluxes vanish at A=0.
Comoving-frame readings exclude bulk transport from a common lab-frame boost.

The wall balance is local to L/4<w<3L/4 and uses +w on both axial cuts. In this
frame, out - in + side = -m integral n. Signs for 'wall_total' give the force
the carrier exerts on that wall segment. The carrier mass is the inventory in
that interval. The opening at the sleeve/receiver join is internal; the reported
far-bath flux is downstream of that join. Receiver support and missing body
terms preclude summing these readings into a physical body force. The measured
local residual is not a bound on the separate wall or bath integrals. F and F/2
were not applied to a freely moving mouth.

Run provenance. The positive and zero fine-grid fields were newly solved at
64 and 128 cells. The previous 8/16/32 saved states also supported an initial
wall-only diagnostic (_scratch/s11c/sleeve_lateral_balance01); this record
cites the fine-grid runs. The uniform-bath solver version is preserved in its
guarded run directory. The current source adds the optional far-bath gradient
(default zero) and an explicit bath advection readout. All four runs report
guard/child exit codes 0, verified 4 GiB hard memory, zero swap and no deadline.
