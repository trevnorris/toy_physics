**Agreement.** Hold mouth 1 still and prescribe companion approach speed $s=-\dot d$ at fixed separation $d$ for the comparison. On a sphere centered on mouth 1, the outward interaction energy rate is $A(d)s$ in the ideal monopole benchmark. Here $A(d)$ is the attractive force magnitude from the static momentum balance. The fixed center does zero work. The medium does $A(d)s$ of work per unit time on the moving center, and its centered moving surface has zero relative interaction energy flux. Reversing the motion reverses the energy transfer. Both reviewers endorse subtracting both isolated controls on the same domain, an independent static-force reading, and separate measurements of the matched receiving-side current and the total holder reaction for the physical test.

**Rate conventions and the overall energy balance.** Grok requested an explicit map from positive intake $D(a_i)$ to signed outward rate. The feeding brane has $q_i=-D(a_i)$ and the receiving bulk has $q_i=+D(a_i)$. Grok also raised a possible far-field contribution, describing the interaction integral as conditionally convergent and invoking $\dot U=-As$. For the specified exterior *kinetic* interaction energy, the two velocity fields each decay as $r^{1-n}$, so the absolute radial tail is bounded by a constant times $\int_R^\infty r^{1-n}\,dr$, finite for $n=3,4$. The interaction energy current through a distant sphere likewise vanishes as $R\to\infty$. The source calculation and the local referee give $\dot K_\times=+As$ during approach. Adding center work gives a required pair increment of $2As$ relative to isolated controls. The energy supplier and free acceleration remain open.
