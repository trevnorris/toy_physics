Setup
Use the unit-radius, unit inward surface speed spherical benchmark, density 3/(4 pi).
The source bare mass is one. Only the target bare mass changes from one to two.
Both radii, both volume intakes (4 pi), and both outlet rules stay fixed at each distance.
Mass exits each outlet with zero mean velocity along the line of centers and no extra
axial traction. There are no slab faces in this geometry. The prescribed extra mass
is mechanically coupled to the target without affecting the stationary flow. This is
one draining configuration with two assigned solid masses. Using its acceleration
ratio to assess free fall for two physical throats was an error. Both centers are at
rest initially and free after removal of their supports. Positive x points from target
to source.

Computation
The archived unit-mass readings were reused. For the heavier target the new solver
collocates Neumann data on each permeable sphere. It solves the steady potential
and the responses to each unit center acceleration, evaluates the carried momentum,
Bernoulli pressure and added mass on a separate grid, and solves the full inertia
matrix with and without applied probe forces. Forces of +/-0.015 are applied to
each body separately. The inverse target response to its own probe (other body
free) defines calibrated_M. A stationary holder exerts the negative of the load.
The isolated applied-force calibration also gives the asymptotic target inertia.

Independent reference
Unit-mass A uses the degree-9 Wolfram translated-multipole solution archived in
paired_gravity_transfer.txt. That record contains its literal stdout and original
command. For the new bare mass add DeltaM e_t e_t^T to the old inertia matrix.
For any fixed external forces the new target acceleration solves
a_B = a_A - mu_tt,A DeltaM a_B. The same identity applied to differences of
probe forces divides every entry of the target mobility row by 1+mu_tt,A DeltaM.
This is a prediction from the earlier field solution; only the new collocation
calculation directly solves the heavier target. Comparing hold, free acceleration
and both mobility entries uses 2e-12 absolute tolerance in benchmark units.
Absolute surface boundary tolerance is 1e-8 (speed/acceleration boundary data).
The reported ratio is the acceleration response to adding mass to that sphere.
