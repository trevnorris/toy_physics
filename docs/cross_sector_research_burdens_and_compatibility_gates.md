**Status:** Reviewer-derived companion memorandum

**Role:** Supplemental research-risk and prioritization document; not a new canonical source of ontology or definitions

## Purpose and scope

This memorandum records the largest unresolved cross-sector burdens identified during review of:

- [Ontology and Closure Ledger of the Toy Analog Physics Model](<toy_model_ontology_summary.md>)
- [Guided Light and the Photon-Soliton Research Program](<light_guided_photon_soliton_research_plan.md>)
- [Opposite-Orientation Throats, Projected Coincidence, and Core Contact](<opposite_orientation_throat_coincidence.md>)

The three source documents remain authoritative for the model's definitions, epistemic labels, frozen assumptions, and formal failure criteria. This memorandum does not add a new field, force, coefficient, ontology branch, or physical mechanism. It collects reviewer-level inferences about where otherwise separate requirements may place competing demands on one frozen parent system.

The analysis below distinguishes four different statuses:

- **Present burden:** a calculation or closure obligation that has not yet been discharged.
- **Branch risk:** a result that may reject one conservative, relaxational, mixed, spectral, or topological realization without rejecting every possible realization of the architecture.
- **Architecture risk:** a conflict that would reject the broader candidate only if it persists across the allowed frozen realizations and cannot be resolved without prohibited retuning or an unsupported new mechanism.
- **Present contradiction:** an inconsistency already established from the current assumptions.

## Executive conclusion: these are not presently fatal

No present contradiction or no-go theorem was identified in this review.

The issues recorded here are serious because each can become a failure gate once the parent equations and boundary data are frozen. They are not presently fatal because the model has not yet selected and solved the complete parent dynamical system needed to decide them. In several cases, failure would initially reject only one branch or one intended interpretation:

- Failure of a particular conservative or dissipative implementation rejects that implementation, not automatically the entire one-medium ontology.
- Failure of the self-bound photon packet would leave the conditional linear two-transverse-mode light sector intact, although it would defeat the intended classical free-photon ontology.
- Failure of exact speed equality could leave a preferred-medium analog if the resulting departures are acceptable, whereas failure of light confinement would reject the selected guided-light branch.
- Failure of a simplistic geometric annihilation picture does not reject the finite-thickness geometry; it means annihilation must be supplied by full nonlinear topology and dynamics.
- Failure of one candidate parent system, designated here as **Candidate Parent System V1**, rejects V1. It rejects the broader architecture only if the required capabilities prove mutually incompatible throughout the permitted theory class or can be obtained only through post-hoc fitting.

The correct interpretation is therefore:

> These are burdens and potential falsification gates, not established fatal flaws. They become fatal only when a frozen, properly closed calculation proves that a required capability is impossible or necessarily produces an unacceptable consequence.

The central risk is not that any one requirement is obviously impossible. It is that the intersection of all requirements may be empty:

\[
\boxed{
\text{stable slab}
\cap
\text{guided light}
\cap
\text{Coulomb range}
\cap
\text{acceptable electric dynamics}
\cap
\text{universal throat response}
\cap
\text{stable photon packet}
\cap
\text{closed reservoir}
\cap
\text{consistent topology}
\stackrel{?}{\ne}\varnothing
}
\]

That common-intersection question is the decisive test of the program.

# 1. The current program is an architecture, not yet one fully specified candidate model

## Source-derived starting point

The ontology permits a generalized NLS/GP-like substrate with \(P=Kn^5\), an ordered variable \(\chi_B\), and conservative, relaxational, or mixed order dynamics. It also states that finite-thickness selection, microscopic shear provenance, the complete parent dynamics, and the reservoir closure remain open. The light document uses variable-coefficient actions and response operators as templates rather than as a final frozen action.

These are appropriate research-stage statements. They also mean that the present documents describe a **candidate architecture**: a disciplined space of related realizations with common ontology and capability requirements. They do not yet specify one complete set of equations whose successes and failures can all be combined.

## Why this matters

The conservative, relaxational, and mixed branches are not merely alternative numerical methods. They can differ in:

- field content;
- phase-space dimension;
- conserved quantities;
- constraint structure;
- admissible boundary ensembles;
- stability criteria;
- definitions of force and interaction energy;
- radiation and damping;
- entropy production;
- reservoir response;
- particle longevity.

Likewise, permitting higher gradients, nonlocal kernels, mixed derivatives, additional order structure, or reservoir fields leaves a large operator space. That flexibility is scientifically legitimate before the parent system is selected. It becomes dangerous after downstream results are known, because a failed calculation could always motivate another still-permitted term.

The frozen-parameter rule controls numerical retuning. A further **operator-basis freeze** is needed to control structural retuning.

## Why this is not presently fatal

The model explicitly allows its medium and constitutive laws to be postulated. It is therefore not a flaw that the final common medium has not yet been chosen. The burden is to make that choice before interpreting separate sector results as evidence for one model.

A failed V1 would not prove that no V2 can work. The architecture becomes endangered only if repeated frozen candidates fail for the same structural reason or if every success requires a newly introduced sector-specific term.

## What would make it fatal

This issue becomes fatal to a selected candidate if:

1. no stable or sufficiently metastable finite slab follows from its fixed equations;
2. its constraint and energy structure is inconsistent;
3. one sector can be repaired only by changing frozen operators or adding a term not propagated through all dependent sectors;
4. results derived from incompatible branches are required simultaneously;
5. no bounded, well-posed common parent system can supply the declared capability contract.

It becomes fatal to the broader architecture only if the allowed common-medium realizations cannot satisfy the required sectors without prohibited independent tuning.

## Required artifact: Candidate Parent System V1

Before the next major derivation campaign, freeze a versioned parent system containing at least:

1. **Fundamental and effective variables**
   - \(\psi\), \(n\), \(\theta\);
   - \(\chi_B\);
   - a separate ordered fraction \(f_B\), only if genuinely required;
   - displacement, elastic-order, or other variables needed for shear;
   - every reservoir variable retained in a dissipative or mixed branch.

2. **Dynamical branch**
   - conservative;
   - relaxational;
   - explicitly mixed inertial-dissipative.

3. **Complete equations**
   - action and constraints for a conservative branch;
   - evolution equations and constitutive currents for a relaxational branch;
   - one nonredundant coupled system for a mixed branch.

4. **Operator basis**
   - maximum derivative order;
   - locality or declared nonlocal kernels;
   - permitted mixed derivatives;
   - parity- and time-reversal-breaking terms, if any;
   - terms explicitly forbidden from later introduction.

5. **Symmetries and background assumptions**
   - parent translation and rotation structure;
   - brane reflection symmetry;
   - co-orientability;
   - in-brane isotropy;
   - time-reversal and parity assumptions;
   - conserved currents and topological sectors.

6. **Boundary and reservoir data**
   - admissible asymptotic ensembles;
   - closed versus open computational domains;
   - outgoing-wave conditions;
   - reservoir state variables and conservation ledgers.

7. **Frozen parameters**
   - dimensions and units;
   - independent dimensionless combinations;
   - where each parameter is fixed;
   - every downstream dependency.

## Cheapest decisive calculation

From V1, derive in this order:

\[
\text{stable slab}
\longrightarrow
\text{complete constrained linear operator}
\longrightarrow
\text{all poles, continua, constraints, and source residues}.
\]

This single calculation will inform the electric, light, drag, gravity-response, and helper-mode programs simultaneously.

# 2. The odd Coulomb sector has a dynamic degree-of-freedom trilemma

## Source-derived starting point

The electric program requires a healthy reflection-odd static eigenbranch with nonzero throat coupling and

\[
\lambda_E(k)
=
\kappa_E k^2+O(k^4),
\qquad
\kappa_E>0,
\]

so that

\[
G_E(0,k)\sim\frac{1}{\kappa_Ek^2}
\]

and the static potential behaves as \(1/R\).

The ontology also states that the present system does not possess an established first-class \(U(1)\) Gauss constraint. It therefore does not yet have the conventional electromagnetic mechanism by which a static Coulomb field is tied to a constrained gauge sector rather than an extra freely propagating scalar-like polarization. The moving-throat program correctly requires emitted powers into the odd sector, transverse light, even thickness, longitudinal, bulk, and conversion channels to be calculated separately.

## Reviewer inference

Once dynamics are supplied, a long-range static odd carrier must fall into some version of the following possibilities.

### A. Ordinary local inertia

With a conventional positive kinetic term, the same \(k^2\) static stiffness generally produces a gapless propagating branch,

\[
\omega_E(k)\simeq c_Ek.
\]

Then the model possesses an additional dynamical mode beyond the two transverse photon polarizations. Because throats must couple to it strongly enough to produce Coulomb forces, moving or accelerating throats may excite it. The resulting consequences can include:

- extra radiation;
- an additional polarization-like channel;
- modified radiation reaction;
- mode conversion near defects;
- Cherenkov-like emission if another branch is slower than a moving throat;
- observable energy loss not tied to the transverse light field.

### B. Relaxational or diffusive dynamics

A static long-range response can coexist with a non-wave-like retarded pole or branch. The cost may be:

- phase lag;
- dissipation;
- a wake behind a moving charge;
- preferred-frame drag;
- low-frequency noise or reservoir excitation;
- nonconservative pair forces.

### C. A derived nondynamical constraint

The static odd field might be determined by a constraint rather than an independent radiative mode. That would require the complete parent system to generate a legitimate constrained structure, including:

- the constraint equation;
- its preservation under time evolution;
- the associated conserved current or identity;
- the correct source coupling;
- positivity and stability on the physical constrained subspace.

Calling a variable “electrostatic” or omitting its kinetic term is not enough. The absence of a kinetic term can indicate a true constraint, an auxiliary variable, strong coupling, or an incomplete action; the full Dirac or equivalent constraint analysis must decide which.

## Why this is not presently fatal

The full odd retarded block has not yet been derived. Mixing with density, phase, interface, order, and reservoir variables may change the naive mode count. A genuine emergent constraint, a symmetry-protected decoupling, a sufficiently suppressed source residue, or another healthy kinetic structure may be possible.

The current documents already refuse to assume that the odd field is nondynamical and explicitly demand measurement of \(P_{\rm odd}\). That is the correct posture.

## What would make it fatal

The selected electric branch fails if every healthy dynamic completion that supplies the required static \(1/R\) response also necessarily produces one or more of the following at an unacceptable level:

- unsuppressed extra radiation;
- low-frequency drag;
- an active or nonpassive reservoir response;
- a ghost or negative-energy mode;
- instability or strong coupling;
- a third freely radiating electromagnetic polarization incompatible with the intended phenomenology;
- independent electric, magnetic, and radiation normalizations;
- violation of the conserved oriented-current target.

The broader architecture is threatened only if no permitted common-medium realization avoids that result.

## Early calculation program

1. Construct the complete constrained odd quadratic system from V1.
2. Separate true dynamical variables, auxiliary variables, and constraints.
3. Compute
   \[
   \mathcal G_{R,\rm odd}(\omega,\mathbf k)
   =
   \mathcal O_{R,\rm odd}^{-1}(\omega,\mathbf k).
   \]
4. Record every pole, branch cut, continuum, norm sign, damping rate, and source residue.
5. Verify that the zero-frequency limit produces the required \(1/k^2\) response.
6. Derive the moving-source overlap at \(O(v)\) and accelerating-source overlap at \(O(a)\).
7. Estimate the low-\(k\) scaling of
   \[
   P_{\rm odd}
   \]
   relative to transverse-light radiation.
8. In a dissipative or mixed branch, compute the \(\omega\to0\) friction kernel.
9. Test whether the same one-throat coupling controls static electricity, magnetism, and transverse radiation without an independent normalization.

This is an architecture-level early gate because a successful static Coulomb calculation alone would not close the electric sector.

# 3. The common-speed target may sit at a guided-mode continuum threshold

## Source-derived starting point

The ontology keeps \(c_s\), \(c_\gamma\), and the electric-sector speeds distinct and states that \(c_\gamma=c_s\) is a target rather than a derived consequence. The light plan correctly treats guided-mode confinement as an essential-spectrum and interface-overlap problem, not as a consequence of “zero bulk shear.”

For the free longitudinal branch, the light plan derives the simple phase-matching relation

\[
k_w^2
=
k^2
\left(
\frac{c_L^2}{c_{s0}^2}-1
\right),
\]

with:

- \(c_L<c_{s0}\): bulk channel kinematically closed;
- \(c_L=c_{s0}\): grazing threshold;
- \(c_L>c_{s0}\): bulk channel kinematically open.

It also correctly states that kinematic opening alone does not prove nonzero coupling or leakage.

## Reviewer inference for the transverse branch

Wherever the transverse guided mode has nonzero overlap with an acoustic bulk channel having the simple asymptotic dispersion

\[
\omega^2=c_s^2(k^2+k_w^2),
\]

and the guided transverse branch has

\[
\omega\simeq c_\gamma k,
\]

the same phase-matching logic gives

\[
\boxed{
k_w^2
=
k^2
\left(
\frac{c_\gamma^2}{c_s^2}-1
\right).
}
\]

This is a cross-sector inference, not a source-derived result for the complete transverse operator. It applies only when the receiving bulk branch, frequency range, and interface coupling justify the comparison.

The resulting tension is:

- **\(c_\gamma<c_s\):** the simple acoustic receiving channel is kinematically evanescent, which favors confinement.
- **\(c_\gamma=c_s\):** the guided branch lies at a grazing threshold.
- **\(c_\gamma>c_s\):** a propagating bulk channel is kinematically open.

Thus exact cone locking may place the light mode at a delicate continuum edge rather than safely below it.

## Why this matters

A threshold state can exhibit:

- long penetration depth in \(w\);
- vanishing spectral separation;
- enhanced sensitivity to slab gradients or defects;
- small denominators in mode conversion;
- nonuniform behavior as \(k\to0\);
- a resonance width controlled by a high-order overlap rather than by a true gap;
- loss of brane participation or pole residue despite formal normalizability at finite \(k\).

The risk is broader than light leakage. Every slower branch coupled to a moving throat contributes to the Landau-like threshold

\[
v_c
=
\min_a\inf_{k>0}\frac{\omega_a(k)}{k},
\]

or to its directional generalization. A flexural, odd-electric, conversion, longitudinal, or reservoir branch can therefore generate low-threshold emission or drag even if the transverse guided mode itself is acceptable.

## Why this is not presently fatal

Equality at a continuum threshold does not automatically imply leakage. A zero interface overlap, symmetry-protected decoupling, a bound state in the continuum, a shifted full-slab threshold, a gap in the actual mixed receiving mode, or sufficiently weak higher-order coupling could preserve an acceptable guided branch.

Exact equality may also turn out not to be required. The model can retain distinct preferred-medium speeds if their differences and induced effects remain inside its declared phenomenological acceptance budget. That would change the strength of the emergent-Lorentz claim, not necessarily destroy the analog.

## What would make it fatal

A selected common parameter region fails if:

- the photon mode necessarily delocalizes at the required speed relation;
- its brane participation or observable pole residue vanishes;
- its leakage width is unacceptably large;
- defects or ordinary nonlinearities open an unavoidable bulk channel;
- a slower coupled branch gives an unacceptable zero or very low critical velocity;
- exact cone locking and acceptable confinement cannot coexist, while the intended model requires both;
- avoiding the conflict requires a light-only term or retuning that is not propagated through the force and throat sectors.

## Required spectrum audit

For the complete V1 slab operator:

1. Compute all conservative eigenbranches or all retarded poles and continua.
2. Record the essential-spectrum edge in the same parity and polarization block as each candidate guided mode.
3. Track
   \[
   \Delta_{\rm spec}(k),\quad
   \Gamma_0(k),\quad
   \ell_w(k),\quad
   \mathcal P_{\rm br}(k),\quad
   \mathcal I_{\rm 3D}(k),
   \]
   and the brane-observable pole residue.
4. Compute interface and source-projector overlaps, not only continuum placement.
5. Sweep through \(c_\gamma/c_s=1\) and the full-slab replacement of the simple threshold.
6. Repeat the overlap calculation in the presence of:
   - slab gradients;
   - curvature;
   - a throat background;
   - the nonlinear helper dressing;
   - the leading symmetry-allowed mode couplings.
7. Compute the directional critical velocity over every branch coupled to an ordinary moving throat.
8. In dissipative and mixed branches, separately compute low-frequency friction even when no propagating channel is open.

# 4. Charge universality, mass universality, conservation, and species discreteness need an enforcing mechanism

## Source-derived starting point

The ontology deliberately keeps active, passive, and inertial mass distinct and requires species-universal low-energy ratios only after each response is independently healthy. It also distinguishes:

- throat orientation \(s\), which supplies electric sign;
- a possible leading coupling magnitude \(g_a\);
- a target conserved oriented count or intersection number;
- the need for a projected Gauss-like law;
- species-dependent core and support data.

The electric program requires approximately universal \(g_a\) for ordinary unit-charge throats. The gravity program requires approximately universal

\[
\frac{M_{\rm passive}}{M_{\rm inertial}},
\qquad
\frac{M_{\rm active}^{\rm local}}{M_{\rm inertial}}
\]

in the declared low-energy regime.

## Reviewer inference

The freeze rule prevents these ratios from being tuned independently. It does not itself explain why they should be universal.

The quantities arise from different parts of the complete object:

- **Active gravity:** transport, conversion, stress, return, and source-side reduction.
- **Passive gravity:** response of the entire dressed throat to the calibrated environmental field.
- **Inertia:** support-mode momentum, near-field rebuilding, geometry, conversion, and reservoir response.
- **Electric magnitude:** coupling of the nonlinear oriented core and its boundary data to the long-range odd eigenmode.
- **Species identity:** support-mode family, topology, radius, shape, internal phase, circulation, or composite structure.

A successful model must allow enough internal variation to produce different particle species and masses while suppressing variation in unit electric coupling and the required mass ratios.

A related issue is discreteness. In a classical conservative periodic system, support-mode action is often a continuous conserved parameter unless topology, boundary conditions, or another selection mechanism restricts it. A continuous action can generate continuous families of:

- throat radii;
- support frequencies;
- throughputs;
- active masses;
- inertial masses;
- electric couplings.

A dissipative attractor can select isolated amplitudes, but then its reservoir, power, entropy, noise, and lifetime must be closed.

## Possible enforcing structures to search for

This memorandum does not postulate any of the following. It records the classes of mechanisms that could make universality nonaccidental:

- a symmetry or Noether identity;
- a topological index or intersection number;
- a shared conserved current;
- a variational identity tying source and response derivatives;
- a universal critical-flow or boundary condition;
- an attractor with branch-independent asymptotic coupling;
- insensitivity of the far-field source residue to internal core moduli;
- discrete nonlinear branches selected by topology or boundary regularity.

The point is not to choose one in advance. It is to determine whether V1 supplies one.

## Why this is not presently fatal

No complete family of supported throat solutions exists yet. The parent equations may produce:

- isolated stable branches;
- topologically discrete sectors;
- a universal asymptotic boundary condition;
- protected far-field residues;
- shared scaling relations among source, passive, and inertial responses.

Universality cannot be judged before at least a provisional multi-branch throat family is solved.

## What would make it fatal

The intended particle sector fails if, after all calibrations are frozen:

- ordinary unit-charge species have unacceptably different \(g_a\);
- the leading odd coupling matrix is not approximately positive rank one where factorization is required;
- active/passive/inertial ratios vary unacceptably across stable species;
- a continuous modulus produces arbitrary charge or mass where the target requires a fixed unit;
- orientation is not associated with a conserved count or current;
- particle–antiparticle creation and annihilation cannot preserve the derived topological or current ledger;
- a universal result appears only after species-specific normalization.

## High-value early throat-family test

Once provisional one-core solutions are available, continue at least one controlled family in a support or core parameter \(\lambda\). Extract:

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm throat}(\lambda),\quad
\omega_{\rm support}(\lambda),\quad
\mathcal I_{\rm support}(\lambda),\quad
Q_n(\lambda),\quad
Q_\chi(\lambda),\\
g(\lambda),\quad
M_{\rm active}^{\rm local}(\lambda),\quad
M_{\rm passive}(\lambda),\quad
M_{\rm inertial}(\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\]

Then calculate:

1. logarithmic sensitivities such as
   \[
   \frac{d\ln g}{d\ln\lambda},
   \qquad
   \frac{d}{d\ln\lambda}
   \ln\!\left(
   \frac{M_{\rm passive}}{M_{\rm inertial}}
   \right);
   \]
2. whether the asymptotic electric coefficient matrix is approximately rank one across branches;
3. whether charge and mass ratios approach protected plateaus;
4. whether topology or regularity selects isolated solutions;
5. whether orientation reversal preserves every required even quantity and reverses every required odd datum;
6. whether a conserved parent current or intersection number exists.

A leading-order scaling incompatibility discovered here would save substantial later computation.

# 5. The photon program must balance strong self-binding against weak vacuum nonlinearity

## Source-derived starting point

The light plan correctly distinguishes a linear wave, an ordinary packet, a solitary wave, a soliton, and a classical photon analog. It requires a guided transverse carrier with two freely selectable polarizations, finite-thickness dispersion, and a slaved reflection-even helper response. The same document also requires:

- no collapse;
- acceptably small speed variation;
- weak or soliton-like packet interactions;
- negligible persistent wakes;
- acceptable polarization behavior;
- compatibility with the force, throat, and drag sectors;
- no photon-specific hidden coefficient.

## Reviewer inference

A three-dimensionally localized packet needs enough nonlinear response and dispersion or nonlocality to resist spreading. The ordinary vacuum-like light sector simultaneously requires that the same nonlinearity produce very small effects for generic low-intensity or overlapping waves.

The required window is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{nonlinearity strong enough to bind one finite packet}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{weak enough to preserve nearly linear ordinary propagation}.
}
\]

The tension appears in several forms:

- stronger focusing improves localization but increases collapse risk;
- stronger helper coupling improves self-guiding but increases wake and radiation risk;
- stronger nonlinear index change increases amplitude-dependent speed;
- stronger binding generally increases photon–photon interaction;
- stronger finite-thickness dispersion helps balance nonlinearity but risks observable frequency dispersion;
- a fixed \(H_{\rm br}\) must support a connected frequency family rather than one isolated packet frequency.

The same concern feeds into the throat sector. If the trapped support mode remains part of a continuous classical family, throat size and mass may inherit a continuous modulus unless the nonlinear free-boundary problem selects isolated branches.

## Why this is not presently fatal

Saturating, competing, nonlocal, and finite-range responses can in principle produce stable classical localized waves without the simplest cubic-collapse behavior. The model has not yet derived the sign, strength, range, or frequency dependence of its helper-mediated interaction, so no no-go result follows from the current documents.

The light program already treats this as an early falsification track and includes virial, collapse, leakage, stability, and collision tests.

## What would make it fatal

The self-bound photon branch fails if every shared constitutive region with acceptable linear light has one of the following outcomes:

- no three-dimensional localization;
- collapse or energy unbounded below;
- localization only at one isolated frequency;
- unacceptable amplitude- or frequency-dependent speed;
- strong generic photon–photon scattering;
- persistent longitudinal, bulk, odd, even, or conversion wakes;
- a helper that becomes an independent third polarization;
- coefficients incompatible with the stable slab or force sectors;
- a maintained packet requiring an unclosed power source or reservoir.

This would not erase the homogeneous two-transverse-mode result. It would leave the medium with a classical radiation sector but no closed classical free-photon object.

## Recommended inexpensive scaling gate

Before full four-dimensional continuation, derive from the V1 projected action:

1. packet width versus carrier frequency and conserved action or stored energy;
2. peak strain and helper amplitude versus packet energy;
3. nonlinear speed correction
   \[
   \delta v_\gamma(I,\omega);
   \]
4. nonlinear phase shift for an extended low-amplitude wave;
5. two-packet interaction energy or scattering strength;
6. collapse or virial scaling;
7. DC, \(2\omega_0\), sideband, and higher-harmonic helper radiation;
8. polarization-dependent nonlinear splitting;
9. the coefficient region satisfying both localization and weak-interaction limits.

A compatibility plot using the same frozen coefficients should mark at least:

- stable slab;
- bounded nonlinear energy;
- localized packet;
- acceptable dispersion;
- acceptable speed shift;
- acceptable collision inelasticity;
- acceptable helper leakage;
- acceptable force-sector behavior.

# 6. Persistent drainage is also a particle-longevity and reservoir problem

## Source-derived starting point

The ontology distinguishes three order-dynamics branches:

- dissipative;
- reversible/inertial;
- explicitly mixed inertial-dissipative.

It requires the final parent theory to choose one and close material, momentum, energy, inertia/drag, entropy, and reservoir accounting as appropriate. It also treats the local supported throat and the global drain-return system as a fixed-point problem.

## Reviewer inference

A permanent throat with nonzero \(Q_n\) or \(Q_\chi\) is not closed merely because removed material returns somewhere else.

### Dissipative interpretation

A stationary particle becomes a maintained nonequilibrium structure. The calculation must identify:

- power consumed by de-structuring and reordering;
- entropy production;
- heat or unresolved internal excitation;
- reservoir depletion and replenishment;
- noise or fluctuation partners if required by the effective description;
- lifetime and aging;
- low-frequency drag during motion.

A spatial return loop restores material. It does not automatically restore free energy or erase entropy.

### Conservative interpretation

The apparent drain must be part of coherent persistent transport, periodic conversion, or circulation. Then:

- total energy and momentum must be conserved;
- no unaccounted dissipative sink may be used;
- the gravity mechanism must survive without appealing to irreversible “consumption” of brane material;
- the stationary particle is more accurately a periodic or steady-current solution than a dissipative drain.

### Mixed interpretation

Both reversible storage and irreversible transfer exist. The model must derive both from one closed system and may not use a conservative functional for convenient calculations while omitting the reservoir from dissipative ones.

## Why this is not presently fatal

The ontology explicitly recognizes these alternatives and has not committed to an inconsistent blend. A stable conservative circulation, a closed dissipative attractor, or a healthy mixed fixed point could exist.

The word “drainage” may ultimately require a more precise branch-specific interpretation, but changing the interpretation from irreversible loss to coherent throughput would not by itself contradict the one-medium ontology.

## What would make it fatal

A selected branch fails if:

- a stationary throat requires an unchanged inexhaustible bulk battery;
- material returns but energy, momentum, or entropy does not close;
- particles necessarily age or decay too rapidly;
- a dissipative branch has unacceptable uniform-motion drag;
- a conservative branch cannot sustain the proposed throughput or gravity source;
- local throat data and global reservoir data have no self-consistent fixed point;
- the return profile screens, reverses, or species-dependently alters gravity beyond the declared acceptance budget;
- the model silently switches branch-specific definitions between calculations.

## Required fixed-point calculation

For provisional asymptotic data \(\mathcal B_\infty\):

1. solve the local supported throat;
2. extract:
   \[
   Q_n^{\rm net},\quad
   Q_n,\quad
   \widetilde Q_n^{(w)},\quad
   Q_\chi,
   \]
   plus stress, momentum, energy, radiation, and entropy where applicable;
3. solve the global bulk, return, and reservoir problem;
4. update backpressure, chemical potential, temperature or internal state, and the return profile;
5. resolve the local throat;
6. iterate to a fixed point;
7. linearize the full iteration map to test fixed-point stability.

The output should include:

- stationary power and entropy budgets;
- particle lifetime or exact conservation statement;
- reservoir capacity and relaxation time;
- return-screening scale;
- low-frequency moving-throat friction;
- sensitivity of throat radius, support mode, charge coupling, and active source to reservoir state.

\[

\qquad
h_t^{(+)}+h_t^{(-)}=a_c.

In the graph regime, the normal core gap is

\[
d_w^{\rm core}
=

The document therefore establishes that projected coincidence and electric neutralization do not imply four-dimensional core contact or material disappearance.

## Why this matters

The original intuitive picture “opposite charges meet and cancel” is insufficient. The full theory must separately explain:

- how same-species antiparticle cores approach or change topology;
- whether a neutral intermediate state forms;
- how support modes transform and dispose of their energy;
- how material, momentum, and energy are carried away;
- what prevents a different-species opposite-charge pair from following the same annihilation route;
- which invariant distinguishes annihilation from stable neutral binding.

The even thickness field may provide:

- a short-range barrier;
- a neutral dressed remnant;
- a metastable pair;
- a reconnection precursor;
- an additional conversion zone.

None of those outcomes should be inserted by hand.

## Why this is not presently fatal

The graph calculation is a far- and intermediate-field geometric result, not a no-go theorem for contact. Contact may occur through:

- inward core extension;
- a core spanning the slab;
- interface folding;
- neck formation;
- reconnection;
- pinch-off;
- another topology-changing parent-field evolution.

Annihilation need not occur at the instant the projected positions coincide. A neutral intermediate configuration is compatible with later annihilation, scattering, or binding.

The corrected geometry is therefore a useful constraint, not a failure. It prevents the model from claiming an annihilation mechanism that the current variables cannot actually represent.

## What would make it fatal

The intended particle–antiparticle sector fails if:

- a same-species \(\mathcal C_{\rm th}\)-related pair has no material-, momentum-, and energy-conserving annihilation or neutralization channel;
- the oriented count cannot be created, transported, or annihilated consistently;
- topology change forces a singularity, arbitrary branch choice, or loss of conservation;
- distinct-species opposite charges are forced to annihilate merely because the leading electric monopole cancels;
- no internal invariant distinguishes a positron-like partner from a proton-like or composite positive object;
- the complete antiparticle map fails to transform support phase, circulation, chirality, topology, or reservoir data consistently.
