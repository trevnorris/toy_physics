# What moves a draining throat at release

**Working note for the eventual two-throat simulation.** The force requirement is the acceleration of a freely moving throat due to its inward-draining companion. A steady far-field calculation with prescribed drain strengths found an attractive inverse-square matter-stress contribution. The permeable-sphere release calculations use an assigned solid mass. The physical throat requires the total force and the translational inertia of the same draining configuration. Both remain open. [Physical free-fall comparison](#physical-free-fall-comparison-at-0pn) · [Newtonian requirements register](../research/pde_ledger_v3/SUBSTRATE_REQUIREMENTS.md#what-two-inward-drains-must-reproduce-in-the-newtonian-limit) · [Earlier matter-stress calculation](../software/stage1_solver/reports/pathA_21c_force_from_noether_stress_tensor.md)

**Scope of the 0PN gravity calculation.** The mouth is the opening into the throat. At fixed ambient conditions, the model takes the mouth radius $a$ as the physical input. Its isolated inward mass throughput is a single function $D(a)$ common to both mouths. While this function is open, the 0PN balance can use $D(a)$ symbolically. Prescribed speeds and independently varied rates in the benchmarks below are boundary conditions used to study the response. On the brane side of the target mouth, integrate the momentum carried inward and the stress transmitted across the full opening, projecting both toward the companion. Subtract the same integral for the same drain alone. Relate that change in load to the free motion of a mouth with that radius and inflow. The interior calculations recorded below are exploratory. The structure inside the throat and its feedback are deferred to 1PN and higher. ## Paired-minus-alone load across the 3D mouth at 0PN

Take two flat, round openings at fixed $w$ with nonoverlapping footprints $B_t,B_s$ in the three brane coordinates. Assume steady, irrotational flow at constant effective brane mass density $\rho$. Represent each mouth by an inward volume rate per unit brane volume $s_i(\mathbf x)\ge0$ supported on its opening, so $Q_i=\int_{B_i}s_i\,d^3x$. Here $Q_s$ is the effective source strength in the brane continuity equation. The calculation identifies it with the given drain rate and holds the target's intake profile fixed when adding the companion. The geometry uses the same $4\pi r^2\,dr$ radial measure as the Stage-1 aperture readout. [Reduced 3D source convention](../software/stage1_solver/reports/pathA_21b_force_closure_and_profile_bvp.md#L34) · [Aperture measure](../software/stage1_solver/tools/one_throat_force_probe_inputs.py#L44)

Let $+x$ point from target to source. If entering material carries the local brane velocity, a patch that takes in mass at rate $\rho s_t\,d^3x$ receives that rate times $v_x$ in sideways momentum. Subtracting the isolated integral cancels the target's own flow. In this linear potential model the remaining velocity is the gradient of the companion's drain potential. For any radial profile on either opening, at every separation $d>a_t+a_s$,

$$
\Delta F_{\rm carried,x}=\rho\int_{B_t} s_t(\mathbf x)\,v_{s,x}(\mathbf x)\,d^3x
=\frac{\rho Q_tQ_s}{4\pi d^2}.
$$

The angular average of the companion velocity on each target shell equals its value at the target centre. This holds even though the companion-facing side samples a stronger flow. Ordinary pressure points normal to the flat mouth, so its projection toward the companion vanishes point by point. Under this model's pressure-only stress assumption, the displayed integral is the entire sideways load. [Exact integration and direct numerical check](_measurements/brane_mouth_pair.txt)

The direct calculation integrated over both finite openings, with radii 1 and 1.5 in test units. For a uniform target and a radially tapered source, and with those profiles interchanged, $4\pi\Delta F_x/(\rho Q_tQ_s)$ was $0.062500000000$ at $d=4$ and $0.015625000000$ at $d=8$ on the finer quadrature. Biasing the target's intake toward its companion by a factor $1+x/(2a_t)$ at the same total rate gave $0.065625000000$ and $0.016015625000$. This control tests sensitivity to a departure from radial weighting. [Numerical profiles and outputs](_measurements/brane_mouth_pair.txt)

**Physical force and free acceleration.** The measurement on the brane side is $\Delta\int_{B_t}(j_m v_x-\sigma_{xN})\,dS$, where $j_m$ is the inward mass flux relative to the mouth, $N$ points into it, and $\sigma$ is the Cauchy stress in the tension-positive convention. Each preparation uses its actual opening. For a flat mouth, an off-diagonal stress can still carry sideways momentum. The earlier weak-flow example changes this stress even at unchanged normal current. The saved physical grids have zero inward current and supply no paired, draining measurement. The one-mouth experiment below measures these two changes for prescribed brane-side boundary laws. An independent weak-force measurement on the same radius and inflow state would give its translational response. If that response has a scalar inertia $M_{\rm eff}$ at release, then $\Delta a_x=\Delta F_x/M_{\rm eff}$. The model has yet to supply that physical acceleration. ### Can the draining brane-side mouth be released?

**The physical acceleration at release is unmeasured.** Both fine-grid preparations drain at the prescribed rate $0.25$. Across the six archived states at incident speeds $0$, $0.01$ and $0.02$, the geometric opening occupied exactly the same cells. At $U=0.02$ the centre of incoming current shifted by $5.15201001853\times10^{-5}$ under the zero-slope density law and $4.82070644309\times10^{-5}$ under the restoring law. The geometric centre stayed at $x=0$ in both. Moving the input aperture by one grid cell gave the expected centroid change of $0.125$. Moving just the carrier field across the original sink violated the imposed current profile. These comparisons distinguish a change of intake location from a change of the mouth position. [Saved-field measurements and source excerpts](_measurements/mouth_release_input_check.txt)

The solver fixes the opening during initialization and returns a stationary carrier field for a supplied flow speed. The dipolar rim load is already calibrated for forces $0$, $0.0075$ and $0.015$. The solver has no time evolution for the geometric centre to which that load can be applied. The one-body action contains a conditional translational coefficient, but the momentum response of this draining state, including acceleration-dependent exchange, is still open. [Solver](../software/stage1_solver/tools/mouth_feed_weak_flow.py#L68) · [One-body coefficient and exchange](../software/em_charge_attribute/reports/u1_body_dynamics.md#L424) · [Inertial response requirement](../software/stage1_solver/reports/pathA_21_emergent_G_mass_bridge.md#L100)

**What would make the test possible.** Let the aperture translate as $B_t=B_0+X(t)$ and carry the same radius and intake law with it. The 0PN boundary model needs an equation for the momentum associated with $X(t)$ under a lateral load. At release, if the response is local and isotropic, its measurable coefficient is $M_{\rm eff}$ in $\Delta F_x=M_{\rm eff}\,\Delta\ddot X$. A brane-side derivation must normalize that response for the same radius and inflow and account once for momentum exchanged at the mouth. With this relation, the test can compare the initial accelerations at zero, half and full calibrated force. ### Brane momentum accompanying slow mouth translation

Use the same flat radius-1 mouth and inward rate $0.25$, with the density-responsive intake law. The feed has zero sideways velocity in the lab. Prescribe a constant mouth velocity $V$ along $x$ and write the neutral carrier as $\psi=e^{-i\mu_0t}f_V(x-Vt,\ldots)$. Its field equation gains the term $+iV\partial_x f_V$ on the Hamiltonian side. The stationary held-mouth solver, after removing the feed phase $e^{iUx}$, has the same equation at $U=-V$. The normal boundary law moves with the aperture. These facts permit reuse of the saved driven envelopes. In this frame the lab momentum density is $\operatorname{Im}(f_V^*\partial_x f_V)$ in the test units. Using the laboratory momentum from the held-flow run would include the uniform feed contribution $U|f_V|^2$. [Carrier momentum density](../software/stage1_solver/reports/pathA_21c_force_from_noether_stress_tensor.md#L22) · [Frame transformation](../software/stage1_solver/tools/mouth_carrier_frame.wl)

Set $g=\left.\partial_V f_V\right|_0$. Differentiate the carrier and boundary equations together, with zero sideways drive at the lab feed and unchanged total inward rate. For any region $\Omega$ carried with the mouth, the slope of its stored carrier momentum along this steady family is

$$
K_\Omega=\left.\frac{dP_{\Omega,x}}{dV}\right|_0
=\int_\Omega \operatorname{Im}\!\left[g^*\,\partial_x f_0+

### What the brane boundary fixes about translation of the opening

For the 0PN translation test, \(X\) denotes the geometric center of a spherical opening with fixed radius 1 and inward rate \(J=0.25\). For a smooth, single-valued scalar phase the local circulation \(\oint(\nabla\theta-\dot X)\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell\) on a closed contour at fixed time is zero. A net circulation from collective motion or a transverse brane response remains a separate hypothesis for later work.

**Density-restoring force on a translated mouth.** Write \(u=|\psi|\) and \(g_X=(1-|\mathbf x-X|^2)_+^3\). The actual restoring condition is \(\partial_w u=-g_X(u-1)\). The explicit force conjugate to translating this coupling toward the companion, with the carrier trace held fixed, is \(-\partial_{X_\parallel}E_R\). Integration by parts gives the existing boundary-law stress integral \(-\int g_X(u-1)\partial_\parallel u\,d^3x\). It enters the momentum budget once. On the width-parameter \(L=48\) grid at separation 4, differentiating the weight gave a paired-minus-alone force \(1.07427402356\times10^{-8}\). **Why that energy does not close the intake.** Let \(\theta\) denote phase. The carrier's phase variation at the opening contributes \(j\,\delta\theta\). ### Which part of the rim must respond to a known force?

In the existing shape representation, \(R(\Omega,0,t)\) gives the radial distance to the rim in the brane plane. About a centered sphere of radius \(a\), the linearized volume centroid along the force direction is \(X=(3/4\pi)\int \cos\theta\,\delta R\,d\Omega\). A dipole displacement \(\delta R=q\cos\theta\) therefore moves the center by \(q\). Breathing and a centered quadrupole both integrate to zero. The normal probe traction \(t_n=3F\cos\theta/(4\pi a^2)\) does work \(F\,\delta X\), so its force calibration and geometric observable refer to the same coordinate. [Parent shape definition](../research/pde/paper/pde.tex#L589) · [Exact projection and probe check](_measurements/mouth_rim_dipole.json)

Project the parent geometry equation onto \(\cos\theta\). With its densitized coefficients and the same normalization for all projected sources \(S_1\), the mode equation is
\[
\mu_\eta\,\partial_t^2 q-\partial_w(T_w\partial_w q)+(K_\eta+2T_\Omega)q=S_1,\qquad X(t)=q(0,t).
\]
This is a distributed effective equation. An actual load on the rim enters by endpoint work \(F\,\delta q(0)\). Reading the distributed source at \(w=0\) as a calibrated point force would mix the two load measures. The projected equation still contains \(\partial_w(T_w\partial_w q)\). As a trace check, vary a profile by \(b(w/L)^2(1-w/L)^2\): its value and first derivative agree at both ends before and after the variation, while the acceleration contribution from the axial operator at the mouth differs by \(2bT_w(0)/[L^2\mu_\eta(0)]\) for regular nonzero coefficients. ## Momentum arriving and momentum leaving

Around throat 2, put two spheres of fixed radii $b<R\ll d$ outside its core. They move with $X_2$. $$
I_s^i=-\int_{S_s}(\Pi^{ij}-G^i\dot X_2^j)n_j\,dA,
\qquad
I_b^i=I_R^i+\int_{b<r<R}f^i\,d^3x

$$

$$

\qquad

### How the intake density and phase set the current entering the sleeve

$$

$$
