## 1. Executive synthesis

### 1.1 The standard of explanation

The intended model makes a deliberately non-fundamental claim about familiar
forces. Gravity, light, electricity, and magnetism are to arise as different
responses of one structured medium and its defects. It would therefore be
surprising if every concept used in Maxwell theory or point-particle mechanics
appeared as a microscopic object with the same name and organization.

An analog succeeds when the same frozen material architecture produces the
relevant observations with controlled approximations and an honest parameter
ledger. It does not have to reproduce the ontology of the comparison theory.
For example, a fluid disturbance can propagate in an effective metric without
the fluid possessing fundamental diffeomorphism invariance. Likewise, a
physical transverse brane wave may reproduce the propagation of light without
the microscopic displacement field being a fundamental \(U(1)\) gauge
potential.

The complementary discipline is that no result follows merely from applying a
familiar name. Calling a throat property *charge* does not also derive
additivity, conservation, a Gauss constraint, a current law, or a magnetic
moment. Calling a circulating mode *spin* does not derive spin-\(1/2\), a
\(720^\circ\) return law, quantization, or a gyromagnetic ratio. Each piece must
be assigned a native mechanism and then calculated or retained as open.

### 1.2 The working physical picture

The working arena is a compressible medium with four spatial coordinates
\((x,y,z,w)\) and a preferred material time. An ordered, finite-thickness region
near \(w=0\) supplies the effective three-dimensional brane on which ordinary
observers and guided modes reside. The surrounding phase is the bulk. The
brane and bulk are different states or organizations of the candidate medium,
not two unrelated substances.

That last sentence states the intended architecture, not a completed
dynamical reduction. The current knit finds no parameter that requires a
fourth arena or separately named substance, but it still leaves
\(\{\rho_{B0},\chi_c,C_{hu}\}\) as irreducibly independent embedding or
surface inputs. The brane is not yet shown to be dynamically yielded by the
underlying medium. Until those inputs are reduced, *one medium* is an honest
architectural hypothesis and labeling closure, not an earned microscopic
derivation.

A particle is not a point inserted into this arena and not a small rigid body
moving through it. It is an extended vortex throat: a localized defect with a
mouth on the brane, a structured core or sleeve, and an open connection to the
bulk. Material may enter, circulate around, deform, and pass through the
defect. The defect's observable properties must consequently be built from its
flow, topology, core response, trapped modes, and collective motion.

A useful provisional decomposition is

\[
v=v_{\rm drain}+v_{\rm swirl}+\delta v,
\]

where \(v_{\rm drain}\) is the intake/return flow associated with the open
throat, \(v_{\rm swirl}\) is circulation around or through that intake, and
\(\delta v\) contains deformations and trapped excitations. This is a
bookkeeping decomposition, not yet a theorem that the nonlinear equations
separate into three independent sectors.

The oriented throat label \(s=\pm1\) records the two \(\pm w\) orientations.
It is not, by itself, a complete electric-charge theory. Likewise, circulation
around a throat is not automatically electromagnetic magnetism: it may belong
to the drain, to a trapped shear mode, to an internal material rotation, or to
a mixture that only a solved branch can distinguish.

### 1.4 Four distinctions that must remain visible

Every force calculation must keep four questions separate:

1. **What labels the source?** Examples include throat orientation, drain
   throughput, circulation, trapped energy, and translational velocity.
2. **What carries the response?** Examples include \(h\), \(u_T\), \(u_L\),
   bulk sound, wall thickness, or a coupled eigenmode.
3. **What does a brane observer measure?** Force, phase shift, trajectory,
   stress, flux, mode conversion, or radiation are observational definitions,
   not automatically identical to the microscopic field.
4. **Which familiar comparison does it satisfy?** An inverse-square falloff,
   Coulomb sign, dipole tensor, two-polarization spectrum, or boost relation is
   a result to compare, not a definition to insert.

One vortex may carry several independent source properties, and one material
field may contain several eigenmodes. Conversely, one familiar force may be a
long-distance summary of more than one material response. These possibilities
are expected in an emergent analog and must be resolved by the common branch,
not prohibited by vocabulary.

### 1.5 What the active v3 light calculation contributes

For the supplied homogeneous, isotropic-inertia quadratic action on the
selected \(D=3\) brane, the transverse and longitudinal branches take the form

\qquad
\omega_L^2=\frac{B_{\rm comp}}{\rho_{\rm br}}k^2.

In the selected homogeneous \(D=3\) action, the two blocks are linearly

That is why the current S11c work should complete before this paper assigns a

adequately confined. The useful output is not a binary verdict that the medium
is or is not Maxwell. It is a response map: which channels exist, how they
couple, where energy goes, and what a brane observer can detect.

The separate MacCullagh reference-state investigation is similarly upstream

### 1.6 What counts as success

The light and electromagnetic analog need not be exact Maxwell theory. It does
need to reproduce the pieces of known physics it claims to explain, within an
explicit regime. At minimum, the completed program must ask whether:

- a stable, guided transverse branch exists with the required polarization
  count, speed, dispersion, and loss bounds;
- additional longitudinal, thickness, and bulk modes are absent from ordinary
  observations or have acceptably weak, gapped, screened, or otherwise
  characterized couplings;
- the oriented vortex throat produces a stable static interaction with the
  measured far-field form and sign;
- translating and circulating that same defect generates the corresponding
  velocity-dependent and dipolar responses without inserting their source
  laws from Maxwell theory;
- energy, momentum, and angular momentum balance after brane--bulk and
  reservoir exchange are included;
- all compared observables come from the same frozen material and throat
  branch;
- the number of independent postulates and calibrations leaves meaningful
  held-out relationships rather than one adjustable input per observation.

A departure is not automatically a failure. A growing mode, unbounded energy,
inconsistent balance law, unacceptable optical loss, wrong measured force, or
uncontrolled preferred-frame signal can be a failure. The mere presence of a
physical variable that Maxwell theory represents as gauge-redundant is not.

## 2. Translating standard-physics language into native meaning

### 2.1 The semantic-import problem

Standard physical terms are compressed packages. In Maxwell theory, *charge*
usually arrives together with a conserved current, a Gauss constraint,
additivity, an electrostatic field, a force law, and a magnetic response under
motion. In quantum theory, *spin* arrives with a rotation representation,
discrete measurement outcomes, quantized angular momentum, statistics, and a
magnetic moment. Those packages are extremely useful after the theory has been
specified. They are hazardous while constructing an analog from different
microscopic objects.

The recurring error has the following form:

1. identify one resemblance, such as two throat orientations or a transverse
   wave;
2. apply the familiar name, such as *charge* or *photon*;
3. unconsciously import the remaining properties attached to that name;
4. later report those imported properties as consequences of the medium.

The opposite error is also possible: reject a viable analog because the native
object is not organized the way the comparison theory organizes it. This
paper avoids both errors by translating one operational role at a time.

### 2.2 The native-meaning card

Every borrowed term will be evaluated with the same nine-part card:

| Item | Question | Required output |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Standard operational role:** What observation or relationship does the familiar term summarize? | A statement in terms of measurements, not formalism alone. |
| 2 | **Native object:** What field, flow, defect datum, collective coordinate, or boundary condition could perform that role here? | A model variable and its locus. |
| 3 | **Degrees of freedom:** Is it a propagating mode, constrained variable, topological label, zero mode, or composite response? | A mode/constraint count and regime. |
| 4 | **Source law:** How does the vortex throat excite it? | Variation of an action, matched core condition, or collective-coordinate overlap--not an analogy-defined source. |
| 5 | **Observable:** How does a brane-bound apparatus respond to it? | A force, trajectory, phase, flux, stress, conversion probability, or radiation observable. |
| 6 | **Transformations:** What happens under rotations, reflection, time reversal, \(w\to-w\), circulation reversal, and reversal of motion? | A transformation table for native variables and observables. |
| 7 | **Protection and balance:** What conserves or stabilizes it, and where can it leak? | A Noether, topological, boundary, or open-reservoir ledger. |
| 8 | **Comparison regime and departure:** Where does it reproduce the familiar result, and what else does it predict? | Controlled limits, residuals, and observational bounds. |
| 9 | **Evidence and cost:** Which pieces are derived, conditional, postulated, calibrated, candidate, or open? | A claim-status row and count of independent inputs. |

No familiar term is accepted or rejected on a single row. A mode can be an
excellent classical light analog without being a localized photon. A binary
vortex orientation can be a useful charge-sign carrier without yet furnishing
additive conserved charge. A stable circulating defect can provide intrinsic
angular momentum without yet realizing spin-\(1/2\).

### 2.3 Direction of derivation

The required direction is

\[
\text{native mechanics}
\longrightarrow
\text{projected brane observable}
\longrightarrow
\text{comparison with known physics}.
\]

The reverse direction is useful for discovering requirements but not for
claiming a derivation. Starting from a target force and choosing a source term
that produces it identifies a possible completion; it does not show that the
vortex throat carries that source.

Requirement discovery and realization must therefore be kept separate:

- **Requirement discovery:** translate known physics into falloff, tensor,
  sign, polarization, transformation, conservation, and loss requirements.
- **Realization:** solve or reduce the candidate medium and export those same
  quantities without redefining the source after seeing the comparison.
- **Comparison:** classify matches and departures on the frozen branch.

This rule is particularly important for the moving-charge relation. The
expression \(j=sV\) is a legitimate comparison target and may emerge as a
leading collective-coordinate result. It is not the native definition of a
current. The native calculation translates the complete vortex solution and
computes which field overlaps are proportional to its velocity, circulation,
orientation, acceleration, and deformation.

#### Gauge

In standard electromagnetism, gauge redundancy helps organize two transverse
photon polarizations, the electrostatic constraint, source conservation, and
relations among electric and magnetic couplings. In this material model, the
first question is not *where is the microscopic gauge field?* It is *which
native mechanisms perform each of those jobs?*

The curl stiffness being insensitive to a longitudinal spatial gradient is
one fact. A redundancy of the full time-dependent action is a stronger fact. A
Gauss constraint is stronger again. The analog may replace these roles with a

## 3. Native ontology: medium, brane, bulk, and vortex throat

### 3.1 One medium in two organized phases

The one-medium hypothesis does not mean that every region has the same
effective mechanical properties. The working architecture distinguishes an
ordered brane phase from a de-structured bulk phase:

| Region | Working material character | Required role |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordered brane** | finite-thickness, inertia-bearing, compressible, and capable of transverse response | supports guided light, material response, and the mouths of vortex throats |
| **De-structured bulk** | the same underlying medium without the brane's ordered shear response | receives throat throughput and return flow without furnishing an ordinary competing light channel |
| **Interface** | a finite transition region with thickness, tilt, conversion, and response laws | determines localization, leakage, mode conversion, and the throat's connection to the bulk |

The order field commonly denoted \(\chi_B\) labels this organization in the
current architecture. The existence of a suitable two-phase material and the
required contrast in shear response are postulated, not derived from the GNLS
field alone. In particular, the statement that the ordered phase carries
transverse stiffness while the de-structured phase carries none is a
requirement on the deeper substructure. It is not established by calling the
first region a brane and the second a bulk.

The knit currently closes this as one medium only at the labeling and
bookkeeping level. It leaves \(\rho_{B0}\), \(\chi_c\), and \(C_{hu}\)
irreducibly independent, and it does not prove that the bulk dynamics generate
the ordered brane. Those are architecture inputs to be counted, not a second
substance that has already been discovered and not a reduction that has
already been completed.

This distinction is physical rather than merely geometric. The brane is not an
infinitely thin coordinate surface, and the bulk is not an empty exterior.
Both contain the candidate medium. The intended localization mechanism comes
from a change in material organization across a finite transition region.

The two sides of that contrast are equally important. If the ordered phase has
no transverse stiffness, the proposed light branch does not exist. If the
de-structured phase carries the same transverse branch, light need not remain
confined and a trapped shear mode need not remain trapped. This two-sided
requirement is recorded in
research/pde_ledger_v3/steps/S9_light_requires_shear.md and
research/pde_ledger_v3/SUBSTRATE_REQUIREMENTS.md.

### 3.2 The fields are not interchangeable descriptions

Several fields and reduced variables appear near the brane. Their geometrical
proximity does not make them alternative names for one degree of freedom.

| Object | Native role in the current architecture | What it is not automatically |
|---|---|---|
| \(\psi=\sqrt{\rho}\,e^{i\theta}\) | coarse-grained complex medium/order parameter in the GNLS description | the deeper shear-bearing substructure |
| \(\rho,\theta\) | density and phase variables of that coarse-grained medium | transverse photon polarizations |

The identification of \(u_a\) as material displacement is load-bearing. Its

The separation above is a kinematic and interpretive starting point. Coupled

When that occurs, the physical carrier is the complete eigenvector and its
observable overlap, not whichever component supplied its historical name.

### 3.3 A particle is an extended vortex throat

The particle ontology begins with a negative statement: a particle is neither
a point source nor a small solid inclusion. It is an extended defect in the
medium. The working defect punctures the ordered brane, opens into one of the
two transverse directions, and permits material and field structure to connect
the brane mouth to a bulk-facing interior.

The phrase *vortex throat* joins two necessary aspects:

- **Throat:** the defect is an open conduit with a mouth, interior or sleeve,
  and bulk connection. Throughput, endpoint behavior, and return flow are part
  of its state.
- **Vortex:** its identity and response may involve circulation, winding,
  vorticity, or framed core structure rather than a rigid surface.

The word *vortex* is not yet a completed topological classification. The
current model still has to determine whether the protected object is a phase
vortex, a circulation defect, a vortex sheet or tube in four dimensions, a
composite of the brane order and the medium phase, or a dynamically maintained
open structure without strict topological protection. Those possibilities
have different codimension, conservation, reconnection, and stability laws.
The common name records the intended physical character; it does not settle
the missing classification.

The throat should be resolved into at least five loci:

1. **Mouth:** the brane-facing region where far-field modes and observable
   boundary data are attached.
2. **Core:** the region in which the ordered structure, density, phase, and
   flow cease to be small perturbations.
3. **Sleeve or interior:** the finite bulk-facing support through which modes,
   material, and stress may travel.
4. **Endpoint or open port:** the region supplying outgoing, return, or
   reservoir conditions.
5. **Exterior brane and bulk:** the domains in which far-field observables and
   leakage are extracted.

A boundary condition at the mouth is therefore a reduced statement about the
core solution. A fixed-value, fixed-flux, fixed-source, or mixed ensemble may
be a useful far-field problem, but the physical vortex must select or realize
that ensemble through its core dynamics. Describing a datum as *clamped* does
not imply the existence of a solid clamp. In a vortex model it would have to
mean something such as protected winding, fixed flux, a nonrelaxable framed
core, or a dynamically maintained boundary value.

### 3.4 One defect can carry several source properties

A standard point particle packages mass, charge, spin, and position into a
compact list of labels. The vortex throat distributes the analogous
information across its solution:

| Native property | Possible far-field role | Present status |

| circulation or vorticity | supplies a possible angular-momentum or magnetic source | candidate; its allowed planes, protection, and coupling remain to be classified |
| trapped shear energy | supplies possible outward support and part of the dressed inertia | geon/support hypothesis; a solved stable configuration is still required, and the old support-only lepton mass tower is falsified |
| shape and thickness moments | determine finite-size response, boundary selection, and multipoles | branch-dependent |
| core phase and order data | may determine additivity, annihilation, reconnection, and allowed species | open |

These properties should not be collapsed into one scalar *particle strength*.
They may be related on the final branch, but that relation is itself a
prediction or calibration. For example, the same core solution may connect
drain throughput to trapped energy and electric source strength. Until it
does, mass coupling, charge coupling, and circulation are separate entries in
the ledger even though they belong to one defect.

### 3.5 Motion is translation of a field configuration

The position of a vortex throat is a collective coordinate, not the location
of an underlying bead. A leading moving ansatz has the form

\[
\Phi(X,t)
=
\Phi_0\!\left(X-X_{\rm th}(t);s,\Gamma,\ldots\right)
+\delta\Phi(X,t),
\]

where \(\Phi_0\) denotes the complete stationary or steadily driven defect
profile, \(X_{\rm th}(t)\) is its brane position, \(\Gamma\) schematically
denotes circulation data, and \(\delta\Phi\) contains the deformation and
radiative response induced by motion.

Substitution into the action and balance laws can then determine:

- the translational zero mode and effective inertia;
- added mass and dressing from displaced or co-moving material;
- Berry, gyroscopic, or Magnus-type terms;
- velocity-dependent deformation of the core and sleeve;
- source overlaps onto \(h\), \(u_T\), \(u_L\), thickness, and bulk channels;
- dissipative or radiative forces;
- the conditions under which a leading source happens to be proportional to
  \(s\dot X_{\rm th}\).

This is the native replacement for inserting \(j=sV\). If that relation is
recovered, it will be an output of vortex translation in a stated regime. If
additional acceleration, circulation, memory, or deformation terms appear,
they are part of the analog's characterized response.

Solid-body phrases such as *rolling without slipping* should likewise be
translated before use. The relevant vortex questions are whether the core is
advected with the local medium, whether circulation is frozen into the flow,
how phase and traction match across the core, and whether relative motion is
accommodated by slip, conversion, mutual friction, or radiation.

### 3.6 The defect and the brane are open subsystems

Material passing into a throat does not disappear from the complete model.
The local brane, the throat interior, and any finite numerical domain are open
subsystems of the common medium. Their continuity, energy, momentum, and
angular-momentum equations must include:

- flux through the mouth and interior;
- conversion between ordered and de-structured phases;
- work done by moving interfaces;
- outgoing wave or sound flux;
- distributed or remote return;
- exchange with any maintained background flow or named reservoir.

Consequently, a nonconserved quantity on the projected brane may still belong
to a closed full-system ledger. Conversely, calling the full medium conserved
does not prove that a particular throat label, circulation, or effective
charge is protected. The balance law must identify the current and every port
through which it can change.

This is especially important for stationary backgrounds. A time-independent
defect profile can be a driven steady state carrying nonzero throughput. It is
not necessarily an equilibrium extremum of a conservative energy. Passivity,
Onsager symmetry, and equilibrium stability cannot be imported without
checking whether the background drain and return make their hypotheses
applicable.

## 4. Four-dimensional vortex kinematics and the meaning of rotation

The word *spin* is especially dangerous in this model. A particle is not a point carrying an abstract label, and it is not a little rigid ball rotating about an axis. It is an extended vortex-throat configuration in a four-dimensional medium. Any proposed spin-like property must therefore be translated into a specific motion, circulation, internal orientation, or topology of that configuration.

### 4.1 Rotation occurs in a plane

In three spatial dimensions, a rotation plane can be represented by the single axis perpendicular to it. That convenient axis picture does not generalize unchanged to four spatial dimensions.

An infinitesimal four-dimensional rotation is described by an antisymmetric tensor

\[
\Omega_{IJ}=-\Omega_{JI},
\qquad I,J\in\{1,2,3,w\},
\]

with six independent components. A generic finite rotation can be reduced to simultaneous rotations through two independent angles in two orthogonal planes. It is therefore better to ask *in which plane or planes is the configuration turning?* than to ask for its one rotation axis.

Once the brane selects a normal direction \(n^I\), locally identified with the \(w\) direction, the six planes separate into:

- three planes tangent to the brane, described by \(\Omega_{ab}\);
- three mixed brane-normal planes, described by \(\Omega_{aw}\).

From the brane viewpoint, the first set can be dualized into a familiar three-component axial vector. The second set transforms as a brane vector and has no ordinary three-dimensional rigid-body analogue. Thus “rotation in 4D” does not by itself explain a spin-like state; it creates several physically distinct candidates that the model must distinguish.

### 4.2 Vorticity and angular momentum are not the same object

For a bulk velocity field \(v_I\), the local vorticity is naturally the antisymmetric two-form

\[
\varpi_{IJ}
=
\partial_I v_J-\partial_J v_I.
\]

Relative to the brane it decomposes into the tangential components \(\varpi_{ab}\) and the mixed components \(\varpi_{aw}\). Only the tangential part has the familiar brane dual

\[
\omega_{\rm br}^{\,a}
=
\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{abc}\varpi_{bc}.
\]

Calling either \(\varpi_{IJ}\) or \(\omega_{\rm br}^{a}\) “spin” would be premature. They measure local circulation of the medium. The conserved angular momentum, when one exists, is instead an integral quantity with a schematic form

\[
J_{IJ}
=
\int d^4X\,
\left(X_I T_{0J}-X_J T_{0I}\right)
+J^{\rm internal}_{IJ}.
\]

The first term is orbital angular momentum of the distributed fields. The second is present only if the medium possesses an independent internal rotation, director, microrotation, or couple-stress degree of freedom. This distinction matters directly for the MacCullagh question: a curl-elastic medium can carry antisymmetric stress, and local angular-momentum balance may then require precisely such an internal channel. It cannot be silently identified with ordinary fluid vorticity.

### 4.3 Drain and swirl are independent defect properties

A throat can absorb or expel medium without circulating, and a vortex can circulate without having a net drain. The source sign \(s\), the radial flux, and the circulation orientation must therefore remain separate labels.

At least four kinematic patterns are available:

1. circulation wholly within planes tangent to the brane;
2. circulation in one or more mixed \(a\)-\(w\) planes;
3. helical intake, in which inward transport and tangential circulation coexist;
4. circulation stored primarily in a field trapped around the throat rather than in steady transport through it.

Four dimensions also allow a double rotation: the configuration may circulate in two orthogonal planes at once. Whether a stable throat selects one plane, a paired set, or a correlated texture is a dynamical and topological question. None should be assumed merely from the dimensionality of the bulk.

### 4.4 Reversals that must not be conflated

Several operations can look like “turning the particle around” from the brane while changing different physical data:

| Operation | What is reversed | What need not reverse |
|---|---|---|
| source reversal | \(s\), hence source versus sink behavior | circulation |
| circulation reversal | orientation of \(\varpi_{IJ}\) or a circulation integral | source sign |
| translational reversal | throat velocity \(\dot X_{\rm th}^{a}\) | internal circulation |
| time reversal | all genuinely velocity-odd quantities, subject to the model’s reservoir rules | geometric handedness |
| brane parity | orientation of selected brane coordinates | normal orientation |
| normal reflection | \(w\rightarrow-w\), or \(n^I\rightarrow-n^I\) | brane parity |

This bookkeeping is necessary before assigning interpretations such as charge conjugation, helicity reversal, or antiparticle. In the present model those names are targets for a derived correspondence, not synonyms for one of the rows.

### 4.5 A \(2\pi\) rotation is still a full geometric turn

Four spatial dimensions do not change the angular measure of a simple rotation. A rotation by \(2\pi\) in one plane returns ordinary vectors and rigid configurations to their original orientation. For a generic double rotation, both independent plane angles must return to equivalent values.

Consequently, the mere statement that a vortex rotates in four dimensions does not produce spin-\(\tfrac12\), a sign change under \(2\pi\), or a \(4\pi\) return rule. Those behaviors, if the analog supports them at all, must arise from the configuration space of the extended defect and not from there being one extra spatial coordinate.

### 4.6 A framed vortex could have a nontrivial rotation path

There is nevertheless a more promising classical analog. An extended vortex may carry internal framing: for example, a marked phase ribbon, a twist of its core, a linked trapped mode, or an orientation of its connection through the brane. A \(2\pi\) rotation of the visible mouth could restore its apparent brane orientation while leaving that framing on a different path or in a twisted state. A second \(2\pi\) rotation might then unwind it.

For this to be more than an illustration, the model would need to establish all of the following:

- a well-defined configuration space for the finite-energy vortex throat;
- a two-to-one relation, or another nontrivial covering relation, between visible orientation and full internal configuration;
- a noncontractible rotation path or an energetic barrier that makes the distinction persistent;
- protection against unwinding through core collapse, reconnection, or escape into the bulk;
- a stated observable that distinguishes the two configurations or paths.

Even if those conditions are met, a classical \(4\pi\) return property would not by itself derive quantum spin-\(\tfrac12\), fermionic exchange statistics, or measurement outcomes. It would supply a concrete mechanical carrier on which a later statistical or quantum-like construction might act.

### 4.7 Chirality and helicity require native definitions

The familiar three-dimensional helicity density \(v\cdot(\nabla\times v)\) relies on the special duality between a two-form and a vector in three dimensions. In four spatial dimensions, vorticity remains a two-form, so there is no unique native scalar with exactly that interpretation.

Candidate handedness labels in this model include:

- brane-projected helicity of the tangential flow;
- the relative orientation of tangential vorticity \(\varpi_{ab}\) and the brane normal;
- a mixed-plane texture carried by \(\varpi_{aw}\);
- self-dual versus anti-self-dual components of a four-dimensional vorticity two-form, where the background geometry makes that decomposition meaningful;
- circular polarization or orbital winding of a trapped transverse shear mode;
- the source sign \(s\).

These are not interchangeable. A future claim that a defect has “chirality” must name the relevant transformation, the field that carries the handedness, and the mechanism that prevents it from being continuously erased.

### 4.8 Candidate native meanings of spin

The following possibilities should be investigated separately:

| Candidate | Physical carrier | Immediate strength | Missing derivation |
|---|---|---|---|
| hydrodynamic swirl | circulation of the medium around or through the throat | concrete local motion | quantized magnitude and angular-momentum balance |
| trapped-field angular momentum | circulating transverse shear or another bound mode | naturally extended and can couple to the light sector | stable spectrum and relation to defect identity |
| internal microrotation | an added director or rotational field of the ordered phase | can support couple stress and MacCullagh-like mechanics | evidence that this degree of freedom belongs in the substrate |
| framed-vortex topology | twist, linking, or nontrivial rotation path of the core | possible \(4\pi\)-type classical behavior | actual topology and protection of the throat solution |
| composite rotation | correlated throat circulation, trapped field, and core framing | may combine mechanical and topological stability | risks becoming underconstrained without a derived solution |

A standing wave near a throat is not automatically spinning, and circulating energy is not automatically intrinsic spin. The conserved \(J_{IJ}\), its brane projection, and the transformation of the complete defect must be computed before any one candidate receives the familiar name.

### 4.9 What this section commits the model to calculate

For any explicit vortex-throat solution, the model should report:

1. the nonzero circulation or vorticity components;
2. whether they lie in tangential, mixed, or paired rotation planes;
3. the independent source, circulation, and framing labels;
4. the total angular-momentum tensor and any internal contribution;
5. the action of time reversal, brane parity, and normal reflection;
6. the homotopy class of a \(2\pi\) rotation path and whether a \(4\pi\) path is distinct;
7. the coupling of those labels to a propagating or trapped transverse mode.

Until those outputs exist, *spin*, *chirality*, and *helicity* should appear in the ledger as interpretation questions, not as imported properties.

## 5. The light sector: transverse propagation in a physical brane

The light-sector question should be phrased operationally: does the ordered brane support a physical disturbance whose observable branch reproduces the relevant behavior of light? The construction need not begin with a gauge potential, and it need not make electromagnetism fundamental. It must, however, earn the properties being compared with electromagnetism.

### 5.1 Minimum target for the classical light analog

At the linear classical level, the desired branch should provide:

- two transverse propagating polarizations on a locally three-dimensional brane;
- an approximately common propagation cone with speed \(c_\gamma\);
- positive energy and a stable initial-value problem;
- sufficiently weak dispersion and loss over the regime claimed by the model;
- confinement to the ordered phase, or a quantitative account of leakage;
- a defined coupling to source-like vortex throats.

Localized photons, energy quantization, quantum statistics, and measurement rules are stronger targets. They should not be treated as consequences of obtaining two transverse plane-wave solutions. Conversely, a physical medium is not disqualified because it contains additional material modes; the question is whether those modes are consistent, distinguishable, and sufficiently decoupled in the relevant regime.

The confinement requirement is two-sided. It is not enough for the brane to support shear. The de-structured bulk must also lack, gap, strongly attenuate, or otherwise reject the corresponding low-energy channel. Otherwise the light analog leaks away from the brane and cannot support the intended trapped configurations.

### 5.2 The declared scalar GNLS field is not yet the light carrier

The base field

\[
\Psi=\sqrt{\rho}\,e^{i\theta}
\]

naturally supplies density and phase. Away from singular cores, its ordinary superflow is proportional to \(\nabla\theta\), so the regular scalar sector is naturally compressional or longitudinal. Vortex singularities add circulation and topology, but they do not by themselves produce a general two-polarization transverse wave field throughout the ordered brane.

The v3 ledger therefore introduces an ordered-phase displacement field \(u_a\) as deeper substrate structure rather than claiming that the scalar GNLS variables already contain the full light sector. This is a modeling postulate under audit. It does not establish that no multicomponent condensate or more elaborate superfluid could support transverse modes; it says that the presently declared single scalar field does not yet supply the required physical carrier.

That distinction protects the model from an interpretive shortcut: \(\Psi\) may organize density, phase, interface, and throat behavior while the transverse light analog belongs to an additional ordered substructure.

### 5.3 The selected linear brane action

On the locally homogeneous ordered background, the current ledger studies the quadratic action

\[
S_u
=
\frac12\int dt\,d^3x\,
\left[
\rho_{\rm br}\,\dot u^2
-\mu_R(\nabla\times u)^2
-B_{\rm comp}(\nabla\cdot u)^2
\right].
\]

Here:

- \(u_a\) is a material displacement, not an electromagnetic vector potential;
- \(\rho_{\rm br}>0\) is the effective inertia density of the ordered structure;
- \(\mu_R>0\) controls the curl or rotational elastic response;
- \(B_{\rm comp}>0\) controls physical compression.

With the Helmholtz split

\[
u=u_T+u_L,
\qquad
\nabla\cdot u_T=0,
\qquad
\nabla\times u_L=0,
\]

the homogeneous plane-wave equation is

\[
\rho_{\rm br}\omega^2 a
=
\mu_R\!\left[k^2a-k(k\cdot a)\right]
+B_{\rm comp}k(k\cdot a).
\]

It yields

\[
\omega_T^2
=
\frac{\mu_R}{\rho_{\rm br}}\,k^2,
\qquad
\omega_L^2
=
\frac{B_{\rm comp}}{\rho_{\rm br}}\,k^2.
\]

Thus the transverse light speed candidate is

\[
c_\gamma^2=\frac{\mu_R}{\rho_{\rm br}}.
\]

These are properties of a material system: the transverse modes describe shear displacement, and the longitudinal mode describes compression. No redundancy is required to interpret them.

retains \(D-1\) propagating directions but reduces the basis-independent
exactly-transverse count to \(D-2\). Once \(B_{\rm comp}\) is present, the
light candidate and sound branch coexist as different motions of the same
ordered material on the selected homogeneous \(D=3\) branch.

That coexistence is not automatically a defect. It becomes a phenomenological problem only if the longitudinal branch couples to light-like sources or detectors too strongly, propagates in a forbidden regime, or destabilizes the brane.

### 5.5 What “photon” can mean at this stage

The transverse normal modes justify, at most, the term *classical light-sector disturbance*. A localized packet is a superposition of those modes. A trapped standing pattern around a throat may be a classical geon candidate. Neither is yet a photon in the quantum sense.

To use the stronger name, the model would still need a mechanism for at least some of the following:

- stable localized energy packets or emission events;
- a discrete or effectively quantized exchange spectrum;
- a relation between polarization and angular momentum;
- source and detector selection rules;
- statistics for multiple excitations;
- a controlled classical or quantum limit matching known optical behavior.

This hierarchy lets the analog be tested in stages. Failure to derive quantization does not erase a successful classical transverse-wave analog, but a classical mode should not inherit quantum properties by vocabulary.

### 5.6 Uniform decoupling does not imply global decoupling

On the selected uniform background, the v3 audit finds an exact quadratic separation between \(u_T\), \(u_L\), thickness or interface variables, and the homogeneous background sector. That result is important but local to its assumptions.

When coefficients vary across an interface or defect background, the projectors used in the Helmholtz decomposition no longer commute with the full differential operator. Gradients of \(\rho_{\rm br}\), \(\mu_R\), \(B_{\rm comp}\), the order parameter, or throat geometry can then mix the nominal transverse sector with

\[
\{\theta,e_W,u_L\}
\]

and with other background perturbations. The active S11c work is calculating those variable-coefficient off-diagonal blocks. The available per-engine results identify such couplings, while the final high-memory cross-engine residual check is still pending and should not be preempted by this interpretive document.

The physical outputs ultimately needed are scattering amplitudes or transfer coefficients, not merely a statement that mixing terms exist. In particular:

1. how much transverse energy converts into compression near a throat;
2. how much enters thickness or interface motion;
3. whether any mode tunnels or radiates into the de-structured bulk;
4. whether the mixing is suppressed in a controlled long-wavelength or thin-interface limit;
5. whether polarization degeneracy survives on realistic backgrounds.

### 5.7 The MacCullagh question is a material-consistency question

For a rigid infinitesimal rotation

\[
u_I=\Lambda_{IJ}X_J,
\qquad
\Lambda_{IJ}=-\Lambda_{JI},
\]

the symmetric strain vanishes while the antisymmetric gradient, and therefore the curl-like measure, need not. A conventional frame-indifferent elastic solid does not assign bulk energy to a global rigid rotation. A naive curl-energy medium can appear to do so.

This is the central reason to investigate MacCullagh-like mechanics rather than simply adopt its wave equation. The model must explain what the curl measures physically:

- Is \(u\) measured relative to an independently selected ordered reference frame?
- Does the ordered phase contain internal rotors or directors that do not co-rotate with ordinary displacement?
- Are boundary conditions or the brane normal part of the physical reference structure?
- Does the stress obey local angular-momentum balance?
- Are distributed couples or an internal angular-momentum current required?
- Can the proposed substrate remain passive, conservative, and stable?

One origin route has already failed: `pathA_35` attempted to derive \(\mu_R\)
from a polar substructure \(P\) and returned
`FAIL_COUPLE_STRESS_NOGO`. This is a specific route exclusion, not a theorem
that every generalized continuum is impossible, but it must not be rerun under
a broader label.

A Cosserat or micropolar completion remains conceivable because it adds an
independent microrotation and couple stress. It is not the unfinished end of
the failed \(P\)-substructure calculation. It is new parent-action content
with new fields, moduli, modes, and observational consequences. If proposed,
it must receive a new frozen definition and return through S9--S11c rather
than being appended only to the favorable transverse result. The present
stiffness remains postulated while that broader material-admissibility
question is open.

### 5.8 Gauge redundancy is not an admission requirement

The displacement action has a curl sector that is insensitive to adding a gradient *within that sector*. But the kinetic term and physical compression term respond to the same change. The complete material theory therefore does not possess the electromagnetic gauge redundancy

\[
A_\mu\rightarrow A_\mu+\partial_\mu\lambda
\]

merely because a curl appears in its energy.

That is acceptable for this project. Gauge redundancy is one efficient description of classical electromagnetism, not a rule that every tangible analog must adopt at its microscopic level. The analog should instead be judged by its physical observables:

- the propagating mode count and dispersion;
- stability and energy positivity;
- coupling to sources and detectors;
- the absence or suppression of unwanted observable effects;
- the emergence, if any, of a gauge-like low-energy description.

An effective gauge redundancy would be especially useful if it arose as a constraint, a low-energy equivalence, or an unobservable internal relabeling. It is not necessary to declare \(u_a\) gauge-redundant in order to retain the two transverse shear modes. Nor would declaring it so eliminate a real compressional motion of the brane.

### 5.9 Light, throats, and geon-like states

Because a throat changes the background most strongly near its core and interface, it is the natural place for the transverse sector to interact with source sign, circulation, translation, and trapped internal structure. A viable classical source model should derive these couplings from the combined action rather than impose electromagnetic labels on them.

The assumption that the de-structured bulk carries no comparable shear channel helps confine a transverse disturbance, but it is not sufficient by itself. Interface motion, compressional conversion, evanescent tails, and nonlinear radiation can still drain energy. A geon-like state requires a normalizable or sufficiently long-lived bound configuration after all of those channels are included.

The possible support role must not revive the old support-only lepton mass
tower. That construction gives the harmonic family \(1:9:25\), compared with
the observed approximate \(1:207:3477\), and is falsified. This does not rule
out every dressed mass relation once throughput, geometry, and backreaction
are allowed to vary; it does rule out using the trapped support harmonics
alone as the particle-family mass spectrum.

Likewise, a standing transverse pattern does not automatically provide intrinsic angular momentum. Linear polarization can form a standing wave with zero net angular momentum; circular polarization or orbital winding can carry angular momentum, but the relevant integral must be evaluated for the complete throat-plus-field solution. This is the point at which Sections 3 and 4 join the light-sector audit.

## 6. Gauge: a standard-physics mechanism, not a required microscopic ontology

The material analog does not need to contain a microscopic electromagnetic
gauge potential merely because Maxwell theory uses one. It does need to
reproduce, replace, or visibly depart from every observable relationship that
the Maxwell gauge structure helps organize. This shifts the question from
“where is the gauge field?” to “which native mechanism performs each physical
job?”

### 6.1 Gauge symmetry is a redundancy of description

In classical electromagnetism the potentials admit the transformation

\[
A_\mu\longrightarrow A_\mu+\partial_\mu\lambda
\]

without changing the electromagnetic field tensor. Different potentials on
the same gauge orbit describe the same physical state. The redundancy is
powerful because, together with the form of the action and its constraints, it
organizes the physical mode count, the Gauss relation, source consistency, and
the relation between electrostatic and transverse propagation sectors.

None of that makes the redundancy itself a tangible substance. A mechanical
medium may instead have only physical coordinates and still possess two
transverse waves. A constrained lattice can also produce an effective gauge
description even though every microscopic constituent is physical. Gauge can
therefore occur at three conceptually different levels:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| microscopic redundancy | multiple fundamental field descriptions represent exactly the same state |
| emergent redundancy | a constrained low-energy sector is most economically represented by gauge variables |
| no gauge redundancy | every field variable is physical, while the observable spectrum happens to approximate part of a gauge theory |

The present brane-displacement proposal is in the third category unless an
additional constraint or low-energy equivalence is derived.

### 6.2 The observable jobs must be separated

It is too coarse to ask whether the model “has gauge.” The relevant tests are:

| Standard-EM job | What must be checked in the material analog |
|---|---|
| two radiative polarizations | the physical normal-mode count and detector overlaps |
| no independent longitudinal photon | whether longitudinal material motion is absent, gapped, decoupled, or observably distinct |
| Gauss relation | how static source data determine the nonradiative far field |
| local source consistency | whether a source balance law follows from throat and reservoir dynamics |
| conserved additive charge | what defect current carries it and through which boundaries it can change |
| common electric/magnetic normalization | whether static and moving couplings are derived from the same frozen throat branch |
| electric versus current-force pattern | whether the complete action, constraints, and source response yield the observed relative signs and tensors |
| polarization transport | whether background gradients split, rotate, attenuate, or convert the two transverse modes |

A model may pass one row and fail another. Two transverse waves do not imply a
Gauss law; a conserved defect count does not determine a force kernel; and a
curl energy does not fix how a moving throat sources it.

### 6.3 Curl-null stiffness is not full gauge invariance

For the isolated elastic energy

\[
E_{\rm curl}[u]
=
\frac{\mu_R}{2}\int d^3x\,|\nabla\times u|^2,
\]

the replacement \(u\rightarrow u+\nabla\phi\) leaves the curl term unchanged.
This is a null direction of one part of the energy. It is not yet a gauge
symmetry of the complete material action.

The inertia term changes under a time-dependent gradient addition, and the
physical compression energy

\[
E_{\rm comp}[u]
=
\frac{B_{\rm comp}}{2}\int d^3x\,(\nabla\cdot u)^2
\]

changes even statically. The resulting longitudinal displacement changes the
material density and carries kinetic and elastic energy. It is therefore a
real sound-like motion, not a duplicate description to be divided out.

A genuine electromagnetic-style gauge theory would require the complete
action, its temporal variables, its source coupling, and its constraint
algebra to transform consistently. The v3 transverse eigenvalue result does
not establish those additional facts, nor does it need them in order to
establish two physical shear modes.

### 6.4 Gauge symmetry alone does not determine all Maxwell behavior

Earlier discussions sometimes compressed the relative electric and magnetic
force signs into the phrase “the gauge-theoretic dual sign.” That wording is
too strong. Gauge redundancy is an essential organizing principle of Maxwell
theory, but the observed sign and normalization pattern also depends on the
Lorentzian action, positive energy, the way conserved sources couple, the
constraint sector, and the transverse propagator.

Accordingly:

- a curl symmetry by itself does not produce electrostatic repulsion;
- a positive transverse stiffness by itself does not derive a current source;
- a Gauss-like constraint by itself does not select the throat’s charge
  magnitude;
- separately fitted electric and magnetic kernels do not become unified
  merely because both resemble parts of Maxwell theory.

For this analog the meaningful unification test is whether the same solved
throat branch fixes the static source, the moving source, and their relative
coefficient without a second retuning.

### 6.5 Native replacements are allowed, but each needs a balance law

A physical medium has several possible ways to perform jobs that gauge theory
packages together:

- a topologically protected vortex number can provide a conserved defect
  label;
- material continuity can provide a local current law;
- a core boundary functional can select the static source ensemble;
- incompressibility or another hard constraint can remove a low-energy
  longitudinal response;
- a large gap or weak overlap can hide an additional material mode;
- a common collective-coordinate action can tie static and velocity-dependent
  couplings;
- a reservoir-complete conservation law can replace conservation within the
  projected brane alone.

These are possibilities, not automatic substitutions. For example,
\(s=\pm1\) gives two orientations, but it does not by itself provide additive
integer charge. A vortex can reconnect, cross a boundary, reverse through a
core event, or exchange winding with the bulk unless the action and boundary
conditions forbid those processes. The full balance must have the form

\[
\partial_t q_{\rm native}
+\nabla_a j_{\rm native}^{\,a}
=
\mathcal S_{\rm core}
+\mathcal F_{\rm bulk}
+\mathcal F_{\rm reservoir},
\]

with every right-hand-side term either shown to vanish in the claimed regime
or included in the observable accounting.

### 6.6 The repository currently contains distinct gauge propositions

The following statements belong to different action levels and must not be
combined:

| Proposition | Current meaning |
|---|---|
| localized Maxwell sector in the strict parent action | an internally consistent Maxwell carrier that was supplied as part of that parent |
| physical brane displacement \(u_a\) | a material light candidate with two transverse shear modes and one physical compressional mode |
| native polar-field constraint search | a computed generic quadratic result with no emergent first-class Gauss chain on the tested regular families |
| added spin-ice or constrained link construction | a consistent conditional route to emergent compact-\(U(1)\) behavior, but an extra microscopic degree of freedom not derived from the present throat medium |

Thus the repository can already calculate with Maxwell because Maxwell was
placed in one version of the parent action. That is not the same claim as
deriving electromagnetism from the one-medium ontology. Conversely, failure of
the tested native polar field to generate a Gauss chain does not prove that no
physical material analog can reproduce useful electromagnetic behavior. It
rules out a particular route to exact emergent gauge structure within its
stated quadratic scope.

There is also a double-counting danger. Retaining the localized Maxwell photon
and promoting \(u_T\) as a second independent light carrier would generally
create two transverse sectors. A final parent action must say whether Maxwell
is retained, replaced by material shear, or used only as the comparison
theory.

### 6.7 Charge conservation need not be postulated under a new name

If the throat orientation is to function as electric charge, the model must
derive how it is transported and how oppositely oriented defects are created
or destroyed. Merely writing a conserved electromagnetic current beside the
throat would import the desired result.

There are at least three logically distinct conservation strengths:

1. **binary persistence:** an isolated throat cannot continuously change from
   \(s=+1\) to \(s=-1\);
2. **signed defect balance:** creation and annihilation occur only in
   oppositely signed combinations, including flux through the bulk;
3. **additive charge conservation:** arbitrary multi-defect configurations
   carry an integer or continuous total whose local current obeys a closed
   balance law.

The first does not imply the third. The model’s present \(\pm w\) orientation
is a candidate for binary persistence. Its topology, additive composition,
and reservoir-complete conservation are still to be established.

### 6.8 Exact Maxwell and a characterized analog are different targets

An exact Maxwell recovery would require, within its domain of validity, the
full observable package: two photons, Gauss constraint, conserved source,
electric and magnetic tensor structure, common normalization, correct
transformation laws, and the absence of observable extra modes.

The narrower target of this project is a characterized analog. It may contain
a physical sound branch, a preferred material rest frame, weak mode
conversion, or non-Maxwell short-distance response, provided that:

- the departure is derived rather than hidden;
- its size is computed in the regime being claimed;
- it is compatible with the known physics the model is meant to reproduce;
- the model gains enough same-branch predictions to be more than a flexible
  redescription.

Calling the result an analog lowers the ontological requirement, not the
requirement for quantitative consistency.

### 6.9 Section status

Gauge redundancy is not a microscopic admission requirement for the tangible
medium. The currently selected material displacement does not possess full
electromagnetic gauge redundancy, and its longitudinal component is physical.
The native polar-field search also does not supply a Gauss mechanism on the
tested branches. What remains open is whether the vortex-throat dynamics
provide native source balance, static constraint behavior, and a common
static/moving coupling closely enough to reproduce the targeted
electromagnetic observations.

## 7. Electric charge and the static interaction

The word *charge* normally bundles several properties: a sign, a source
strength, an additive quantity, a conservation law, a coupling normalization,
and a characteristic static force. In the vortex-throat model these are not
yet one object. They must be derived separately and then shown to belong to
the same branch.

### 7.1 Orientation is the candidate identity, not the completed charge

The two throat orientations

\[
s=\pm1
\]

distinguish defects opening into the two locally opposite brane-normal
directions. This is the native binary datum proposed to underlie the sign of
electric charge.

That proposal immediately supplies only a two-valued orientation. It does not
yet supply:

- an additive integer carried by several throats;
- a proof that the sign cannot change through a core or bulk process;
- a local continuity equation;
- a universal charge magnitude;
- a static force sign;
- a rule identifying all defects with the same charge unit.

The label becomes electric charge only to the extent that the solved defect
exhibits those operational properties. Until then, *electric-odd throat
orientation* is the more precise term.

### 7.2 Keep the strict-parent and native dictionaries separate

The repository currently supports two different readings:

| Context | Charge variables | Status |
|---|---|---|
| strict parent with localized Maxwell | \(\eta_Q=\pm1\), \(q_\star=\eta_Q e_\star\), and the normalized brane coupling \(q_{\rm eff}=q_\star/\sqrt{Z_{\rm int}}\) | consistent adopted dictionary; \(e_\star\) and its microscopic throat carrier are not derived |
| native material analog | throat orientation \(s=\pm1\), together with whatever \(h\)-source, held datum, or monopole the core solution produces | candidate mechanism; source magnitude, ensemble, and conservation remain open |

The first dictionary is usable when calculating inside the supplied Maxwell
parent. It cannot be cited as evidence that the material vortex generated
charge. The second is the construction this paper is trying to interpret. A
later action decision must either connect them dynamically or declare that
they describe alternative models.

Circulation is not inserted into either dictionary as electric charge.
Circulation, fluxoid winding, and throat breathing may affect a defect’s
energy or magnetic response, but their relation to \(s\) must be derived.

### 7.3 Source, mediator, and observable are different roles

For the current characterized electric route:

- the **source identity candidate** is the oriented vortex throat;
- the **conditional mediator** is the localized normal brane displacement
  \(h\);
- the **held mouth datum or monopole** is selected by the unresolved core
  functional;
- the **observable** is a displacement, stress, energy shift, or force
  extracted from the \(h\)-containing solution.

The geometrical displacement is

\[
\xi_w(x)=\ell h(x),
\]

where \(h\) is dimensionless in the current reduction. It is distinct from the
length-valued in-plane longitudinal displacement \(u_L\). A nonzero mixing
coefficient between the two fields changes the eigenvectors and effective
kernel; it does not make \(u_L=h\).

This retires the earlier proposal that electric charge is a stored unit of the
stray longitudinal scalar. The useful lesson from that proposal survives:
fixed-source and fixed-defect variational problems can have opposite
interaction signs. The surviving conditional mediator is \(h\), and the
physical core must decide which variational problem is realized.

### 7.4 The far-field range is conditional but available

For a localized massless brane mode outside a compact mouth, the leading
static solution has the form

\[
h(r)=h_A\frac{a}{r},
\]

so a nonzero monopole produces an interaction potential proportional to
\(1/R\) and a radial force proportional to \(1/R^2\).

This falloff follows from a three-dimensional brane mode. It does not follow
from the finite thickness of a four-dimensional bulk source. A compact source
in an unlocalized four-dimensional spatial bulk would instead have the
higher-dimensional far-field law. The range claim therefore depends on:

1. existence of the localized \(h\) branch;
2. a nonzero projection of the vortex core onto that branch;
3. absence of a gap large enough to Yukawa-suppress the relevant range;
4. controlled leakage and mixing across the finite interface.

The present far-field calculation establishes the conditional \(1/R^2\)
force once those premises and a mouth class are supplied. It does not derive
the nonzero monopole from an autonomous throat.

### 7.5 The static operator does not select attraction or repulsion

Let the leading pair energy and outward radial force be written

\[
U_X(R)
=
\frac{s_1s_2A_X}{4\pi R},
\qquad
F_{X,\rm out}
=
\frac{s_1s_2A_X}{4\pi R^2}.
\]

With this convention \(A_X>0\) gives like-orientation repulsion and
opposite-orientation attraction. The coupled static kernel is positive, but
the coefficient \(A_X\) also depends on what is held fixed and which reservoir
work belongs in the thermodynamic potential.

The target-blind mouth calculation gives the following qualitative result:

| Mouth ensemble | Leading coefficient | Static sign |
|---|---|---|
| fixed value or Dirichlet datum | \(A_V=m_{gg}\phi^2/S_{gg}^2\) | positive |
| fixed source | \(A_J=-m_{gg}(j+g)^2\) | negative |
| fixed monopole with the existing mouth source | \(A_M=m_{gg}(q^2-g^2)\) | magnitude-dependent |
| mixed value/source condition | \(A_{\rm mixed}\) varies with its mixing data | can be negative, zero, or positive |

Here \(m_{gg}>0\) is the relevant coupled-kernel coefficient; \(\phi\), \(j\),
\(q\), and \(g\) denote the corresponding held value, additional source,
monopole, and existing mouth-source magnitudes. The detailed symbols are less
important than the conclusion: the exterior differential operator and its
positive energy do not determine the force sign without the core ensemble.

This is not an inconsistency. A source-controlled system and a
value-controlled system include different reservoir work. It does mean that
“a scalar attracts” and “positive stored energy repels” are both incomplete
slogans.

### 7.6 A vortex core must select the mouth ensemble

A rigid object could be externally clamped, charged by a power supply, or fed
by a prescribed source. The particle in this model has none of those
unmentioned devices. Its effective boundary condition must arise from the
vortex solution.

Possible native selection mechanisms include:

- protected winding or framing that fixes a mouth value;
- conserved flux that fixes a conormal monopole;
- a freely relaxing core that behaves as a fixed source;
- a finite core susceptibility producing a mixed condition;
- an active drain and return circuit that maintains a nonequilibrium datum.

For each candidate, the derivation must include the core energy or action,
allowed variations, endpoint work, and reservoir terms. A signed stationary
mouth value is not supported by positive exterior curvature alone; a core
barrier, constraint, or drive must hold it away from the relaxing solution.

The current reduced model freezes the sleeve and does not supply that
nonlinear selection functional. It therefore correctly lands on an unresolved
boundary-class result rather than a force sign.

### 7.7 Sign, additivity, conservation, and quantization are separate gates

Even if the static force lands on the Coulomb sign pattern, the throat has not
thereby acquired the rest of electric charge. The following tests remain:

| Property | Native question |
|---|---|
| sign | does \(s\rightarrow-s\) reverse the static source and force cross term? |
| additivity | does a collection of throats source the far field by a sum of protected units? |
| conservation | can signed throat number change through reconnection, bulk escape, or reservoir exchange? |
| universality | do all members of a species carry the same derived source magnitude? |
| quantization | is the magnitude fixed by topology or a discrete core spectrum rather than a continuous modulus? |
| neutrality | which composite throat configurations have vanishing leading monopole? |

The bare \(\mathbb Z_2\)-like orientation is compatible with two signs but is
not itself an additive \(U(1)\) charge. An added constrained spin-ice sector
can supply integer divergence charge conditionally, but the existing
continuum throat has not been shown to contain or bind that additional degree
of freedom.

### 7.8 The physical electric observable must be stated

If \(h\) mediates the force, it is tempting to rename \(-\nabla h\) the
electric field. That name is useful only after the measurement rule is
specified. A throat detector may respond to local normal displacement, slope,
traction, core-energy change, or a combination mixed with \(u_L\) and
interface variables.

The operational field should therefore be defined by the force on a specified
weak probe:

\[
F_a^{\rm probe}
=
\mathcal Q_{\rm probe}\,\mathcal E_a^{\rm native}
+\hbox{resolved scalar, gradient, finite-size, and velocity corrections}.
\]

The coefficient \(\mathcal Q_{\rm probe}\) must be exported from the same
probe-throat solution rather than assigned from its desired electric charge.
Only then can universality, superposition, and composition dependence be
tested.

### 7.9 Calculation order for the static sector

The shortest noncircular route is:

1. solve one stationary oriented vortex throat with its sleeve, endpoint, and
   return conditions;
2. derive its quadratic overlap with \(h\), \(u_L\), and the other accessible
   modes;
3. integrate out the resolved core only after identifying what is held fixed;
4. obtain the effective mouth functional and classify its ensemble;
5. compute all four two-throat sign choices using one action and one
   stress/flux prescription;
6. extract conservative, constraint, flux, and reservoir contributions
   separately;
7. test range, sign, additivity, and magnitude without retuning the branch.

This sequence turns the boundary condition from an analyst’s choice into a
property of the defect.

### 7.10 Section status

The oriented throat supplies a credible native candidate for the *identity*
of positive versus negative charge, and the localized \(h\)-branon supplies a
conditional route to the correct three-dimensional range. The exterior
calculation has shown exactly why the static sign remains open: the unresolved
vortex core selects among admissible mouth ensembles with different signs.

The electric route is therefore neither derived nor excluded. The current
claims are conditional falloff, an explicit sign fork, and a concrete
nonlinear selection problem. Additivity, conservation, quantization,
universal magnitude, and the probe observable remain open.

## 8. Motion, current, and magnetism

Magnetism is the first place where treating the particle as an extended vortex
rather than a point becomes unavoidable. Translating the throat moves its
mouth, deforms its core and sleeve, perturbs the drain, and can excite
transverse shear, compression, interface motion, and radiation. The
velocity-dependent force must be extracted from that complete response.

The physical \(u_T\) shear branch remains the present conditional magnetic
carrier. Demoting the statement “magnetism is the MacCullagh sector” does not
change that candidate carrier; it means that MacCullagh curl stiffness by
itself does not derive the electric source, the moving overlap, or the common
electric--magnetic sign and normalization.

### 8.1 Translation is a collective-coordinate problem

Let the stationary throat be represented by the full field configuration
\(\Phi_0\). A slowly moving branch begins with

\[
\Phi(X,t)
=
\Phi_0\!\left(X-X_{\rm th}(t);s,\Gamma,\ldots\right)
+\delta\Phi[X;X_{\rm th},\dot X_{\rm th},\ddot X_{\rm th},\ldots].
\]

The translated background supplies a kinematic zero-mode contribution, while
\(\delta\Phi\) contains the response required by the field equations,
boundaries, and outgoing conditions. The effective action can then contain
inertia, gyroscopic terms, source overlaps, nonlocal memory, and radiation:

\[
L_{\rm eff}
=
\frac12M_{ab}(X)\dot X^a\dot X^b
+\mathcal A_a(X)\dot X^a
-U(X)
+L_{\rm fields}
+L_{\rm coupling}
+L_{\rm open}.
\]

No single term should be called magnetic until its transformation law and its
force on another solved throat have been identified.

### 8.2 Four notions of current must be distinguished

| Native motion | Associated current | Possible observable role |
|---|---|---|
| translation of signed throat orientation | transport of the \(s\)-odd defect profile | electric-current candidate |
| material drain through the mouth | mass, momentum, and energy flux of the medium | gravity and open-system balance |
| circulation around or through the core | vorticity and angular momentum | magnetic-moment or spin candidate |
| circulation of a trapped transverse mode | bound shear energy and angular momentum | internal magnetic response candidate |

These currents can coexist in one vortex solution, but they are not equal.
The drain is present even for a stationary throat. Translation changes the
position of the entire defect. Core swirl may persist in the throat rest
frame. A trapped mode can carry angular momentum without net transport of
medium through the mouth.

This separation prevents two common mistakes: defining electromagnetic
current as generic medium flow, and identifying every vortex circulation with
electric charge transport.

### 8.3 What translation continuity does and does not derive

Suppose a normalized signed mouth profile is rigidly translated at leading
order:

\[
\sigma_i(x,t)
=
s_i\eta_a\!\left(x-X_i(t)\right).
\]

It then obeys the kinematic identity

\[
\partial_t\sigma_i
+\nabla\cdot\left(\sigma_i V_i\right)
=0,
\qquad
I_i=\sigma_i V_i.
\]

This earns a convection-shaped flux \(I_i\propto s_iV_i\) for that translated
profile. It is more than simply writing \(j=sV\) by analogy.

It does not yet establish that:

- \(\sigma_i\) is the complete conserved electric charge density;
- the real moving solution remains a rigid translation;
- the profile’s transverse-shear overlap is nonzero;
- the coupling normalization is fixed;
- acceleration, deformation, circulation, and memory corrections vanish;
- bulk, core, and reservoir fluxes close the same continuity law.

The kinematic coefficient of rigid advection can be one while the dynamical
coupling of that flux to the light sector remains unknown.

### 8.4 The audited transverse moving-source construction is conditional

The existing moving-throat calculation studies the representative interaction

\[
S_{\rm int}^{T}
=
\int dt\,d^3x\,
q_T\sum_i
s_i\eta_a(x-X_i)\,
V_i\cdot u_T.
\]

The translation identity fixes the shape of the signed flux in this
interaction. The coefficient \(q_T\), however, is not exported from a
nonlinear throat solution. In the audited candidate it is written as an
overlap involving the active drain’s time-arrow. Thus the construction
establishes a consistent possible moving-source row, not that every physical
vortex throat necessarily carries it with the required strength.

A native derivation would substitute the moving vortex ansatz into the full
action and obtain \(q_T\) as an overlap integral of solved core and brane
profiles. It would also show which additional terms occur at the same order.
Schematically,

\[
q_T^{\rm native}
\sim
\int d^4X\,
\frac{\delta^2 S}{\delta u_T\,\delta\Phi}
\cdot\partial_a\Phi_0,
\]

with the precise contraction, boundary contributions, and normalization
determined by the selected parent action.

### 8.5 The conditional transverse exchange has the right kind of tensor

Given the moving-source coupling above and the stable transverse shear
operator, direct elimination of \(u_T\) gives

\[
U_T(R)
=
-\frac{s_1s_2q_T^2}{8\pi\mu_RR}
\left[
V_1\cdot V_2
+(V_1\cdot n)(V_2\cdot n)
\right],
\]

where \(n=(X_2-X_1)/R\). The transverse Green tensor is proportional to

\[
G_{ab}(R)
\propto
\frac{\delta_{ab}+n_an_b}{R}.
\]

This is a substantive conditional result. It supplies:

- the transverse Darwin-type tensor structure;
- a \(1/R\) velocity-dependent pair term and \(1/R^2\) force;
- leading order \(O(V_1V_2)\);
- reversal between parallel and antiparallel side-by-side motion.

For two like-oriented, side-by-side throats with parallel velocities, the
displayed term is attractive. But that statement is downstream of the
supplied nonzero \(q_T\) coupling. It does not derive \(q_T\), the static
electric sign, or their common normalization.

### 8.6 Maxwell-like consistency is a relation between independently open outputs

Write the selected static electric coefficient as \(A_E\). The direct
transverse result can be compared with the velocity correction tied to that
electric branch through

\[
r_{BA}
=
\frac{q_T^2c_\gamma^2}{\mu_RA_E}
=
\frac{q_T^2}{\rho_{\rm br}A_E},
\qquad
r_{\rm cone}
=
\frac{c_E^2}{c_\gamma^2}.
\]

A Maxwell-like Darwin relation would require the appropriate sign branch,
\(r_{BA}=1\), and a common propagation cone \(r_{\rm cone}=1\), subject to
the model’s normalization conventions. None of those equalities follows from
the transverse tensor alone.

This is precisely where the tangible analog can gain predictive content. If
the same autonomous throat solution fixes \(A_E\), \(q_T\),
\(\rho_{\rm br}\), and \(\mu_R\), then \(r_{BA}\) is a held-out
dimensionless result. Fitting the electric and moving coefficients separately
would reproduce the desired relationship without explaining it.

### 8.7 A native magnetic field is defined by response, not by a curl name

The curl

\[
b_T=\nabla\times u_T
\]

is a natural local measure of transverse shear rotation. It is not
automatically the magnetic field. To earn that interpretation, the model
should show that a weak moving probe responds through a force law of the form

\[
F_a^{\rm probe}
=
\mathcal Q_{\rm probe}
\left(
\mathcal E_a^{\rm native}
+\epsilon_{abc}V_{\rm probe}^{\,b}
\mathcal B_{\rm native}^{\,c}
\right)
+\Delta F_a,
\]

where \(\mathcal E^{\rm native}\), \(\mathcal B_{\rm native}\), and
\(\mathcal Q_{\rm probe}\) are all defined from material observables and
\(\Delta F_a\) records resolved departures.

The operational magnetic observable may be \(b_T\), a traction derived from
it, a momentum density, or a mixture on a nonuniform background. The force
calculation, rather than the notation, decides.

### 8.8 Translation-induced magnetism and intrinsic moment are different

The moving-source kernel describes a velocity-dependent interaction between
translated oriented throats. A stationary throat can separately possess
internal circulation or a circulating trapped mode. Its far field may then be
dipolar and can define an intrinsic magnetic-moment candidate.

The two mechanisms should meet through the same action but not be identified:

| Mechanism | Present analog target |
|---|---|
| translation of orientation | current carried by a moving charge |
| closed path of many translated throats | current loop and its external field |
| internal core circulation | intrinsic moment of one defect |
| trapped circularly polarized shear | bound field angular momentum |

Section 9 will ask which, if any, carries spin, chirality, and magnetic moment.
Section 8 requires only that the translational current and its force be derived
without borrowing the answer from that later interpretation.

### 8.9 Time reversal exposes a present departure

In the audited active-drain construction, the candidate coefficient \(q_T\)
contains a time-odd drain arrow. Since \(V\) is also time-odd, the resulting
source, \(u_T\), and \(b_T=\nabla\times u_T\) are time-even within that
assignment. A Maxwell magnetic field is time-odd under the usual reversal.

This does not create an algebraic inconsistency, because the stationary drain
background is itself not invariant under naive time reversal; its full reverse
is a source-like background. It does create an observable interpretation
gate. The model must determine whether:

- the correct comparison reverses the complete drain/reservoir state;
- a different native field combination is time-odd;
- the measured force remains Maxwell-like although the field label does not;
- or the analog carries a genuine preferred-time or dissipative departure.

Until that is resolved, \(b_T\) should be called the curl of the transverse
displacement or a magnetic candidate, not identified without qualification
with Maxwell \(B\).

### 8.10 Open-system force terms can alter a pair potential

The throat is a conduit with active intake and possible return. Its complete
collective-coordinate balance can contain

\[
F_{\rm total}
=
F_{\rm variational}
+F_{\rm flux}
+F_{\rm constraint}
+F_{\rm Rayleigh}
+F_{\rm radiation}.
\]

The conditional transverse exchange computes a variational field-mediated
piece. Momentum carried through the mouth, endpoint response, nonholonomic
locks, dissipative slip, and radiation may add forces at the same velocity
order. If those contributions are nonconservative, no global two-body
potential need exist even when one component can be written as \(U_T(R)\).

The full force must therefore be obtained from a common control surface and
reservoir prescription before the magnetic sign is considered final.

### 8.11 Held-out geometries

Three geometries test different parts of the proposed response:

1. **parallel moving throats:** tests the sign and transverse Darwin tensor
   already available conditionally in the far-field point-profile limit;
2. **side-by-side closed current loops:** tests whether extended current
   integration reaches the comparison target of repulsion for like-oriented
   loops;
3. **coaxial closed current loops:** tests the complementary comparison target
   of attraction using the same coupling sign and normalization.

The latter two have not been earned by the point-throat result alone. They
require a defined continuous distribution or a many-throat construction,
finite core regularization, and the same detector rule. Their value is that a
scalar central-force imitation cannot generally pass all three tensor tests.

Additional controls should include antiparallel translation, common boost,
zero orientation, reversed orientation, vanishing transverse stiffness,
mass/drain-only ablation, and circulation-only ablation.

### 8.12 Section status

The existing work conditionally earns a convection-shaped signed flux under
rigid translation and a transverse \(1/R^2\), \(O(V_1V_2)\) Darwin-type force
once a nonzero coupling \(q_T\) is supplied. It does not yet derive that
coupling from the full vortex, tie it to the unresolved static electric
coefficient, include every open-system force, or establish the Maxwell
time-reversal transformation.

The decisive next output is not another analogy for \(j=sV\). It is the
collective-coordinate response of one solved throat, followed by static and
moving two-throat calculations on that same frozen branch. That calculation
will decide whether the model produces Maxwell-like magnetism, a quantified
departure, a different vortex response, or no viable magnetic sector.

## 9. Spin, chirality, helicity, and magnetic moment

These four terms describe different properties even in standard physics. In a
vortex-throat model they can be carried by different parts of the same
extended solution. The paper should not choose one visually appealing swirl
and assign all four names to it.

### 9.1 The terms must be unpacked

| Term | Minimal operational meaning | What does not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| internal angular momentum | rotational Noether charge remaining after the defect’s center-of-mass orbital contribution is removed | visible circulation alone |
| spin-like state | an internal state with a specified transformation under rotations | a rigid-body analogy or a binary label alone |
| helicity | projection of angular momentum onto momentum for a propagating state, or a precisely defined wave-polarization analog | handed appearance of a stationary throat |
| chirality | inequivalence under a named spatial reflection, with a protected handed label | circulation sign without a parity analysis |
| magnetic moment | coefficient governing energy, torque, or force in an externally defined magnetic response | nonzero angular momentum alone |

The same defect can possess all of these, some of them, or none. Their
relationships are results to derive.

### 9.2 Begin with the angular-momentum tensor

For a localized configuration in four spatial dimensions, total angular
momentum is an antisymmetric tensor \(J_{IJ}\). Given a chosen collective
center \(X_{\rm th}^{I}\) and total momentum \(P_I\), define the internal
remainder schematically by

\[
S_{IJ}
=
J_{IJ}
-\left(
X_{{\rm th},I}P_J-X_{{\rm th},J}P_I
\right).
\]

This definition is meaningful only after the stress tensor, boundary fluxes,
and center convention are fixed. For an open throat, angular momentum can
cross the mouth, sleeve, outer boundary, and reservoir, so the internal tensor
may obey a balance law rather than a closed conservation law.

Relative to the brane normal, \(S_{IJ}\) separates into:

\[
S_{ab},
\qquad
S_{aw}.
\]

The tangential part can be dualized for a brane observer,

\[
S_{\rm br}^{\,a}
=
\frac12\epsilon^{abc}S_{bc},
\]

and behaves like an ordinary three-dimensional axial angular momentum. The
mixed components \(S_{aw}\) form a different brane vector. A claim about
particle spin must say which sector is nonzero and why the others are absent,
hidden, or dynamically locked.

### 9.3 Candidate physical carriers remain distinct

| Candidate carrier | Possible contribution | Main unresolved issue |
|---|---|---|
| material circulation around the mouth | tangential \(S_{ab}\) | circulation law, core regularity, and far-field cost |
| mixed-plane helical intake | \(S_{aw}\) and possibly \(S_{ab}\) | separation from the gravity drain and reservoir torque |
| circularly polarized trapped shear | bound field angular momentum | existence, localization, spectrum, and backreaction |
| orbital winding of a support mode | integer-like classical angular momentum about the throat | whether it is intrinsic or a composite orbital excitation |
| independent microrotation of ordered substructure | internal spin density and couple-stress balance | requires a new material degree of freedom and constitutive law |
| framed-vortex topology | nontrivial rotational path or binary sector | actual configuration-space topology and protection |

Several may coexist in the final solution. If so, the total \(S_{IJ}\) must
include their cross terms and fluxes. Selecting one carrier for interpretation
does not delete the angular momentum stored in the others.

### 9.4 Helicity has several non-equivalent native uses

For a moving localized object on the brane, a particle-like helicity candidate
would be

\[
\lambda_{\rm th}
=
\frac{S_{\rm br}\cdot P}{|P|}.
\]

It is undefined for a throat at rest and changes meaning if mixed
\(S_{aw}\) components participate. For a freely propagating transverse shear
mode, right- and left-circular polarization may instead furnish two
wave-helicity states, but their angular momentum density and detector response
must be calculated.

Fluid mechanics uses the word helicity for a different quantity, such as the
brane-projected integral

\[
\mathcal H_v
=
\int d^3x\,
v_{\rm br}\cdot
\left(\nabla\times v_{\rm br}\right).
\]

This can measure linking or twisting of brane-projected flow. It is not the
same as the helicity of a particle representation, and it is not a complete
four-dimensional invariant. A four-dimensional vorticity is a two-form, so
the model must state which contractions, projections, and boundary terms
define any proposed generalization.

### 9.5 Chirality requires a named reflection

A throat is chiral only if its complete configuration cannot be mapped to its
mirror by an allowed proper rotation and a continuous deformation preserving
the branch. The relevant mirror operation must be named:

- reflection of one or all brane coordinates;
- reversal of the brane normal \(w\rightarrow-w\);
- reversal of a circulation plane;
- interchange of self-dual and anti-self-dual parts of a four-dimensional
  vorticity texture;
- reflection of an internal frame or knot.

These operations need not agree. In particular, the electric orientation
\(s\) is odd under normal reflection but is not automatically the particle’s
chirality. Likewise, clockwise versus counterclockwise brane circulation can
be parity-related without being topologically protected.

The electroweak meaning of chiral fermions is a much stronger,
representation-level statement and is outside the present electromagnetic
analog. A native geometric handedness would be an input to that later
question, not its solution.

### 9.6 A magnetic moment is a response coefficient

Operationally, a magnetic moment is identified by the response of a localized
throat to a weak, slowly varying external magnetic candidate. For example,

\[
\Delta E
=
-\mu_{\rm th}\cdot\mathcal B_{\rm ext}
+O(\mathcal B_{\rm ext}^2,\nabla\mathcal B_{\rm ext}),
\]

or equivalently by the resulting torque and force. In a conventional
three-dimensional current distribution one writes

\[
\mu^a
=
\frac12\int d^3x\,
\epsilon^{abc}x_bj_c.
\]

The model may recover this expression for a derived oriented-throat current,
but should not assume it before the native source and probe laws are known.

Circulation does not guarantee a nonzero dipole moment. A toroidal or
anapole-like current can carry local circulation and angular momentum while
its leading external magnetic dipole vanishes. Conversely, a composite
current distribution can have a magnetic moment whose relation to the
material angular momentum depends on how charge and mass are distributed
through the core.

### 9.7 The gyromagnetic relation is a held-out result

If a branch carries both internal angular momentum and magnetic moment, write

\[
\mu_{\rm th}^{\,a}
=
\gamma_{\rm th}S_{\rm br}^{\,a}
+\Delta\mu^a,
\]

where \(\Delta\mu^a\) contains anisotropic, mixed-plane, finite-size, and
environmental terms. The coefficient \(\gamma_{\rm th}\) must follow from the
same core solution that fixes the electric and moving-current couplings.

A classical circulating distribution generally produces a model-dependent
charge-to-inertia ratio. The electron’s quantum gyromagnetic relation,
including its approximately \(g=2\) leading factor and corrections, is a
substantially stronger target. It is not implied by a \(4\pi\) return path, a
binary circulation, or the existence of two transverse light polarizations.

### 9.8 A trapped standing wave is not automatically spinning

A real linearly polarized standing wave can have zero time-averaged angular
momentum. Two degenerate modes with a relative phase can form a circularly
polarized bound pattern that carries angular momentum. Orbital winding can
also contribute. These are specific mode combinations, not consequences of
the word *geon*.

For a candidate trapped shear mode \(\phi_T\), the model must calculate:

- the canonical or stress-tensor angular-momentum density;
- the contribution of both counter-propagating components;
- the phase relation between degenerate polarizations;
- angular-momentum leakage into compression, interface, and bulk channels;
- backreaction on the throat shape and circulation;
- whether the state is stationary, periodic, or externally maintained.

Only a nonzero integrated, reservoir-complete result can support the claim
that the throat’s support field carries spin-like angular momentum.

### 9.9 Antiparticle reversal need not reverse spin

The candidate antiparticle map begins by reversing the normal orientation
\(s\), but the full map may also act on circulation, trapped-mode phase,
framing, and internal handedness. Those transformations must be solved rather
than chosen to mimic familiar particles.

This matters for the magnetic moment. If the moment is proportional to an
electric-odd coupling times an independently selectable angular momentum,
reversing \(s\) can reverse \(\mu_{\rm th}\) without reversing
\(S_{\rm br}\). If circulation itself is locked to \(s\), both may reverse.
If the moment is carried by a separate texture, neither implication is
automatic.

Thus source reversal, spin reversal, and antiparticle transformation remain
three different operations until the core establishes their relation.

### 9.10 Five achievement levels

| Level | What has been shown |
|---|---|
| classical angular momentum | a finite vortex solution has nonzero internal \(S_{IJ}\) |
| binary internal state | two protected, approximately degenerate internal branches exist |
| spin-like response | the branches transform under rotations and couple to probes in a specified way |
| spin-\(\tfrac12\) kinematics | the state space realizes the appropriate double-valued rotational structure |
| quantum fermion | quantization, exchange statistics, multiparticle antisymmetry, and measurement rules are supplied |

Progress at one level does not automatically promote the model to the next.
The present document is primarily preparing the first three levels. Section
10 examines a possible classical configuration-space ingredient for the
fourth.

### 9.11 Required calculation package

For one solved throat branch and each internal candidate state, calculate:

1. the full \(J_{IJ}\), its orbital subtraction, and all boundary fluxes;
2. the decomposition into \(S_{ab}\) and \(S_{aw}\);
3. circulation, vorticity, trapped-mode, and microrotation contributions;
4. transformation under brane rotations, parity, time reversal,
   \(w\)-reflection, and \(s\)-reversal;
5. energy splitting and transition path between candidate binary states;
6. response to an externally generated magnetic candidate;
7. the magnetic dipole and any toroidal or higher moments;
8. the gyromagnetic relation and its finite-size corrections;
9. the response of the reflected or antiparticle branch.

The calculations should use the same branch and source normalization as
Sections 7 and 8. Otherwise a fitted moment cannot test the common vortex
ontology.

### 9.12 Section status

The model presently has several plausible physical carriers of angular
momentum but no derived assignment of particle spin. The older weak-field
gravitomagnetic construction accepts a source angular momentum as input; it
does not derive the throat’s intrinsic spin. The trapped-light proposal is
likewise a candidate until a localized mode with nonzero angular momentum is
solved.

Spin, chirality, helicity, and magnetic moment therefore remain separate open
outputs. This is not a demand that a classical toy analog reproduce all of
quantum field theory. It is a demand that the model state which level it has
reached before borrowing the names attached to higher levels.

## 10. The framed-vortex and \(720^\circ\) hypothesis

A \(720^\circ\) return rule is a plausible topology question for an extended
vortex throat. It is not a consequence of adding a fourth spatial coordinate,
and it is not by itself a derivation of quantum spin. This section states the
precise classical question that can be tested.

### 10.1 A returned orientation can still trace a nontrivial path

A \(360^\circ\) rotation returns an ordinary vector or rigid orientation to
the same direction. Nevertheless, the *path* through orientation space need
not be contractible. For ordinary three-dimensional rotations,

\[
\pi_1\!\left(SO(3)\right)=\mathbb Z_2.
\]

The loop represented by one \(2\pi\) turn is in the nontrivial class, while
traversing it twice is contractible. Four-dimensional rotations retain this
basic property:

\[
\pi_1\!\left(SO(4)\right)=\mathbb Z_2.
\]

The extra dimension therefore does not change a full turn into some angle
other than \(2\pi\). It changes the available rotation planes and the internal
geometries a defect may carry.

The brane also breaks the practical symmetry. Rotations tangent to an
unperturbed brane form the residual \(SO(3)\); a mixed \(a\)-\(w\) rotation
tilts the throat relative to the brane and need not be a zero-energy symmetry.
The relevant rotation group must therefore be obtained from the actual
background and boundary conditions rather than assumed to be all of
\(SO(4)\).

### 10.2 Path memory and a second static state are different hypotheses

Two \(720^\circ\) mechanisms are often merged in informal pictures:

1. **Noncontractible-loop mechanism:** after \(360^\circ\), the complete
   physical configuration is exactly the same, but the path taken represents
   the nontrivial element of the configuration space’s fundamental group.
   After \(720^\circ\), the combined path is contractible.
2. **Hidden-framing mechanism:** after the visible mouth turns through
   \(360^\circ\), an internal ribbon, phase frame, or connection is left in a
   physically different twisted state. A second turn restores the complete
   configuration.

The first gives two path classes, not two static endpoints. The second gives
two distinguishable full configurations above one visible mouth orientation.
Either might be useful, but they require different mathematics and different
observables.

### 10.3 The throat configuration space must be defined

Let \(\mathcal C_s\) denote the space of admissible finite-energy or
controlled steady throat configurations with orientation \(s\), fixed
asymptotic brane state, and specified reservoir class. It should include:

- medium density, phase, and velocity;
- brane order, displacement, thickness, and interface geometry;
- throat mouth, core, sleeve, and endpoint fields;
- circulation and any internal frame;
- trapped-mode amplitude and phase;
- constraints and allowed reconnection events.

Fields related only by a genuine descriptive redundancy may be identified.
Physically different material displacements, core twists, or reservoir states
may not be quotiented away merely to obtain a desired topology.

A rotation experiment is then a continuous path

\[
\gamma:[0,1]\longrightarrow\mathcal C_s
\]

whose visible mouth orientation performs a \(2\pi\) turn. The questions are
whether \(\gamma(1)=\gamma(0)\), whether the loop is contractible, and which
constraints are preserved during a proposed contraction.

### 10.4 Candidate carriers of framing

| Candidate framing | What a \(2\pi\) turn might change | Main failure route |
|---|---|---|
| phase ribbon attached to the core | twist or winding relative to the far phase | density may vanish and let the phase unwind |
| marked circulation plane | orientation of tangential versus mixed vorticity | circulation plane may rotate continuously through the bulk |
| throat sleeve frame | twist of the extended conduit between mouth and endpoint | free endpoint or return path may absorb the twist |
| trapped-mode nodal frame | phase and orientation of a bound shear pattern | degeneracy splitting, radiation, or phase relaxation |
| linked or knotted vortex structure | linking class of core and companion filament | reconnection or core crossing |
| ordered-substructure director | relative orientation between displacement and microrotation | requires an additional real material field |

These examples are not interchangeable. A phase winding, a framed curve, and
a director texture live in different configuration spaces and have different
unwinding rules.

### 10.5 A two-sheeted visible map is the sharp hidden-framing proposal

Let

\[
p:\mathcal C_s\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\rm visible}
\]

forget the internal framing and retain only the mouth orientation visible on
the brane. The strongest classical version of the proposal is that, over the
relevant branch, \(p\) is effectively two-to-one:

\[
p(\Phi_+)=p(\Phi_-),
\qquad
\Phi_+\neq\Phi_-.
\]

A lifted \(2\pi\) visible rotation would take
\(\Phi_+\rightarrow\Phi_-\), while a second turn would take
\(\Phi_-\rightarrow\Phi_+\).

For this to represent a physical binary state rather than bookkeeping, the
two sheets must be protected or metastable, possess a measurable distinction,
and remain connected by the stated rotational path. If they are exactly
physically identical, then the construction reduces to the noncontractible
path mechanism of Section 10.2 rather than a pair of static states.

### 10.6 The bulk direction can help or destroy the construction

The throat’s extension into \(w\) offers possible framing data unavailable to
a point confined to the brane. It can supply a sleeve, normal orientation,
mixed rotation plane, or remote attachment against which a twist is measured.

The same extra freedom can make the proposed topology less stable. A twist
that cannot unwind within the brane may:

- rotate into a mixed \(a\)-\(w\) plane;
- slide down the sleeve and leave through the endpoint;
- pass through a de-structured region where the order parameter vanishes;
- reconnect with another vortex or the return flow;
- be absorbed by the reservoir or interface.

Consequently, “the twist goes into the fourth dimension” is neither an
explanation nor a protection theorem. The bulk must be included in every
homotopy and energy-barrier test.

### 10.7 The topology calculation has a definite sequence

For each candidate throat branch:

1. specify the defect type, codimension, asymptotic fields, and allowed core
   zeros;
2. identify the actual symmetry group of the brane--throat background;
3. define \(\mathcal C_s\) with its endpoint and reservoir conditions;
4. construct an explicit \(2\pi\) rotation path;
5. determine whether its endpoint is identical, differently framed, or on a
   different branch;
6. attempt to contract the path while allowing every physically permitted
   deformation in four dimensions;
7. repeat with core collapse, reconnection, interface crossing, and endpoint
   twist escape as adversarial controls;
8. compute the minimum energy or action barrier if the protection is
   energetic rather than topological;
9. identify an observable that distinguishes the path class or final framing.

A numerical relaxation that fails to unwind is evidence only after the
allowed move set and convergence are controlled. An analytic homotopy
classification is stronger but still applies only to the declared
configuration space.

### 10.8 A classical \(\mathbb Z_2\) path does not force a quantum minus sign

Quantum spin-\(\tfrac12\) states transform under a double cover of the
rotation group. A \(2\pi\) rotation changes the sign of a spinor, while a
\(4\pi\) rotation restores it. In four spatial dimensions the relevant full
cover would be \(\operatorname{Spin}(4)\); a brane observer normally probes
the residual \(\operatorname{Spin}(3)\simeq SU(2)\) rotations.

A classical throat whose configuration space has
\(\pi_1=\mathbb Z_2\) provides a place where a double-valued quantum
representation *could* be assigned after quantization. It does not require
that assignment. The quantum amplitude could transform trivially around the
nontrivial loop unless an additional quantization rule, Berry phase, action
holonomy, or boundary condition selects the sign representation.

The model would therefore need to derive something like

\[
\exp\!\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}S[\gamma_{2\pi}]\right)=-1
\]

relative to the trivial path, or an equivalent state-space rule. The current
classical medium action supplies no such result.

### 10.9 Rotation and exchange must also be related

Fermionic behavior concerns exchange of two identical excitations, not only
rotation of one. Let \(\mathcal C_{2}\) be the configuration space of two
indistinguishable, noncoincident throats. The exchange path must be classified
and compared with the one-throat \(2\pi\) rotation path.

To obtain a fermion-like sector, a later quantized model would need:

- identical throat branches and an indistinguishable-particle quotient;
- a protected exchange path;
- a reason the exchange and rotation classes carry the required common sign;
- multiparticle amplitudes or states realizing antisymmetry;
- consistency through close approach, reconnection, and annihilation.

A \(720^\circ\) classical animation can pass while the exchange sector remains
bosonic or undefined.

### 10.10 Observable signatures before quantization

Even without claiming quantum spin, a framed vortex could produce testable
classical structure:

- two metastable framing states at the same visible orientation;
- hysteresis or a torque barrier during a \(2\pi\) manipulation;
- a geometric phase in a trapped mode;
- selection rules for circulation reversal;
- a coupling between framing and magnetic moment;
- distinct reconnection or annihilation channels.

These are useful analog outputs in their own right. They should be calculated
before treating the framing as invisible quantum phase information.

### 10.11 Falsifiers

The framed-vortex hypothesis fails in its strong form if:

- every proposed framing unwinds continuously through allowed bulk or core
  configurations;
- a \(2\pi\) path is contractible once the true sleeve and endpoint are
  included;
- the apparent two sheets differ only by notation;
- the protected sectors exist but are unrelated to physical rotations;
- the framing carries no observable or dynamical consequence;
- the topology is destroyed by the same nonzero throughput required for the
  stationary throat.

Failure would remove one possible mechanical route to a \(4\pi\) structure.
It would not falsify the light, electric, magnetic, or gravity analogs.

### 10.12 Section status

The \(720^\circ\) idea is now a well-posed candidate program: define the full
vortex configuration space, lift a visible rotation, and test its endpoint
and homotopy class against all bulk and core escape routes. Ordinary
four-dimensional rotation neither supplies nor forbids the desired behavior.

No present result establishes a two-sheeted throat state, a protected
\(2\pi\) path, a quantum minus sign, or fermionic exchange. The proposal is
worth testing because the particle is an extended framed defect, but it
remains a hypothesis whose classical and quantum claims must be kept
separate.

## 11. Gravity and gravitomagnetism firewall

Gravity, gravitomagnetism, electric charge, and electromagnetic magnetism may
all involve flows or transverse responses of the same medium. That does not
make them one source property. The firewall in this section is a bookkeeping
and operational separation: it prevents a generic swirl from being counted as
whichever familiar force is needed at the moment.

### 11.1 Gravity is the drain-response analog

The native gravity proposal begins with the orientation-even environmental
response of the medium to a throat’s drain, conversion, pressure, stress, and
return pattern. The raw four-dimensional material velocity is one input to
that response; it is not automatically the measured gravitational
acceleration.

Schematically, a source-side field has the form

\[
\mathfrak g_{\rm src}
=
\mathcal G_{\rm src}
\!\left[
J_{\rm med},v_{\rm med},D_tv_{\rm med},
\rho,P,\chi_B,\text{interface, return, reservoir}
\right],
\]

followed by one fixed operational calibration into acceleration units. A test
throat then has a separately derived passive response to that environment.
This prevents universal free fall from being inserted by allowing the
calibration to depend on the probe species.

The target ordinary branch is orientation-even, attractive, and
inverse-square on the brane:

\[
g_{\rm eff}(r)
=
-\frac{C_g\overline Q_g(r)}{r^2}\,\hat r,
\qquad
\overline Q_g^{(+)}(r)=\overline Q_g^{(-)}(r).
\]

The claimed mechanism that changes the four-dimensional medium falloff into
this three-dimensional law is a normalizable zero mode of the finite-slab
drain/density response. It is not the finiteness of the throat and it is not
an automatic reuse of the electrostatic \(h\)-branon. Without localization, a
compact source in four spatial dimensions has the steeper \(1/r^3\) force
law when sampled on the brane. In the idealized reduction, a true bound zero
mode retains its \(1/r^2\) force law rather than crossing over at large
distance; a leaky or DGP-like quasi-localized mode instead crosses over toward
the four-dimensional \(1/r^3\) law. That crossover is a direct localization
falsifier.

The existing gravity calculation does not yet earn even the bound-mode branch
for the committed drain. Its \(1/r^2\) law and attractive sign were obtained
using a Gauss number-flux sink, while the committed dynamical
\(\Gamma_B\) order-conversion law severed that derivation chain. The v3
charter therefore carries the range, sign, and return-residual prediction as
**conditional pending the S14a drain bridge**, not as earned results. The
normalization \(G\) is calibrated. The displayed \(g_{\rm eff}\) is thus the
conditional target of that bridge; the nonlinear source map, reflection
covariance, passive response, and return profile remain open. This is a
gravity analog, not a derivation of the full constrained, helicity-two
structure of general relativity.

### 11.2 Gravitomagnetism belongs to the mass and momentum channel

In the weak-field comparison theory, gravitomagnetism is the
velocity-dependent or rotational part of gravity sourced by mass current and
angular momentum. In the medium, its candidate carrier is therefore the
orientation-even momentum and vorticity response associated with:

- translation of the dressed drain configuration;
- circulation of mass-energy in or around the throat;
- momentum carried by the support mode;
- return-flow and reservoir momentum;
- the off-diagonal response of those quantities to a rotating source.

A brane-projected vorticity such as

\[
\omega_g^a
=
\frac12\epsilon^{abc}
\left(
\partial_bv^{\rm mass}_c-\partial_cv^{\rm mass}_b
\right)
\]

is a possible carrier. The physical gravitomagnetic observable is whatever
combination produces precession, velocity-dependent acceleration, or clock
and propagation effects for a specified probe. It is not established merely
by naming \(\omega_g\).

### 11.3 The existing weak-Kerr construction is calibrated, not microscopic

The earlier spin/gravitomagnetic paper assumes a sink bound to a localized
vortex-ring-like flow with far-zone azimuthal speed proportional to

\[
v_\phi
\propto
\frac{D\sin\theta}{r^2}.
\]

It then fixes \(D\) by matching the weak-Kerr off-diagonal field, with
\(D=4GJ/c^2\) up to the stated sign conventions. Once inserted into the
comparison metric, that calibrated field reproduces the corresponding
Lense--Thirring precession formula.

This is useful evidence that a medium flow with the declared tensor and
falloff can encode the known weak-field observable. It does not derive:

- the vortex-throat structure that generates the flow;
- the relation between its circulation and the source angular momentum
  \(J\);
- the stability and energy of the required far field;
- its coexistence with throat throughput and return;
- the coefficient from the same branch that fixes ordinary gravity.

The same work also shows that simpler pictures are insufficient: a line
vortex has the wrong angular and radial structure for the target, while
ordinary rigid-body backflow decays too quickly for the required long-range
interaction. The native throat must generate the correct multipole rather
than generic rotation.

### 11.5 Orientation reversal is the clean first discriminator

In a reflected, symmetric environment, reversing the throat orientation
should reverse electric-odd observables while preserving the leading gravity
source:

\[
\mathcal O_E[\mathcal C_{\rm th}\mathcal T]
=
-\mathcal O_E[\mathcal T],
\qquad
\mathcal O_G[\mathcal C_{\rm th}\mathcal T]
=
\mathcal O_G[\mathcal T].
\]

Here \(\mathcal C_{\rm th}\) is the complete candidate antiparticle or
orientation-reversal map, not merely the coordinate replacement
\(w\rightarrow-w\). It may have to transform internal phase, circulation,
framing, and reservoir data.

If the two bulk-facing environments are not reflection partners, an
orientation-correlated gravity signal can arise from environmental
asymmetry. That is a physical cross-coupling to compute and constrain, not
evidence that charge and gravity were the same source all along.

### 11.6 A circulating structure can source both sectors

Any circulating material or trapped field carries energy and momentum, so it
can contribute to the gravity and gravitomagnetic source. If the same
structure transports electric-odd throat identity, it may also produce an
electromagnetic magnetic moment.

This is not double counting if the observables are derived from different
source projections:

\[
\text{circulating solution}
\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
T^{0a}\ \text{and energy} & \text{gravity/GM response},\\
j_s^a\ \text{or electric-odd overlap} & \text{EM magnetic response}.
\end{cases}
\]

The first cannot generally be set to zero merely because the circulation was
introduced to explain magnetism. The second cannot be inferred merely from
nonzero mechanical angular momentum. An anapole-like circulation, for
example, could gravitate while having no leading electromagnetic dipole.

### 11.7 Operator mixing does not erase source identity

On a uniform background, gravity-like compression, transverse shear,
\(h\)-displacement, and interface variables may separate at quadratic order.
On a moving, tilted, or nonuniform throat background, symmetry permits
off-diagonal response.

A useful organization is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathcal O_G\\
\mathcal O_E\\
\mathcal O_{\rm GM}\\
\mathcal O_{\rm M}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
R_{GG}&R_{GE}&R_{GJ}&R_{GI}\\
R_{EG}&R_{EE}&R_{EJ}&R_{EI}\\
R_{JG}&R_{JE}&R_{JJ}&R_{JI}\\
R_{IG}&R_{IE}&R_{IJ}&R_{II}
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
Q_g\\
sQ_e\\
P_{\rm mass},J_{\rm mass}\\
sI,\mu_s
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The labels are schematic. Their purpose is to distinguish a source basis from
the coupled observable eigenmodes. Nonzero off-diagonal blocks mean that an
electric source can excite a gravity-like field, or a mass current can excite
a shear observable. They do not make \(Q_g=sQ_e\) or electromagnetic
magnetism identical to gravitomagnetism.

The v3 nonuniform operator work and the older moving-sector parity findings
should eventually supply or constrain these blocks.

### 11.8 The similar velocity suppression is not the primary hierarchy

Both comparison theories contain a common structural pattern:
velocity-dependent magnetic or gravitomagnetic effects are typically
suppressed relative to their static parent interaction by powers of
\(v/c\). A successful medium may reproduce that pattern because translating a
source excites transverse or retarded response only at higher velocity order.

But

\[
\frac{F_{\rm magnetic}}{F_{\rm electric}}
\sim
\frac{v^2}{c_\gamma^2},
\qquad
\frac{F_{\rm GM}}{F_{\rm gravity}}
\sim
\frac{v^2}{c_g^2}
\]

does not explain why the primary electric and gravitational forces differ so
enormously for an electron-like object. That hierarchy compares the
orientation coupling with the drain/mass coupling, not a static force with
its own velocity correction.

Once one throat branch fixes both source strengths, their dimensionless ratio
is a powerful held-out test. At present the gravity and electric
normalizations are independently calibrated or unresolved, so the hierarchy
has not been predicted.

### 11.9 The observables are operationally different

Electromagnetic magnetism is tested through charge-sign-dependent forces,
torques, induction, and radiation on oriented probes. Gravitomagnetism is
tested through orientation-even velocity corrections and precession of
mass-energy probes. A field picture is acceptable only if it reproduces those
probe responses.

The minimum separation tests are:

1. reverse \(s\) at fixed mass, motion, and internal angular momentum;
2. reverse the circulation at fixed \(s\);
3. reverse translation at fixed drain throughput;
4. remove the electric-odd overlap while preserving mass flow;
5. remove mass/drain flow while preserving a formal electric source, where
   the action admits that control;
6. compare a neutral circulating composite with a charged noncirculating
   throat;
7. evaluate both force and torque on mass-like and charge-like probes.

These ablations expose whether one fitted swirl has been asked to imitate two
different observables.

### 11.10 The common reservoir closes both ledgers

Because all channels live in one medium, energy, momentum, and angular
momentum transferred between them must appear in one full-system balance. The
source classification does not permit separate invisible reservoirs for
gravity and electromagnetism unless the parent action actually contains them.

For a control volume containing a throat,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}
\begin{pmatrix}
E\\P_I\\J_{IJ}
\end{pmatrix}_{\rm volume}
=
-\begin{pmatrix}
\mathcal F_E\\
\mathcal F_{P,I}\\
\mathcal F_{J,IJ}
\end{pmatrix}_{\rm boundary}
+\begin{pmatrix}
\mathcal W\\
\mathcal F_{\rm ext}\\
\mathcal T_{\rm ext}
\end{pmatrix}_{\rm reservoir}.
\]

The sector labels describe how the exchanged quantity is observed, not
permission to violate the common balance. This is especially important when
a trapped shear mode supplies particle support, magnetic moment, and part of
the gravitating energy at the same time.

### 11.11 Section status

The gravity analog is presently based on an orientation-even drain and
material response. Its finite-slab zero-mode \(1/r^2\) law, attractive sign,
and return-residual prediction remain conditional pending the S14a bridge;
\(G\) is calibrated. The weak-field gravitomagnetic flow has been calibrated
to the Kerr/Lense--Thirring target but has not been derived from the vortex
microphysics. Electromagnetic magnetism remains an electric-odd moving or
circulating response with its own unresolved native coupling.

The firewall does not assert exact decoupling. It requires every calculation
to label the source moment, transformation law, carrier, probe observable,
and reservoir flux. Mixing is allowed and potentially predictive; silent
relabeling of the same swirl is not.

## 13. Success, departure, and falsification for a toy analog

This project is not trying to derive all of nature from no assumptions. It is
asking whether one tangible brane--bulk system can support defects and
responses that reproduce recognizable parts of known physics. That narrower
aim still needs a sharp success standard; otherwise every mismatch can be
renamed an interesting feature and every missing mechanism can be supplied
after the fact.

### 13.1 The target is operational, scoped, and layered

A claim of success should always state:

- the observable being reproduced;
- the spatial, temporal, amplitude, and frequency regime;
- the branch and environmental state;
- the postulates and calibrations used;
- the extra modes or departures present;
- the independent comparisons not used to set parameters.

“The model supports light” can therefore mean the defensible limited claim
that a supplied ordered medium has two stable transverse waves. It should not
silently mean localized photons, quantum electrodynamics, Lorentz invariance
at every scale, or a completed particle sector.

### 13.2 Five levels of success

| Level | Required achievement | Example in this program |
|---|---|---|
| kinematic | the needed degrees of freedom and tensor structures exist | two transverse brane modes |
| dynamical | a stable action or balance law propagates them consistently | positive transverse dispersion with controlled additional modes |
| operational | specified probes experience the targeted force or signal | inverse-square force or polarization response |
| integrated | several sectors follow from the same frozen branch and parameters | static electricity, magnetism, and radiation share one throat coupling |
| predictive | held-out dimensionless results agree without retuning | cone ratios, magnetic/electric coefficient, leakage, or force hierarchy |

A partial analog may legitimately stop at an earlier level. The paper must
name the level instead of allowing familiar terminology to imply all five.

### 13.3 Postulates are legitimate inputs, but they spend explanatory budget

A toy medium may be assigned compressibility, an ordered phase, rotational
stiffness, interface structure, or constituent degrees of freedom. Failure to
derive a legitimately declared postulate is not by itself a contradiction.

It does have a cost. Each independently selectable physical property is a
knob, even if it appears through several symbols. Conversely, several
coefficients tied by one constitutive law may represent one input rather than
many. The accounting question is:

\[
\text{predictive surplus}
=
\text{independent held-out outputs}
-\text{independent fitted or selected physical inputs}.
\]

This expression is schematic, not a literal universal score. Its purpose is
to expose whether the same material assumptions explain several phenomena or
whether each phenomenon receives its own adjustable mechanism.

At the date of this draft, \(\rho_{B0}\), \(\chi_c\), and \(C_{hu}\) remain
three irreducibly independent embedding or brane-surface inputs in the
one-medium knit. They must be counted on the input side until a dynamical
reduction removes them; `no_fourth_arena=True` is only labeling closure.
Conversely, the gravity \(1/r^2\) law, attractive sign, and return-residual
prediction must not be counted as earned held-out outputs while they remain
conditional on the S14a drain bridge. Any numerical surplus total that treats
the trio as reduced or those gravity results as earned is overstated.

### 13.4 Exact effective agreement is welcome; imported ontology is not an explanation

The model is not required to use Maxwell potentials, point particles, or the
geometric ontology of general relativity. It may nevertheless recover an
exact Maxwell or GR-like equation in a controlled effective limit. Such an
agreement would be valuable if the variables, sources, constraints, and
coefficients arise from the material system.

What would *not* answer the native question is inserting an independent
Maxwell sector with assigned charge and then citing its exact Maxwell behavior
as an emergence result. That construction can be mathematically consistent
and useful as a strict parent or comparison theory, but the source and carrier
were supplied.

Thus “exact Maxwell is not the goal” means exact microscopic duplication is
unnecessary. It does not mean the model should avoid accurate observable
predictions.

### 13.5 Characterized departures can be successful outputs

Potentially acceptable departures include:

- a real longitudinal material mode distinct from the two light
  polarizations;
- a preferred medium frame with sufficiently suppressed observable effects;
- weak dispersion, birefringence, attenuation, or interface conversion;
- finite-size corrections near a throat;
- a non-Maxwell microscopic transformation law for a magnetic candidate;
- a classical defect model without quantum particle statistics;
- different microscopic carriers for static and velocity-dependent response,
  if their observed relation is nevertheless derived rather than fitted.

They are acceptable only after their size, regime, and probe coupling are
computed. An unbounded “small correction” is an open debt, not a successful
characterized departure.

Some departures may become especially useful predictions. A stable extra
mode, polarization-dependent throat scattering, or a finite conversion
threshold could distinguish the analog from its comparison theory.

### 13.6 Internal inconsistency is a genuine falsifier

Within the frozen candidate architecture, the following are failures:

- energy unbounded below or a ghost required for the desired sign;
- loss of hyperbolicity or an ill-posed initial-boundary problem;
- an unavoidable growing mode on the proposed vacuum or particle branch;
- failure of material, momentum, energy, or angular-momentum balance after all
  declared reservoirs are included;
- incompatible boundary conditions or overdetermined order dynamics;
- a required source property that cannot be defined consistently on the
  extended throat;
- mutually incompatible requirements imposed on the same physical degree of
  freedom with no additional native structure.

These failures do not depend on comparison with Maxwell or GR. They mean the
proposed material system is not internally viable in the claimed regime.

### 13.7 Phenomenological contradiction is also a falsifier

Once the model claims to reproduce a known sector in a stated regime, it fails
that claim if a frozen calculation necessarily gives:

- the wrong force sign or tensor pattern;
- the wrong number of observed low-energy polarizations;
- excessive light leakage, mode conversion, dispersion, or attenuation;
- an unsuppressed additional long-range force;
- unacceptable charge-correlated gravity;
- vacuum drag or radiation during ordinary uniform motion above the allowed
  level;
- nonuniversal probe response where universality is part of the claim;
- a propagation or preferred-frame effect incompatible with the target
  observations.

A calibrated datum cannot validate or falsify the coefficient used to fit it.
Only an independent prediction can perform that test.

### 13.8 Open, excluded route, sector failure, and architecture failure

These verdicts must remain distinct:

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| open | a required action, branch, source, boundary class, or computation is missing |
| candidate excluded | one concrete carrier or closure fails while alternatives within the declared architecture remain |
| sector failure | no allowed realization of a claimed sector passes its requirements |
| architecture failure | the frozen one-medium structure cannot satisfy the required sectors simultaneously |
| out of scope | the property was never part of the stated success claim |

For example, failure of the native polar field to generate a Gauss constraint
excludes that tested route to emergent gauge structure. It does not by itself
exclude a characterized material electromagnetic analog. Failure of every
stable transverse carrier, by contrast, would close the present light route.

Likewise, not deriving fermionic statistics does not falsify a paper claiming
only a classical vortex analog. Claiming an electron analog would raise the
bar and reopen that issue.

### 13.9 The no-retuning rule makes the tests able to fail

Before exposing a held-out comparison, freeze:

1. the parent and effective actions;
2. the background and throat branch;
3. source and probe definitions;
4. boundary and reservoir ensembles;
5. normalization and calibration data;
6. extraction windows and error measures.

If a later omitted term is physically required, it may be added only by
reopening every dependent result. It may not be used solely to repair the
failed observable while preserving earlier claims unchanged.

Particularly valuable held-out tests are dimensionless and cross-sector:

- \(r_{BA}\), tying magnetic and electric response;
- electric-to-gravity strength on the same throat;
- cone and dispersion ratios;
- leakage and conversion fractions;
- gyromagnetic response;
- reflected-branch degeneracy;
- static, moving, loop, and radiation tests with one source normalization.

### 13.10 A stable extra structure is not automatically a blemish

The model’s physical longitudinal branch illustrates the point. Exact Maxwell
has no propagating longitudinal photon, but a material brane can have a real
sound mode in addition to two transverse light-like modes. The existence of
that mode is:

- a dynamical success if it is stable and follows from the material action;
- a characterized departure if its coupling and observability are bounded;
- a phenomenological failure if it produces forbidden signals;
- an architectural failure only if it cannot be removed, suppressed, or
  consistently accommodated anywhere in the claimed model.

This prevents both errors: rejecting physical material structure merely for
not being Maxwell, and excusing an experimentally fatal extra mode merely
because the project is called a toy analog.

### 13.11 Present assessment

The current work has genuine kinematic and conditional dynamical successes:
the supplied homogeneous \(D=3\), isotropic-inertia brane action supports two
exactly-transverse modes and one physical longitudinal mode; the conditional
electric and moving-source calculations produce the relevant far-field and
tensor structures; and the calibrated weak-Kerr construction reproduces its
selected comparison behavior. The gravity range and attractive sign remain
conditional pending S14a and are not included here as earned successes.

It has not yet reached integrated success. The stable brane, autonomous
vortex throat, core-selected electric ensemble, common static/moving/radiative
coupling, intrinsic angular momentum, and global return closure remain open.
The model is therefore neither a completed electromagnetic/gravity theory nor
a failed toy architecture at this stage.

The next work should seek the cheapest calculations that convert open entries
into derived ratios or clean exclusions while preserving the same branch and
frozen inputs.

The result is not a smaller physical picture. It is a better-separated one.
