# Development plan for Newtonian free fall of two draining mouths

**Decision, 22 November 2026.** The first question is about drag. When a draining mouth moves relative to resting brane and bulk, does an appreciable force proportional to its velocity survive at low frequency? Audit the saved force and motion first. In parallel, specify what would make the stationary mouth a physical equilibrium. The rigid carrier is a useful test of drag even while the physical support remains open. Free fall of a material mouth requires both questions to be resolved.

The finish condition is a prediction for two unequal, freely moving openings in the same medium. Their material law and ambient preparation must determine both source strength and translational response. At large separation each target must give the same positive value of $d^2 a_t/M_s$, with independently measured source inertia $M_s$, subleading drag, and closed momentum and energy budgets. The targets can be tested without computing an orbit. [Five-page research brief](zero_pn_gravity_reviewer_brief.pdf) · [Exterior derivation](zero_pn_gravity.md)

## 1. Establish what drag the carrier calculation can resolve

**Archive audit complete.** On rigid mesh 34 the lag of the reconstructed held load approximately doubled between frequencies 0.01 and 0.02. The apparent wall resistance from a push remained near +0.0005421. The steady wall resistance agreed, but two independent spatial momentum estimates for the same tow gave −0.00000607 and −0.00000836. The archive therefore leaves physical drag unresolved. The occupied-mouth runs provide conservative controls across three reservoir sizes. [Step 1 table, diagnostic plot and missing work measurement](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_drag_response_audit.md) [Rigid force and motion](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_carrier_0pn_methods.md) · [Archived rigid measurements](../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_carrier_0pn.json) · [Occupied push measurements](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_occupied_slow_push.md)

For the project convention $e^{+i\omega t}$, let $X$ be entrance displacement, $V=i\omega X$, $a=-\omega^2X$, and $F$ the applied push in resting baths. Measure $Z_X=F/X$ and $Z_V=F/V=i\omega/\chi_a$, where $\chi_a=a/F$. The regular low-frequency expansion has the form

$$
Z_X=K_{\rm box}+i\omega\gamma-\omega^2M+\cdots,\qquad
\gamma_{\rm eff}(\omega)=\Re Z_V=-\omega\,\Im(1/\chi_a).
$$

A positive real velocity impedance requires positive mean actuator power, $\tfrac12\Re Z_V\,|V|^2$. Independently determine the static box spring, subtract its contribution to the reactive impedance, and test for a positive, stable inertia. Fit the complex response and inspect residuals for radiation or memory before using this expansion. A constant-speed tow supplies a second estimate of resistance. Keep the total tow work in the resting frame, including any sign-even dissipation that pair-minus-alone subtraction would remove. Measure the total held reaction $H$ in a separate brane-stream drive and check whether $-H/U_{\rm br}$ approaches a positive, nearly real $C_t$.

At angular frequency 0.01 on rigid mesh 34, the archived incident response $a/U_{\rm br}$ was $0.0204083-0.0002012i$. The separate push response $a/F$ was $0.0815601+0.0003606i$. Even at zero intake an oscillating remote phase gradient can generate pressure. The incident lag gives no drag coefficient or 35-radius cutoff. The occupied calculation also uses a Hermitian finite-reservoir dynamic stiffness at real frequency. Its collocated response is a conservative control with limited power to diagnose an open-system loss. [Moving-center implementation](../software/stage1_solver/tools/mouth_carrier_free_center.py) · [Occupied solver](../software/stage1_solver/tools/mouth_occupied_slow_push.py)

**First new experiment.** At frequency 0.01 compare actuator power with independently integrated cycle-mean energy currents in both reservoirs on the same rigid mesh-34 push. Average opposite amplitudes and subtract the undriven draining state to retain the quadratic change in power. The half-strength check should give one quarter of the excess. Include the second-order change in mean flow and reservoir power. Close the balance on each of two surfaces with the rate of stored-energy change, reservoir work and outgoing power accounted for once. Include transport by the surface velocity on mouth-following cuts. A force imbalance diagnoses missing momentum. Energy flux, storage and any heat distinguish reversible export or reservoir work from dissipation. Separate mesh refinement from bath enlargement. Extend in frequency once the error in this measurement is known.

**Frequency and tow controls.** At radius 1 and intake 0.25, use opposite weak pushes and half pushes, plus a separate slow tow at prescribed velocity through resting reservoirs. Match position, support, intake and bath frame. Compare actuator force with the rate of change of stored momentum and the rate of export across each of two enclosing surfaces, and actuator work with energy storage and flux. Start with the proposed angular frequencies 0.02, 0.01, 0.005 and 0.002, reusing qualified data. At each frequency enlarge the reservoirs or verify an outgoing-wave boundary against an enlarged domain, refine the mesh, and quantify reflection, force-readout disagreement and numerical error. Establish the open-boundary response at fixed frequency before drawing a low-frequency conclusion. A small imaginary part in a closed lossless cavity cannot establish a physical upper bound.

**Decision.** Declare a separation range, motion relative to the baths, and acceptable fractional force error before extending the sweep. For a target orbiting a fixed source as a reference trajectory, $U_s=D_s/(4\pi\rho d^2)$, $\Omega_{\rm dyn}^2=C_t U_s/(M_t d)$, and $V_{\rm char}\sim\Omega_{\rm dyn}d$. Thus the linear-drag fraction is approximately $|\gamma_{\rm eff}(\Omega_{\rm dyn})|/(M_t\Omega_{\rm dyn})$. Use the actual speeds for an unequal pair. A persistent nonzero linear resistance gives a drag fraction that grows as $d^{3/2}$. The asymptotic Newtonian claim requires $|\gamma_{\rm eff}(\Omega)|/(M_t\Omega)\to0$ along the stated low-frequency, open-reservoir limit. A loss measured at finite frequency can satisfy this condition. A claim of exact cancellation calls for an identity with verified hypotheses or an analytic limiting bound. A finite bound earns only the separations it covers. Even when the constant term vanishes, test frequency-dependent losses and nonlinear towing forces against the same force budget. If boundary or force-readout error dominates, record the upper bound or the unresolved sensitivity and move no claim downstream.

## 2. Select a physical mouth independently of gravity

Start with the existing radial equilibrium exclusion. On the archived cubic flare, no choice of one ground-wave action, uniform tension and uniform backing pressure balances the measured radial work at every depth. A constraint maintaining this shape can carry a reaction during translation. The rigid and flexible calculations remain benchmarks for their specified supports. The physical test needs an independently prepared mouth or a predictive effective law at its opening. [Eight-band equilibrium test](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_occupied_radial_balance.md) · [Existing depth-selection exclusions](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_carrier_depth_selection.md)

* **Shape route.** Start with a free radial profile and measure the jump in total normal traction, the work of the occupied wave, and the material’s restoring work in the same configuration. Use the three-dimensional wall area in four spatial dimensions and state the curvature and normal conventions. Include the carrier pressure once, including its flow contribution. Identify any end or support reactions. A positive order–disorder surface tension is a hypothesis for the mechanical wall tension. It applies only if the order boundary and load-bearing wall coincide or a measured attachment connects them. Fix surface energy on a neutral interface, wave action by an independent preparation or a stable gain–loss balance, and any bending modulus from independent curvature work. First relax the profile with known loads. Measure depth-dependent backing if residuals demand it, then consider curvature elasticity if an independent measurement supports it. Calibrate each addition before comparing it with a gravity observable. [Surface location and permeability](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_surface_registration.md)
* **Effective opening route.** Start from a steadily draining, stable shape with all static support reactions identified. A physically prepared mouth can then supply measured total force, motion, intake and energy exchange. An impedance measured on the unsupported cubic reference held in shape by a fixture includes that fixture. Such a measurement cannot establish the response of the physical mouth. A common independently calibrated boundary law can be tested across different radii without resolving the full interior. A separate fit at every radius characterizes those samples but leaves the radius dependence unexplained. Either route must predict a reserved held load or bend on the same state. With entrance and middle held, the two archived supports predict flare force per displacement of 0.2159234 and 0.3676533 on the original bath. Stability of the unforced shape and recovery under small opposite steps are essential checks. [Flare discriminator](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_occupied_slow_push.md)

**Decision.** On the shape route require normal work to balance at unused depths within independently assessed errors. On the effective route require a reproducible, steadily draining mouth whose measured response is independent of fixture load. Both routes require stability of the relevant perturbations and an independently fixed support or boundary law that predicts a new force reading. If neither route can supply these data, record the required mouth law and stop the physical release work there.

## 3. Measure who receives the sideways momentum

On the same selected mouth separate the two stream drives and the tow. Hold the entrance and drive only the brane, then only the bulk. For the tow, move the mouth through two resting reservoirs. Apply opposite and half-strength drives with a matched zero. In the steady relative-stream hypothesis the force is $A(U_{\rm br}-V)+B(U_{\rm bulk}-V)$. Then $C_t=A$ and tow resistance is $A+B$. Equal throughput with the ideal potential exteriors and a single holder already gave $A=D_t, B=-D_t$. Reserve the tow or a mixed drive as a prediction. An unknown physical conversion surface can change either coefficient independently. [Already checked ideal cancellation](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_tow_and_companion.md)

Check the equivalence between towing and a common stream by transforming the entire preparation, including far boundaries and actuator references. For each drive measure total actuator reaction and, independently, advected momentum and stress through nested receiving hemispheres, the feeding boundary and both slab faces. In four spatial dimensions the hemispherical boundary is three-dimensional. Include the closing plane or its disjoint physical partitions, local storage, moving-cut transport, and bath, fixture or thermostat impulse in a common frame. In brane-only cross-flow report the signed downstream fraction $\alpha(r)=\Delta I_{\rm bulk,x}(r)/(D_t U_{\rm br})$. A decaying bulk term still leaves the slab faces and other recipients. A nonzero plateau represents export on that cut. Its reversibility and its relation to towing require separate work and tow measurements.

**Material decision after the carrier drag and momentum tests.** Treat the coherent carrier and the classical thermal receiver as competing material descriptions until an independent measurement connects them. Freeze the allowed mouth resistance over the proposed separation range. On a thermal branch, require measured bulk momentum transport at the matched density and flow, with the thermostat impulse removed or explicitly accounted for, and test the resulting interface force against that same tolerance. On a coherent branch, bound energy and momentum export under the imposed motion, including allowed wave emission and any onset of vorticity. If both descriptions meet the tests, the data leave the choice open. If only one does, state its verified regime.

**Existing material evidence.** Record 5 uses classical four-dimensional particles with Morse pair forces and a Langevin heat bath. Its dense disordered sample relaxed held shear. The coherent phase carrier used for the drain is a separate model. Their density, stress, time and length scales, and force transfer across a mobile interface, have yet to be matched. A thermostat can absorb the lateral momentum under study. If the force budget depends appreciably on tangential transport or wall permeability, measure shear stress under opposite weak strain rates or measure the low-frequency shear loss modulus divided by frequency. Use independent preparations and control the bath momentum. Compare with a momentum-conserving measurement and, where relevant, circulation around loops or four-dimensional vorticity components $\partial_i v_j-\partial_j v_i$. Finite viscosity alone fixes neither $C_t$ nor the drag. Reopen the mobile-interface test only for a residual that these observations can distinguish, after demonstrating compatible normal and tangential tractions. [Receiver model](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_bulk_isotropic_4d.md) · [Held shear](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_receiver_transit.md) · [Common-face stopping point](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_receiver_common_face.md) · [Viscosity measurement methods](https://docs.lammps.org/Howto_viscosity.html)

**Decision.** Advance when two cuts agree after intervening storage, the force budget closes within the declared gravity tolerance, and the independent coefficients predict the reserved drive. Check $a=-\chi_a H$ on a separately calculated steady harmonic free-center response at the same incident waveform and frequency, using the same entrance coordinate for holding and pushing. This identity tests linear superposition and consistent boundary forces. Predicting sudden removal of a holder requires a transient response over the drive band and matched initial fields and history. The independent selection of the material and successful prediction on another state supply the physical test.

## 4. Test reciprocity and radius dependence before choosing a new particle model

Keep the physical radius-dependent mouth family as the primary hypothesis. Measure isolated far-field **material mass** intake $D(R)$, signed held coefficient $C(R)$ and push inertia $M(R)$ at the same ambient conditions and on the same stable branch. Convert the solver’s number current to mass flux explicitly and check shell exchange. For a leading force $F_{t\leftarrow s}=C_tD_s/(4\pi\rho d^2)$, equal-and-opposite center forces require $C/D=\kappa$ across radii if there is no other leading load or net momentum exported by the pair. A center imbalance calls for a full material momentum audit. Total conservation includes the medium. Universal source and target scaling further require common positive $D/M$ and $C/M$. On a reciprocal branch, $G_{\rm eff}=\kappa(D/M)^2/(4\pi\rho)$. The time-dimensional ratio $M/D$ may contain stored wave and added-fluid inertia. Interpreting it as a constituent transit time requires separate evidence. [Newtonian requirements](../research/pde_ledger_v3/SUBSTRATE_REQUIREMENTS.md#what-two-inward-drains-must-reproduce-in-the-newtonian-limit)

Calibrate on radii 1 and 2. Use the published radius-4 result as a retrospective check outside the fit. Before proceeding, reserve a genuinely unexamined radius or depth and reservoir combination for a prospective prediction. The measured 9.88% shortfall at radius 4 was in $C/M$ for a fixed-depth, fixed-phase, finite-bath carrier family. Plot $D/R^3$ and $M/R^3$ separately against $R/L$ alongside $C/D$. At fixed depth the proposed crossover predicts a falling normalized intake and a rising normalized inertia where both corrections become appreciable. Changes in capture can also alter $C/M$. Reserve a wider radius such as 6 or 8 only if the same stable family and well-separated boundaries persist. A monotonic trend alone cannot identify its mechanism. To test the proposed geometric explanation, measure the phase drop per unit current through the feed, neck and receiving regions separately. Partition kinetic energy or push momentum at matched cuts, including wave or interfacial storage and any cross terms. Independently vary throat depth, slab thickness and reservoir extent. Test the proposed resistance scalings $L/R^3,1/R^2,1/R$ and bulk added mass proportional to $R^4$ only in geometries supporting those asymptotics. Keep the radius-4 component data out of calibration. Check the known trend and predict the separate reserved case before reading it. Establish convergence before attributing the drift to a material law. [Radius and conductance controls](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_carrier_0pn_methods.md)

**Decision about alternative particles.** Change the physical family only on independent evidence. A discrete elementary radius would lead to a separate test of clusters, including intake shielding, binding energy, shared flow and additivity of translated inertia. A choked slender throat requires an independently selected depth and a regular sonic solution (or a derived dispersive replacement). Vary downstream pressure at fixed upstream state and allow intake to respond. The existing nozzle calculation supplies a conditional coefficient but leaves the stationary profile open. A wave-dominated mouth requires measured wave energy, incremental push inertia under changing occupation, and an intake law predicting the same ratio. None follows from the radius-4 shortfall alone. A same-radius change of wave occupation is a particularly useful test if both states can be prepared stably. Bring a change to the particle hypothesis back for discussion before adopting it. ## 5. Close the energy balance and predict an unequal pair

Design the energy accounting with the force measurements from the outset. In the prescribed-intake radial benchmark, one fixed mouth and one approaching at speed $s$ give outward interaction-energy fluxes $Fs$ and zero, respectively, and center powers zero and $Fs$. Each inner region owes net incremental power $Fs$ when the benchmark’s entire static load is assigned to its center. This does not identify either physical reservoir as the supplier. Under fixed distant phase the throughputs change and the stationary energy has a different derivative. Measure actual boundary power, conversion and wave storage, heat, and actuator work separately in paired, both isolated, and background controls. Reverse and halve approach speed, retain the corresponding moving-surface terms, and report absolute towing work alongside the interaction subtraction. [Fixed-intake work identity](../software/stage1_solver/reports/two_drain_energy.md) · [Stationary-mouth energy flux](../software/stage1_solver/reports/stationary_mouth_energy_test.md) · [Fixed-phase pair energy](../software/stage1_solver/reports/mouth_carrier_0pn_methods.md#two-mouth-energy)

Freeze the validated geometry or effective mouth law, force coefficients, intake law, and all calibrations. Predict both free-center accelerations for unequal independently measured masses at multiple far-field separations, using unequal radii on the primary branch. Record the prediction before calculating the pair. Interchange source and target, compare with isolated controls, and bound mutual inertial response and other loads of inverse-square order. Check the common positive $d^2a_t/M_s$, sideways drift, momentum and energy closure, and sensitivity to domain size and resolution. Report a finite validity range wherever the data yield bounds. If a required interface or support law is still missing, state the independent measurement that would decide it and keep the result conditional. Each stage ends with an independent check of its inputs, observable and decision before it is used in the next stage.
