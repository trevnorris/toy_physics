# Development plan for Newtonian free fall of two draining mouths

## Material assumed throughout

The brane and receiving bulk are two phases of one material. The ordered brane is a supersolid. Its shear stiffness supports transverse waves for the light sector, and its coherent component can flow without friction. At the mouth the material turns into the fourth spatial direction and loses crystalline order. The receiving phase is a disordered, coherent superfluid with zero static shear modulus and zero shear viscosity in the model. Mass, pressure and momentum continue through the conversion. Gravity, light and charge must follow from compatible lower-level laws of this material. These are physical premises set by the user. The current coherent-carrier calculation tests one sector of them.

Record 5 is retired as a physical bulk candidate. Its classical particles and Langevin bath remain an archived demonstration of shear relaxation. Future microscopic receiver or interface calculations must retain superfluid coherence and account for the material's energy and momentum. Apparatus work, outgoing waves and any allowed quantized vortices still require measurement in their applicable regimes. Zero shear viscosity alone does not set the force on a moving mouth.
