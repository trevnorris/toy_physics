# Part I — Starting Point and Theorem Target
**Handoff notes for the 2.5PN program**

## Purpose of this part

This part is meant to be the **front door** for any future session that needs to pick up the 2.5PN work without re-reading the whole conversation. Its job is to state, as cleanly as possible:

1. what the program looked like **before** the 2.5PN push,
2. why 2.5PN was chosen as the next pressure test,
3. and what exact theorem question the session was trying to answer.

This is not yet the place for the channel-by-channel results. Those belong in later parts. Part I is the setup ledger and theorem target.

A future session should be able to read only this part and immediately know:
- what was already frozen,
- what was still open,
- why the missing PDE mattered,
- and what counted as success or failure for the 2.5PN campaign.

## 1. Project status before the 2.5PN push

### 1.1 The project was already strong on the conservative side

Before this session, the program had already built a substantial conservative backbone.

The carry-forward lower-order ledger included:
- the effective-side Newtonian / 0PN inverse-square sector,
- the 1PN perihelion and EIH bookkeeping,
- the fixed effective coefficients
  \[
  \kappa_\rho = 1,\qquad
  n = 5,\qquad
  \kappa_{\rm add} = \frac12,\qquad
  \kappa_{\rm PV} = \frac32,\qquad
  \beta_{\rm 1PN} = 3,
  \]
- and the preferred throat/cavity aspect ratio
  \[
  L/a \approx 1.85.
  \]

The important interpretation is that these were not random tunings. They were treated as the surviving viability conditions from the earlier falsification-first program. The methodology had already been:

1. identify a hard external destination,
2. work backward to the required structure,
3. see whether the toy model could host it,
4. and only then attempt deeper derivations.

That workflow mattered for the 2.5PN pivot because it set the default research style: **known-destination falsification first, first-principles PDE later**.

### 1.2 The 1PN result was frozen only within a declared closure hierarchy

A critical precondition for the 2.5PN push was the correct reading of the 1PN result.

The 1PN assembly was **not** being treated as a fully assumption-free theorem of the moving-throat PDE. It was frozen only **within a declared closure hierarchy**. In that hierarchy, the conservative 1PN two-body sector was considered solved, but several major sectors remained explicitly open:

- dissipative leakage / radiation reaction,
- spin couplings,
- strong-field completion,
- higher-PN completion beyond the solved conservative regime,
- and the fully solved moving-throat PDE itself.

This distinction is one of the most important handoff points in the entire project. If a future session forgets it, it will overstate what the 1PN and 2PN papers actually proved.

### 1.3 The 2PN result was a major conservative success

The immediate technical starting point for this session was the full conservative 2PN assembly.

The 2PN paper claimed, within the same declared closure hierarchy, that the complete conservative two-body near-zone ledger through order \(c^{-4}\) was closed, and that the resulting two-body Lagrangian Legendre-transformed exactly to the standard generic-frame ADM Hamiltonian through 2PN.

That gave the project a genuinely strong conservative platform. By the time this session began, the natural question was no longer “can the model reproduce conservative PN structure at all?” The answer to that, within the frozen hierarchy, was already yes through 2PN.

What remained unresolved was the next structural step:
- not another conservative coefficient ledger,
- but the first genuinely **time-asymmetric / retarded / dissipative** sector.

### 1.4 The 2PN paper also changed the ontology of the particle

The 2PN result was important for a second reason: it changed how the defect had to be viewed.

After 2PN, the particle could no longer be treated as “just a scalar monopole with corrections.” The conservative 2PN cross sector already looked like a **small throat-response theory** with at least three distinct ingredients:

- a carried-forward odd dipole wake sector,
- a genuinely new even \(P_0 \oplus P_2\) mouth/support layer,
- and a separate geometry-closure channel.

That was a major conceptual shift.

It meant the model was already revealing hidden internal structure as soon as it was pushed beyond the simplest conservative regime. So the move to 2.5PN was never going to be “just add one more PN term.” It was always going to test whether this emerging throat-response structure could support the first dissipative sector without breaking the GR-like target.

### 1.5 The later lepton / atom work sharpened the same bottleneck

After the 2PN paper, the work moved into lepton and atomic directions. At first glance this looked like a detour. In practice it turned out to be diagnostic.

Those later notes repeatedly exposed the same missing ingredient from a different angle:

- the conservative stack was strong,
- but the missing theorem was not another static closure,
- it was a **dynamic constitutive law** for open-system transport / leakage / radiative response.

Several later conclusions were especially relevant for the eventual 2.5PN push:

1. static conservative response was not the same thing as an actual transport law,
2. the exact trapped D/N branch behaved more like a boundary hammer than a direct trans-brane current source,
3. static scalar response gave compliance rather than DC flux mobility,
4. and the missing amplitude / transport law was genuinely dynamic.

That was the key reason the 2.5PN pivot made sense. The later particle-side notes had already suggested that the unresolved physics was likely to show up precisely where time-asymmetric response first mattered.

### 1.6 The right pre-2.5PN summary

So the clean “state of play” before the 2.5PN campaign was:

- **Conservative Newtonian + 1PN + 2PN:** strong and internally organized.
- **Particle ontology:** no longer a pure scalar monopole; already showing dipole wake plus \(P_0\oplus P_2\) structure.
- **Open sectors:** dynamic response, leakage, radiation reaction, and the fully solved moving-throat PDE.
- **Strategic lesson from later work:** the missing physics looked more like retarded open-system response than like another static conservative coefficient.

That is the true launch point for the session.

## 2. Why 2.5PN was the decisive test

### 2.1 In standard PN theory, 2.5PN is the first dissipative order

The basic reason for choosing 2.5PN was standard PN structure.

Through 2PN, the ordinary two-body problem is conservative. The first genuinely non-conservative term appears at **2.5PN**, where radiation reaction enters. So 2.5PN is the first place where a GR-like theory must leave the purely conservative near-zone ledger and admit:

- retarded response,
- time asymmetry,
- energy loss,
- and near-zone / far-zone balance.

That made 2.5PN the natural next theorem gate.

If the toy model could not support the right kind of dissipative opening there, then the 2PN conservative success would not automatically extend into the first radiative regime.

### 2.2 2.5PN was also the right test for this specific model

For this model, 2.5PN was even more informative than in ordinary PN theory.

The reason is that the missing PDE was not yet solved. So the question became:

> Can known low-frequency physics, plus the already exposed throat/worldtube structure, tell us what the missing PDE would have to look like?

That made 2.5PN attractive for two reasons at once:

1. it was the first order where the model **had** to show retarded/open behavior if it wanted to stay GR-like,
2. and it could potentially constrain the missing PDE by forcing specific low-frequency selection rules and response kernels.

So the 2.5PN campaign was never only about “deriving the next PN coefficient.” It was a deliberate attempt to pressure-test the ontology and use the answer to learn what the missing PDE would have to contain.

### 2.3 2.5PN is where the model could fail cleanly

This was a major strategic advantage.

If the model had failed only after a huge new PDE build, it would have been unclear whether the failure came from the ontology or the particular PDE attempt. But 2.5PN offered a much cleaner falsification route.

A GR-like 2.5PN sector requires, at minimum:

- no dangerous lower odd scalar channel contaminating the relative dynamics,
- no dangerous same-order dipole channel that breaks universality,
- and a positive retarded quadrupole response producing the correct sign and order of damping.

That is a sharp failure ledger. It means the model can fail in specific, interpretable ways:
- scalar leakage appearing too early,
- dipole dissipation surviving in the relative sector,
- or no viable retarded quadrupole branch.

That made 2.5PN the right place to look for a “spot for failure.”

### 2.4 The session’s working physics target

Very early in the session, the working mathematical target became:

- determine whether the frozen conservative hierarchy alone can yield a nonzero 2.5PN theorem,
- and if not, determine the **minimal missing retarded structure** required to rescue one.

In practical terms, the target was to see whether the model could support a low-frequency retarded sector of schematic form
\[
Y^{\rm ret}(\omega)
=
Y^{\rm even}(\omega)
+
i\,(\text{odd dissipative part}),
\]
with the wrong odd channels forced to vanish and the right quadrupole odd term surviving with the correct sign.

That is the bridge between Part I and the later parts of the report.

### 2.5 Why this was better than waiting for the full PDE

There was an explicit methodological decision here:

**Do not wait for the full moving-throat PDE before asking whether the model can survive 2.5PN.**

The reason was the same one that had already guided the earlier successful papers and the later lepton audit:
- first identify what structure must exist,
- then ask whether the model can support it,
- then derive more deeply only if the necessary structure survives.

That is what made the 2.5PN campaign useful even before a full PDE existed. It turned the missing PDE from a vague wish into a list of concrete low-frequency requirements.

## 3. The central theorem question

### 3.1 The main question

The exact theorem question for the session was:

> **Can the 4D throat model support a full GR-like point-particle 2.5PN theorem, or does it fail at or before the first dissipative PN order?**

This was not framed as a demand that success was guaranteed. The point was to test the model hard enough that a real failure, if present, would become visible.

### 3.2 What counted as success

At the beginning of the campaign, “success” did **not** mean proving the entire moving-throat PDE.

A successful 2.5PN program would have been any of the following, in increasing strength:

1. a clear proof that the current hierarchy can be extended to a full GR-like 2.5PN theorem;
2. or, failing that, a clean conditional theorem showing that a full GR-like 2.5PN sector follows once a sharply stated retarded closure is assumed;
3. or, at minimum, a precise derivation of what low-frequency retarded structure the missing PDE would have to produce.

So success included narrowing the problem to a short list of theorem gates rather than leaving it as “we need more physics somewhere.”

### 3.3 What counted as failure

Failure also had a precise meaning.

The model would count as having failed the GR-like 2.5PN target if any of the following happened:

- a genuine lower-order scalar dissipative channel survived in the relative problem,
- a same-order dipole dissipative channel survived and could not be demoted or absorbed,
- the quadrupole channel failed to produce the right retarded odd structure,
- the damping sign came out wrong,
- or the source-to-radiative map projected away the universal orbital/worldtube STF quadrupole.

This is one of the most important handoff lists in the whole report. It says exactly where future sessions should still look for collapse.

### 3.4 The working theorem target in mathematical form

As the session began, the desired low-frequency structure could be summarized schematically like this:

1. dangerous scalar odd terms must be absent or sufficiently suppressed;
2. dangerous dipole odd terms must vanish, decouple, or be pushed into higher finite-size order;
3. the quadrupole branch must admit a retarded completion of the form
   \[
   Y_2^{\rm ret}(\omega)
   =
   Y_2^{\rm even}(\omega)
   +
   i\,\gamma_5 \omega^5
   + O(\omega^7),
   \qquad \gamma_5>0;
   \]
4. and the effective source map must preserve the orbital/worldtube STF quadrupole so that the resulting local force is gauge-equivalent to the standard 2.5PN family.

At the start of the session, none of that was yet proven. It was the target ledger.

### 3.5 The practical research strategy adopted in this session

The session therefore adopted a layered audit strategy:

1. **First:** test whether the frozen conservative hierarchy alone can generate 2.5PN.  
2. **Second:** if not, introduce the minimal retarded scaffold and see what it would need to look like.  
3. **Third:** split the problem into channel-by-channel gates:
   - scalar,
   - dipole,
   - quadrupole.
4. **Fourth:** try to rescue or kill each gate using only structure already present in the ontology plus the lightest plausible low-frequency completion.  
5. **Fifth:** use the surviving gates to isolate the exact remaining PDE question.

That layered structure is what the later parts of the report follow.

### 3.6 The most important conceptual conclusion already visible at the start

Even before the detailed channel audits, one conclusion was already becoming visible:

The real unknown was **not** another conservative coefficient.  
The real unknown was the model’s **retarded open-system response law**.

That is why the session repeatedly turned away from purely static closures and toward:
- projected leakage,
- mouth admittances,
- spectral no-go theorems,
- outgoing partial-wave structure,
- and retarded matching to source multipoles.

Any future session that restarts this work should keep that point front and center.

# Part II — The First Decisive Benchmark
**Handoff notes for the 2.5PN program**

## Purpose of this part

Part II records the **first hard benchmark** of the 2.5PN campaign.

Its job is to preserve the moment where the session stopped speaking vaguely about
“missing dissipative physics” and turned that into a precise mathematical test.
This part answers two questions:

1. **Why the frozen conservative hierarchy cannot, by itself, produce a real 2.5PN theorem.**
2. **What the minimum successful retarded structure must look like if the model is to recover a standard GR-like 2.5PN radiation-reaction sector.**

This is the benchmark against which all later scalar, dipole, and quadrupole audits should be read.
A future session should be able to read this part and immediately know:

- what was disproved,
- what was positively demonstrated,
- and what exact missing theorem was identified.

---

## 4. Decisive test from the frozen conservative hierarchy

### 4.1 What was being tested

At the start of the 2.5PN push, the conservative 2PN hierarchy was already strong,
but it was still a **real-even low-frequency theory**.
That made the first question very sharp:

> Can the frozen conservative hierarchy alone generate a nonzero local 2.5PN reaction term?

The answer was tested directly at the level of low-frequency kernels.

### 4.2 The structural obstruction

A purely conservative low-frequency response has the form
\[
K(\omega)=K_0 + K_2\omega^2 + K_4\omega^4 + \cdots,
\]
with all coefficients real and with only even powers of \(\omega\).

Passing to the time domain gives
\[
K(\partial_t)=K_0 + K_2\partial_t^2 + K_4\partial_t^4 + \cdots.
\]

So a real-even kernel produces only **time-reversal-even local operators**.
It can generate conservative corrections, but not a genuine dissipative reaction force.

That immediately gives the first no-go statement:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The frozen conservative hierarchy alone cannot generate a nonzero local 2.5PN reaction term.}
}
\]

This was the first real theorem-level negative result of the session.
It did **not** say the broader model fails.
It said something narrower and more useful:

- the current frozen conservative files are insufficient by themselves,
- therefore any successful 2.5PN theorem must come from a **new retarded sector**, not from more conservative algebra.

### 4.3 Why this mattered

This result was strategically important because it moved the project from
“maybe more bookkeeping will finish 2.5PN”
into
“the missing ingredient is definitely a causal, time-odd response law.”

That was the first place where the session really isolated the difference between:

- **what was already solved** (conservative 2PN), and
- **what still had to be derived** (retarded/open-system physics).

### 4.4 The minimum admissible escape hatch

Once the no-go was established, the next question was the minimal form a successful low-frequency completion could take.

At leading order, the smallest possible correction that can generate a local quadrupolar 2.5PN reaction term is
\[
K^{\rm ret}(\omega)=K^{\rm even}(\omega)+ i\,\lambda_5\,\omega^5 + O(\omega^7).
\]

This is the decisive ansatz because:

- the odd term is the first time-asymmetric/dissipative piece,
- the \(\omega^5\) scaling is the right local signature of a leading quadrupolar radiation-reaction sector,
- and the sign of \(\lambda_5\) determines damping versus antidamping.

So after the no-go, the 2.5PN question became:

> Can the model derive, rather than merely assume, a retarded quadrupole channel whose first physical odd term is \(i\omega^5\)?

That is the exact theorem gate that the rest of the session kept returning to.

## 5. Constructive benchmark: minimal retarded quadrupole prototype

### 5.1 Why a constructive benchmark was needed

Once the frozen hierarchy failed, the session needed to check whether the target itself was internally coherent.
In other words:

> If one gives the model the *minimum* retarded quadrupole structure it seems to need, does the resulting local force really land on the standard 2.5PN family?

This was the purpose of the Burke–Thorne prototype.

The benchmark was not meant to prove the full model.
It was meant to answer a narrower but crucial question:

- is a minimal retarded quadrupole kernel **sufficient** to generate a known correct 2.5PN radiation-reaction force?

### 5.2 Prototype setup

The prototype used the standard STF quadrupole of the relative two-body motion,
\[
Q_{ij}=\mu\left(x_i x_j-\frac13\delta_{ij}r^2\right),
\]
and the Burke–Thorne relative-force law
\[
a_{\rm rel}^i = -\frac{2G}{5c^5}\,x^j Q^{(5)}_{ij}.
\]

The calculation then:

1. took fifth time derivatives of the STF quadrupole,
2. used the Newtonian relative equations of motion for order reduction,
3. reduced the answer to the standard local 2.5PN basis,
4. and compared the result to the known Iyer–Will two-parameter family.

This was done explicitly in SymPy.

### 5.3 What the prototype derived

At a convenient orbital phase choice, the symbolic reduction gave a local relative acceleration of the form
\[
\mathbf a_{2.5\mathrm{PN}}
=
\frac{8G^2m^2\nu}{5c^5r^3}
\left[
A\,\dot r\,\mathbf n + B\,\mathbf v
\right],
\]
with
\[
A = 18v^2 + \frac{2Gm}{3r} - 25\dot r^2,
\qquad
B = -6v^2 + \frac{2Gm}{r} + 15\dot r^2.
\]

This is already the standard local 2.5PN structure.
The key step was then to compare these coefficients to the Iyer–Will gauge family.

### 5.4 Matching to the standard 2.5PN family

The symbolic solve showed that the prototype lands exactly on the Burke–Thorne member of the standard 2.5PN family:
\[
\alpha = 4,
\qquad
\beta = 5.
\]

That was a major positive result.
It showed that a minimal retarded quadrupole kernel is not merely qualitatively plausible.
It is already enough to reproduce a known correct gauge representative of the GR 2.5PN radiation-reaction sector.

### 5.5 Meaning of the constructive benchmark

This benchmark established the positive half of the theorem ledger:

\[
\boxed{
\text{A minimal retarded quadrupole sector is sufficient to reproduce a valid standard 2.5PN local force.}
}
\]

Combined with the earlier no-go, this gave the crucial two-part benchmark of the whole session:

1. **Negative half:** the frozen conservative hierarchy alone cannot produce 2.5PN.
2. **Positive half:** once a minimal retarded quadrupole odd term is supplied, the correct standard local 2.5PN structure appears.

That is why these two results belong together as Part II.

## 6. What Part II changed in the program

### 6.1 The problem stopped being vague

Before this benchmark, “missing physics” could still have meant many things.
After the benchmark, it became much more specific.

The missing theorem was no longer “derive more of the model.”
It was:

\[
\boxed{
\text{derive the retarded quadrupole response law that supplies the }i\omega^5\text{ term.}
}
\]

Everything that came later in the session — scalar audits, dipole audits, quadrupole source-map work — was downstream of this clarification.

### 6.2 The natural failure ledger became visible

Once the benchmark was in place, the possible failure modes also became explicit.
A successful 2.5PN completion would need to avoid all of the following:

1. **No odd term at all**  
   Then the program stops at conservative 2PN.

2. **The wrong odd channel**  
   If a scalar or dipole odd term survives where it should not, the model misses the GR-like selection rules.

3. **The wrong sign**  
   If the odd quadrupole term comes with the wrong sign, the model predicts antidamping rather than radiation reaction.

4. **The wrong representation content**  
   If the retarded completion does not actually couple to the canonical STF quadrupole source, the universal 2.5PN route fails.

These became the working gates for the rest of the session.

### 6.3 The distinction between “full theorem” and “conditional theorem” also became sharp

Part II made it possible to say clearly what the project could and could not yet claim.

At this stage, the session had **not** derived a full first-principles 2.5PN theorem from the moving-throat PDE.
What it had done instead was identify the exact form of the missing structure and show that, once supplied, it lands on the correct 2.5PN benchmark.

So after Part II, the program had moved into a very useful intermediate state:

- not a full theorem yet,
- but no longer vague,
- and already constrained strongly enough to make later audits decisive.

## 17. Why the scalar sector was the first serious kill switch

### 17.1 The scalar channel sat at the bottom of the odd hierarchy

Part III had already organized the low-frequency odd channels as
\[
K_0^{\rm odd}(\omega)\sim i\gamma_1\omega,
\qquad
K_1^{\rm odd}(\omega)\sim i\gamma_3\omega^3,
\qquad
K_2^{\rm odd}(\omega)\sim i\gamma_5\omega^5.
\]

So the scalar channel was immediately the most dangerous one.
If a physical monopole odd term survived in the reduced subsystem, it would contaminate the theorem **before** the desired quadrupole 2.5PN structure even had a chance to matter.

That was the basic reason the scalar sector had to be audited first.

### 17.2 The danger was already explicit in the 2PN packaging

The conservative 2PN throat packaging had already reduced the scalar-like part of the response to
\[
S_{\rm eff}(\omega)=J_0^2Y_0(\omega)+J_{20}^2Y_{20}(\omega)-\Delta_{\rm geom}Y_g(\omega),
\]
with fixed static weights
\[
J_0^2=\frac{16}{5},
\qquad
J_{20}^2=\frac{25}{16},
\qquad
\Delta_{\rm geom}=\frac{281}{80}.
\]

So if the scalar channels picked up odd low-frequency pieces
\[
Y_0^{\rm ret}=Y_0^{\rm even}+i\delta_{01}\omega+\cdots,
\qquad
Y_g^{\rm ret}=Y_g^{\rm even}+i\delta_{g1}\omega+\cdots,
\]
then the effective scalar odd coefficient was already known to be
\[
\gamma_1^{\rm eff}
=
\frac{16}{5}\delta_{01}
-
\frac{281}{80}\delta_{g1}.
\]

This mattered because it turned the scalar problem from a vague physical concern into a precise low-frequency theorem gate.

### 17.3 The first scalar theorem target

The first scalar theorem target of the session was therefore
\[
\boxed{
\gamma_1^{\rm eff}=0.
}
\]

Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{16}{5}\delta_{01}-\frac{281}{80}\delta_{g1}=0.
}
\]

That is the coefficient combination that drove the early scalar calculations.
Everything that followed in this part should be read as an attempt either to prove this vanishing, replace it with a stronger theorem, or show that the dangerous scalar channel is nevertheless pushed above the universal point-particle 2.5PN sector.

## 18. Initial scalar cancellation test

### 18.1 What was tested first

The first scalar test asked whether the current stack already forced
\[
\gamma_1^{\rm eff}=0
\]
from the exact projected continuity identity plus the later static no-flux theorem.

The answer was no.

### 18.2 What continuity actually gave

The exact projected brane continuity law gives
\[
\partial_t\rho_{\rm br}+\nabla_3\cdot\mathbf J_{\rm br}=S_{\rm leak}.
\]
Integrating over all ordinary 3-space for an isolated localized defect gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}N_{\rm br}(t)=I_{\rm leak}(t),
\qquad
N_{\rm br}(t)=\int d^3x\,\rho_{\rm br},
\qquad
I_{\rm leak}(t)=\int d^3x\,S_{\rm leak}.
\]
In frequency space,
\[
-i\omega\,\delta N_{\rm br}(\omega)=\delta I_{\rm leak}(\omega).
\]

This is exact and important, but it only says what scalar leakage *means*. It does **not** force the integrated scalar leakage to vanish.

### 18.3 What the no-static-flux theorem gave

The later scalar-mouth work had already established the static theorem
\[
Z^{\rm eff}_{\sigma,\mathrm{flux}}(0)=0.
\]
That killed the idea of a DC scalar bleed channel in the frozen conservative theory.

But it did **not** kill a genuinely dynamical scalar odd term.
It only said the dangerous scalar channel, if present, had to be an authentically retarded/open-system effect rather than something already hidden in the static conservative ledger.

### 18.4 Why a generic outgoing scalar channel naturally gives an \(i\omega\) term

For an outgoing 3D scalar channel,
\[
G_R(\omega,r)=\frac{e^{i\omega r/c_s}}{4\pi r}
=
\frac{1}{4\pi r}
+
\frac{i\omega}{4\pi c_s}
-
\frac{\omega^2 r}{8\pi c_s^2}
-
\frac{i\omega^3r^2}{24\pi c_s^3}
+\cdots.
\]

So if the model carries a nonzero effective scalar monopole coupling to a gapless scalar outlet, a linear odd term is generic.

This is why the first scalar cancellation test was a genuine theorem gate rather than bookkeeping.
The conclusion of this first stage was:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Projected continuity + no-static-flux do not by themselves force scalar cancellation.}
}
\]

That was the first real scalar failure signal of the session.

## 19. Scalar Ward-identity failure and the explicit counterexample

### 19.1 The key conceptual point: projected number is an overlap, not bulk norm

The next scalar step was to test whether a centered isolated breathing defect might nevertheless satisfy a hidden scalar Ward identity.

This failed.

The central reason was that the brane-visible scalar content is not the full bulk norm. It is the **projection overlap**
\[
N_{\rm br}(q)
=

### 36.1 The minimal retarded ansatz

The next step was to promote the dipole channels to a minimal low-frequency retarded completion,
\[
Y_{1\perp}^{\rm ret}(\omega)=R_{1\perp}+i\sigma_\perp\omega+\cdots,
\qquad
Y_{10}^{\rm ret}(\omega)=R_{10}+i\sigma_\parallel\omega+\cdots.
\]

At this stage the goal was not to claim this was the correct completion, only to ask whether any nonzero odd part would already generate a dangerous relative force.

### 36.2 The local odd action in the pair frame

In the relative variables, the local odd part of the influence functional reduces to
\[
S^{(1)}_{\rm odd,loc}
=
\frac12\int dt\,
\Big[
\frac72\,\sigma_\perp\,\mathbf u_-\!\cdot\!\dot{\mathbf u}_+
+
4\,\sigma_\parallel\,d_-\dot d_+
\Big].
\]

This is already enough to show that the wake is dynamical in the relative sector.

### 36.3 The resulting force

The Euler–Lagrange derivative gives
\[
F_x=-\frac{7\sigma_\perp}{4}x^{(3)},
\qquad
F_y=-\frac{7\sigma_\perp}{4}y^{(3)},
\qquad
F_z=-2\sigma_\parallel z^{(3)}.
\]

So any nonzero odd coefficient in either dipole channel generates a genuine relative jerk-type force.

### 36.4 The first apparent conclusion

At this stage of the audit, the dipole wake looked worse than the scalar sector had begun to look, because the wake appeared to give a direct lower-order relative dissipative force with no obvious cancellation partner.

That was the first serious dipole failure signal of the session.

## 37. Why the vector Ward-identity rescue failed

### 37.1 Momentum conservation does not force the dipole odd term to vanish

The first obvious rescue attempt was a vector Ward-identity argument: perhaps total momentum conservation forces the dangerous odd dipole coefficient to vanish.

That failed.

At the body-action level,
\[
L_{\rm body}=a(\dot x_{A-}-\dot x_{B-})(\ddot x_{A+}-\ddot x_{B+})
\]
gives
\[
F_A=-a(x_A^{(3)}-x_B^{(3)}),
\qquad
F_B=+a(x_A^{(3)}-x_B^{(3)}),
\]
so
\[
F_A+F_B=0.
\]

Thus total momentum conservation is perfectly compatible with a nonzero odd dipole wake in the relative sector.

### 37.2 Translational invariance also does not rescue the model

In CM/relative variables the same odd term reduces to
\[
L_{\rm body}=a\,\dot x\,\ddot x,
\]
with no dependence on the center-of-mass coordinate \(X\).

So the dangerous odd dipole term is translationally invariant for any nonzero coefficient.
It does not need to break translational symmetry to survive.

### 37.3 Vanishing defect-centered dipole does not apply to the carried wake

Another obvious rescue was the 1PN condition of vanishing defect-centered dipole.
That also failed.

The old 1PN dipole condition suppresses an internal first moment in the worldtube reduction, but the carried odd wake ports are velocity-like auxiliary channels rather than static internal moments. So the old monopole-centered dipole theorem does not constrain them.

### 37.4 Pure-Schott reinterpretation fails generically

The power associated with the jerk-type force is
\[
P=
-\frac{d}{dt}\left[
\frac{7\sigma_\perp}{4}(\dot x\,\ddot x+\dot y\,\ddot y)
+2\sigma_\parallel\dot z\,\ddot z
\right]
+
\frac{7\sigma_\perp}{4}(\ddot x^2+\ddot y^2)
+2\sigma_\parallel\ddot z^2.
\]

So there is a genuine non-total-derivative dissipative part unless the coefficients vanish.
This means the carried odd wake is not generically “just Schott.”

### 37.5 The interim theorem gate

The interim dipole gate therefore became
\[
\sigma_\perp=0,
\qquad
\sigma_\parallel=0,
\]
or else the dipole wake really would contaminate the reduced relative dynamics.

At this point in the session, the dipole sector looked like a potentially fatal obstruction.

## 38. Outgoing \(\ell=1\) spectral law and the first major rescue

### 38.1 The key idea

The next move was to stop treating the dipole ports as generic Ohmic auxiliary variables and instead ask what happens if they arise from a **clean outgoing 3D partial-wave channel**.

That changed the picture materially.

### 38.2 The low-frequency outgoing \(\ell=1\) law

For a genuine outgoing 3D \(\ell=1\) channel, the Dirichlet-to-Neumann eigenvalue behaves as
\[
\Lambda_1(k)=-\frac{2}{a}+a k^2+i a^2 k^3+O(k^4),
\qquad k=\omega/c.
\]

So the absorptive part begins at
\[
\Im \Lambda_1\sim k^3,
\]
not at \(k\).

Equivalently, after inversion and normalization to a fixed static residue,
\[
Y_{1,\mathrm{norm}}(k)
=
R\Bigl[1+\frac12(ak)^2+i\frac12(ak)^3+O((ak)^4)\Bigr].
\]

Thus the first odd dipole term is
\[
\boxed{
\Im Y_1^{\rm ret}(\omega)\propto \left(\frac{a\omega}{c}\right)^3,
}
\]
with **no** linear \(O(\omega)\) odd part.

### 38.3 Why this mattered

This rescued the model from the earlier worst-case scenario in which any retarded odd dipole completion would immediately produce a lower-order jerk obstruction.

The earlier jerk-type failure was therefore reinterpreted as a warning about **generic Ohmic auxiliary completion**, not as the necessary behavior of a clean outgoing dipole channel.

### 38.4 What survived after the rescue

The rescue was real, but not complete.
Because the ports are velocity-like, an \(i\omega^3\) odd term still produces a fifth-derivative local force,
\[
F_x=-\frac{7\chi_\perp}{4}x^{(5)},
\qquad
F_y=-\frac{7\chi_\perp}{4}y^{(5)},
\qquad
F_z=-2\chi_\parallel z^{(5)}.
\]

So the dipole channel was no longer an *earlier-than-2.5PN* killer, but it still remained a possible **same-order** modifier of the 2.5PN sector.

That is why the next test had to be a direct matching test against the standard local 2.5PN family.

## 39. Order reduction and matching against the standard 2.5PN family

### 39.1 Order-reduced dipole structure

After Newtonian order reduction, the isotropic fifth-derivative structure sits in the same local basis as the standard 2.5PN force,
\[
\{\dot r\,\mathbf n,\mathbf v\}.
\]

So the right question became whether a surviving same-order dipole contribution could be absorbed into the two-parameter Iyer–Will family.

### 39.2 The full matching test

Let \(\lambda_\perp\) and \(\lambda_\parallel\) denote the normalized transverse and longitudinal dipole coefficients after factoring out the standard overall 2.5PN prefactor.

The dipole correction then takes the form
\[
\Delta\mathbf a_{\rm dip}
=
\frac{8G^2M^2\nu}{5c^5r^3}
\left[
\Delta A\,\dot r\,\mathbf n+\Delta B\,\mathbf v
\right],
\]
with
\[
\Delta A=
(9\lambda_\perp+36\lambda_\parallel)v^2
+(-8\lambda_\perp-22\lambda_\parallel)\frac{GM}{r}
+(-45\lambda_\perp-60\lambda_\parallel)\dot r^2,
\]
\[
\Delta B=
-9\lambda_\perp v^2
+8\lambda_\perp\frac{GM}{r}
+45\lambda_\perp\dot r^2.
\]

The symbolic solve of
\[
\mathbf a_{\rm BT}+\Delta\mathbf a_{\rm dip}=s\,\mathbf a_{\rm IW}(\alpha,\beta)
\]
gave the unique solution
\[
\boxed{
\alpha=4,
\qquad
\beta=5,
\qquad
s=1,
\qquad
\lambda_\perp=0,
\qquad
\lambda_\parallel=0.
}
\]

### 39.3 Interpretation

This is stronger than “the dipole term shifts the coefficients a little.”
It means:

> **if a nonzero dipole wake survives at the same formal 2.5PN order, it cannot be absorbed into the standard GR local 2.5PN gauge family.**

So a same-order surviving dipole contribution would represent a genuine universality-breaking deformation of the GR-like point-particle 2.5PN sector.

### 39.4 Two especially clean physical readings

1. **Transverse dipole piece:** a nonzero \(\lambda_\perp\) changes the circular-orbit tangential damping coefficient itself, so it is immediately physical and not a gauge reshuffle.
2. **Longitudinal dipole piece:** even if the circular limit looks harmless, a nonzero \(\lambda_\parallel\) still breaks the generic-orbit structure and cannot be absorbed into the remaining Iyer–Will freedom.

This made the dipole gate fully sharp.

## 40. Finite-size scaling rescue in the strict small-body regime

### 40.1 Why scaling had become the only remaining rescue

After the matching test, there was no longer any gauge-theoretic rescue route for a same-order dipole contribution.
The only remaining rescue was therefore **scaling suppression**.

### 40.2 PN/small-body counting of the outgoing dipole channel

The odd dipole wake sits on top of a conservative 1PN cross-sector ingredient, so it carries one explicit factor
\[
\epsilon\equiv (v/c)^2.
\]

The outgoing \(\ell=1\) odd factor contributes
\[
\left(\frac{a\omega}{c}\right)^3.
\]
Using orbital near-zone scaling,
\[
\omega\sim \frac{v}{r}\sim \frac{c\sqrt\epsilon}{r},
\]
we get
\[
\frac{a\omega}{c}\sim \sqrt\epsilon\,\frac{a}{r}.
\]
Hence the dipole odd correction scales as
\[
\epsilon\left(\sqrt\epsilon\frac{a}{r}\right)^3
=
\epsilon^{5/2}\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^3.
\]

If the small-body family obeys
\[
\frac{a}{r}\sim \epsilon^\delta,
\qquad \delta>0,
\]
then the dipole term enters at
\[
\boxed{
\epsilon^{5/2+3\delta},
}
\]
which is strictly above universal point-particle 2.5PN.

Representative cases:
- \(a/r\sim \epsilon^{1/2}\Rightarrow 4\)PN,
- \(a/r\sim \epsilon\Rightarrow 5.5\)PN.

### 40.3 Main conclusion of the scaling audit

This is the main dipole rescue theorem of the session:
\[
\boxed{
\text{With a clean outgoing }\ell=1\text{ completion, the carried dipole wake is naturally a finite-size correction.}
}
\]

So the dipole wake no longer blocks a **point-particle** 2.5PN theorem in the strict small-body limit.

### 40.4 What this did and did not rescue

This rescued the model from the claim that the dipole wake generically destroys the 2.5PN program.

But it did **not** prove the dipole wake is absent.
It proved something narrower and more useful:

> if the missing PDE gives the wake a clean outgoing \(\ell=1\) completion consistent with the small-body regime, then the dipole wake is demoted above the universal point-particle 2.5PN sector.

That is a conditional but meaningful rescue.

# Part VI — Quadrupole Sector: The Surviving Universal Branch
**Handoff notes for the 2.5PN program**

## 48. Exact representation result: the solved 2PN `P2` ports are the full real STF `\ell=2` basis

Construct the STF tensor

Q_{ij}=v_i v_j-\frac13\delta_{ij}v^2.

## 49. Retarded parity and sign: clean outgoing `\ell=2` automatically gives `+ i\omega^5`

For a 3D outgoing quadrupole partial wave,

\Lambda_2(k)=k\,\frac{h_2^{(1)\prime}(ka)}{h_2^{(1)}(ka)}

Y_2^{\rm ret}(k)

+
\frac{4a^4k^4}{81}

+O(k^6)

\Im Y_2^{\rm ret}(\omega)

with **positive sign** on the outgoing branch.

### 49.2 Why this was exactly the desired parity law

This is precisely the quadrupole analogue of the lower partial-wave threshold laws already used in the scalar and dipole rescues:

So the quadrupole branch lands exactly in the expected 2.5PN slot.

\frac{\gamma}{2}(q^{(3)})^2.

So the positive `+i\omega^5` coefficient has the **correct dissipative sign**. The quadrupole branch therefore passed the sign test cleanly.

## 51. Worldtube/orbital source map: the universal quadrupole route exists structurally already

### 51.1 General worldtube multipole decomposition

For one compact defect `A`, in defect-centered coordinates \(\boldsymbol\xi\), the source quadrupole is
\[
I_{ij}^{(A)}
=
\int d^3\xi\,\rho_A(\boldsymbol\xi,t)
\bigl(X_A^i+\xi^i\bigr)\bigl(X_A^j+\xi^j\bigr)^{\rm STF}.
\]
Expanding gives
\[
I_{ij}^{(A)}
=
m_A X_A^{\langle i}X_A^{j\rangle}
+2X_A^{\langle i}d_A^{j\rangle}
+Q_A^{ij},
\]
where
\[
d_A^i\equiv \int d^3\xi\,\rho_A\xi^i,
\qquad
Q_A^{ij}\equiv \int d^3\xi\,\rho_A\xi^{\langle i}\xi^{j\rangle}.
\]

### 51.2 Vanishing defect-centered dipole removes the mixed terms

The 1PN worldtube reduction already imposes vanishing defect-centered dipole, so
\[
d_A^i=0.
\]
Hence
\[
I_{ij}^{(A)}=m_A X_A^{\langle i}X_A^{j\rangle}+Q_A^{ij}.
\]
Summing the two bodies and going to the center-of-mass frame yields
\[
I_{ij}^{\rm eff}
=
\mu x_{\langle i}x_{j\rangle}+Q_A^{ij}+Q_B^{ij}+O(c^{-2},a^3\nabla^3\Phi).
\]

### 51.3 Meaning of the source map

This is one of the main positive results of the entire session:

> **the effective source quadrupole splits cleanly into the universal orbital STF piece plus internal worldtube quadrupole corrections.**

So the universal source is already identified:
\[
\boxed{I_{ij}^{\rm orb}=\mu x_{\langle i}x_{j\rangle}.}
\]

The internal `P2` throat structure does not disappear. It survives as the finite-size correction sector:
\[
Q_A^{ij}+Q_B^{ij}.
\]

This is the cleanest interpretation of the source side of the model.

## 52. Exact pair-frame source structure and its match to the solved `P2` ports

### 52.1 Newtonian order-reduced orbital source quadrupole

For the orbital source quadrupole,
