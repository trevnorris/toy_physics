\section{How to locate the audits}

The stage number is the reader-facing lookup key.  Raw per-stage filenames are
intentionally omitted from the printed stage cards so the derivation pages stay
legible.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item SymPy symbolic audits follow
  \ReaderPath{moving_throat_pde_stageNNN_*_sympy_audit.py}.
  \item Mathematica symbolic audits follow
  \ReaderPath{moving_throat_pde_stageNNN_*_mathematica_audit.wl}.
  \item Numerical stress harnesses usually follow
  \ReaderPath{stageNNN*_stress.py} and \ReaderPath{stageNNN*_stress.wl}, with
  occasional shared-range names such as \ReaderPath{stage003_021_*}.
  \item The exact path inventory and current coverage state are maintained in
  the control documents listed above rather than repeated inside every stage.
\end{itemize}

\section{What is intentionally omitted here}

The reader build does not include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item raw script transcripts;
  \item the full stage-by-stage audit registry;
  \item the full reproducibility map;
  \item the exact per-stage file-path inventory;
  \item the full source-file index.
\end{itemize}

That omission is deliberate. The reader build is a navigation layer.  The
archive build and repo control documents remain the full audit register.

\section{Citation rule}

Use the reader build to find the right theorem block and the right status
language. Use the archive build and repo artifacts when the claim is
load-bearing enough that a future paper or referee needs the full audit trail.
