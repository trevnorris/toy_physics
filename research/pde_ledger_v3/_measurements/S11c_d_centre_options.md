## The question in ordinary language

Thickness change moves the two slab faces apart or together. Centre motion
moves their midpoint. The model declares these independent. Our existing
five-field scattering calculation carries the in-plane displacement,
densification and thickness, but its saved face drive has no independent
centre velocity.

The recent bounded check found that this existing thickness-driven solution
produces **zero net centre face load in all four cases**. That is encouraging
compatibility evidence. It is not, on its own, a rule determining an initially
or independently moving centre. The practical question is whether we need a
small missing upstream balance/sector argument, an explicit model choice, or
no extra centre construction for the particular claims we actually retain.

Please challenge that framing if it is too strong. We do not want to turn
homogeneous uniqueness, a global spectrum or every conceivable excitation
into a prerequisite for a specified toy-model scattering experiment.

## What is established, and what is not

1. **Real-frequency work exists.** The four-case finite two-ended responses,
   retained-grade/current bookkeeping and selected controls are saved.
   Their independent and cross-engine duties are not all complete. Nothing
   here authorizes regenerating them.
2. **The centre-load check is completed, not just planned.** It derived
   per-face multipliers from the actual normalized b pressure and global
   normal-jet slots, then used the saved c2 closed face half-sum/difference.
   All four centre images are zero. The zero inherits the existing c2 parity
   return; it is not an independent second acoustic derivation. A one-face
   sign change propagated through final assembly and changed its result.
   Generic nonzero of that control was not proved. See `diagnostic-report.md`.
3. **There are useful upstream operands.** a supplies independent `ZETA_C`
   directions and centre virtual-work tests. c1 supplies a generic response
   to prescribed `V_S` and `MU_THETA`. The saved first-shape **bare** DtN
   parity record has literal zero off-diagonal entries for both anchorings.
   These are not yet a complete slab/constraint/current sector argument.
4. **The inspected kinetic construction has no centre velocity.** b's
   supplied energy/basis fields and native kinetic caller carry `u`, `theta`
   and `e_W`; the virtual centre test is retained separately. A missing
   native row is not automatically a supplied zero inertia, and zero
   inertia is not automatically the wrong model either. Whether the given
   balance-law inputs already determine a free-boundary constraint is the
   specific issue to decide from the sources.
5. **The face response is conditional on its own domain.** c1 retains a
   formal noninvertibility condition. Exterior “no incoming bulk waves”
   should not be silently substituted for all centre-sector initial or
   incident data. Conversely, a global uniqueness theorem may be unnecessary
   for a properly specified conditional scattering response.
6. **A separate obstacle remains.** The current finite end/current method
   establishes a decaying-bulk domain. General radiating/continuous-spectrum
   coverage is not supplied merely by completing centre motion. Its method
   and cost need a separate disposition; do not hide it inside any option.

The source packet includes the relevant governing text, whole native
functions and exact saved-result navigation. Evidence must be distinguished
from proposed interpretation. The historical upstream fidelity/cross-engine
debts remain; they do not automatically prescribe a giant repeat campaign.

## Codex's provisional view, open to correction

I currently prefer **A with a hard stop**, attempting A-minimal first. It
preserves the declared model and makes use of the computed zero load and
saved parity/response evidence. The first task is to decide whether the
supplied laws determine the balance, not to compute more rows. If they do
not, return the exact missing model input once. Do not chain diagnostics or
silently turn absence into a massless equation.

I would not choose C merely to remove an implementation inconvenience.
B can be legitimate for a controlled analog experiment but changes the
question and must include the consequences of its constraint. D may be the
best cost decision if general radiating completion dominates the remaining
work; it cannot quietly be called completion of the current contract.

This is a recommendation, not a scientific clearance. In particular I am
not asserting that a complete centre equation is indispensable to every
end-channel formula, or that A must succeed. Please identify any point where
I have overestimated a prerequisite or confused a freely specified incident
sector with a missing physical equation.
