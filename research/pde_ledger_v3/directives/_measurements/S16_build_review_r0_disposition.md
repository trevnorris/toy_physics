## Findings

**B1. Both engines file the term from the moving reference point `X_c` inside other terms.** (Claude 1.)
**CONFIRMED.**
- **The list** (M3).
  - Item 2 asks for "the motion of both surfaces, with the terms that come only from that motion kept apart, in item
    1's coordinates and two pieces".
  - Item 1's two pieces are "the piece from the motion of `X_c`, and the piece from the rest of the surface's
    motion".
- **The engines** (M4).
  - The moment is taken about `X_c(t)`, so its rate carries a term that exists only because `X_c` moves. Both
    engines print that term inside the same integral as the material flux: SymPy
    `dipole.0.bulk.weight = Volume(F1(x1, x2, x3, t) - Sigma(x1, x2, x3, t)*Derivative(Xc1(t), t))` (`:135`), and
    Wolfram `dipole.1.bulkWeight = V[F1[x1, x2, x3, t] - Sigma[x1, x2, x3, t]*Derivative[1][c1][t]]` (`:95`).
  - The derivative of that integral is then split by origin. The part each engine names `localEvolution` contains
    `−Σ` times the acceleration of `X_c` (SymPy `:424`, Wolfram `:369`).
  - Each engine's own code defines that part otherwise. SymPy: "The local derivative is at fixed geometry in the
    original x coordinates. Acceleration and mixed products remain in the center/radius piece generated by the
    differentiated jet." Wolfram: "A chain-rule partition of each derivative by geometric origin" (M4).
- **The evidence.** Claude evaluated the printed objects on live fields of its own (M4).
  - In both engines, the bulk weight minus the integrated flux is nonzero, and adding `Ẋ_c N` to that difference
    gives `0`.
  - With static fields, no flux and an accelerating `X_c`, the part named local evolution plus `Ẍ_c N` is `0` in both
    engines. So that part is exactly `−Ẍ_c N` while no field changes.
  - Grok's report quotes the same two `localEvolution` lines and counts them sound (M4). The legs agree on the fact.
- **Effect.**
  - The record cannot read from the printed objects which terms exist only because `X_c` moves.
  - A part named for evolution at fixed geometry holds the acceleration of `X_c`.
  - Both engines partition the same way, so a cross-engine comparison cannot see it.
- **What must be true.**
  - In the rate of the moment, the term that arises because the moment is measured about the moving `X_c` is
    printed apart from the integrated material flux, as a term of the motion of `X_c`.
  - In every derivative the build prints, each part holds what its name says. A part named for evolution at fixed
    geometry holds no term produced by differentiating the position of `X_c`, either radius, or any of their time
    derivatives. Each such term is in the piece of the motion it comes from.
- **The leg's wording.** The leg asked that "no part named local evolution contains a jet of `X_c`, `r_in` or `R`".
  This disposition states it as no term *produced by differentiating* those quantities. A term already printed as a
  term of the motion keeps that name in its own derivative, so a velocity factor that is not differentiated stays
  apart under that name. Round 1 checks this reading against the list.

**B2. In the Wolfram engine, the motion pieces of a derivative are wrong where the integrand carries a velocity, and
they do not sum to the printed rate.** (Claude 3.) **CONFIRMED.**
- **The list** (M3). Item 2 asks for "every term printed", with the motion kept "in item 1's coordinates and two
  pieces".
- **The engine** (M5).
  - `splitMotion` (`:78–82`) takes `Coefficient[Expand[z], #]` for each velocity of `X_c` and each radius velocity.
    That is exact only for an expression linear in the velocities. For a product of two velocities it counts a mixed
    term under each factor, and it misses a square.
  - `timeParts[V]` (`:130–134`) prints `pieces[[2]]` and `pieces[[3]]` only. The remainder `pieces[[1]]` is printed
    nowhere.
  - The bulk weight's integrand carries `Ẋ_c` (B1), so its surface coefficient is quadratic in the velocities.
- **The evidence** (Claude, M5).
  - The printed parts minus the printed rate is nonzero for `drate1`, `drate2` and `drate3` `bulkWeight` only.
  - Against Claude's own term for each piece: every `bulkWeight` `centerMotion` piece differs, and every
    `radiusMotion` piece is `0`. The control `bulkSource1`, whose integrand is linear, gives `0`.
  - All 12 of the same checks on the SymPy engine are `0`.
  - Claude found the Wolfram rate totals and the centre acceleration correct (residual 0). Only the pieces are wrong.
- **What must be true.**
  - Each printed piece of every derivative holds exactly the terms produced by the time dependence it is named for,
    with none counted twice or dropped.
  - The printed pieces sum to the printed total.
  - This holds for every integrand the build differentiates, whatever its dependence on the velocities. After B1's
    repair the term of the motion of `X_c` still carries a velocity, so this still applies to its derivative.
