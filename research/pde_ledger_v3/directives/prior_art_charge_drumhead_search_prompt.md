# Prior-art search — the DRUM-HEAD charge mechanism (revised formulation)

⚠ **An earlier search asked about "charge = `Z₂` orientation of a puncture" and returned NOT FOUND.**
⛔ That was the **old** formulation. ⭐ The mechanism has since been reformulated and this search is about
the **current** one, which is far more specific — and far more likely to have precedent.

## The mechanism, stated in standard language

A thin elastic sheet under tension (a membrane / drum head) separates two half-spaces. An **inclusion**
pierces the sheet and **deflects it out of plane**, toward `+w` or `−w`. That deflection direction is the
**sign of electric charge**.

⭐ **The interaction is purely elastic energetics:** two inclusions deflecting the sheet the **same** way
superpose and **raise** the total elastic energy ⇒ the configuration relaxes by **separating** (repulsion).
Two deflecting **opposite** ways partially cancel ⇒ it relaxes by **approaching** (attraction).

⇒ ⭐⭐ **Coulomb's sign is claimed to be the ENERGY GEOMETRY of superposed membrane deflections**, with
⛔ **no gauge field anywhere.**

## ⭐⭐ The four questions I actually need answered

### A · Is this a known mechanism, and what is it called?

⭐ Search **membrane-mediated / elasticity-mediated interactions between inclusions**, in particular:
- proteins or inclusions in **lipid bilayers** interacting through membrane deformation
  (Goulian–Bruinsma–Pincus; Kim–Neu–Oster; Weikl–Kozlov–Helfrich; Dommersnes–Fournier);
- **capillary interactions** between particles at a fluid interface (the "Cheerios effect", Vella–Mahadevan);
- point forces, point sources, or pinned defects on a **tense membrane**;
- elastic interactions between defects/inclusions in **plates and shells**.

### B · ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT IS THE SIGN, AND UNDER WHAT CONDITIONS? — the most important question

⛔ **Do like-direction deflections REPEL or ATTRACT?** ⚠ I believe the answer is **not universal** — it
depends on tension versus bending rigidity, on the multipole order of the deformation, and on the boundary
condition at the inclusion.

⭐ **Give me the actual conditions**, with citations:
- tension-dominated versus bending-dominated;
- **monopole** (net force on the sheet) versus **dipole/quadrupole** (no net force, imposed slope or shape);
- and ⭐ **which of those cases gives like-repels**, if any.

⚠ **The Cheerios effect is like-ATTRACTS.** ⭐ If the tension-dominated membrane case is generically
attractive for like deflections, ⛔ **say so plainly** — that would directly contradict what this model
needs, and it is exactly what I want to know.

### C · ⭐⭐ THE BOUNDARY CONDITION AT THE INCLUSION — a known open wall in this project

This project's own build stalled on **which boundary condition the inclusion imposes**, because the
exterior solution **relaxes to zero deflection** unless something pins it. The candidates it identified
were, in effect: **fix the height**, **fix the force**, or **fix the slope/contact angle**.

⭐ **Membrane physics has studied this.** I need:
- what the **standard** boundary conditions at an inclusion are, and their names;
- ⭐ **which is considered physically correct for a particle that pierces the sheet**, and why;
- ⭐ **whether the choice changes the SIGN of the interaction** — and if so, how.

⭐⭐ **This is the highest-value part of the search.** ⛔ Do not skim it.

### D · The force law — what power of `R`?

⭐ Coulomb requires a `1/R²` **force** in 3D. ⭐ **What power laws do membrane-mediated interactions
actually give**, for each multipole order and in each regime? ⚠ If the standard result is `1/R⁴` or
logarithmic, ⛔ **say so** — a mismatch here is a real problem for the model and I need it named, ⛔ not
smoothed over.

⚠ **Note the dimensional setting is unusual and may change the answer:** the sheet here is a
**3-dimensional** hypersurface in **4 spatial dimensions**, ⛔ not a 2D membrane in 3D. ⭐ Say clearly
whether the results you find are 2D-in-3D only, and whether the generalisation to a 3-sheet in 4-space is
known, obvious, or unstudied.
