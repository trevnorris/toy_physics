CLAIM AND LIMIT
For complex amplitudes g, bounded measurable multipliers b with norm at most B,
a nonnegative Abel regulator a, and a measurable kept domain s, the actual
integral error is at most B*(tailMass + a*firstMoment). Integrability of g and
its first absolute moment are explicit premises; the physical application is
Lebesgue measure, and the regulator is exp(-a*abs(xi)) about the fixed origin.

On an explicit Banach domain, a supplied continuous linear equivalence A and
bounded E with norm(A^-1)<=kappa, norm(E)<=epsilon and kappa*epsilon<1 give an
actual inverse for A+E. Its norm is bounded by kappa/(1-kappa*epsilon), and
its difference from A^-1 by kappa^2*epsilon/(1-kappa*epsilon). Source and fixed
bounded-observation errors are also bounded. Completeness is needed for the
inverse construction; the equivalence transports it to the codomain.

No numerical epsilon, outgoing domain, inverse margin, uniform folded-amplitude
moments, complete operator error, quadrature or channel error is established
for the physical scattering problem. Conditional estimates do not establish
scattering convergence. Stronger Fourier-space rates, variable coefficients,
interfaces and nonlinear-pole formalization are outside this increment.
