\section{S9: Light's Requirement on the Medium --- Shear on the Brane, None in
the Bulk}\label{step:S9}

\stagefield{Part / anchor}{Part I --- Light. Sector~1, step~1.}
\claimstatus{\StatusOpen}
\stagefield{Purpose}{State what light needs the medium to have. Light is a
transverse wave and a GNLS superfluid cannot carry one, so light's first act is
to name the structure the GNLS does not supply. The step introduces the two
constants the whole sector rests on and fixes the light cone as their ratio.}

\paragraph{Status.}
\StatusOpen. First step banked under the requirements-first direction: light
states what it needs, and the medium's structure is not assumed in advance. The
result is a \emph{requirement with a live falsifier}, not a derivation --- both
constants enter \textbf{postulated}, and whether one substructure can meet both
halves of the requirement is a knit question this step does not settle.

\paragraph{The argument, in three moves.}
\resultanchor{1 --- What Maxwell demands.} Two transverse polarisations, one
speed, no longitudinal mode.

\resultanchor{2 --- What a GNLS has.} Linearising Madelung about a uniform
\(\rho_0\) gives exactly \emph{one} mode,
\[
  \omega^2 = c_s^2k^2 + \frac{\hbar^2}{4m^2}k^4,
  \qquad
  c_s^2 = \frac{1}{m}\frac{dP}{d\rho} = \frac{5K\rho_0^4}{m},
\]
and it is \textbf{longitudinal}. The obstruction is not incidental but
representation-theoretic: \(\Psi=\sqrt{\rho}\,e^{i\theta}\) is one complex
scalar, its excitations \(\delta\rho\) and \(\delta\theta\) are both spin-0, and
one broken \(U(1)\) gives one Goldstone phonon --- so a scalar's spectrum cannot
contain a spin-1 photon, \emph{in any dimension}.

\resultanchor{3 --- Therefore.} Light requires structure the GNLS does not
contain. Brane elasticity is not a consequence of the GP/NLS mean field: a
GP/NLS superfluid is a \emph{fluid}, with zero shear modulus. The brane's shear
rigidity therefore requires the \textbf{substructure} beneath the mean field ---
the standing postulate that the GNLS is the effective, coarse-grained
description of a medium with constituents of its own.

\paragraph{Provenance of moves 2 and 3 --- one source, not two.}
Both quotations come from the same document,
\StageFile{software/stage1\_solver/decisions/15\_em\_medium\_native\_physical\_picture.md}
(lines 29--40 and 230--233). It is an external review, and \textbf{no script in

that a GNLS cannot carry light therefore rests on a single external document
with no executing script; it is stated here at that strength and no higher.

\noindent
Both halves are light's own bill. The second is \emph{not} magnetism's: without
it a photon has somewhere else to go, light leaks off our three-dimensional
space, and that is an energy sink with no observational room. The requirement is
stressed hardest at the throat, where the brane is bent into \(\pm w\).

A \emph{third} consumer of the second half: the recorded throat-support
mechanism is an outward trapped standing-wave pressure whose trapped mode is a
brane-shear standing wave. If the bulk carried shear, that mode radiates away,
the outward pressure vanishes, and the throat closes. Bulk shear-freeness is
what makes the geon possible at all.

\paragraph{The equations.}
\[
  L=\tfrac12\rho_{br}(\partial_t u)^2-\tfrac12\mu_R(\nabla\times u)^2,
  \qquad
  c_\gamma^2=\frac{\mu_R}{\rho_{br}} \quad (R4).
\]

\paragraph{Dimensions, derived symbolically from the action.}
In \(D\) spatial dimensions the Lagrangian density has dimension
\(M L^{2-D}T^{-2}\). With \([u]=L\), the derivative orders extracted from the
action give
\[
  [\rho_{br}]=(-D,0,1),
  \qquad
  [\mu_R]=(2-D,-2,1),
  \qquad
  [\mu_R]-[\rho_{br}]=(2,-2,0),
\]
where the triples record powers of \((L,T,M)\). Thus the difference is
\textbf{independent of \(D\)}, and \(R4\) gives \(c_\gamma=[1,-1,0]\) in every
dimension. At \(D=3\), \([\rho_{br}]=[-3,0,1]\) and
\([\mu_R]=[-1,-2,1]\), agreeing with the parameter register
(\StageFile{notes/parameter\_register.md}, lines 137--138). This explains and
retires the old audit's supposed insensitivity to the assumed brane dimension:
it is an identity, not a blind spot, because the speed's dimension cannot see
\(D\).

\paragraph{Falsifier, and its status.}
\emph{Requirement:} the substructure carries shear in its ordered phase and none
in its disordered phase. \emph{Falsifier:} a substructure supplying one and not
the other --- in particular one carrying shear in the \emph{disordered} phase,
which would unconfine light \emph{and} kill the trapped-mode geon.
\emph{Status:} \textbf{live}, and it is a knit question. Three independent
consumers (photon propagation, photon confinement, geon support), and nothing
yet shows one substructure can do both. Bulk shear-freeness is currently
\textbf{postulated}; the only machine check on it is dimensional.

This is light's first open requirement conflict, and it is \emph{internal to
light} --- not a light-versus-magnetism conflict. It is a question about the
ordering transition: can one substructure be ordered-and-shear-bearing in one
phase and unstructured-and-shear-free in the other?

\paragraph{Verification.}
The rebuilt evidence uses two independently constructed engines, Mathematica
(\StageFile{research/pde\_ledger\_v3/mathematica/S9\_light\_requires\_shear\_mathematica\_audit.wl})
and SymPy
(\StageFile{research/pde\_ledger\_v3/scripts/S9\_light\_requires\_shear\_sympy\_audit.py}),
together with two reviewers deriving from scratch. Neither engine emits a
verdict or a typed conclusion: each emits evidence for automated comparison.
The consumer reports \textbf{12 cross-engine quantities agreeing and 0
disagreeing}, with all \textbf{1219/1219 dimensionful expressions homogeneous}.

\emph{The two verification mechanisms were complementary, and neither could
have caught what the other did.} The second engine found a \textbf{wrong
dimension}: the Mathematica engine had dropped an explicit wavevector factor
in its implied-speed dimension calculation and computed the resulting
dimension consistently, so two review legs and a full ablation suite saw only
a plausible-looking number. Conversely, the two reviewers independently found
a \textbf{wrong homogeneity test}: its
substitution silently did nothing, producing a false positive for a gapped
root and a false negative for a genuinely dispersive one. Cross-engine
comparison could not expose that defect because it came from the shared
directive, so both engines agreed on the same wrong test.

The rebuilt mode count is a \textbf{rank computation}: the corank of the
dynamical matrix \(M\) stacked with \(k^{\mathsf T}\), not the test
\(M\!\cdot\!T=0\), which gives a false negative under anisotropic inertia.
Non-dispersiveness is tested by scaling \(k\mapsto\lambda k\) and checking
\(\omega^2(\lambda k)-\lambda^2\omega^2(k)\). The dynamical matrix is obtained
by two independent routes --- position-space Euler--Lagrange variation and the
quadratic form of the ansatz-substituted Lagrangian --- and one-sided corruption
proved their independence. Nine actions are exercised: the main action, two
coefficient controls, five form controls, and one sign control; the sign of
each \(\omega^2\) root is emitted so the flipped-potential control can fail.

\emph{Physics sharpening from the form controls.} Transverse propagation does
\textbf{not} require the curl-only form. Ordinary gradient elasticity,
\(-\tfrac12\mu_R\sum_{ij}(\partial_i u_j)^2\), carries two transverse modes at
the same \(c^2=\mu_R/\rho_{br}\), but also propagates the longitudinal mode.
What curl-only uniquely buys is the \textbf{absence of a propagating
longitudinal mode} --- Maxwell's third demand --- not the presence of the
transverse pair. The divergence-only control gives the converse: the roles
swap, the transverse pair falls to \(\omega^2=0\), and the longitudinal mode
propagates. Thus this computation is conditional on the stipulated stiffness
form; it does not by itself establish that ``light requires shear.''

\emph{What this step still does not establish.}
\begin{itemize}
\item P2 --- that a scalar superfluid carries no transverse mode --- remains a
supplied premise from one external review; no script executes it.
\item The absence of a propagating longitudinal wave is assumed, not derived:
the curl-only action sets its restoring stiffness to zero by construction. It
removes the restoring force, not the degree of freedom, so the longitudinal
test is emitted at every root.
\item Bulk shear-freeness is postulated. The bulk is absent from the action, so
nothing here tests it.
\item The assumption set is computationally inert: removing it leaves every
computed value byte-identical. The physics is instead bounded by generic rank
and explicitly emitted exceptional loci. A reviewer verified that the
anisotropic enumeration is complete: roots collide exactly when
\(\rho_z=\rho_{br}\) or \(k_x=k_y=0\), and both loci are emitted. Polarisation
values must therefore be read as generic-locus values with those exceptions.
\item The SymPy engine was written after the Mathematica engine, so this is not
blind-first ordering. Its construction was independent --- different naming,
granularity, idioms, and a hand-rolled Euler--Lagrange calculation where SymPy
has a built-in --- and its builder was barred from reading the Mathematica
source, but the agreement is weaker evidence than when both engines predate
comparison.
\item One consumer tag remains unparsed: the anisotropic third root's sign is
\texttt{Piecewise}, because its sign depends on the parameter domain and the
assumptions are inert.
\item The limits taken are a sharp zero-width sheet, \(v_0\to0\), no
dissipation, frequency-independent moduli, the continuum limit, and amplitude
\(\to0\).
\end{itemize}

\emph{Why the second engine now exists.} The earlier exemption was conditional:
``a second engine earns its place where the algebra is long enough that it
could genuinely disagree.'' That condition expired as the engine grew from 26
tags to more than 1500, but the old conclusion was inherited without
re-checking it. The new engine earned its place by exposing the wrong dimension
that consistent first-engine computation, two review legs, and the full
ablation suite had all missed; it also supplies the independent comparison
summarised above. A conditional rule needs its condition re-checked, not its
conclusion quoted.
