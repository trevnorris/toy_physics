# S11c closure and handoff — PARTIAL

**October 4, 2026 · Closed as PARTIAL by user direction.** No further S11c builds, calculations or reviews are scheduled. Earlier launch/continuation instructions are historical; reopening requires explicit user direction.

## Plain-language summary

S11c asked whether light stays confined to the brane when its thickness or material properties vary, and how much light converts into other motion at a defect. The intended loss measure counts both reflected and transmitted light as survival: reflection alone is not leakage.

We established useful pieces. In the stated uniform model, the selected light modes do not drive the bulk. The later rest-bulk checks remain finite at the tested light/sound speed matches. Nonuniform equations contain conversion channels, and subsequent work checked many source, boundary and normalization details. That is evidence for possible conversion, not a measured loss fraction.

The one completed finite scattering benchmark used a supplied, held profile at a speed ratio about 0.12. Its result is **numerically unresolved**, not a positive leakage measurement or a physical upper bound. No accepted nonuniform leakage number at matched speed was obtained.

S11c therefore closes **PARTIAL**, not passed or disproved. A conditional held-defect benchmark need not wait for a realistic particle model, but this campaign did not complete a trustworthy one at matched speed. Real-particle predictions additionally need later material, flow and throat inputs. We preserve the useful results and stop expanding the calculation. The next planned subject is S12; this handoff does not start it.

## What this earned against the MacCullagh question

The useful distinction is the **explicit confinement mechanism**: this model keeps compression, thickness and bulk channels, checks when uniform light decouples, and identifies nonuniform coupling. The speed-match result removes one concrete obstruction. These are useful conditional results, not a demonstration that realistic defects preserve light.

The deeper material question also remains open: what physically supplies the rotation-based stiffness, and does its stress obey angular-momentum balance? The [newer native interpretation, §§5.7 and 14.3](../../../docs/native_light_em_and_vortex_throat_interpretation.md), assigns that to a separate material-admissibility audit; the stiffness remains postulated. The older [MacCullagh framing essay](../../../docs/s11_maccullagh_differentiation.md) must not be cited as proof that we solved that problem. We tested part of a different architecture, not established a complete physical alternative.

## What future claims must not rely on

- **The old c2 `[0,2] = 0` slot is not a cleared physical absence.** It omits a direct mixed height–slope contribution while retaining an iterated contribution. The independent retained grades include `eta*sigma_W`; calling it “second order” does not discard it. The [focused source diagnostic](../_measurements/S11c_upstream_mixed_tanh_result.md) and later constructions do not quantify its effect on the benchmark or clear the complete production operator. Do not treat old small numerical residuals as a bound on this missing physics.
- **The unresolved omega=3 benchmark is neither “leakage below 1 ppm” nor evidence of physical gain.** The floor is empirical, and refinement moved the signed results. Its old matrices are not matched-speed results. Preserve the report's unresolved verdict.
- **Source identities and intermediate checks are not a loss calculation.** In particular, [first-order transverse flux cancellation](../_measurements/S11c_d_first_order_transverse_flux_continue_result_record.json) and [scoped receiving regularity](../_measurements/S11c_d_first_order_receiving_regular_continue2_result_record.json) do not supply the full field or work balance. Neither uniform decoupling nor a small-defect estimate proves that particles cannot scatter light, that only long-range tails matter, or that a strong edge is harmless.
