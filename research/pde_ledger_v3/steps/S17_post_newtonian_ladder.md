# S17. What the seven orbital papers establish

The papers give orbital benchmarks for a future drain simulation. The first gives a perihelion advance. Others give effective coefficients or equations for two bodies in specified coordinates. Each entry identifies what the authors put into the calculation, what follows within their model and what a simulation would have to obtain from the drains. S17 imports those results by citation. It adds no physical law, fitted coefficient, numerical run or independent verification of the PN algebra. ## Where the Newtonian force enters

The first orbital paper starts with a defect of supplied mass `M` and a Poisson equation sourced by that mass. For a stationary source, the relaxed retarded potential and the Poisson potential obey the same boundary conditions. Their difference vanishes for the stated decay at infinity. This static-limit theorem identifies the two potentials. The point-source solution is `Φ = −GM/r`, and the paper assigns the test defect the radial acceleration `a_r = −∂ᵣΦ = −GM/r²`. In the scalar-only test-body equation, this inverse-square acceleration gives a Keplerian orbit. [Orbital paper, static boundary conditions](../../1pn_orbital_dynamics/paper/1pn_orbital_dynamics.tex#L349) · [Point source and test-body acceleration](../../1pn_orbital_dynamics/paper/1pn_orbital_dynamics.tex#L376)

The full 1PN paper treats the **source** and **receiving body** separately. On the source side it takes a quasi-static near zone with slowly varying projected density, subleading advection and a negligible or perturbative transverse contribution. A localized effective monopole then gives the longitudinal scalar a `1/r` profile. The authors define `Φ_N = λ_Φ φ_br`, with “λ_Φ fixed by monopole calibration.” The authors use this calibration to set the strength of the companion’s potential in the reduced model. A drain simulation would need the relation between its own mass flux and that source strength. [Reduction to the Poisson equation](../../4d_1pn_full/paper/4d_1pn_full.tex#L550) · [Monopole calibration](../../4d_1pn_full/paper/4d_1pn_full.tex#L673)

On the receiving side the paper assumes coherent translation of a compact defect of size `a ≪ r` in a smooth external field. It uses the same integrated mass `M` for inertia and coupling to the field. The force integral includes a term for momentum exchanged across the body's boundary, which the authors take to be “beyond the retained Newtonian order.” Under those assumptions the leading equation is `M Ẍ_cm = −M ∇Φ_N`. The finite-size correction is relatively of order `a²/r²` for a distant compact source. The later papers import lower-order reduced dynamics. The 2PN and 2.5PN papers explicitly carry forward the Newtonian terms, as do the conservative 3PN and 4PN papers. [Compactness and flux assumptions](../../4d_1pn_full/paper/4d_1pn_full.tex#L697) · [Monopole response and force integral](../../4d_1pn_full/paper/4d_1pn_full.tex#L812) · [Equation of motion](../../4d_1pn_full/paper/4d_1pn_full.tex#L862) · [2PN](../../4d_2pn/paper/4d_2pn.tex#L103) · [2.5PN](../../4d_2_5pn/paper/4d_2_5pn.tex#L36) · [3PN](../../4d_3pn/paper/4d_3pn.tex#L34) · [4PN](../../4d_4pn/paper/4d_4pn.tex#L870)

**What to measure for two drains.** Compare a free mouth's acceleration with its companion present and with the same mouth isolated under the same ambient conditions. The Newtonian target is an acceleration toward the companion with inverse-square separation dependence and the correct magnitude for an independently determined source mass. S15 supplies an expression for the signed rate of momentum entering a shell around that mouth, with the physical fields still unspecified. Momentum can accumulate in the shell or pass between the shell, sleeve, core and slab faces. Connecting that rate to the free mouth requires the companion's field, the momentum exchange and the core's inertia. Whether inverse-square shell crossing is necessary or sufficient for inverse-square acceleration is open. ### 1. Newtonian and 1PN Orbital Dynamics from a Superfluid Defect Toy Model

**Result and assumptions.** In the static central-field, test-mass limit the scalar potential gives zero perihelion advance. At leading post-Newtonian order, a position-dependent inertia gives an advance per orbit of `2πβμ/[c_s² a(1−e²)]`, where `a` here is the orbital semimajor axis and `e` the eccentricity. The paper also reports a moving-source grid experiment with density recomputed from the defect ensemble. That experiment retains the scalar-potential and inertia prescription. [Static theorem](../../1pn_orbital_dynamics/paper/1pn_orbital_dynamics.tex#L349) · [Analytic and numerical orbit comparisons](../../1pn_orbital_dynamics/paper/1pn_orbital_dynamics.tex#L870)

**Matching and open input.** With `μ = GM` and `c_s = c`, matching Schwarzschild precession fixes `β = κ_ρ + κ_add + κ_PV = 3`. The paper gives a simplified density-depletion argument for `κ_ρ = 1` and uses approximately fixed displaced volume and potential flow for `κ_add = 1/2`, supported by its dipole-energy calculation. The remainder `κ_PV = 3/2` is set by the orbit match. The orbital tests for a free drain are its leading acceleration and perihelion advance. Testing this paper’s proposed mechanism additionally requires an independent determination of how the drain’s inertia varies with position. [Coefficient definitions](../../1pn_orbital_dynamics/paper/1pn_orbital_dynamics.tex#L38) · [Added mass and orbital fit](../../1pn_orbital_dynamics/paper/1pn_orbital_dynamics.tex#L709) · [Assigned DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19449058)

### 2. Deriving Key Post-Newtonian Coefficients from the Unified 4D Toy Model

**Result and assumptions.** Exterior potential flow gives `κ_add = 1/2` for a throat uniform along the extra coordinate. The paper contrasts a four-dimensional bubble's `1/3`. Wave-supported rest energy gives the quartic coefficient `3/8` in the expansion of the moving defect's energy `E = γE₀`. [Topology and geometry](../../4d_1pn_bridge/paper/4d_1pn_bridge.tex#L91) · [Energy expansion](../../4d_1pn_bridge/paper/4d_1pn_bridge.tex#L1278)

**Matching and open input.** Weak-field refractive matching selects the equation-of-state exponent `n = 5`. The EIH velocity cross terms constrain a family of wakes. The additional choice `α² = 1 − U/P` for the fraction of energy available to the wake selects `α² = 3/4`, `a_H = 0` and `K_vec = 2/π²` within that model. A moving throat would have to realize the assumed geometry, exterior flow and energy partition for these coefficients to apply. [EIH match](../../4d_1pn_bridge/paper/4d_1pn_bridge.tex#L1065) · [Wake assumption and selected coefficients](../../4d_1pn_bridge/paper/4d_1pn_bridge.tex#L1163) · [Assigned DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19449653)

### 3. A Controlled Derivation of the Full First Post-Newtonian Sector from the Unified 4D Toy Model

**Result and assumptions.** With the compact-body reduction above, local mass scaling, the selected equation of state, the wake construction and an adiabatic model with one breathing degree of freedom, the authors use the EIH velocity targets to fix wake coefficients. With those choices the conservative two-body Lagrangian agrees algebraically with the Einstein–Infeld–Hoffmann (EIH) Lagrangian. Its test-mass limit recovers the perihelion advance. [Paper's result](../../4d_1pn_full/paper/4d_1pn_full.tex#L97) · [Coefficient classifications](../../4d_1pn_full/paper/4d_1pn_full.tex#L3094)

**Matching and open input.** Newtonian matching fixes `κ_ρ = 1`. The EIH targets `C_∥ = −7/2` and `C_L = −1/2` constrain the velocity cross terms, followed by the thermodynamic wake closure. The paper calls `κ_PV = 3/2` “protocol-fixed.” Its calculation uses the required slope `d ln F_eq / d ln ρ = 5/2` to select the internal flow-to-wave energy ratio `x = 2/11`. The internal energy partition `E_w:E_f:E_PV = 11:2:5` then follows. Measuring the breathing response or that partition in a drain solution would provide information independent of the orbital fit. The general frequency-dependent response remains open. [EIH conditions](../../4d_1pn_full/paper/4d_1pn_full.tex#L2074) · [Target slope and implied partition](../../4d_1pn_full/paper/4d_1pn_full.tex#L1712) · [Assigned DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19450102)

### 4. A Controlled Derivation of the Full Conservative Second Post-Newtonian Sector from the Unified 4D Toy Model

**Result and assumptions.** Starting from the reduced Newtonian and 1PN terms, the paper chooses a minimal one-body boundary response, a Bernoulli relation and quadratic local mass scaling. After matching the residual coefficients to the ADM reference, its assembled two-body Lagrangian transforms to the generic-frame conservative Arnowitt–Deser–Misner (ADM) Hamiltonian through 2PN. The object compared is the Hamiltonian in that representation. [Reported construction and equality](../../4d_2pn/paper/4d_2pn.tex#L101)

**Matching and open input.** The one-body response reproduces the Schwarzschild test-mass target. The static match fixes `λ_ρ = 1/2`, and the comparable-mass coefficients `{q_i, t_i, s₁}` solve the residual against the ADM reference. A moving-throat solution would have to deliver the boundary response and the interactions represented by those fitted coefficients. ### 5. A Conditional Derivation of the Point-Particle 2.5PN Sector from the Unified 4D Toy Model

**Result and assumptions.** The dissipative result concerns two nonspinning bodies in the strict small-body limit. Its channel argument assumes no additional direct scalar source, compact outgoing scalar and vector channels with the stated low-frequency thresholds, rotational invariance, a passive outgoing quadrupole, and a static orbital/source overlap on the natural branch, `m̂₀ = 1 + O(a²/r²)` with `m̂₀ ≠ 0`. Here `a/r` is the small-body size relative to separation. Under those conditions the lower scalar and dipole terms are suppressed. The orbital symmetric trace-free quadrupole supplies the local Burke–Thorne radiation-reaction form in the Iyer–Will family. [Channel argument](../../4d_2_5pn/paper/4d_2_5pn.tex#L35) · [Explicit theorem assumptions](../../4d_2_5pn/paper/4d_2_5pn.tex#L2456)

**Matching and open input.** The absolute coefficient in canonical source units is `γ_quad^eff = m̂₀² Γ₅`. The GR reaction strength requires `γ_quad^eff = 2G/(5c⁵)`. The actual moving drains must realize the branch and its amplitude. The reduced-model normalization comparison belongs to S18. [Burke–Thorne target](../../4d_2_5pn/paper/4d_2_5pn.tex#L791) · [Canonical coefficient](../../4d_2_5pn/paper/4d_2_5pn.tex#L2552) · [Assigned DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19492270)

### 6. A Full Conservative Derivation of the Two-Body 3PN Sector from the Unified 4D Toy Model

**Result and assumptions.** Given the lower-order dynamics and the choice of a grouped quadrupole channel, the paper uses the Schwarzschild and comparable-mass GR targets to assign the full conservative two-body interaction through `c⁻⁶` in fixed ADM coordinates. The free kinetic contribution follows from the relativistic Hamiltonian. [Fixed-chart result](../../4d_3pn/paper/4d_3pn.tex#L34)

**Matching and open input.** The Schwarzschild one-body limit fixes three coefficients. For comparable masses the authors invert the grouped response map on nine known GR residual coefficients. The imported static target specifies the full static coefficient in the generic frame. Subtracting the static contribution already fixed by the grouped response gives the remaining geometry coefficient. The physical response represented by that map and the throat geometry and compressibility that would produce the remaining coefficient are open. ### 7. A Conditional Full Conservative Derivation of the Two-Body 4PN Sector from the Unified 4D Toy Model

**Result and assumptions.** Using the Schwarzschild one-body condition and the comparable-mass reference, the paper reconstructs the local, instantaneous 4PN interaction as an ordinary two-body Lagrangian in its chosen generic-frame representation. For the history-dependent piece, the paper adopts the standard conservative GR tail functional. Its canonical coefficient satisfies `C_tail = (GM/2c³) γ_quad^eff`. [Local reconstruction](../../4d_4pn/paper/4d_4pn.tex#L3420) · [Choice of tail functional](../../4d_4pn/paper/4d_4pn.tex#L2169)

**Matching and open input.** For the conservative quadrupole response, the moving-throat calculation must supply the three grouped components (20, 21, 22) and test their isotropy. On the paper’s branch the anisotropy coefficients satisfy `a₂ = b₂ = 0`. Failure of this test rules out that branch before the amplitude comparison. [Open grouped response](../../4d_4pn/paper/4d_4pn.tex#L3541) · [Isotropy test](../../4d_4pn/paper/4d_4pn.tex#L3579)

The canonical result gives `C_tail = G²M/(5c⁸)` if the 2.5PN branch supplies `γ_quad^eff = 2G/(5c⁵)`. This is the same quadrupole normalization. For a toy-medium measurement the paper separately writes `α_tail^toy = Θ_tail (GM/2c_s³) γ_toy`. Agreement with the canonical bridge requires `c_s = c`, the same canonically normalized source, and a monopole-scattering transport factor `Θ_tail = 1`. A measured 2.5PN amplitude alone leaves that transport test open.
