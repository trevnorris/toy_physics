# What does a trapped shear wave follow when one mouth bends?

**A reflecting wave can load the rim, and the change in its frequencies can reveal whether its far end moves with the rim.** The order balance admits different front motions at the same inward intake. Identifying the reflecting end with the conversion front is a testable additional assumption. [Order balance (§1)](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/steps/S12_order_source.md) · [Candidate finite-throat modes](../../../research/pde/paper/pde.tex#L1158)

**One moving patch.** Along the axis toward bulk, put the rim at \(z(t)\), the front at \(Y(t)\) and \(\ell=Y-z>0\). Use a planar, fixed-area example with constant number density \(n\), material speed \(V+\dot z\), \(V>0\), and an order profile dropping from one to zero as a fixed shape translates with \(Y\). The inward number current per unit rim area stays \(nV\). Define the effective order loss per area as \(\mathcal C=-\int n\Gamma_B\,dq+[J_\chi]_{\rm ordered}^{\rm bulk}\), where the bracket is the outgoing minus incoming normal order flux. The local balance gives
\[
\mathcal C-nV=n(\dot z-\dot Y).
\]
A front following the rim needs \(\mathcal C=nV\). A front stationary relative to the bath needs \(\mathcal C=n(V+\dot z)\). Both have the same resting state and sustain inward flow for weak drives. The difference goes into changing the amount of ordered material in a rim-following window. This identifies the conversion response needed in this patch. The S12 source and flux functions remain open, and its graph-face construction excludes the puncture interior. [Scope and source forms](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/steps/S12_order_source.md#1-supplied-object-domain-and-standing)

**Where the wave and the reaction go.** Trial a straight, uniform reflecting segment with a node at the rim and zero strain at the far end. Assume the reflection tracks \(Y\), negligible effects of through-flow on dispersion, and weak exchange over a slow bend. The fundamental has strain energy proportional to \(\cos^2[\pi(q-z)/(2\ell)]\). Its centroid is \(X=z+(\tfrac12-2/\pi^2)\ell\). It follows a rigid translation exactly. With the far end stationary it moves by \((\tfrac12+2/\pi^2)\,dz\).

For fundamental mode energy \(E\), frequency \(\omega_0\), constant material coefficients and fixed action \(E/\omega_0\), the mean wave forces are \(-\beta E/\ell\) on the rim and \(+\beta E/\ell\) on the reflecting end. Here \(\beta=(c\pi/2\ell)^2/\omega_0^2\), with \(c\) the axial shear-wave speed. Their combined work is \((\beta E/\ell)(dY-dz)\). The same endpoint loads follow from the wave momentum current. This includes the mode's shear energy once. How the reflecting end transfers its load to bulk or back to the slab remains open. [Fixed-action conditions](../../../docs/oriented_throat_core_response_proposal.md#L25) · [Existing rim readouts](rim_bend_readouts.md)

**A discriminator.** For the two lowest modes on the same branch, \(\omega_j^2=\omega_*^2+c^2[(j+1/2)\pi/\ell]^2\). Thus \(\omega_1^2-\omega_0^2=2\pi^2c^2/\ell^2\), even when the common gap \(\omega_*\) changes. With known or independently tracked \(c\), the two peaks give the reflecting length. Compare its change against the conversion reading above for the same bend. A discrepancy tests the assumed reflection point, mode model and front kinematics; it cannot identify a culprit on its own. Without a speed calibration the spacing gives \(\ell/c\). The current one-mouth runs supply neither paired spectrum nor independent conversion reading for this drive. Physical rim load and electric strength remain open.
