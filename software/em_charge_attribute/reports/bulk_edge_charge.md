# Can charge survive on the shear-free side of the mouth?

**The Gaussian Maxwell theory extends into the bulk.** Its field \(A_M(\mathbf x,w,t)\) has a kinetic weight \(Z(w)=e^{-w^2/\lambda^2}\), positive at every finite depth. The massless mode is constant in \(w\), with field energy weighted toward the slab. Modes that vary in depth have a gap. The far-field reduction imposes \(J^w=0\) and cannot resolve a discharge. In the material model \(\chi_B=0\) removes the stiffness that supports transverse light. The model has yet to derive a Maxwell weight or an electric boundary condition from that loss. **A finite-width test.** Prescribe a conserved excess charge \(Q\), with no other excess sources, on a fixed flat background. Give it a normalized three-dimensional Gaussian profile \(f_\sigma(\mathbf x)\) of standard deviation \(\sigma\) per coordinate. In depth use \(h(w)=15(1-[(w-b)/a]^2)^2/(16a)\) on \(|w-b|<a\) and zero elsewhere. The half-width \(a\) and centre \(b\) may place the entire packet past a planar material edge. Under translation \(J^w=\dot b\,Q f_\sigma h\). One complete outward passage transports \(Q\). The overlap with the normalized massless mode is \(Q/\sqrt{Z_{\rm int}}\), independent of \(b\), where \(Z_{\rm int}=\sqrt{\pi}\lambda\). This tests an already charged packet. [SymPy and Mathematica measurements](../../../docs/_measurements/bulk_edge_charge.json)

The isolated packet has finite electrostatic energy at any finite depth. With \(\epsilon Z\) the physical electric-energy coefficient (\(\epsilon=1/\mu_0\) in the paper’s \(c=1\) units), the bounds are
\[
 \frac{Q^2}{8\pi^{3/2}\epsilon\,\sigma Z_{\rm int}}
 \ \le U_{\rm field}\le\
 \frac{5Q^2\,e^{(|b|+a)^2/\lambda^2}}{56\pi^{3/2}\epsilon\,a\sigma}.
\]
The massless mode gives the lower bound. For the upper bound minimize \(\int |\mathbf D|^2/(2\epsilon Z)\) subject to Gauss’s law. The trial displacement \((h\mathbf D_3,0)\), with \(\nabla_3\cdot\mathbf D_3=Qf_\sigma\) and \(\mathbf D_3\) the decaying Coulomb solution, is admissible. Its depth factor is \(\int h^2/Z\le e^{(|b|+a)^2/\lambda^2}5/(7a)\). The exponential enters through the trial field and the smallest Maxwell weight on the chosen support. At fixed depth and depth width, forcing the lateral size to zero costs unbounded energy. Allowing arbitrarily broad packets drives the field energy toward zero. These limits say nothing about the time or material work required to spread. **Which carrier?** The full matter action already provides \(J_\psi^w=q_\star j^w\). A constituent pulse that takes over a lost unit \(sg\) must carry \(q_\star\Delta N=sg\) in excess of ordinary throughput, with the corresponding charge removed from the mouth. A deficit is possible relative to a populated background. The saved unwinding calculation gave zero excess carrier current. Bulk vorticity couples to the magnetic field away from phase singularities and supplies no independent electric monopole in Gauss’s law. **What would establish an electrical barrier?** The conversion law must say whether the unit stays concentrated at the slip point, spreads over the interface, or enters the constituent current. One then needs the charge distribution and receiver energy along the path, including the initial charged mouth, in the same units as the released mechanical energy.
