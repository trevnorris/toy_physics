# Does the bending force reach the slab when both faces move together?

**The frozen mouth and cap move together under a rigid translation of the prescribed geometry. Their wall energy does zero work in that motion. Which part of the end load reaches a freely moving slab depends on how the cap is supported.** The present model gives alternatives for the receiving material and leaves their physical selection open. [Frozen geometry and its holding reaction](../g0_closure_card_v0.md#L23) · [Free-wall proposal](../../../research/pde_ledger_v2/notes/stage044_v2_unfreeze_prep.md#L19)

**Displacement and work.** Move both local faces by the same lab displacement \(z\) along \(w\), at fixed radius, slope and thickness. An attached mouth moves by \(z\). Write the far-end displacement as \(y\). A straight wall with orientation \(s=\pm1\) exerts \(sP\) at the mouth and \(-sP\) at the far end, where \(P=4\pi\sigma a^2+16\pi\kappa_b/9\). Its work is \(sP(\delta z-\delta y)\). Both terms in \(P\) have the same displacement factor. Comoving ends cancel both contributions to wall work. A fixed far end transmits both. [End force and assumptions](order_rim_radius.md) · [SymPy and Mathematica checks](../../../docs/_measurements/common_face_work.json)

**Endpoint motion alone leaves the external reaction undetermined.** For an explicit control, attach the far end elastically to the slab with energy \(k(y-z-d)^2/2\), and let the bulk apply a constant signed force \(B\) to that end. This is an illustrative support. Its equilibrium gives \(y=z+d+(B-sP)/k\). The total slab force, including the elastic support, is \(sP+k(y-z-d)=B\). For either zero or nonzero \(B\), a different initial support strain gives the same endpoint positions and \(dy/dz=1\). A motion measurement alone cannot distinguish these preparations.

**Receiving-side test.** For a hemispherical cap with its tip toward \(+w\), an excess lumen-to-bulk pressure gives axial force \(\int_{\rm cap}\Delta p\, n_w\,dA=(4\pi a^3/3)\overline{\Delta p}\), with the mean weighted by projected area. Mirror the cap to reverse the lab sign. If this pressure alone balances the far-end pull, it must satisfy

\[
\overline{\Delta p}=\frac{3\sigma}{a}+\frac{4\kappa_b}{3a^3}.
\]

Doubling \(a\) halves the tension pressure and divides the bending pressure by eight. This is a required load, not a measured pressure. A uniform pressure acting on the closing mouth cut supplies the opposite projected force. Follow that reaction when inferring the net force on the slab. On the permeable E3 branch, the full carrier traction is continuous across the wall, so it provides zero carrier **jump** there. On the impermeable E2 branch, the wall can sustain a normal reaction whose magnitude requires the bulk and support solution. For a draining cap the complete measurement includes carried momentum, stress and other support forces. [Contact conditions](../g0_closure_card_v0.md#L431)

**Electric projection.** Translating the orientation kernel, wall and background together gives zero change in \(Q_\chi=s\) by a change of integration variable. In the existing fixed-source mouth functional, the generalized load on \(h\) is \(\eta(sg_{\chi h}-k_m h)\). A rigid translation of the parent scalar profile and its sampling plane leaves the sampled value unchanged. For a physical local shift relative to a distant reference the work instead contains \(\int\eta(sg_{\chi h}-k_mh)\,\delta h\,d^3x\). The identity \(\xi_w=\ell h\) identifies the embedding mode. Projecting the two moving material faces onto that mode still requires their relation to \(\xi_w\). The separate mouth source specifies neither the cap load nor a mechanical transfer of only the radius-independent bending force. A definitive check needs the signed receiving and support reaction for the same drain, and the displacement or stress in the exterior mode with one normalization across radii.
