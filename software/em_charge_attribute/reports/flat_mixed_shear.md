# Does mixed shear carry a long-range electric signal on the flat brane?

**The flat slab transmits a mirror-odd twist over a finite range. Its transverse shear response has zero coefficient of the \(1/r\) potential.** This tests the sharp ordered slab with two shear-traction-free faces, both tangential polarizations, and a frozen elastic reference. Shear is confined by the face conditions in this benchmark. The steady relaxation law and normal loading from a dynamical bulk remain open. [Material assumptions](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/directives/O2_SHARED_PHYSICS.md#L93) · [Guided-sector action](../../../docs/light_guided_photon_soliton_research_plan.md#L329)

**Test.** Put faces at \(w=\pm a\), with thickness \(H=2a\). At in-brane wave number \(k\), the energy of a tangential displacement \(u\) transverse to the propagation direction is \(\tfrac12\int[\mu_\parallel k^2|u|^2+\mu_w|u'|^2]\,dw\). Both moduli are positive. The undriven faces give \(\mu_w u'=0\). The mixed rotation \(C_{wi}=\partial_w u_i-\partial_i u_w\) becomes \(u_i'\) in this polarization. Apply a localized tangential load at depth \(+b\) and compare with the identical load at \(-b\), \(0<b<a\). Their difference couples to \(\Delta u=u(b)-u(-b)=\int_{-b}^b u'\,dw\). Their sum is a long-range positive control. These are test loads. [Curl energy](../../../research/pde_ledger_v2/notes/stages/ledger_stage006_two_phase_chiB_ontology.md#L98) · [Earlier free-face benchmark](incident_shear_work.md)

**Range and sign.** With constant moduli and \(q=k\sqrt{\mu_\parallel/\mu_w}\), the response of \(\Delta u\) to the difference source is
\[
G_{\Delta\Delta}=\frac{2\sinh(qb)\cosh(q(a-b))}{\mu_w q\cosh(qa)}.
\]
The same probe gives opposite readings from the two individual source depths. At \(k\to0\), \(G_{\Delta\Delta}\to2b/\mu_w\). Its \(1/k^2\) coefficient is zero, while the sum-to-sum control has coefficient \(4/(H\mu_\parallel)\). The gapless displacement is constant across depth and has exactly zero mixed shear. The first mode that twists has inverse range \((\pi/H)\sqrt{\mu_w/\mu_\parallel}\). For \(b=H/4\) and equal moduli, the polarization-resolved response at \(r/H=1,2,4\) is respectively 0.043295, 0.0018674 and 0.0000034873 times the even control’s \(1/r\) contribution. Each massive mode gives an exponential times \(1/r\). [Three-dimensional Green function, Eq. 3.269](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S3.html) · [Both calculations and outputs](../../../docs/_measurements/flat_mixed_shear.json)

**Two controls.** For even, varying positive stiffnesses on a connected slab, integrating the zero-wave-number stress gives \(G_{\Delta\Delta}(0)=\int_{-b}^{b}dw/\mu_w(w)\) when this is finite. The constant mode again drops out. Allowing normal displacement can also contribute \(-\partial_i u_w\) to a twist reading. In an illustrative uniform four-dimensional curl-plus-compression model, a unit normal force at the midplane gives \((-\partial_w^2+k^2)P=k\delta(w)\) for mixed stress amplitude \(P\), with \(P(\pm a)=0\). Its integrated force fraction \(k\int P\,dw=1-\mathrm{sech}(kH/2)\) tends to zero. The remaining force requires normal face reactions compatible with static equilibrium. The separately modeled scalar bend can carry \(1/r\) if it receives a net normal load. Its physical source and face reaction are separate data. **Disposition.** The mixed shear of the supplied transverse displacement fails the long-range test, so no paired draining-mouth run was triggered. An independent mixed field or a new interface mode would need its own physical action, face matching and static source. The calculation does not assign a steady shear modulus to a relaxing drain.
