# Does the bend and inward drain select the wave tilt at pair formation?

**For two round openings forming above and below the same place in an initially uniform brane, the signed drive is zero.** Depth bending, radial inflow and changes in radius or length preserve a rotation by \(\pi\) that reverses the measured in-brane direction \(x\) and leaves \(w\) fixed. In the undriven real-wave equation, combining that rotation with the overall wave-sign symmetry preserves the untilted preparation. It exchanges the two circulation assignments. The two reversed disturbances therefore have equal gains through symmetry-preserving feedback. A coherent external wave drive or an initially oriented material texture would require its own data. [Wave and wall equations](../../../notes/inner_throat/inner_throat_hard_mode.md#L459) · [Previous pair test](forming_pair_circulation.md)

**Drain load.** On a moving round cut let \(t_r,t_w\) be the radial and depth components of the momentum flux relative to the cut, including flow and stress. Around each angular section the flux of mixed angular momentum in the fixed \(x,w\) plane is \(\hat r_x[r t_w-(w-w_c)t_r]\), about a point on the depth axis at \(w_c\). It integrates to zero even when the bracket is large. For example, material turning into the throat can carry local mixed stress \(m\rho v_rv_w\). Likewise, a trial lateral displacement of a radial confinement couples to density with angular factor \(\hat r_x\), and to the initial wave intensity with factor \(\hat r_x^3\). Both average to zero. A load weighted by \(1+\delta\hat r_x\) instead gives \(\delta/3\) times the bracket, a check that the calculation detects unequal loading across the rim. **A deformation that does drive the tilt.** In the previous dimensionless splitting well, allow the centerline to move sideways with depth. Set \(V=[x-X(w)]^2+(w^2-d^2)^2/4\), \(G=e^{-(x^2+w^2)/2}\), and \(h=w^2-1/2\). The initial wave \(P\propto xG\) and the measured tilt \(T\propto hG\) have unit norm. For \(X=b_0+b_1w+b_2 h\), projecting the wave equation at a standing start \(u=P,\ \dot u=0\) gives **\(\langle T,\ddot u\rangle=b_2\)**. A common offset and a straight slant give zero for this particular tilt. A sideways bow with the same sign at opposite depths and a different displacement at the midplane drives it immediately. This shape is a sensitivity test. The reduced wall law evolves radius and length and leaves this lateral degree of freedom unspecified. **Can formation at least favor tilting?** At fixed squared field norm and zero instantaneous velocity, write \(u=P\cos\beta+T\sin\beta\). At zero bow the wave-energy curvature in \(\beta\) changes from \(+9/4\) at \(d=0\) to \(-7/4\) at \(d=2\). Both small tilts lower the trial energy at the latter geometry. Allowing the bow to relax with an added elastic cost \(k b_2^2/2\) preserves \(E(b_2,\beta)=E(-b_2,-\beta)\). This is an instantaneous two-mode energy comparison. Growth, retention and the electric response require separate dynamics.

**Open input.** Measure or derive the signed, depth-dependent lateral deformation or the phase-coherent conversion drive during formation, with the incoming preparation and its reflection controlled.
