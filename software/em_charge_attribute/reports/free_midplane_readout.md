# What can one drain tell us about the bend of the freely moving slab?

The stationary drain produces an opposing load on the intact faces of the feeding slab. The available one-mouth fields resolve that load. Their two material faces are held flat, so their locations supply no measurement of the bend of a free slab. [Feeding solver](../../stage1_solver/tools/lateral_reservoir_field.py) · [Archive contents and definition of the faces](../../../docs/_measurements/width_pressure_patch.txt)

**Signed feeding-side result.** Orient \(+w\) through the upper mouth into the bulk. With mouth radius one and inward number rate 0.25, integrate the excess outward carrier stresses \(P_+-P_-\) over the intact faces inside each in-brane radius. Exclude the upper aperture and include the lower face beneath it. Weight the axisymmetric cells by their three-dimensional volume. Within 16 mouth radii the finer grid gives the following integrated rates of \(+w\) momentum in solver units. The integrated intact-face load already reaches −0.00705872682199 within four radii in the field-equation readout. Its direction is consistent on all three grids. The load at the opening has greater reconstruction sensitivity. The signed sum is a partial balance of the feeding field. Momentum through the opening enters the receiver, which might retain it, export it, or transmit it back to the slab. Inferring a residual physical charge or a fraction returned from the sum would assume its destination.

**Free-slab readout.** For an isolated stationary drain with independently located material faces \(w_\pm\), form the sphere-averaged midpoint \(m(r)=\langle(w_++w_-)/2\rangle\) relative to the distant resting slab. In the unpinned, constant-stiffness exterior, if \(m=m_0+A/r\) beyond the return region, each exterior pair must yield the same \(A=[m(r_1)-m(r_2)]/(1/r_1-1/r_2)\). This cancels a common vertical offset and needs no numerical derivative. Use at least three separated radii and repeat with the outer boundary farther away. With an independently known midpoint stiffness, the signed force inferred from that amplitude is \(4\pi\kappa_m A\). Compare it to the signed force deposited on the slab from a closed momentum balance for the same drain, including the intact-face load, the receiver's export, and any support or stored momentum. [Exterior assumptions](two_face_rim_bend.md) · [Momentum balance and orientations](rim_normal_return.md)

**Missing input.** The receiver enforces a uniform inlet field even in the resting case where the feeding solution varies across the opening. A common intake does not match the two solutions. The active-brane equations leave the normal response and support reaction open. The older electric localization calculation supplies its compact source amplitude \(Q_E\) as an input. To perform the proposed test we need a common stationary mouth state with freely located shear-supporting faces and an independently determined normal traction or momentum export at its contact with the receiver. A matched boundary law could supply this without resolving the entire throat. The remaining force-sign question also requires the response of a second mouth.
