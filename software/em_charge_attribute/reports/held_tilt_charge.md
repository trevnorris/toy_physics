**Matched control.** At each tilt \(\beta\), freeze a copy of the same mouth and attachment while keeping the radius \(R\), inward number rate \(D\), material parameters, wave action and constituent charge \(q_\star\) fixed. The relative rates compare a moving history with a held history at their common shape. Equal-time geometric densities coincide, so subtracting two snapshots alone would erase the signal. For example, halfway through conversion the moving layer changes at \(-15s\dot\beta/8\) while the individually held layer stays constant. **Ordinary charge.** A material section at fixed \(r\) moves in depth as the mouth tilts. Its flux uses material velocity relative to that section. In the prescribed carrier flow, the surface density \(n=n_0/W\) cancels the graph volume factor \(W\). Every shell has charge \(4\pi q_\star n_0r^2\,dr\) and downstream coordinate speed \(D/(4\pi n_0r^2)\). Each run therefore carries \(q_\star D\) across every layer cut and contains \(76\pi q_\star n_0R^3/3\) between \(2R\) and \(3R\). Both differences vanish before any further carrier subtraction. For a time-dependent physical flow, equal inlet rates alone permit a difference \(B(t)\) in the number in transit, producing stored charge \(q_\star B(t)\) and an outlet flux difference \(-q_\star\dot B(t)\) for a region with no other exits. Both raw differences and their number-current contributions matter.
