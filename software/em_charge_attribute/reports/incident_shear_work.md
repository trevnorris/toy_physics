# How much sideways work can incoming brane shear do on a forming mouth?

**The incoming shear can load the two ends more strongly than the midplane.** In the archived flare, the cycle-averaged sideways force per unit depth at either end was **2.95977 times** the midplane force at the tested wavelength. Reflecting the interference pattern reversed both forces. The wave was launched uniformly through the slab depth. The difference came from the shape of the material that lost shear order. This gives the work on that material boundary. How much reaches the moving rim depends on their attachment. [Archived flare](../../stage1_solver/src/stage1_solver/m1c_physical_run.py#L186) · [Guarded results](../../../docs/_measurements/incident_shear_work.json)

**Incoming state and material law.** In a uniform ordered slab with shear-traction-free faces, use the depth-independent transverse mode \(u_y=A\cos(kx+\phi)\cos(\omega t)\), \(\omega^2=\mu_0 k^2/\rho_0\). This is the interference of equal coherent waves incident along \(+x\) and \(-x\). Treat the geometry as frozen over an optical cycle. Give the interior of each spherical section a small loss of order \(\epsilon\), with stiffness \(\mu_i=\mu_0(1-\epsilon)\) and transverse inertia \(\rho_i=\rho_0(1-\epsilon)^m\). The two previously studied inertia laws have \(m=0,1\). Drainage is a common preparation; the test omits advection, dissipation and conversion into other modes. The unperturbed incident wave suffices at first order because its correction multiplies the already small material contrast. **Work on a section.** Let \(\Delta\rho,\Delta\mu\) denote exterior minus interior values. Vary the transverse action with respect to a sideways displacement \(Y(w)\) of the contrast. Holding the wave fixed in laboratory coordinates gives \(\delta W=\int \bar f_x(w)\delta Y(w)\,dw\), where, to first order,
\[
\bar f_x(w)=\frac{\pi A^2}{2}\bigl(\omega^2\Delta\rho+k^2\Delta\mu\bigr)
 R(w)^2 j_1(2kR(w))\sin(2\phi).
\]
The inertia and curl terms add because velocity and strain peak at different positions in the standing wave. On this incident branch their sum measures the weak change in shear impedance \(Z=\sqrt{\rho\mu}\). An unchanged \(Z\) gives zero at this order. A single traveling plane wave also gives zero at this order for this lossless round contrast. A stationary boundary receives no mechanical power; the displayed force gives the work for a virtual displacement or, multiplied by velocity, the leading power during slow motion. **Depth and sign checks.** Mirror the archived \(R=1+(3s^2-2s^3)/2\), \(s=|w|/L\), \(L=37/20\), across the midplane. At \(k=1/2\) and \(\phi=\pi/4\), the geometric factors are **0.301169** at \(R=1\) and **0.891389** at \(R=1.5\). To remove a uniform displacement, evaluate work on \(\delta Y=\delta b\cos(\pi w/L)\). Its generalized force divided by the common-shift force is **−0.253218**; a straight constant-radius control gives **0**. A trial displacement that moves the wider ends along the load and the midplane the other way therefore receives positive work. This ratio compares two specified virtual displacements; it does not measure an energy-capture fraction or actual acceleration. Changing \(\phi\) to \(-\pi/4\) reverses the work on each displacement. Centering a crest gives zero. The long-wavelength end/midplane force ratio tends to **27/8**. As a separate check, exact incoming/outgoing momentum fluxes for a planar layer recover weak force coefficients \(1/4\) and \(1/2\) for \(m=0,1\) in the normalized quarter-wavelength-thick layer test \(ka=\pi/4\). **Physical discriminator.** At fixed intake, track the signed center of an order contour \(Y(w,t)\) and the geometric rim independently during the two reflected drives. Subtract the no-wave run and the common translation. Their relative motion determines whether the computed shear load follows the rim or reorganizes the conversion region. A second input for the actual force magnitude is the transverse inertia through that region. The forming pair, its late circulation and its electric flux have yet to be calculated.
