# Can incoming waves bend a forming pair sideways?

**A standing wave can give a round wall a sideways bow from rest. Its sign depends on where the pair forms within the wave.** The calculation joins the existing angular wall equation to the neutral trapped-wave Hamiltonian by translating its radial confinement with each wall section. This is a test of a specified wave already present at the wall. Transfer from an incoming transverse brane wave into that state remains open. **Load from the wave.** Let \(X(w)\) move a spherical section in the \(x\) direction, \(\nu=x/r\), and let the wave confinement translate as \(V(|\mathbf x-X(w)\hat x|,w)\). The physical force per unit depth at \(X=0\) is \(f_x=\tfrac12\int u^2\,\nu\,\partial_r V\,d^3x\). Prepare a real standing trace \(u=A\,g(w)\cos(kx+\phi)\), \(\dot u=0\). The angle \(\phi\) is measured at the pair center. For \(z=2kr\), integration over each sphere gives
\[
\int_{S^2}\nu\cos^2(kr\nu+\phi)\,d\Omega
=-2\pi\,\frac{\sin z-z\cos z}{z^2}\sin(2\phi).
\]
At \(z=\pi\), the angular factors for phases \(+\pi/4,-\pi/4,0\) are \(-2,+2,0\). The reflected waves have equal energy in the centered geometry and zero initial wave momentum. The centered intensity peak and node both give zero signed force. The magnitude and absolute sign of the radial integral depend on the confinement profile. **Bending versus translating.** Take a straight round segment of depth \([-L,L]\), with uniform wall coefficients, a smooth midplane join and free axial end traction. Write \(X=X_0+b\cos(\pi w/L)\). Its dipole inertia per unit depth is \(I=(4\pi/3)\mu_\eta\) in the densitized convention. At rest with zero displacement the local equation gives \(I\ddot X=f_x\). For radially identical wall sections take \(g=\cos(\pi w/2L)\), so \(f_x=Sg^2\) with signed midplane load \(S\). Both \(\ddot X_0\) and \(\ddot b\) equal \(S/(2I)\). The midplane acceleration minus the mean end acceleration is **\(S/I\)**. A depth-uniform wave with the same \(S\) gives **zero** for that difference. This establishes an initial deformation for prescribed traces and wall geometry. Its growth or retention during formation has yet to be established. **Does one photon from each direction supply the sign?** As a separate quantum-optics check, give the two oppositely traveling modes identical depth envelopes and polarization, with one photon in each. The spatially odd part of the number-conserving intensity is proportional to \(Y=(a_+^\dagger a_- -a_-^\dagger a_+)/(2i)\). In \(|1,1\rangle\), \(\langle Y\rangle=0\) and \(\langle Y^2\rangle=1\). An ideal measurement of \(Y\) gives \(-1\) and \(+1\) with equal probabilities. The local two-photon annihilation amplitude has spatial factor \(2e^{ikx}e^{-ikx}=2\), whose lateral dipole is zero. These are properties of the prepared modes. The model has yet to specify which observable, if any, is recorded by a newly forming mouth.
