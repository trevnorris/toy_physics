# Can the feed and receiver select a common mouth flow and normal load?

**Yes for a transparent aperture.** A single stationary carrier can drain from the slab into the receiver with continuous phase and current. The calculated forces on the two sides oppose one another. The net carrier load enclosed within increasing radii gets smaller over the resolved range. The physical converting throat still needs its own normal-force law.

**Contact tested.** A slab of unit depth feeds a radius-one opening in a flat reflecting screen. The receiving region is on the \(+w\) side. Both regions solve \(-\nabla_4^2\psi/2+(|\psi|^8-1)\psi/4=0\) with the same material coefficients. In the opening a conservative finite-volume link connects the two cells, implementing continuity of \(\psi\) and its normal derivative in the continuum. There is no separate surface energy or conversion stress. Each distant bath has unit density. A single far-feed phase adjusts to set the integrated current. Its local profile is free. This replaces both the prescribed feeding profile and the ideal incompressible receiver of the previous test. The fixed plane and the lower reflecting face represent the slab geometry. **Flow.** At 24 cells per mouth radius and box extent 10, the feed supplied 0.25, the aperture passed 0.249999999999, and the receiver discharged 0.249999999999. Every aperture cell carried current into the receiver. The inner half-radius carried 0.0146449030973. Independent extrapolations of the center-minus-half-radius phase contrast from the two sides gave \(+0.000127049233853\) and \(+0.000123644594543\) radians. Their difference decreased at each refinement from 8 to 16 to 24 cells per radius in this box. Continuity is the chosen law. The phase profile and current distribution are solved quantities. **Force.** Use the stationary excess carrier stress \(\Pi_{ij}=\mathrm{Re}(\partial_i\psi^*\partial_j\psi)-\partial_i\partial_j|\psi|^2/4+\delta_{ij}(|\psi|^{10}-1)/5\). On the intact screen between 1.5 and 3 radii, the fine-grid feeding side pushed by \(-0.00233048578758\) and the receiving side by \(+0.00183078476369\) in solver units relative to the resting bath. To include both slab faces and the rim, enclose the fluid within in-brane radius \(r\), from the bottom face to \(w=r\). The fluid's force on all enclosed solid surfaces is minus the momentum exported through the top and radial side. This avoids estimating pointwise pressure at the sharp lip. Mathematica checked the continuum stress divergence and the vanishing stress jump inside the transparent opening. **Charge versus mouth radius.** For throats representing the same single unit of charge, the leading far-field electric strength must have the same nonzero magnitude across physical mouth radii \(a\) on the same brane. To test this, use the same distant unit-charge probe and medium, and compare \(d^2 F_{\rm odd}(d;a)\) at separations \(d\) much larger than both mouths and the slab thickness. Here \(F_{\rm odd}\) is half the difference in radial probe force when the source orientation is reversed with its other preparation mirrored. Use each radius's physical intake and one common field normalization. Equal near-mouth stresses or bend heights are unnecessary. The calculations above varied the enclosing radius \(r\) around a single mouth of radius \(a=1\). The charge-strength comparison remains open.
