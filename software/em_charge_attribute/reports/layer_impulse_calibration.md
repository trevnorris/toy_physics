# Can the opposite drives calibrate the layer’s transverse mass?

**The four saved drives at inward number rate \(0.25\) give no independent mass measurement for the converting layer.** They prescribe the motion of the radius-one opening. Their readings end at the feeding face. At \(t=0.001\), half the positive-minus-negative carrier load was \(-4.6838303353\times10^{-10}\) in solver units at full drive and \(-2.34191506574\times10^{-10}\) at half drive. These are momentum rates at one cut. The records lack the layer material velocity, stored transverse momentum and receiving-side impulse for the same drive. Python and Mathematica independently read the signed carrier values. [Saved trajectories](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_prescribed_acceleration.txt) · [Receiving-domain inventory](../../stage1_solver/reports/rim_cut_inventory.md) · [Commands and results](../../../docs/_measurements/layer_impulse_calibration.json)

**Separating storage from export.** Use fixed cuts around the same patch and take half the difference between opposite drives, denoted by \(\delta\). From the same initial preparation, the stored momentum change is the net impulse of all forces on the material, including boundary tractions, less the signed net advective export integrated over the drive. Include each contribution once and keep any end support outside the inventory or account for its momentum separately. Either measure the resulting \(\delta P\) independently or reconstruct it from this complete balance. Track the transverse velocity of the material across the patch. Geometry supplies the three-area \(A\). [Material exchange convention](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/directives/O2_SHARED_PHYSICS.md#L217) · [Phase and transverse mass](layer_transverse_mass.md)

If the local incremental inertia is positive, the material preparation is matched, and \(0<v_{\min}\leq\delta v(q)\leq v_{\max}\) throughout the patch, the single-time result is
\[
\frac{\delta P}{A v_{\max}}\ \leq\ \Sigma_\perp\ \leq\ \frac{\delta P}{A v_{\min}} .
\]
This follows by integrating the velocity with its inertia weight. The interval collapses for uniform velocity. At a matched time an exactly proportional half-strength response checks linearity but supplies the same interval. Even this bound needs both stored momentum and the velocity range.

**Check on existing benchmark fields.** The separate collar calculation records a wall and a moving wave envelope with supplied line masses. On the finer grid at \(t=1\), the full signed pair gave the envelope an impulse of \(0.00134482562411\) from the wall. An impulse of \(0.00015550951079\) crossed the downstream cut as wave stress. Subtraction recovered the independently tabulated stored momentum, \(0.00118931611332\). The signed velocity ranged from \(0.00113947623706\) to \(0.00348395477875\) over the included nodes. This yielded an integrated benchmark mass interval of \([0.341369560987,\ 1.04373928533]\), containing the supplied continuum mass \(0.439448148111\). Halving the drive gave the same bounds to the displayed first nine decimal places. The coarser grid also enclosed the supplied mass. This checks the reduction in the benchmark’s own units; its wave envelope, prescribed inertia and stress export cannot supply the converting material’s mass or advective loss. [Benchmark equations and inputs](../../stage1_solver/reports/signed_mouth_collar.md)

**Disposition.** Carry the physical \(\Sigma_\perp\) as an open material input. The observation that would change this status is one matched pair of transverse *material* velocity profiles across the conversion layer and its stored momentum or complete impulse budget. A nearly uniform velocity would give a direct mass and hence an independent prediction of the long-wave reflection depth under the free-edge assumptions.
