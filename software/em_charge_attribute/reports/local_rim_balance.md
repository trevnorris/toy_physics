# Where does the sideways load go at the rim?

**A smooth continuation of the order layer produces no concentrated force where we draw the rim cut.** Stress can cross that cut and continue into the brane. A physical attachment, support, or finite conversion region can take some of the load. In the quasistatic two-channel test, let \(P\) be the signed force the layer delivers to the junction, \(S\) the force the junction delivers to the surrounding brane, and \(H\) the external force holding the geometric rim still. Then \(H+P-S=0\). All three are increments relative to the same intake and initial preparation. The previous benchmark computed \(P=T(0)q'(0^+)\) for one side. Its pinned value \(0.0000888947\,\kappa_B\) at far displacement \(+0.002\) assumed a fixed near trace. [One-sided force](held_rim_shift.md)

**Local check.** For the bare order energy density \(e=f_B(\chi)+\kappa_B|\nabla_4\chi|^2/2\), the stress for a virtual translation is \(S^\chi_{xj}=e\delta_{xj}-\kappa_B\partial_x\chi\,\partial_j\chi\). Its divergence is \((f'_B-\kappa_B\Delta_4\chi)\partial_x\chi\). The tractions from a smooth shared cut cancel pointwise with opposite normals; a nonzero chemical potential can still exchange force over a finite volume. On a cut across the depth the integrated stress gives \(Tq'\). A real draining collar also requires carried momentum, other stresses, any local source and changing stored momentum in its full balance. The shape-holding multiplier in the frozen model is an explicit support. **What the exterior can carry.** Use the homogeneous three-dimensional brane displacement energy \(\int[\mu_R|\nabla\times u|^2+B|\nabla\cdot u|^2]\,d^3x/2\) outside a round mouth of radius \(a\), with an undisturbed far field and a frozen material reference. This tests the elastic contribution for positive moduli. Take \(\theta\) from the applied force direction. Write \(u_r(a)=Z_n\cos\theta\) and \(u_\theta(a)=-Z_t\sin\theta\). Solving the exterior equations gives the same signed force on every concentric cut,
\[
S=K_b\,\frac{Z_n+2Z_t}{3},\qquad K_b=\frac{12\pi a B\mu_R}{2B+\mu_R}.
\]
If all boundary material follows a translation \(Z\), \(S=K_b Z\). If only its normal displacement is prescribed and tangential traction is free, the solution has \(Z_t=-Z_n/2\) and \(S=0\). The displacement is the gradient of a harmonic dipole; its curl and divergence both vanish outside. This zero is specific to the stated energy and boundary condition. The full-follow result agrees with the classical adhering-sphere coefficient on identifying \(B=\lambda+2\mu_R\). The material relaxation and traction of the draining brane remain open. **Matched limits and deciding measurement.** As a control, let the near order trace move by \(Z\) and attach it to the held rim with stiffness \(c\). For effective exterior stiffness \(K_e\) and far shift \(Y\), stationarity gives \(K_{\rm layer}(Y-Z)=K_eZ+cZ\). With \(c=0,\ K_e>0\), all arriving load continues outward. With \(c=K_e=0\), the layer translates and no load arrives. Both give \(H=0\). A pinned near trace gives \(S=0,\ H=-K_{\rm layer}Y\) in this undriven exterior. To distinguish these cases in a draining solution, record the order slope at the rim, the *material* displacements at the pole and equator, and the independent holder reaction for the same opposite shifts. Intake alone does not select the attachment.
