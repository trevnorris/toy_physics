# Does an oriented mouth carry an additive signed source?

**Working result.** The turning of the brane face supplies a dimensionless count under specified rim conditions. One upward mouth gives \(+1\), its mirror gives \(-1\), two upward mouths give \(+2\), and opposite mouths give zero. The count survives smooth reshaping, including changes of radius, while the lips stay vertical and the distant brane stays flat. The wave has yet to select these boundary conditions dynamically. [SymPy and Mathematica runs](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_curvature_source.json)

**What is counted.** Take a face \(w=H(\mathbf x,t)\) over the three brane coordinates outside disjoint round mouths. Use the same \(+w\) normal on both faces. Put \(p=\nabla H\), \(M_{ij}=\partial_j p_i\), and \(W=1/[\pi^2(1+|p|^2)^2]\). The signed curvature per projected volume is \(\rho_g=W\det M\). Its integral counts how much of the unit sphere of normal directions the face covers, with sign, in units of one hemisphere. For smooth moving graphs the associated current is \(\mathbf j_g=-W\,\mathrm{cof}(M)^T\partial_t p\). It satisfies \(\partial_t\rho_g+\nabla\cdot\mathbf j_g=0\) in the exterior. Boundary motion and transfer across the rim must be included for an open throat. [One-face conventions](oriented_rim_geometry.md) · [Piola identity used in the derivation](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/jgm.2019004)

**Why the counts add.** In slope coordinates a radial primitive has vector \(p\int_0^{|p|}v^2(1+v^2)^{-2}\,dv/(\pi^2|p|^3)\), regular at zero. The divergence theorem pulls its flux back to a sum over the outer surface and each lip. The outer term vanishes for a flat asymptote with decaying angular derivatives. At each round vertical lip the slope direction tends smoothly to \(-s\hat r\); its inward boundary orientation contributes \(s\). This holds without radial symmetry between mouths. Sum the two faces when mouths occur on opposite sides. A \(45^\circ\) lip gives only \(1/2-1/\pi\) in magnitude. A regular dimple that turns and returns to flat has zero net count. Extension through the physical conversion region could therefore matter.

**What reaches a distant sphere.** For a radial \(H=C/r\) tail, the geometric primitive's sphere integral is \(-4C^3/(3\pi r^6)+o(r^{-6})\). The unit count comes from the inner boundary. In a separate mechanical control the wave-supported mouth has a genuine scalar flux that is constant over enclosing spheres. Two equilibria at assigned radii \(1,2\), with identical wall and rear-support parameters, had wave energies \(1,5/3\) and scalar fluxes \(1/2,1/3\), respectively. These controls test the ordinary bend field; they do not determine the radius selected by a wave. [Mechanical energy and support](supported_mouth_bend.md)

**Missing physical relation.** The frozen bend action sources the scalar with a normalized orientation projection and prescribed coupling. The localized Maxwell model already assigns \(J_\psi^0=q_\star|\psi|^2\). Neither supplies an identification of the physical electric source with this geometric current. The decisive test is to derive the source of a weak electric probe from the existing matter, rim and conversion equations, including any current into the bulk. A nonzero common coupling to the count across different wave preparations, with its compensating return accounted for, would connect geometry to a field. Inserting that coupling as an extra source would assume the connection and could double-count matter charge.
