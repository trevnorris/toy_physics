# How one electric mouth responds to a distant bend

**Result.** Opposite weak changes in the distant brane deflection distinguish a mouth held at a fixed bend from one held at a fixed total normal load. They can also measure a finite restoring stiffness if the core reference stays fixed. Different ways of supplying the load can give identical readings. The electric force sign of the physical throat remains open. [Existing electric-sign branches](../puncture_deflection_electric_sign_result.md)

**Preparation.** Use the earlier static, spherically symmetric electric-scalar exterior with positive gradient stiffness \(\kappa\). Keep the radius \(a\), orientation, and inward preparation the same. Subtract the original state. On a sphere of radius \(L>a\) impose a uniform scalar offset \(b=0,\pm\epsilon,\pm\epsilon/2\), measured against the original core reference. This represents the spatially constant part of a distant bend across one mouth. Let \(x\) be the change in its scalar value. The physical displacement along the fourth direction is \(\ell x\). Intake is a fixed preparation label in this reduced exterior. [Exterior energy and definition of the field](../../../research/pde_ledger_v2/notes/stages/ledger_stage031_puncture_deflection_field_identity_source.md#3-exterior-stationary-solution--the-named-core-gap-fact-earned)

The radial field obeys \((r^2h')'=0\). Its incremental profile is \(h(r)=b+(x-b)(1/r-1/L)/(1/a-1/L)\). The quadratic exterior energy is \(K(x-b)^2/2\), with \(K=4\pi\kappa/(1/a-1/L)\), tending to \(4\pi\kappa a\) for a distant reservoir. Write \(P=K(x-b)\) for the change in total load the mouth must exert on the exterior, positive toward increasing \(h\). The corresponding force along \(w\) is \(P/\ell\). The exterior applies the opposite load to the mouth.

| Condition maintained during the probe | Mouth bend \(x/b\) | Required load change \(P/b\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mouth value held against the original reference | \(0\) | \(-K\) |
| Integrated normal stress of the scalar field held fixed | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| Ideal fixed scalar source supplies the whole load | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| Core supplies a finite restoring slope \(k>0\) about a fixed reference, \(P=-kx\) | \(K/(K+k)\) | \(-Kk/(K+k)\) |

**What the two readings identify.** A mouth with finite restoring slope follows part of the imposed bend. Its stiffness is \(k=-P/x\) when \(x\ne0\), and the two readings must satisfy the exterior work relation above. A fixed *bare* source can still have a changing total load. For example, retaining the existing local term \(k_m h^2/2-jh\) on a uniform mouth mode at fixed \(j\) gives \(P=-k_m x\). It reads exactly like a mixed boundary law of slope \(k_m\). The fixed-source and fixed-total-load readings coincide when that curvature vanishes. A restoring reference that shifts along with the bath also gives \(x=b,\ P=0\). Record the core reference and which source or constraint does work before interpreting an observed response. The measured slope \(k\) has no established map to the mixing weight in the two-mouth sign calculation.
