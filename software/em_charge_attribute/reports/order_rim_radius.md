# Does the conversion wall deliver the same axial force at different mouth radii?

**The wall can transmit a radius-independent part of the force between its ends. The total force on the slab is still open.** The derived surface tension gives an area-dependent pull. The proposed curvature energy gives a second term with different scaling. Both reverse their lab direction when the whole throat is mirrored. [Order energy and planar tension](../../../research/pde_ledger_v2/notes/stages/ledger_stage006_two_phase_chiB_ontology.md#L133) · [Free-wall proposal and restrictions](../../../research/pde_ledger_v2/notes/stage044_v2_unfreeze_prep.md#L19)

**Rim and curvature.** Write the circular section as \(R(z)\), with \(z\) increasing from the mouth toward the bulk and \(w=w_0+s z\), \(s=\pm1\). The static wall energy per axial length is \(\mathcal L=4\pi R^2\sqrt{1+R'^2}(\sigma+\kappa_b H^2)\), where \(H\) is the average of the three principal curvatures. Varying the end position at fixed local radius and slope gives the axial pull on the near attachment, \(sP\), where \(P=\mathcal L-R'(\mathcal L_{R'}-\partial_z\mathcal L_{R''})-R''\mathcal L_{R''}\). Local curvature transmits this force along the wall. For tension alone its integrated axial force on a patch is the difference of \(P_\sigma=4\pi\sigma R^2/\sqrt{1+R'^2}\) across the ends. Counting that difference again at an artificial cut would double-count internal forces. A constant collar energy \(4\pi\tau a^2\) gives zero axial force on translating the collar at fixed radius. [Earlier curvature and collar balances](../../../docs/drain_driven_initial_acceleration.md#rim-attachment-and-power-for-steady-inward-flow-at-0pn)

At a rim of radius \(a\) where the wall is tangent to the \(w\) axis (\(R'=0\)), the result is

\[
P=4\pi\sigma a^2+\frac{4\pi\kappa_b}{9}\big[4-(aR'')^2\big].
\]

For a straight cylindrical segment the bending contribution is \(16\pi\kappa_b/9\), independent of \(a\). This also follows by differentiating the recorded cylindrical bending energy with respect to its length. For geometrically similar flares, \(aR''\) is constant. The archived cubic flare, rescaled at fixed aspect ratio, gives \(1514161/1874161\) times the cylinder's bending pull. Holding the axial length fixed while varying \(a\) changes this coefficient. These are imposed shapes with one common set of material parameters. A free rim would also need its slope or moment condition and mechanical support. **What the original order layer contributes.** As a separate test, wrap the symmetric planar order profile of width \(\ell\) around a straight cylindrical wall with \(a\gg\ell\). Its energy density \(e(d)\) at normal distance \(d\) has \(\int e\,dd=\sigma\). The end pull integrates \(4\pi(a+d)^2e(d)\). Through constant order it gives \(4\pi\sigma[a^2+(\pi^2/3-2)\ell^2]\). The constant is the second moment of the profile. The wrapped planar profile has a nonzero order-equation residual. An allowed trial change in its width of relative size \(c\ell/a\) changes the constant-order pull by \(4\pi\sigma c^2\ell^2/2\). The physical relaxed coefficient remains undetermined. Adding the trial correction to a fitted curvature stiffness could count the same physics twice. [Guarded moment and profile checks](../../../docs/_measurements/order_rim_radius.json)

**Does the force reach the distant brane?** A straight segment pulls its two attachments with equal and opposite forces. If both attachments transmit to the same slab displacement, their work under that common displacement sums to zero. If the receiving bulk takes the far reaction, a load can remain on the slab. The current free-wall proposal has zero direct coupling to the electric displacement field \(h\); it keeps a separate mouth term. The next test is whether that term and the physical attachment conditions transfer a nonzero signed load to the slab midplane, with the area-dependent contribution accounted for. Radius-independent electric strength and the two-mouth force sign remain open.
