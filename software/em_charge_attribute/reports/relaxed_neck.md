# Does the barrier survive neck relaxation?

**No positive energy barrier was resolved in the matched collar model.** Relaxing the shape between the neck and the conversion layer gave a downhill route from signed count \(+1\) to zero. The route remained downhill even with the angle at the neck held fixed. The earlier \(0.418529\,\sigma R^3\) barrier belonged to the prescribed attachment shape. **Matched preparations.** Energies are in units of \(\sigma R^3\), relative to each preparation's own untilted state. In both relaxed cases the attachment was first allowed to settle at \(\beta=0\). The mouth position and radius, positive prescribed number intake, material parameters \(k=\kappa_b/(\sigma R^2)=5\), order profile, and dimensionless wave action \(j=582.371869550\) were held fixed during comparison. The same continuity construction carries the same number flux across every section. The energy contains area, mean curvature squared and the lowest standing-wave frequency at fixed action. Work done by the open flow or a downstream support has no constitutive law in this calculation. [Geometry and flow](mouth_count_unwinding.md) · [Wave functional](wave_at_level_patch.md)

**What was freed.** On \(R<r<2R\) add smooth axisymmetric variations of the graph height. With \(x=r/R-1,\ u=\cos\theta\), the basis is \(x^p(1-x)^2P_m(2x-1)P_l(u)\). The height and tangent match the original conversion region at \(2R\). The neck height is fixed. The choices \(p=2,1\) respectively fix or release the neck angle. The coarse and fine fits used 9 and 16 shape coefficients, including angle-independent changes in the untilted state. The wave eigenfunction was recomputed at every variation. The original material order drops over \(2R\le r\le 3R\), where the count still starts to fall at \(\beta=2\) and reaches zero at 3. [Minimization and derivatives](../relaxed_neck.py) · [Saved paths](../../../docs/_measurements/relaxed_neck_paths.json)

**Why the barrier disappeared.** With the neck angle fixed, at the former peak tension and bending cost \(2.74+5.33\), while the wave releases \(11.17\). Releasing the angle lets bending release energy as well. The fine calculation gave negative initial tilt stiffness for both attachments (\(-5.63\) with fixed angle, \(-42.45\) with free angle). Connecting neighboring relaxed shapes continuously and evaluating eight subintervals per segment gave negative slopes at every tested positive tilt. Mathematica independently recomputed energies on six saved shapes per attachment. The coarse and fine shape bases gave complete-loss energies of \(-15.92,-16.50\) with fixed angle and \(-47.09,-49.45\) with free angle. The endpoint energy changes depend on resolution. Both bases give a downhill route. This test gives a numerical escape route for this preparation. Preserving an electric charge through such a deformation would require an additional physical constraint, a compensating current, or a different conserved quantity. A dynamical discharge and an electric coupling remain open. Working calculation on base e0a94594, uncommitted pending independent review.
