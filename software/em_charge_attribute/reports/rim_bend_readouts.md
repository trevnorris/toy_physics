# Can one draining mouth measure how shear loads a bending rim?

The current one-mouth solver cannot make this measurement. The requested drive is a local displacement \(z\) of both material faces along \(w\), with the distant slab and bath held at rest. Radius, intake and initial shear preparation are to be matched. The three readings must come from the same drain. [Previous shear-work calculation](conversion_shear_work.md)

| Requested reading | What the current implementation supplies |
|---|---|
| Motion of the conversion front | The joined carrier solve fixes the impermeable part of the screen at one grid face and leaves the opening transparent. Its solved field is the complex carrier amplitude. There is no ordered-fraction contour to track. In the frozen wall specification, the \(r_B=1/2\) contour is constrained to the supplied surface. Moving that constraint prescribes the contour motion. [Joined geometry](../../stage1_solver/tools/joined_mouth_normal.py#L26) · [Wall constraint](../g0_closure_card_v0.md#L63) |
| Motion of the shear pattern | The separate collar benchmark saves independent wall and wave displacements as functions of depth and time. Both displacements describe *sideways* motion. Its material coupling and downstream load are supplied for that benchmark. The depth coordinate is a sampling coordinate. It cannot serve as a measurement of motion along \(w\). [Benchmark equations and drive](../../stage1_solver/reports/signed_mouth_collar.md) |
| Total force exerted on the rim along \(w\) | The joined solver measures carrier momentum export and its force on all enclosed solid surfaces, including the intact faces. The archive confirms an inward mouth rate of \(0.250000000001\) for the requested \(0.25\). It contains no separate rim reading during a bend. The frozen wall specification assigns its shape-holding reaction to the scaffold. [Carrier force measurement](joined_mouth_normal.md) · [Saved field names, commands and outputs](../../../docs/_measurements/rim_bend_readouts.txt) |

**Minimum physical input.** On the same patch, the model needs the response of the local order-conversion rate and flux to material motion, and the normal mechanical matching between the shear-bearing material, the converting layer, the rim and the receiving material. The latter must account for the equal and opposite traction. The existing order balance allows conversion to move a front relative to the material. The shear energy gives work for a specified relative shift. Neither specifies the attachment in this drive. The proposed free-wall action adds tension and bending but leaves its direct coupling to the electric bending field zero. [Order balance](../../../docs/moving_interface_conversion_work_proposal.md#L13) · [Material/reference obligations](../../../docs/elastic_reference_limit_comparison.md#L85) · [Free-wall proposal](../../../research/pde_ledger_v2/notes/stage044_v2_unfreeze_prep.md#L19)

**How to read the test once that input exists.** Drive the local slab gently to \(+z\) and \(-z\) with a distant material reference. At the tip, locate the half-order crossing independently and track the shear profile from the material displacement. The peak of the product of order and shear energy can shift when only the order changes. Record each profile so deformation is distinguishable from translation. At each position subtract the relaxed preparation's holder force from the sheared preparation's holder force, with the same drive and intake. Measure the axial rim reaction separately from forces on the faces and remote supports. A held static bias can survive at \(z=0\), so differencing only the two opposite drive forces would lose that bias. These readings would test how much of the previously calculated internal load reaches the brane.
