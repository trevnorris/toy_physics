# Does the slip point bind a weak charge when the standing wave is on?

**The saved standing mode gives no direct preference for putting a charge at the slip.** At the midpoint of unwinding (\(\beta=5/2\)), compare the pole where the projected normal vanishes with the equator on the same \(r=5R/2\) section. Both have \(\chi_B=1/2\), the same mouth radius and the same total inward number rate. For each of the two saved attachment shapes, keep the geometry fixed and compare the prepared wave action \(J=582.371869550\) with \(J=0\). External holding is implicit in the wave-off control. The measurements concern the first-order insertion energy per added constituent at fixed shape, order and occupation. **Wave signal.** The saved frequency depends on geometry and order. Its stiffness and inertia matrices contain no added-carrier number. For test constituents of charge \(q\), the direct difference from wall energy, \(J\omega\), and an applied potential is therefore \(q(\Phi_{\rm slip}-\Phi_{\rm eq})\). It vanishes at a common electric potential with the wave on or off. The two sites are at different depths, so a depth-dependent potential is a separate input. The physical mean curvature is finite at both sites, \(RH=1/5\) and \(8\sqrt{2/3}/45\). No term in the saved Hamiltonian attaches carrier number to the singular **direction** of the projected normal. \(U\) is the mass-normalized fundamental displacement in solver units. Its squared amplitude is positive and has nonzero radial derivative at the slip in both preparations. The last column deliberately changes the wave inertia at fixed stiffness. A weak local mass load shifts \(J\omega\) by \(-J\omega U^2\,\delta m/2\), favoring the equator in this comparison. Setting \(J=0\) gives zero for this control. SciPy finite-differenced the generalized eigenproblem and Mathematica recomputed the site readings with a separate basis. Identifying wave inertia with an inserted constituent would require a material law. **Flow check.** On the archived current field, the slip and equator have surface densities \(n_0\) and \(n_0\sqrt{2/3}\), and physical speeds \(v_0\) and \(v_0\sqrt{5/4}\), where \(v_0=D/(4\pi n_0r^2)>0\). The slip is traversed by flow. If both sites have the same normal thickness, their volume-density ratio is the same and the stiff equation of state gives higher pressure at the slip. With fixed local velocity on insertion, the EOS and kinetic contributions give \(\Delta\mu=(5/9)h_{\rm slip}-mv_0^2/8\); here \(h_{\rm slip}=5K n_{\rm slip}^4/4\) uses the volume density. The prescribed field satisfies continuity but supplies no solved Bernoulli or shear-stress balance. **Remaining measurement.** The total insertion work needs the local confinement/contact energy, the physical density and phase including density-gradient energy, and any response of wave stiffness or inertia to added material. Comparing equal weak transfers from a common reservoir at the two sites, in the same open-flow preparation, would measure that work. A lower value at the pole would then require a neighborhood check to establish a well.
