# What can stay charged at a stationary draining mouth?

**Working result.** A candidate current can remain attached to the layer where the brane loses shear order while constituents flow through it. Its integral is one signed unit per round mouth and is unchanged by smooth reshaping under the conditions below. A neutral current satisfies the same conservation test. The existing action has yet to select the charged one. [SymPy and Mathematica runs](../../../docs/_measurements/stationary_mouth_charge.json)

**The localized difference.** Follow a material collar from ordered feed (\(\chi=1\)) to bulk (\(\chi=0\)). Use the same \(+w\) face-normal convention on both reflected preparations. Let \(n\) be the unit direction of the face normal projected into the three brane directions wherever order changes. A candidate geometric current on the collar is

\[
\mathcal J_{\rm layer}=-d\chi\wedge n^*(d\Omega)/(4\pi).
\]

Here \(n^*(d\Omega)\) measures the signed solid angle swept by that direction around the mouth. A round mouth has \(n=s\hat r\) and covers the direction sphere once, with sign \(s\). The order drop integrates to one. Doubling the radius quadruples the area and divides the count per area by four at the same order drop. A transverse stretch by \(1/2,1,2\) gave \(+1\) for each upper mouth and \(-1\) for each mirror. The current is conserved because the sphere area form is closed. Its integrated count persists while the conversion cross-sections stay closed, the direction stays defined there, and no current escapes through other boundaries. Wave energy enters only insofar as it preserves these conditions. [Degree formula](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.952/2020SP/differential_forms.pdf) · [Mouth geometry](mouth_curvature_source.md)

**Which current does conservation select?** Write \(B\) for the cumulative signed turn from the flat feed and \(u\) for the meridian coordinate. Represent line density and flux by the coefficients of \(du\) and \(-dt\). Within the local first-derivative form \(\chi\,dB+f(B,\chi)d\chi\), conservation for arbitrary shape and order changes requires \(\partial_B f=1\). A family with total count \(K\) is \(d[\chi B+(1-\chi)K]\). At a vertical conversion segment \(B=s\), the layer correction integrates to \(K-s\). Requiring zero additional charge there would select \(K=s\); ordinary conservation also permits \(K=0\), where the layer cancels the exterior. The difference between these two currents is precisely \(-s\,d\chi\) after angular integration. For a fixed layer it carries zero transport current. Changing the total \(K\) requires charge exchange at a boundary. [Conversion controls](mouth_turn_conversion.md)

**What the electric probe sees.** At steady number throughput \(F\), constituents of charge \(q\) carry \(qF\) across each cut. If instead they lose charge on conversion and no other current leaves, the layer accumulates \((q_{\rm in}-q_{\rm out})F\) per unit time. Even at matched throughput the amount of carrier charge in transit can differ by \(qF\,\Delta\tau\) for a change in residence time. A trial electric coupling \(g\) to the localized difference would contribute \(gs\) to the distant constant-mode source, subject to compensating bulk charges and boundary flux. The present matter action supplies \(q_\star|\psi|^2\); adding a second copy of that same charge would double count it. The layer coupling \(g\) is undetermined. To test it, an independently identifiable material collar must cross the order gradient. On the frozen surface defined by a fixed value of that same order field, its tangential derivative is zero.
