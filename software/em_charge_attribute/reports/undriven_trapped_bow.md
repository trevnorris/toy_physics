# Can a trapped wave sustain a sideways load without an external push?

**Only the symmetric fundamental fits below the archived mouth’s trapping cutoff. Its mean sideways force on each round section is zero.** A longer ideal cavity supports an undriven sideways bow when a particular excited wave is populated. Its strength varies with both wave action and radius. [Archived cavity](../../stage1_solver/reports/pathA_26_derrick.md#L21) · [Guarded calculations and outputs](../../../docs/_measurements/undriven_trapped_bow.json)

**Archived mouth.** The neutral scalar wave has a spherical Dirichlet side, a mouth node and a reflecting end. Its frequencies obey \(\omega^2=m^2+c^2[(z_{ln}/R)^2+((j+1/2)\pi/L)^2]\), with \(z_{ln}\) a spherical Bessel zero. At \(R=1.892410005412,\ L=1.81206770991\), the fundamental gives **1.88345**. The lowest angular dipole, axial overtone and radial overtone give **2.53562, 3.09174 and 3.43732** against a cutoff of **2.5**. Bowing at fixed \(R,L\) cannot lower the fundamental frequency in this sharp tube. Each translated section obeys the same ball ground-energy bound. Extending the field by zero through the Dirichlet side gives the axial Dirichlet–Neumann bound on each vertical line. The straight fundamental attains both. [Wave Hamiltonian](../../../notes/inner_throat/inner_throat_hard_mode.md#L479) · [End conditions](../../../notes/lepton_mass_notes.md#L266)

**Excited-state exception.** Translate section centers by \(b\cos(\pi z/L)\). At \(L/R=1.38293373\) the angular dipole with axial index 0 and the angularly uniform mode with axial index 1 share a frequency. Side pressure and the volume wave operator give squared-frequency slopes \(\pm c^2\pi z_{11}/(\sqrt3 R^3)\). The interference load varies as the cosine of relative phase and vanishes at a quarter-cycle offset. Occupying the lower split branch reduces \(J\omega\) linearly in \(|b|\) near zero. Equal actions on both branches cancel this linear effect. [Shape variation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00273)

With \(R=2,\ m=0.2,\ c=1\) and wall energy \(b^2/2\), the minima are **\(b=\pm0.0107981\)** at action \(J=0.05\) and **\(\pm0.00543669\)** at \(J=0.025\). Stability was checked along this bow coordinate at fixed excited-branch occupation. If the archive’s optional hard cap \(L\le2.5\) is imposed, this crossing lies above the cap because trapping requires \(L>2.57206\). This example uses the uncapped geometry. [Volume solver](../undriven_trapped_bow.py) · [Surface calculation](../undriven_trapped_bow.wl)

**Charge test.** Doubling \(R\) from 2 to 4 at the crossing reduces the leading sideways load per action from **0.218997 to 0.0541520**. Holding the far depth fixed gives opposite axial wave loads on mirrors, with magnitudes per action **0.250673** and **0.0619847** for unit bend-to-length conversion. This includes ordinary axial wave pressure. Both ends translating together give zero work. Allowing size to relax at fixed shape also gives a test. Write \(\beta=b/R\), \(\omega^2=m^2+c^2\Lambda(\beta)/R^2\) and wall/volume energy \(A R^3+V R^4+W R\). Eliminating \(J\) by dilation equilibrium gives sideways wave force \(-(\partial_\beta\Lambda/2\Lambda)(3A R^2+4V R^3+W)\). Positive tension or volume cost retains size dependence in this family. Curvature alone leaves a constant term and still requires shape stability and attachment. [Scale-balance inputs](wave_load_vs_radius.md)

**Disposition.** Keep the resonance as a possible internal stress. The written wave has zero direct electric source. The draining mouth’s spectrum, excited-state retention and coupling to a signed far field remain open. Reopen this route if an undriven mouth retains the relevant modes and an independent probe finds opposite excess charges of equal magnitude across radii at fixed constituent charge.
