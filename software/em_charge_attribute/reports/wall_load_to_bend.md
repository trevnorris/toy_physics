# Can the mouth attachment produce a common repulsive electric strength?

**A passive local attachment to the existing scalar bend gives attraction between like mouths, even if the attachment is arbitrarily stiff.** The frozen model assigns a separate signed source to the bend and books the shape-holding reaction to the scaffold. The proposed free-wall energy depends on wall shape and has zero direct variation in the electric field \(h\). Neither gives a mechanical law that determines the load delivered to \(h\) under an incident bend. **Magnitude at two radii.** At a straight cylindrical section the wall pulls with \(P(a)=A+C\), where \(A=4\pi\sigma a^2\) is tension and \(C=16\pi\kappa_b/9\) is curvature. For physical near and far displacements \(X,Y\) along \(w\), wall work is \(sP(dX-dY)\), with orientation \(s=\pm1\). If this is the only projected load, define \(t=(dX/dh-dY/dh)/\ell\) at fixed preparation. Then the magnitude of the isolated signed exterior load is \(|L|=z\ell P|t|\), with \(z\) the annulus transmission factor. This relation tests a proposed mechanical attachment. An independent return or support force must have its own work included. [Wall and end-support balance](common_face_work.md) · [Two-radius annulus solution](unequal_mouth_electric.md)

For same-sign nonzero transfer, matching the two far-field strengths requires
\[
\frac{t(2a)}{t(a)}=\frac{z(a)}{z(2a)}\frac{A+C}{4A+C}.
\]
The required ratio is **0.960235** for curvature alone and **0.240059** for tension alone. Nonnegative mixtures lie between the limits. Equal nonzero transfer gives a larger exterior load at \(2a\) in every case. If both ends move equally, this wall term supplies zero work. The actual transfer at either radius remains undetermined.

**Sign including attachment work.** Use the two local mean bends \(h_i\), \(x=1/(4\pi\kappa d)\), the isolated annulus responses \(G_{ii}=S_i\), and \(G_{12}=xz_1z_2\) to leading order. Add each passive local attachment energy \(V_i(h_i,u_i)\) to the exterior energy \(h^TG^{-1}h/2\). Here \(u_i\) denotes relaxed internal coordinates. On a smooth stationary branch with fixed preparations and separation entering only through \(G\), all implicit variations of \(h_i,u_i\) cancel. Writing \(y_i=(G^{-1}h)_i\) in isolation, the derivative at \(x=0\) is **\(-z_1y_1z_2y_2\)**. This gives the orientation-sensitive outward force coefficient for mirrored isolated states. Like nonzero loads attract. For fixed loads, the field storage contributes one positive product and the two attachments together contribute twice that product with the opposite sign. A fixed-reference spring gives the same attractive sign up to the rigid limit. The earlier repulsive held-bend calculation includes a separate conjugate reservoir in its energy. A stiff wall supplies no such term by itself. [Boundary work definitions](../puncture_deflection_electric_sign_result.md#b-full-ensemble-definitions)

**Open measurement.** At each radius, apply a weak, slowly varying incident bend on the same draining preparation. Record the exterior signed load from its \(1/r\) tail and the incremental work at the rim, receiver and supports, including energy carried through the cuts. Subtract steady drain power at the same intake. The saved solver holds the faces fixed and lacks this matched work measurement. SymPy and Mathematica agreed on the sign and transfer bounds.
