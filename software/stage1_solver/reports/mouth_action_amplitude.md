# What displacement does the exit-balancing action require?

**The degree-12 wave leaves the small-strain regime at both entrances.** We used the same fine material and order fields that supplied the signed exit forces. The native-mesh replacement actions are 2.32792784 and 1.86299782. The refined results below apply those same actions with wave mesh 48. At each meridian we reconstructed the real harmonic displacement and analytically maximized over its phase. The maxima shown sample the supported cavity cells on both sides of each cell edge and 385 polar angles. They are lower bounds on the spatial peak. Speed is relative to the flat-brane shear speed. Strain is the dimensionless equivalent engineering shear $s=\sqrt{2e:e}$ for the symmetric displacement gradient $e$ in the current crystal. At radius 1 the largest strains occur in cells with very little shear order. The standing-wave peak changes from 302.15 to 473.84 across wave meshes 20 and 48. At radius 2 it changes from 10.71 to 12.94. The material-weighted check is more stable. On mesh 48, **99.75% and 94.82%** of cavity kinetic energy occupies meridian positions where the root-mean-square shear over angle and phase exceeds one. Mesh 20 gives 99.70% and 94.59%. This statement holds for any normalized superposition of the degenerate angular states at the specified frequency. The two states place 99.74% and 6.35% of cavity kinetic energy, respectively, in wave elements of order below one half.

**Normalization and cross-check.** We set $\int_{S^2}|T_{12m}|^2\,d\Omega=1$ for the toroidal harmonic and wrote the displacement as $\mathrm{Re}(A r f T_{12m} e^{-i\omega t})$. For the stored-energy convention inside the radiation port, $A^2=J\mathrm{Re}\,\omega/E_1$, where $E_1$ is the physical time-averaged kinetic plus symmetric-strain energy of the recovered unit modal vector. The squared shear averaged over angles and phase at fixed $(r,w)$ is $A^2[r^2(|f_r|^2+|f_w|^2)+(12\times13-2)|f|^2]/(8\pi)$. Explicit angular quadrature recovers both the unit kinetic norm and angular shear coefficient 154 for standing $m=0$ and travelling $m=12$. Independent split-cell integration recovers the cavity mass fraction. Radiation slowly decays the amplitude; these bounds use the instantaneous cycle envelope at the stated stored action.

**Limit of the conclusion.** The exact axisymmetric azimuthal twist preserves volume. Its relative deformation is a simple shear with largest principal stretch $(\sqrt{4+s^2}+s)/2$. On mesh 48, the sampled peak gives 473.84 and 13.02 times the initial length in the most stretched local direction, respectively. At fixed order and fixed meridian the finite-strain benchmark can carry mathematically arbitrary volume-preserving shear. The measurements disqualify a perturbative certification of a wave-supported exit. They do not specify a fracture threshold, an order-melting threshold or a nonlinear periodic solution with shoulder, exit and order responding.
