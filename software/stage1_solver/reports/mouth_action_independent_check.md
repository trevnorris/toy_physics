For exit radius $b$, write $E=E_{\rm mat}+J\,\operatorname{Re}\omega_{12}$. We recovered $E'_{\rm mat}$ from differences of the archived total work after subtracting the degree-four frequency change. Both signed differences used the physical separation $b(e^h-e^{-h})$. The coarser backgrounds give 2.25639 and 1.85757. Retaining the degree-four wave instead would leave only the small signed residual of that preparation. Releasing additional material or replacing its interior order changes the inputs to both forces.

**Amplitude independent of angular polarization.** Let $T$ be any complex superposition in one toroidal degree, with $\int_{S^2}|T|^2=1$. For the displacement $\operatorname{Re}[A r f(r,w) T e^{-i\omega t}]$, the trace-free angular strain block is the real part of $A f\operatorname{sym}\nabla_{S^2}T$. Its two principal values are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign. Integration by parts on the unit sphere gives $\int |\operatorname{sym}\nabla T|^2=[\ell(\ell+1)-2]/2$. At each supported meridional point the peak positive principal strain therefore exceeds or equals the angular and phase RMS bound $|Af|\sqrt{[\ell(\ell+1)-2]/(32\pi)}$. The corresponding speed bound is $|A f|r\operatorname{Re}\omega/\sqrt{8\pi}$. We set $A^2E_{\rm unit}=J_{\rm eq}\operatorname{Re}\omega$ using the physical symmetric-strain stored energy inside the same outgoing port. This is an instantaneous oscillatory-envelope check on the leaky resonance.

Sampling only the registered collar on the finer wave mesh gives positive principal strain bounds **38.06 and 1.292** and speed bounds **18.33 and 1.133** in flat-brane shear-speed units. The strain witnesses have cell-mean crystalline order 0.4484 and 0.5016 respectively. At entrance 2 the strain bound remains 1.271 in cells with order at least 0.9. The native wave mesh gives strain bounds 36.36 and 1.285. Restricting the angular polarization cannot make either threshold perturbative. An incremental wave calculation supplies no physical tear or melting threshold. Finite volume-preserving torsional displacements exist kinematically. Their coupled nonlinear stresses and order response would have to be checked before inferring destruction or persistent support.

**Linear instability.** The frozen toroidal action has positive inertia wherever shear is supported, meridional derivative energy with tensor $FF^{\mathsf T}$, and a nonnegative angular stiffness proportional to $\ell(\ell+1)-2$. The flat exterior is passive. This linear problem has no rotating reservoir or term that transfers background carrier energy to a growing torsional wave. A shape that has nonzero material virtual work is also an unsteady reference for a full coupled stability analysis. The specified stationary toroidal equation provides no selection of angular degree or stored action. **Order response.** The torsional contribution to the energy is quadratic in displacement. The first-order scalar order response to this pure wave vanishes on the frozen symmetric preparation. At finite action, the signed single-site energy differences reproduce the sum of the material and wave derivatives. At radius 1 the wave favors increasing order at the largest available absolute wave-work density. At radius 2 it favors decreasing order at that test site, while the material contribution there has the opposite sign. Along the previously calibrated collective order displacement, the maximum paired difference between the threshold and wave-off minima is +0.04366 and +0.00708 on the fine material grid. The coarse readings are +0.04347 and +0.01993. The radius-2 trend gives a sign and a scale with appreciable grid dependence. The actual time-dependent peak over independently moving sites is undetermined. We verified both snapshot hashes, all four action hashes, the two-sided slopes and the common material-coordinate change from the recorded logarithmic order coordinates.
