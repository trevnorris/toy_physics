# Which side of a draining mouth captures more material?

**More material flows past the far side of the mouth, but in the sampled pole preparations the companion-facing side captures slightly more when the closer surface retains its mean attraction.** Motion of the surface sites can absorb enough energy to bind a passing constituent. The accumulated time those constituents spend attached on each side remains unresolved. That time determines whether this mechanism contributes to attraction.

We held one mouth of radius 1 at intake 0.25. At the depth where the brane has lost half its shear order, the carrier passes the wall at a mean angle of only 0.447° from the tangent. We sampled the inward current within 0.01 radii of the wall. We placed a cold sheet of mobile sites at two possible distances behind the carrier boundary and added Gaussian fluctuations to the incident normal velocity at fixed axial flow. Both the microscopic spacing from the boundary and the velocity distribution are assumptions. The receiving geometry includes the fourth coordinate. The microscopic collision resolves two tangents and one normal on a local patch held at its sampled order. At the closer placement with mean attraction retained, we integrated over launch distance for 32 separately sampled combinations of incident speed, site position and approach direction. The undriven early-capture fraction was 3.724%. At opposite poles the fractional change in captured supply per unit incident speed was **+0.41013** at drive 0.002 and **+0.41158** at half strength. All four eight-sample groups gave a positive sign. Holding the capture intervals fixed gave **−0.10758**. On one matched group, halving the timestep and enlarging the sheet preserved the positive sign. Varying launch velocities alone reproduced it. A previous 32.79% threshold assumed a constant chance to bind per unit time and so cannot be applied to these collisions. **Implication for free fall.** For dilute steady arrivals that stay at the same depth and angle, the resident count equals the capture rate times the mean residence time of captured arrivals. We use the pole contrast as the coefficient of a linear cosine variation around the mouth. Equal residence times would make this calibrated order-force contribution point away from the companion. For it to point toward the companion, the fractional residence response per unit speed must be more negative than −0.4293 at full drive (−0.4307 at half drive), using the linear local calibration. The signed difference in bound time inside the slice changed sign or magnitude under numerical refinement. Some tags remained bound when observation ended. The stationary contribution and the total mouth acceleration are open. The distinguishing measurement is the time and position of attachment under opposite flows at a physically located surface, including where captured material exits or releases.
