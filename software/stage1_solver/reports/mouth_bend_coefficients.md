# Can a draining mouth resolve two trapped-wave excitations?

**The same intake, radiation loss and radial inertia calibration permit either one peak or two.** The two answers come from placing the same amount of moving wall material at different depths. Material near the maximum of the bend has further to travel on each cycle and contributes more inertia. We used the previously measured mass intake **0.0373999726942** throughout. All quantities are in model units. **What the equations fix.** Each circular cross section translates sideways by \(b f(w)\), where \(f=\sin^2(\pi w/H)\), \(H=1.0459197524\) and \(b\) is the peak displacement. Following the earlier trapped-wave coupling, both halves of the symmetric scalar well bend. One arm drains and radiates carrier waves. Translation preserves section volume and end-cap area. The archived side tension gives stiffness **0.212330176148 per arm**, or **0.424660352295** for the symmetric bend. At fixed supporting-wave action, allowing its spatial profile to relax gives **0.0724130916555** more static stiffness. These account for **0.49707** before any separate wall elasticity or steady carrier contact load. This scalar preparation has an occupied supporting wave and an initially empty dipole at the archived reference length. **Decisive input.** On the same draining preparation, prescribe this bend and determine its total in-phase holding force per displacement at zero and two low frequencies. Remove the calculated carrier and supporting-wave reactions at those frequencies. The intercept gives the remaining static wall stiffness and the slope against frequency squared gives minus its modal mass. The second nonzero frequency tests the assumed inertial law. A material action yielding the same force independently would serve as well. This bend has fixed endpoints. Free translation of a mouth for 0PN gravity requires a different force measurement.
