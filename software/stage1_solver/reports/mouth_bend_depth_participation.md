**Material hypothesis.** A stronger local wave pressure could keep more of the ordered material moving normally with that part of the wall. We assumed proportionality to the prepared, cycle-averaged pressure, with the attached material inventory fixed during the weak probe. The shape of the pressure profile follows from differentiating the scalar wave energy at fixed action under a small local radial dilation. The archived radial mass of **59.3830741481** supplies the overall calibration under the assumption that it all belongs to the normally moving sidewall. The calculation uses both mirrored halves of the wave well, one draining arm, and the earlier side tension and field reactions. For a local wall inertia density \(\mu(w)\), including the wall area per solid angle, the radial calibration weights all depths equally. The sideways bend weights them by \(f^2=\sin^4(\pi w/H)\), with an angular factor of one third. Thus \(M_{\rm bend,wall}=(M_{\rm radial}/3)\langle f^2\rangle_\mu\). The pressure profile gives a weighted mean of **0.394164662562**. With the measured radiation its predicted linewidth is **0.00633157909942**, compared with a transition spacing of **0.00406283876392**. Both numerical resolutions give one peak. The prior readout assumes equal areas, a 1:3 width ratio, a common shift and no extra dephasing. [Readout and force assumptions](mouth_bend_holding_force.md)

**A restriction beyond this pressure law.** Any nonnegative attached-mass profile that only decreases from the entrance along each arm has \(M_{\rm bend,wall}\le 9.74289764697\) at this radial calibration. Such profiles are positive mixtures of layers extending from the entrance to a depth \(tH\). The largest average \(t^{-1}\int_0^t\sin^4(\pi x)dx\) occurs at \(t=0.683958062171\), and that limiting profile gives one peak. The previously resolved high-inertia branch starts at **13.0047915856**, requiring a weighted mean above **0.656994864555**. The measured frequency band does not classify every smaller mass.
