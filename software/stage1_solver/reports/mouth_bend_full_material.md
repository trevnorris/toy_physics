# Full-depth wall momentum in the draining bend

**The existing wall action fixes the weighting. It leaves the local inertia of the moving material as a constitutive input.** At the same inward mass rate **0.0373999726942**, a homogeneous inertial order front gives a weighted wall momentum of **\(1.48458\times10^{-6}\)** at peak bend speed \(2\times10^{-7}\), or bend inertia **7.42288** in model units. Both spectral grids give **one peak**. This answers the question for a specified material hypothesis. [Guarded calculations and review](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_bend_full_material.json)

**What can be derived.** Let \(u=w/H\) on each half, \(f(u)=\sin^2(\pi u)\), and let the local normal displacement be \(b f(u)\cos\theta\). The wall action yields normal momentum equal to the local normal velocity times the wall’s effective mass density. If \(dM\) is the radial mass in a full angular slice, the sideways projection is \(dP_x=\dot b\,f\,dM/3\). Summing both halves and weighting by the prescribed shape gives

\[
P_b=\int f\,dP_x,\qquad M_b=P_b/\dot b=\tfrac13\int f^2\,dM .
\]

The static order equation fixes the tension of an equilibrium front. Its thickness, the carrier current and the wall shape supply no independent coefficient for motion of that front on the drain. The earlier soft-wall inertia calculation concerns hydrostatic fluid in a breathing mode. The carrier and supporting-wave reactions in this bend are already included in its spectrum. **Material hypothesis tested.** Suppose the sidewall is a thin, translating equilibrium order profile \(\chi(n-\eta)\), where \(n\) is normal distance. Give it reversible kinetic density \(I_B(\partial_t\chi)^2/2\) relative to the unperturbed material frame. For constant coefficients through the front, its mass per area is \(\Sigma_m=I_B\int(\chi')^2dn\). The static order energy gives \(\sigma_\chi=\kappa_B\int(\chi')^2dn\), hence \(\Sigma_m=\sigma_\chi/c_\chi^2\), where \(c_\chi^2=\kappa_B/I_B\). For the trial, the front supplies the archived side tension and all the assigned radial mass **59.38307**. Both halves have identical material and area per depth. The trial treats the front as a separate wall inventory moving relative to a fixed reference, with no extra loss or dispersion. If the kinetic term uses the flowing carrier’s material derivative, its normal speed is relative to the carrier and this calibration must be rederived. The fit gives \(c_\chi=0.06896\) in model units. For uniform properties, \(\int_0^1 f^2du=3/8\), so \(M_b=M_{\rm radial}/8\). The unweighted sideways momentum per unit peak speed is larger, \(M_{\rm radial}/6\). **How different would the middle have to be?** Holding the radial mass, stiffness and previously measured field reactions fixed, the fine-grid two-peak transition occurs at \(M_b=13.00479\) on the tested branch. Even the most favorable placement needs a ratio of largest to smallest wall mass per depth of **5.45779** to reach that transition. The optimal distribution puts the larger density between **0.35666 and 0.64334** of each half-depth and the smaller density elsewhere. To see the bound, set \(t=3M_{\rm fold}/M_{\rm radial}\). The fold requires \(\int(f^2-t)dM=0\). At fixed density contrast this integral has its most favorable sign when the density is highest everywhere \(f^2>t\) and lowest elsewhere. Any other placement requires at least as much contrast. The coarser spectral grid gives **5.43772**. A smooth distribution can approach the sharp limit. The bound concerns the prior equal-area, 1:3-width readout with a common shift and zero additional dephasing. **An independent material test.** At uniform tension the bound corresponds to at least a **2.33619** ratio of fastest to slowest intrinsic wall disturbance speed. A weak ripple along the same material interface, with fluid and trapped-wave loading accounted for, could measure this speed without using the two peaks as calibration. With depth-resolved intrinsic speed and tension, integrate \(\sigma_\chi f^2/c_\chi^2\) with the wall area and angular factor above. Using the ripple as a test requires a locally resolved, weakly dispersive interface mode on this preparation. The light speed and the longitudinal carrier speed do not supply this datum. Free translation at 0PN remains a separate open measurement.
