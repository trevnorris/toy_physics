# Holding a bend at fixed intake

**The field reactions are available at all three settings. The solver supplies no independent total holding force for this bend.** Prescribing the wall trajectory makes the carrier and supporting wave respond identically for different wall masses. An external constraint on the present field equations can recover their reaction. To include the wall’s own restoring force and momentum it needs the wall action being tested. The peak sideways displacement was \(b=10^{-5}\) in the same \(\sin^2\) bend. Both halves of the fixed-action scalar well followed it and one arm drained. The target inward mass current was **0.0373999726942**. The primary run returned 0.0373999726942 at the inlet and 0.0373999726908 through the neck and into the bulk. The angular perturbation preserves total intake to first order. The entries are in-phase external forces needed to counter the two modeled field reactions, in model units. We average the coherent support over its fast oscillation and hold all other wall harmonics fixed.

The carrier column is the induced reaction of the moving reflecting boundary. Static direct contact or pinning beyond the modeled terms would enter the residual stiffness. The calculation uses 48 cells per radius for the carrier and a wave grid spacing of 0.015. The carrier’s emitted work agrees with the sum of the brane and bulk fluxes at both drive frequencies. Increasing the far boundary from 36 to 48 radii changed the carrier’s in-phase force per displacement by less than \(3\times10^{-10}\) at each frequency. An independent local calculation checked the carrier pressure at the wall, the translated-well force and the three-reading inversion. **What an independent force reading would tell us.** Let \(R(\omega)\) be the in-phase total holding force minus the last column. For a local elastic and inertial wall, \(K=R(0)/b\) and \(M=[R(0)-R(\omega)]/(b\omega^2)\). The residual drop at 0.02 must be four times the drop at 0.01. This third reading tests the law over the measured band. Inferring peak survival also requires the wall law to hold near the two spectral drive frequencies, 0.45661 and 3.93100, with the assumed losses.

As a control, the two prior wall profiles were each supplied to the force equation. Both use the surface-tension stiffness **0.424660352295** and give a static total of **4.97038084524 × 10⁻⁶**. At 0.02 the uniform wall predicts **4.92495295591 × 10⁻⁶** and the centrally weighted wall **4.89175155662 × 10⁻⁶**. Each supplied mass is recovered from the first two values and predicts the third. Both share the field readings above. The older driven solver also supplies its mass and stiffness explicitly, and the separate quadrupole operator uses a surrogate drive. **Do both peaks survive?** On the previously tested stiffness and mass branch, the one-to-two-peak boundary corresponds to a residual force drop from static to 0.02 of **5.20191663424 × 10⁻⁸**. The uniform profile predicts **2.9691537074 × 10⁻⁸** and one peak. The centrally weighted profile predicts **6.2892936358 × 10⁻⁸** and two. This applies to the prior equal-area selective readout, with widths in a 1:3 ratio and no extra dephasing. To select the physical result we need an independent generalized holding force for this displacement or a material law for the wall’s elastic energy and the momentum that follows it at each depth. If the stiffness differs from the tension value, the peak threshold must be recalculated. This fixed-end bend does not calibrate free translation at 0PN.
