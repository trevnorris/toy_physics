# Can the brane’s shear keep a draining mouth aligned?

**Yes for one tested preparation.** A mouth whose flare opens later along the fourth direction can straighten both sideways bends using the existing brane shear energy and a finite attachment to the wall. The original flare still amplifies the deeper bend at the same shear strength. Both counting openings have radius 1 and inward intake 0.25. Each calculation concerns one held, wave-empty throat. We tested the material energy \(\mu_0\chi|\mathrm{curl}_4 u|^2/2\) about a stress-free reference. The material can rearrange in all four spatial directions. The two prescribed sideways offsets bend the entrance and the deeper flare independently. We let the adjacent reservoir surfaces slip, held the far reservoirs fixed, and used the carrier reaction calculated separately for each geometry. The static ordered-material modulus \(\mu_0=1\) is a trial value in carrier units, also used for the earlier wave runs. Optical wave speed fixes a ratio of modulus to inertia. Separate displacement and stress trials bound the response after the material relaxes. If every section of the original deep flare is forced to move rigidly, its deeper shear stiffness is 0.05225. Allowing rearrangement gives 0.004774–0.004856 against an opposing flow stiffness of 0.008757. Independent trial fields verified the signs for both deep preparations. **Attachment matters.** Following the wall only in its normal direction admits an exactly curl-free displacement. As an additional hypothesis, we attached the ordered material with a tangential spring per wall area of strength \(k\chi\). On the later-opening deep preparation, \(k=1\) sufficed for both bends. At \(k=0.01\) an amplifying direction survived. The bounds at \(k=0.1\) straddled zero. The units of \(k\) are four-dimensional modulus per length.
