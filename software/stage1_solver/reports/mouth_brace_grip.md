# What happens to grip when the brane releases its normal support?

**A passing patch retained ample sideways grip at 1% shear order. It also retained most of its resistance to shear after its support was completely removed.** Gradually releasing normal support therefore fails the combined lining and receiving-bulk test in the material and time window studied.

We used the radius-one mouth’s measured flow and order history at intake 0.25 to carry a neutral patch through conversion. The patch had two periodic tangent directions and one free normal direction. Sites retained their original labels for the force probe and moved under the existing separation-dependent pair energies. We tested both previously specified microscopic laws, which agree on the flat ordered lattice. The new hypothesis was that order controls only the outward preload and elastic ties to the two original planes. The ties’ normal stiffness was set equal to the stretching stiffness of one neutral bond, 98 in patch units. That identification and unit mobility per carrier time are trial material assumptions. The lateral force and its decay were outputs. At depth 1.42748 the two material groups still occupied disjoint height ranges. Opposite and half-size displacements gave consistent force derivatives. The local values exceed the earlier 18.91% and 21.93% benchmarks, which applied to particular depth profiles in a fixed-gap coating. The new gaps, anisotropy and material preparation preclude transferring that coating’s force law directly to the moving mouth.

**Receiving-bulk check.** Starting from the same warm states at depth 2, we removed the brace entirely, preserved temperature and area, stretched the whole periodic cell slightly in one tangent direction and compressed it in the other. The sites could rearrange throughout the hold. The stress difference retained **90.53–96.45%** of its initial incremental response when averaged over the last quarter of 48.8817 model time units, the remaining transit to the outlet. Holding only the original labels apart played no role in this test. The result measures a finite warm response in a closed patch. The six separately cooled states also had positive incremental fixed-cell shear stiffness. One of those states carried appreciable pre-existing shear stress. Longer-time relaxation and exchange with an open reservoir remain untested. [Same-temperature shear test](../tools/mouth_brace_bulk_shear.py)

**Control and decision.** The balanced outward pressure alone left a normal ripple unstable even at full order. With the elastic ties removed from the start the two layers separated in both the converting and full-pressure controls. This cannot identify a conversion-induced loss of grip. Its cold endpoint was unresolved. The elastically braced runs included checks of patch size, force range, time step, carrier mesh and material mobility. A local independent reviewer checked the normal instability, signed tractions and warm passage and reproduced the warm bulk hold for the anisotropic seed-319 case. For 0PN we need a material that transmits the lip load during passage and can subsequently shed shear stress in the bulk. The next discriminator is a **bulk-only shear hold** on a sample that can exchange constituents with its surroundings at independently specified density, temperature and normal pressure. Track neighbor replacement and residual stress together. A material that relaxes in that test can then be taken back through the mouth to measure how long its grip survives.
