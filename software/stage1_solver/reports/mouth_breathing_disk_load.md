**A disk carrying the measured planar normal traction leaves the lowest breathing trial outward at both radii.** In this approximation the radius-2 mode loses 0.00885% of its energy per cycle and needs peak principal strain 0.259 at the wall to balance the closing load. The radius-1 mode needs peak principal strain 0.850 at the wall and 1.096 in the outer registered material. No fixed failure strain has been measured. **What is coupled.** An axial terminal displacement translates a planar supersolid–superfluid half-order surface. The two open half-spaces carry sound and the converting layer exerts the measured complex force $k(\omega,j)$ per unit area and unit displacement on its driver. At each physical exit $b$, the curved-mouth stationary solve supplies its own mean normal current $j=D/(4\pi b^3/3)$. We use the wave-empty lattice Hessian and inertia, and apply the normal reaction uniformly over the terminal three-ball. For generalized fractional depth $q_0$ the terminal displacement is $Lq_0$. The external dynamic stiffness is

$$\mathsf D=\mathsf H-\omega^2\mathsf M+(4\pi b^3/3)L^2k(\omega,j)\,e_0 e_0^T.$$

A freely translated homogeneous plane has zero static restoring stiffness. Radius 2 uses all three lattice coordinates in the determinant. The missing off-center mixed curvatures at radius 1 restrict that solve to the tracked lowest-mode direction within the three-coordinate space. The static applied loads that hold the underlying wave-empty shapes remain fixed while the mode oscillates. An actual curved cap can diffract sound, exchange a spatially varying relative current, and receive waves emitted elsewhere on the side walls or in the feeding slab. The plane approximation omits these paths. **Force, energy and amplitude.** We continued the outgoing pole about its real-axis resonance using the measured complex traction at frequencies displaced by ±0.8% and ±0.4%. The continued linewidth agrees with the independent power balance using dispersive modal energy $E=-\omega q^2 v^T(\partial_\omega\operatorname{Re}\mathsf D)v/4$ for a real oscillation amplitude $q$ and unit lattice-mass vector $v$. Signs were checked with ±1.2% and ±0.6% physical exit changes. The double-step loaded forces per unit action were +0.53702 and +1.07081, giving threshold actions 0.64110 and 0.36056. Radius 1 retains substantial step sensitivity. The table uses the smaller step, the same empty-background closing load and the dispersive energy to normalize displacement. The outer registered principal strain also reaches 0.37482 at radius 2. These strains are linear extrapolations to the balancing action.

**What moves in the order field.** At fixed laboratory position a translating front gives a large apparent crystalline-order change $-X\chi'_0$. The linearized profile change in its own moving coordinate is $\delta\chi+X\chi'_0$. A second boundary-value solve with adaptive collocation recovered the dissipative traction to within $6\times10^{-10}$ relative and measured the translated-profile peaks in the table at both resolutions. This is a readout of the local planar layer. It leaves order in the rest of the strained mouth and nonlinear evolution undetermined.
