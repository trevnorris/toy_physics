**Power normalization.** In unit-density material write the forced continuity and phase equations as $\dot{\delta n}+\nabla^2\theta=s$ and $\dot\theta=-(1-\nabla^2/4)\delta n$. For $\delta n,\theta,s\propto e^{-i\omega t}$, the retarded denominator is $k^2+k^4/4-(\omega+i0)^2$. Integrating the work $\langle\int (1-\nabla^2/4)\delta n\,s\,d^4x\rangle$ over the propagating shell yields

$$P=\frac{k^3(1+k^2/4)}{32\pi v_g}\left\langle|\widetilde s(k\hat n)|^2\right\rangle_{S^3},\qquad v_g=\partial_k\sqrt{k^2+k^4/4}.$$

The far-field outgoing Green function gives the same coefficient from its group-energy flux. For side radius $r(w)$ the surface source per unit modal speed integrates $4\pi r^2(\delta r-r'\delta w)dw$, multiplied by $j_0(k_\parallel r)e^{-ik_w w}$. For the terminal three-ball it integrates its swept volume times $3j_1(k_\parallel b)/(k_\parallel b)e^{-ik_w L}$. The finite differences of the Fourier transform of the displaced four-volume independently recover the summed source at each sampled angle. Doubling the angular and meridional quadratures checked all six shape trials. The imposed sources introduce no change in time-averaged intake. **Physical inference.** An available sound channel and a nonzero geometric volume change suffice to radiate for the specified sources. Determining the actual time-dependent normal mass current and stress on both sides of the converting terminal surface is necessary to select a physical source and calculate a ringdown.
