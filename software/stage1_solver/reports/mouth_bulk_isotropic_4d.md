# Is loss of a preferred bond direction enough to make the bulk release shear?

**The tested material still exerted a restoring force when sheared after its pair force became identical in every direction.** All three four-dimensional preparations resisted compression and every independent shear at zero applied pressure. The sample with the least positional order had the weakest shear response.

**Four-dimensional bulk.** We used 128 mobile sites in a periodic cell. Each pair interacted through the neutral brane’s within-sheet Morse well in all four directions. We started from a layered D4 crystal and two independent random arrangements. At fixed four-volume, the rigid cube trapped both random preparations in states with at least one downhill change of shape. Releasing the cell shape lowered the energy and restored positive stiffness in all nine independent shears. We then released the volume as well, permitting each sample to select its density at zero applied pressure. These are cold, local energy minima. **Warm comparison in the original mouth-patch geometry.** At temperature 0.008 we switched the pair law as the two patches first met. After one transit for contact formation, the full-depth isotropic sample retained 85.75% of its initial shear response over the final quarter of the next transit. A weaker isotropic well matched to the same starting energy retained 85.66%. The original directional control retained 87.30%. All three retained the same 336 neighbor pairs at each hold reading and resisted projected compression. This is the original 64-site sample with two periodic tangents and a free normal. Its lateral pressures differed between laws. [Matched warm patch](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_bulk_isotropic_patch.json)

**Warm four-dimensional bulk.** At density 2 and temperature 0.008, the crystal retained 99.87% of its initial shear response and 99.72% of its compression response over the final quarter of one transit. In the fixed cube, random-start sample 41 retained 24.72% of the tested shear and 98.94% of its compression over the same interval. The finer step changed its late shear slope from 27.44706 to 27.44652. Halving the strain at the finer step gave 27.44587. In the undriven run at each step size, all 1,334 initial neighbor pairs were present at every saved reading, with up to 44 additional pairs. Cooling each endpoint returned it to the original local minimum. This comparison covers one thermal history, one tested shear direction and one compression step size. Two earlier runs failed the time-step check before integration and yielded no force reading. An independent energy calculation checked the signed stresses at frames 0, 24 and 48 of each driven hold and reproduced the final-quarter averages from the recorded stresses. [Warm histories and verification](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_bulk_isotropic_4d_warm.json)

**Consequence for 0PN.** Isotropy alone supplies no rule for when constituents exchange neighbors or how long the converted material remembers a shear. At the same external pressure, the next test is to vary thermal energy relative to the bond depth and look for a dense phase whose neighbors exchange and whose shear decays within a transit. Measure its compression and the energy required to reach it independently of the desired mouth acceleration. The existing bare order potential has equal-energy endpoints and supplies no specified heat of conversion. Once a candidate passes that test, its force history during passage can be used to predict the load delivered from the lip to the flare. Free fall and optical shear confinement remain open.
