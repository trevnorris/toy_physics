# How precisely must the bulk force be known to predict free fall?

**The trial mouths set a force scale at the flare. Applying that scale to a homogeneous bulk measurement requires knowing how the passing material attaches to the moving wall.** With entrance and middle held, the two radius-one trial mouths required 0.2159234 and 0.3676533 units of holding force per unit sideways flare displacement. A symmetric measurement error smaller than 0.07586493 about either prediction would separate these two trial readings in this fixture. The corresponding whole-mouth acceleration still depends on support through depth and on the material and wave that move. [Slow push and flare-force predictions](mouth_occupied_slow_push.md)

**Force during a bulk shear.** We used all 49 saved times from the matched positive, negative and undriven branches of each heating history. In the hottest material at 1.024, the full- and half-strain slopes differed by less than 0.006 immediately after the displacement. Their first-quarter averages already differed by 14.14 to 28.57 in magnitude. In the final quarter the deviations from the undriven stress were also asymmetric under reversal. One history had an odd stress slope of 65.07 and an even stress increment, divided by the strain amplitude, of 69.99. Those two quantities probe force reversal along that history. The three thermal histories provide three independent observations of this preparation. At 0.008 all three final-quarter full and half slopes differed by less than 0.063. [Signed force readout and independent review](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_bulk_shear_precision.json)

**Force across a cut in the same material.** At the beginning and end of one undriven transit, we gave the two halves of the periodic sample opposite virtual displacements. A positive slope means an external force was required to hold the fresh displacement. Each of the three hot samples resisted a step at the end. The inferred force depends on which constituents the test moves. The table gives the slopes per unit displacement, normalized by the combined three-area of the two periodic faces at the endpoint.

Once sites exchange sides, the original populations interpenetrate and their normalization by planar area is a reference convention. These measurements test immediate response. How much of a force survives when the displacement is *held* is a distinct observable. **Connection to the flare.** Reexamining two earlier wall models with identical neutral material, aligned normal binding and trial drag gives coating-only holding slopes of 3.40668 and 7.06735 after one whole-flare transit. A smooth wall permits lateral slip. A wall with binding sites in register with the adjacent material transmits additional tangential force. Both wall models can share the same isolated bulk measurement. The bulk hold lasts 48.88171 model time units. The transport fixture spans a different depth interval, with transit 77.31274. These coating slopes and times establish sensitivity to the attachment assumptions. Neither fixes a physical coupling. [Renewing-wall force calculation](mouth_replacing_flare_slip.md)

**One-transit hold.** In each of four preparations we held the relative sideways displacement of the original two populations at 0, ±0.0002 and ±0.0001 bond lengths for 48.88171 model time units. The four-dimensional cell kept its starting volume. All five runs within one preparation received identical thermal increments. The zero-offset control imposed the same centroid constraint as the signed holds. All 20 trajectories, the signed reduction and the independent force calculation passed their guards. Each signed slope is the positive-minus-negative traction difference divided by the displacement difference. The first two columns average every integration step of the final quarter. The last two use its 12 saved frame ends. The spatial reading counts interparticle forces across two planes following the sample’s overall translation. All slopes are normalized by the two faces’ combined three-area. The independent checker reconstructed the tagged and spatial pair forces from the saved coordinates and pair energy at both endpoints of every arm. It checked the centroid separations throughout the saved histories and reproduced the late signed slopes, including cancellation of the shared thermal contribution.

The single cold control and hot history 1 gave consistent holding forces at both amplitudes. In hot history 1 the full-step holding slope started at 114.516 and averaged 58.465 in the final quarter. At matched saved times, moving material through the planes made the spatial reading differ from the tagged reading. Hot histories 2 and 3 failed the amplitude check. In history 3 the full-step slopes measured separately from the matched zero were +61.12 and −10068.68 for the positive and negative steps, respectively, after division by their signed displacements. A material stiffness or a zero-force limit cannot be assigned from those two histories.

**Roadmap decision.** Phase 1 supplied a material measurement. Step 2 still needs the tangential force on a physical feature of the ordered mouth wall as material is replaced. At the reference intake of 0.25, identify a feature that follows the wall independently of the passing sites. Keep the local shear order, normal load and wave preparation matched. Give the feature opposite small sideways displacements, repeat at half strength and matched slip speeds, and read the reaction on that feature against a zero-step control as fresh sites arrive. Track their locations and velocities relative to it. The offset and speed dependence distinguishes binding to particular sites from sliding drag. The resulting traction could then be integrated over the flare and tested against an independent push on the same mouth. The whole-mouth release and unequal-mouth comparison remain open.
