# Carrier force, release and radius checks

## Geometry and observable

The model has three brane coordinates and a fourth spatial coordinate \(w\). On the single-mouth chart, \(r\) is the radial distance in those three brane coordinates. The lateral response is proportional to \(Y=x/r\). Integrating this dipole over its two angular directions uses \(\int Y^2 d\Omega=4\pi/3\). This reduction is exact at first order about the spherical baseline. The geometry includes a finite feeding slab, rounded lip, narrow section, flared conduit and receiving reservoir. The same complex carrier crosses the opening smoothly. The reflecting walls have zero normal current in their own frame. The distant baths set the carrier phases and densities. The translating finite container defines the towing preparation. The force acts on whichever portions of the wall mechanically move with the center. Other physical recipients, trapped modes, conversion stresses, extra wall inertia and causal selection of the background flow remain independent questions. For density \(n=|f|^2\), the stationary equation and energy density are

\[
-\tfrac12\Delta f+W'(n)f=0,\qquad W'(n)=(n^4-1)/4,\qquad
\mathcal E=\tfrac12|\nabla f|^2+(n^5-5n+4)/20.
\]

A unit incident stream gives the boundary variation \(i r fY\) on the chosen remote bath. The real-linear derivative of the equation acts on the radial dipole amplitude, adding \(+1/r^2\) to its scalar kinetic operator. The intake stays fixed at first order by angular symmetry. Integrate the linear variation of \(\Pi_{ij}=\mathrm{Re}(\partial_i f^*\partial_j f)-\delta_{ij}\mathcal E\) with the outward-from-fluid normal. A reflecting surface receives \(-\delta\mathcal E\,n_x\) per unit area. A remote cut also receives the normal-gradient terms, including the two angular derivatives. This stress is fixed by virtual displacement of the specified reflecting walls. The alternative density-gradient stress differs by a divergence-free term. When subdividing the wall, its segment endpoints can contribute. [Dipole solver and surface integrals](../tools/mouth_carrier_lateral_response.py)

Two independent compact integration windows give the total force from smooth interior stresses and the change in stored momentum. They avoid differentiating the carrier at the sharp flare exit. At mesh 48, the finite standard domain gave towing coefficients of \(+6.91\times10^{-6}\) and \(+7.05\times10^{-6}\) from the two windows. Direct wall integration gave \(-5.21\times10^{-4}\). In the enlarged domain of lateral radius 20 the windows straddled zero. Window location, mesh and outer boundary position limit what can be said about the residual. The coherent-carrier result revises the earlier argument that *adding* a bulk force of \(+0.0708542V\) could cancel contact drag. A calculation with both contacts and carrier must include their reciprocal forces in one momentum balance.

## Acceleration and free-center check

For imposed speed \(V\) and acceleration \(a\), the co-moving gauge has inertial potential \(+a x\). If \(h_VY\) is the unit towing field and \(L\) the linearized stationary operator, the quasistatic acceleration amplitude obeys \(L h_a=i h_V-r f\), with zero acceleration perturbation at both far baths. The isotropic stress uses \(nr+\mathrm{Im}(f^*h_V)\) along with the static energy variation. A localized momentum balance includes the rate of change of the fluid momentum. The actuator coefficient is minus the material force divided by imposed acceleration. At zero intake and mesh 26, direct wall work gave 12.28573982 and the independently integrated kinetic energy norm gave 12.28573988. At intake 0.25 on that mesh the interior estimate was 12.25758615. Driving at 0.04 and 0.02 gave 12.25995864 and 12.25817908. Frequencies are angular frequencies in model time. [Imposed acceleration and harmonic check](../tools/mouth_carrier_lateral_inertia.py)

For a separate calculation, keep the laboratory phase in translated coordinates and solve simultaneously for the two real carrier components and the center velocity. The moving wall has \(\partial_n\delta\psi=iV n_x f\). A final equation sets the force on the center plus the applied push to zero. With the \(e^{i\omega t}\) convention, \(a=i\omega V\). This solve uses neither the held-force coefficient nor the inferred mass as an input. Its brane-flow drive is a remote phase gradient. A time-dependent gradient can drive pressure even at zero intake, which produces a quadrature response. The observed slow response is in a frequency window where inertial force dominates the unresolved towing residual. Long-time uniform free drift and release of two simulated physical centers remain open. [Free-center equations](../tools/mouth_carrier_free_center.py)

## Two-mouth energy

At weak phase bias let \(g_i\) be the single-mouth conductance. The trial resistance matrix has diagonal \(1/g_i\) and common off-diagonal \(S(d)\). The latter averages the feed and receiver surface Green functions over two nonoverlapping round profiles. A unit normal point current on the receiving plane gives \(G_b(s)=1/(2\pi^2 s^2)\). Reflecting images separated by twice the feed thickness \(t\) give \(G_f(s)=\coth[\pi s/(2t)]/(4\pi t s)\). The paired throughputs satisfy \(\mathcal R D=\Delta\theta\,\mathbf1\) at common fixed distant phases. The stationary energy is \(E_*=\tfrac12\Delta\theta\,\mathbf1^T D\). Thus the force on either rigid shape toward the other is

\[
\partial_d E_*=-D_1 D_2 S'(d).
\]

Finite differences of the energy check the sign and normalization at each separation, allowing both intakes to change. For disjoint radial profiles the slab zero mode gives \(D_1D_2/(4\pi t d^2)\) in unit-density material. This power of distance follows from the slab's long-distance phase equation. It is independent of the radial footprint in this approximation. The receiver adds a leading \(d^{-3}\) force. The pair test uses isolated diagonals calibrated in a finite box with infinite-domain mutual kernels. The trials vary the two prescribed radial profiles and quadrature. A direct coupled two-mouth calculation would also allow angular redistribution, mutual inertia and changes in each self response. Dividing the leading force by each independently measured target inertia then gives conditional accelerations. Source and target both require a common physical intake-to-inertia ratio for universal Newtonian gravity. [Two-port calculation](../tools/mouth_carrier_pair_energy.py)

## Does radius-independent acceleration follow?

The numerical experiment holds the three-dimensional far density, unit slab thickness, material coefficients and phase bias 0.4510084282857874 fixed. In the first family, all linear dimensions of the throat scale with radius \(R\). In the second, its lateral coordinates scale with \(R\) while the axial depth and longitudinal flare profile stay on a fixed physical length scale. This stretches the rounded lip and changes its physical slope. The model does not select one geometry. The common imposed phase difference is an experimental operating condition. Intakes were measured from the two bath currents. In the physical-coordinate check they also agreed with the derivative of the stationary action with respect to bath phase. [Throat families](../tools/mouth_carrier_radius_family.py) · [Finite-bias check](../tools/mouth_carrier_matched_bias.py)

In the wider finite box, \(C/M\) at \(R=1,4\) was 0.02038899 and 0.01837396 in the second window. Independently, the small-bias conductance divided by zero-drain inertia gave 0.04565086 and 0.04082360, respectively, with the finer mesh at radius 4. The latter measures the derivative of intake with bias, divided by inertia. At radius 0.25 only the weak-bias result passed the checks. The finite-bias calculation there failed the energy-derivative comparison. In a still larger box of radius 40, the finite-bias dipoles failed their equation-residual check at both sizes. These runs supply no additional acceleration result. [Independent conductance and kinetic energy](../tools/mouth_carrier_weak_radius.py) · [Figure](mouth_carrier_radius_response.png)

For a slender reflecting conduit of radius \(R b(s)\), with \(w=Hs\), weak bias and nearly uniform density, continuity gives \(D\simeq(4\pi/3)R^3\Delta\theta/[H\int b^{-3}ds]\). Transverse motion of the enclosed carrier has leading mass \((4\pi/3)R^3H\int b^3ds\). The common factor \(R^3\) cancels. This explains why a common material depth is worth testing. Reservoir access and exterior inertia correct these expressions differently. The finite-radius results quantify the departure. A sufficiently slender physical throat, constant effective depth and a radius-independent bias have yet to be established in the material model.

## How much load must travel through the attachment?

Two smooth displacement profiles across depth sum to one rigid translation. Each drives a separate laboratory velocity potential \(\Phi_i\) in resting material. Their kinetic-energy products give \(M_{ij}=\int\nabla\Phi_i\cdot\nabla\Phi_j\). Independently, a weak axial current and incident stream generate the held-load vector \(f\) by virtual wall stress. Common acceleration needs an internal generalized force \(M\mathbf1\,\sum f/(\mathbf1^TM\mathbf1)-f\). For a transition over \(w=0.55\) to 1.15 the incident flow puts only 0.88% of the held force directly on the distal coordinate. The connector must transmit another 94.22% there. For the transition over 1.2–2.0 the direct fraction is 0.11% and the required transfer is 72.61%. These weights specify two different deformations. They do not locate a unique material seam. [Independent load and mass partitions](../tools/mouth_carrier_attachment_load.py)

An illustrative wall with uniform tension \(\sigma\) and no additional intrinsic inertia contributes relative stiffness
\(k=(4\pi\sigma/3)\int a(w)^2[s'(w)]^2[1+a'(w)^2]^{-3/2}\,dw\), where \(s\) interpolates the transverse displacements. Direct area changes under opposite tilts check this expression. Driving the measured held-force pattern at frequency 0.02, keeping relative excursion within a chosen 5% of common displacement takes tensions of 0.04644 and 0.009483 in the two partitions. Halving frequency quarters these thresholds. This is a conservative two-coordinate test with zero-drain fluid inertia. A physical harmonic brane drive can add pressure, drag and wall inertia. Measure lateral wall stiffness or relative motion independently on the draining shape. Tension of the surface where shear order disappears belongs to a distinct interface, unless a material attachment law identifies them.
