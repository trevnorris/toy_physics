# How much energy does the draining carrier take from a bending wall?

**A prescribed sideways bend radiates carrier waves even at zero intake. In the one-outlet model, this loss still allows two distinguishable trapped-wave excitations under the earlier readout assumptions.** We kept the previous radius and depth, drove the same angular bend, and held the stationary mass intake at 0.0373999726942. The carrier obeys its existing nonlinear Schrödinger equation linearized about that flowing state. The sidewall reflects material in its moving frame. The feed and downstream receiver in the primary test each continue into a uniform guide that carries waves away. **What this means for the wave.** With the earlier trial bend mass 12 and static stiffness 6, one draining arm and both ends transmitting, a three-frequency response calculation gave a dipole width of **0.000995379428922** against the conservative spacing **0.00406283876392**. The selective, equal-area readout gave two peaks. One arm with a reflecting feed also gave two. Two independent arms loading the same bend with reflecting feeds gave one. These counts assume one cold loss channel, a common line shift and no additional phase noise. The coherent supporting wave has its own energy exchange with the prescribed wall. At the exact combination frequencies it can drive the dipole resonantly. That stored energy was accounted for separately from carrier radiation.
