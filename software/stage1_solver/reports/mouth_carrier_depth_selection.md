# Can pressure or tension set the depth needed for 0PN?

The [radius experiment](mouth_carrier_0pn.md) found that a common physical depth along \(w\) makes the slow response approximately independent of mouth radius over a limited range. Here the depth is allowed to change. I tested two possible material costs, an excess energy \(P\) per occupied throat volume and a surface tension \(\sigma\) on its wall. Each is calibrated to make the radius-one mouth stationary at the same reference depth. Neither coefficient is supplied by the carrier phase equation.

**What is varied.** Write \(w=Hs\) and the radius of each cross-section as \(R b(s)\). A larger \(H\) stretches the lip, collar and flare along the fourth dimension. The slab thickness and the clearance from the flare exit to the receiving bath stay fixed. At weak fixed phase difference \(\Delta\theta\), the stationary carrier energy is \(E_c=g(R,H)\Delta\theta^2/2\). The excess cost is relative to the bulk material displaced by the confined portion. Uniform ambient pressure on the whole numerical bath supplies no such excess. Wall tension and an excess pressure are separate, testable additions to the model.

The carrier force on this axial deformation is \(-\partial_H E_c\). I obtained it by re-solving the carrier at four nearby depths and checked it against the difference of radial and axial gradient energies integrated through the conduit. The rigidly translated receiving bath and fixed feeding bath contribute no interior deformation work. Separate hypotheses predict stationarity when \(-\partial_H E_c=P\partial_H V\) or \(-\partial_H E_c=\sigma\partial_H A\). For a weakly driven slender throat, \(E_c\propto R^3\Delta\theta^2/H\), \(V\propto R^3H\) and \(A\propto R^2H\). A common excess volume cost can therefore select a common depth in that limit. [Axial work and shape integrals](../tools/mouth_carrier_depth_work.py)

| Radius at the reference depth | Required excess pressure / \(\Delta\theta^2\) | Required wall tension / \(\Delta\theta^2\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.5 | 0.015545 | 0.0032093 |
| 1 | 0.010096 | 0.0043732 |
| 2 | 0.0041954 | 0.0040908 |
| 4 | 0.00089504 | 0.0022235 |

On these finite shapes, neither constant cost selects the reference depth across radii. Any nonnegative mixture calibrated at radius 1 gives a weighted average of the two restoring forces at another radius. At radii 2 and 4 even the smaller restoration exceeds the computed force required to keep the same depth. This tests the entire set of nonnegative mixtures for that specific common depth. An energy minimum describes a possible passive equilibrium along the prescribed shape variation. Physical relaxation to it additionally requires dissipation or energy export. [Guarded results](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_carrier_0pn.json)

**Letting the depth move.** At radius 2, pure tension selected an axial minimum at \(H=0.92295\) on mesh 32. Its conductance per unit of resting carrier inertia was 16.90% above the reference value. At radius 4 the corresponding minimum was at \(H=0.34479\), giving 8.34 times the reference on mesh 24. Pure pressure had positive total energy slope at all nine sampled depths from 0.25 to 2.5 at both radii. This leaves unsampled minima and other deformations open. [Forward equilibrium calculation](../tools/mouth_carrier_depth_selection.py)

**An inverse test of every calibrated mixture.** On the resolved branch, independently select the depth that would give the reference weak-intake conductance per resting carrier mass. This is a requirement check, not a physical depth prediction. At radius 0.5 a mixture can meet both requirements. At radius 2 the required axial restoring force was 0.61115 in units of squared bias, below the minimum 0.65043 that any nonnegative calibrated mixture can give. At radius 4 it was 1.04334, below the minimum 1.99847. An independent stress integral and an energy difference agree on each required force. Thus this pair of positive material costs cannot supply universal weak intake per carrier inertia on that branch. The selected depths, reference surface tension and pressure fractions need independent material evidence before physical use. The test leaves extra carrier or wall stresses, mode energy, additional inertia and different geometric families open. [Inverse test](../tools/mouth_carrier_depth_requirement.py)

**Independent input for a trapped-wave test.** At matched radius and intake, change the axial extent slightly and measure the incremental axial force from an occupied mode against the same empty mouth. For a conservative slowly deformed mode with fixed action \(J\) and angular frequency \(\omega(H)\), that force is \(-J\,\partial_H\omega\). The occupied action and its scaling across radii must be fixed independently of gravity. The saved wave tests use prescribed shapes and excitations. They do not supply that material law.
