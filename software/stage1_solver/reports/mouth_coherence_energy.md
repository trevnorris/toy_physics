# Can a draining mouth energetically prefer one electric flux unit?

**Working result.** A candidate material law confined to a spherical collar gives an energy preference for one flux spacing at every fixed mouth radius in its scale-invariant limit. A simpler thin-surface model prefers one spacing over a finite range and fails when tested at half and twice its reference radius. The sign of the charge requires an additional handed material response. The electric spacing and the existence of the two material coherence fields are inputs. ### What is allowed to change

Compare sectors \(q=n q_0\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\), at the same prescribed radius \(R\) and matched inward intake. The two circular polarizations \(h=\pm1\) of a degree-one mouth have geometric integers \(C_h=-2hs\), with \(s=\pm1\) the mouth orientation. Their complex relative coherences \(\Psi_h\) can vary in amplitude and phase, form vortices, mix, or vanish. For a component with isolated zeros the total signed winding at its zeros is \(n-C_h\) on a closed section. An identically zero component has no defined phase winding. Neither helicity occupation nor the winning \(n\) is fixed. The curvature of the angular connections is held uniform **within** each sector. The corresponding radial exterior field contributes \(k_e n^2/R\), where \(k_e=q_0^2/(8\pi\epsilon)\). We compare equilibrium energies against the same zero-potential charge reservoir. An isolated mouth changing charge would also need a compensating current. Throughput is common to the preparations and has no dynamic equation in this test.

### First test — order confined to a surface

On the round mouth add \(K\sum_h |D_h\Psi_h|^2-\sum_h\alpha_h|\Psi_h|^2+uS^2/2\) to the energy per area, with \(S=\sum_h|\Psi_h|^2\) and \(D_h\) the covariant derivative for degree \(n-C_h\). Positive \(K,\alpha_h,u\) set the gradient cost, energy gain from coherence and saturation. Write \(\alpha=\max_h\alpha_h\), \(B=2\pi R^2\alpha^2/u\), \(x=K/(2\alpha R^2)\), \(y=k_e/(RB)\). The lowest covariant gradient eigenvalue on a round sphere is \(|n-C_h|/(2R^2)\). Bounding the quartic by the square of the mean density and minimizing that mean gives an energy floor for all profiles and mixtures,
\[
\frac{E_n}{B}\ \ge\ y n^2-\left[\max_h\!\left(\frac{\alpha_h}{\alpha}-x|n-C_h|\right)\right]_+^2 .
\]
Here \([v]_+=\max(v,0)\). For a competing upper bound, use a section with a single zero, angular amplitude proportional to \(\sqrt z[1+t(1-z)]\), \(z=(1+\cos\theta)/2\), and minimize its overall amplitude. SymPy used \(t=2/5\), Mathematica \(t=1/2\). A trial energy below every other sector's floor proves which charge sector wins without assuming that either trial is the exact minimizing field. [Monopole harmonics](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(76)90143-7)

Choose the material constants once so that \(x(R_0)=1/3,\ y(R_0)=2/9\). These values test existence. No coefficient was measured. With equal helicity gains the energies favor \(|n|=1\) throughout \(0.97\le R/R_0\le1.01\) by the SymPy bounds. Mathematica independently certifies the subinterval from \(1\) to \(1.01\). At \(R_0/2\) the neutral incoherent state wins. At \(2R_0\) a state with \(|n|=2\) wins. Equal gains leave the signs degenerate on either mouth. With one gain reduced to \(4\alpha/5\), the selected sign within the unit interval is \(n=-s h_{\rm favored}\). The mirror therefore selects the opposite sign if it retains the same favored helicity *relative to its local normal*. Reversing the preference as well preserves the charge sign. The control with both native integers zero prefers \(n=0\).

### Second test — order in a three-dimensional region around the mouth

Hypothesize that the responding material occupies \(R<r<\Lambda R\) in the ordered brane, with free material boundaries. On nested round material sections let curvature favor coherence through a local negative quadratic coefficient \(-K g_h/r^2\). With \(K,u>0\), the trial material energy in this volume is
\[
 \int \left[K\sum_h |D_h\Psi_h|^2-\frac{K}{r^2}\sum_h g_h|\Psi_h|^2+\frac u2 S^2\right]\,d^3x .
\]
The curvature energy and a thickness proportional to \(R\) are new constitutive assumptions. The scalar shear-loss layer in the existing model does not establish either. The exterior field energy is counted once from \(R\) to infinity. Outside the collar the far-brane wave equation is unchanged by construction. Global propagation, including scattering at the edges, has not been tested.

With \(\Lambda=5/4,\ g=\max_h g_h=3/2\), choose \(k_e\) once so \(k_e/(R B_3)=2/9\), where \(B_3=2\pi K^2 g^2(1-1/\Lambda)/(uR)\). The angular lower bound applied on every sphere allows arbitrary radial profiles because radial gradients have nonnegative energy. A radially constant field is used only to construct an upper bound. Relative to the surface trial its ordering gain is multiplied by \(3\Lambda/(1+\Lambda+\Lambda^2)=60/61\). In units of B₃ the neutral and multiple-unit competitors have energy at least −1/9. SymPy puts the unit trial below that bound by at least 274657/18128529. Mathematica obtains 7363/515511 with its different trial shape. Both bounds scale as \(1/R\), and a strict separation of sectors persists for every \(R>0\) in this idealized family. Equal gains select one spacing with either sign. Taking \(g_-/g_+=4/5\) selects opposite signs for mouths with the same handed preference relative to their local normals. This construction assumes compact flux and identifies its allowed spacing \(q_0\) once for the medium.

**What would rule this out.** A fixed physical collar thickness makes the integrated ordering gain fall too rapidly at large \(R\), and the neutral state wins there. A positive residual energy density \(\mu_0 S\) also suppresses coherence when \(R^2\mu_0\ge Kg\). With the curvature attraction removed, the incoherent neutral state already minimizes the energy. The scale-free result therefore depends on how the material susceptibility and coherence extent change with radius. The energies compare fixed, open mouths. The winning contribution is negative and scales as −1/R relative to the chosen incoherent reference. Taken by itself it would favor shrinking the radius. Any free-radius calculation must include the other core and brane energies. A slip lifetime and the force transmitted to a moving rim require dynamics.

**Next material test.** Prepare weak right- and left-circular relative coherence on the same uncharged material patch and its mirror. Extract the small-amplitude energy curvature for each helicity after accounting for the gradient cost. Repeat at a second physical radius and resolve the response versus distance from the rim. This independently tests the handedness, the \(1/r^2\) binding term, and whether the responsive width scales with \(R\). A fixed charge spacing alone measures none of these. The existing degenerate standing-wave action gives equal energies for opposite circular actions and supplies no such coherence coefficients. On the field side, an independent test is to relax the angular flux profile in each sector. A zero-net-flux preparation could lower its material energy by concentrating positive and negative flux on different patches. The new binding stress would enter the 0PN momentum budget of a moving mouth. Its contribution to inertia, the current during a slip, magnetic and gravitomagnetic response, and the symmetry that could supply a handed preference remain open.
