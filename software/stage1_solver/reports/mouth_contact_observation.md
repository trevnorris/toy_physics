# Does the draining flow erase a contact’s shear reference?

**The saved flow gives a conditional survival result without assigning a contact lifetime or fitting a force.** I tested the hypothesis that adjacent material rows follow the measured carrier, transmit incremental transverse shear while their features overlap, and acquire a fresh, unloaded reference after a break. For initially equal row spacing and feature pitch, at least **98.0% of exit contacts** retain their reference across every row orientation on the four fine reconstructed paths. The archive contains no direct observation of the features themselves. [Earlier contact law](mouth_contact_age.md) · [Carrier and order preparations](mouth_free_order_depth.md)

**What was counted.** Follow a parcel from order $\chi=0.75$ to $0.25$ at fixed inward intake 0.25. At entry, lay two short orthogonal material segments in the radial and $w$ directions. Move their endpoints with the saved four-spatial-dimensional flow at the same physical times. If their deformed vectors are $\mathbf a,\mathbf b$, the sideways displacement of the neighboring row, in units of current feature pitch, is $d=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b/|\mathbf a|^2$. Uniform initial offsets and a capture width equal to $\chi$ times the pitch give exactly the saved mean order. A pair retains its reference only if it overlaps the same partner throughout. Summing the extensions of individual contacts after weak held and alternating strain steps supplied a separate check of the overlap calculation. The last column is rounded downward. It uses the finer carrier mesh, tracer spacing $0.005R$, spherical symmetry for the two angular directions, and linear interpolation of the material metric and order between samples. The enclosure allows either edge to lose overlap between reads. Some oblique arrangements on the near paths do lose contacts. These bounds concern the tested paths and reconstructed local deformation. With 100% retention, every **exit** contact carries the entry strain, while only one-third of the original contact population survives. At unchanged full-order stiffness per area, the absolute held-strain traction therefore falls to one-third of its entry value. Independent sliding could change the answer: uniform slip at 0.5 pitch per model time gives full retention near the waist and zero in both deep preparations. That speed is a sensitivity test. The force calculation assumes a weak shear component that leaves the background registration unchanged. **What the native fields can distinguish.** Across the four matched carriers and eight stationary order files, the archive supplies phase, flow, geometry and a scalar order profile. It contains no reference-sensitive time series or independent driven traction. In the native order equation a locally uniform weak order offset grows initially at one-quarter, half and three-quarter order, even while the weighted norm of the entire imposed perturbation decays. At half order the continuum local rate is +125 per model time unit. This rules out identifying that response with the decay of independent contacts at fixed formation and destruction rates. Order-dependent feedback and reference resets remain possible. [Native order perturbation](../tools/mouth_native_order_contrast.py)

**A readout that could distinguish renewal.** In synthetic undriven parcels, exactly the same occupation-count histories admitted 58.49% and 25.06% retention on the deep path when an occupied contact could reset its reference. Give each reference an independent, zero-mean passive mark at formation or reset and sum the marks, $Z(t)$. For equal contact stiffness, $\langle Z(T)Z(u)\rangle/\langle Z(T)^2\rangle$ measures the fraction of exit contacts continuously carrying their reference since $u$, weighted by exit contact count. This predicted the normalized held force and three stepped-frequency responses to within 0.013 in complex amplitude in independent synthetic force samples. A physical readout must demonstrably lose its mark at the mechanical reset. A mark following a constituent indefinitely cannot resolve that event. [Fluctuation and force check](../tools/mouth_contact_fluctuations.py)

**Decision.** Keep carrier-following overlap as a high-retention candidate for the converting layer. A lower-level material realization can falsify it by measuring relative feature displacement and persistent contact identity in an undriven traversal, then comparing the predicted and directly measured incremental shear traction. An independent identification of the moving material and its transverse inertia is still needed for a wave lifetime, a wall pressure or a free-mouth acceleration at 0PN.
