# Reversible and downhill conversion at a free throat

**Both candidate laws allow inward conversion in a coherent material. A stable physical mouth at radii 1 and 2 remains open.** A free-shape calculation identified a specific deformation that a successful law must resist. The neck widens while the flare narrows, or vice versa, at unchanged axial length and enclosed volume. A standing wave whose energy depends only on those two quantities cannot restore the tested flare. Radius 3 remains reserved.

### What each conversion law achieved

| Candidate | What drives the current | Result on a material patch | What remains free |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Reversible conversion | A fixed phase difference drives the coherent carrier. An added Hamiltonian momentum for crystalline order allows order and carrier to exchange energy reversibly. | At phase difference 0.45100843 across 32 interface widths, the coupled equations give +0.01401963 current per area with zero net steady power. Reversing the drive reverses the current. | Translating the front within the homogeneous sample leaves its energy unchanged to the resolved precision. |
| B. Downhill conversion | Ordered material carries extra specific energy \(\epsilon H(\chi)\). Maintaining the prepared phases supplies energy as flow crosses a finite-mobility front. | At the trial \(\epsilon=0.015\), a planar front selects +0.045 current per area relative to the front. Release and loss are each 0.000675 power per area. The term gives zero added hydrostatic pressure at equal density. | Two independently sized spherical patches carried steady current. On release both initially moved farther in the direction of each weak displacement, at full and half strength. |

The measurements use the natural reservoir controls of each hypothesis. Comparing the two current magnitudes does not rank their throughput at an identical drive. A neutral-patch measurement of the order-amplitude frequency and extra phase accumulated across a moving layer can calibrate A. Measuring the specific enthalpy of each pure phase at equal pressure, together with heat produced per constituent at the interface, can test B. In B an unprotected face can also melt. Localization at the mouth still uses the working assumption about intact faces. The spherical controls measure the melting front in interface-width units. Neither is a freely moving particle throat. ### Can pressure and a standing wave hold a free neck and flare?

We allowed a surface of revolution in four spatial dimensions to find its entire meridional profile \(b(w)\), with end radii and axial separation free in the variation. The first candidate support law has constant isotropic surface tension \(\sigma\), spatially uniform outward total pressure load \(p\), and fixed-action axial wave energy \(\alpha/L\). Its energy is
\[
 E=\sigma A-pV+\alpha/L,\quad
 A=4\pi\int b^2\sqrt{1+b'^2}\,dw,\quad V=\frac{4\pi}{3}\int b^3\,dw .
\]
The wave reflects at the two moving end sections. Its thrust and the pressure work on displaced volume enter the end conditions. We assume no additional rim or cap energy. The model omits spatial variations of wave stress and of the coherent momentum flux along the wall. It is a separate test of mechanical support that either conversion route would need. It does not assign a conversion energy release to \(p\).

The natural end conditions give \(b'=0\) at both ends and axial balance \(4\pi[\sigma b^2/\sqrt{1+b'^2}-pb^3/3]=\alpha/L^2\) along the wall. A zero pressure jump with the axial-only wave requires equal turning radii. We gave the model a more favorable test by selecting the pressure and action that support waist radius 1 and exit radius 1.5 at first order. It selects \(L=2.76301782\) when \(p/\sigma=30/19\) and \(\alpha/\sigma=45.44291\). This is a stationary saddle. Inward and outward parallel displacements of the wall both lower its energy. With those same parameters a radius-2 waist has \(b''=2/2-30/19=-0.578947\) at the entrance. It turns inward and cannot start a flaring minimum at that radius, even with the exit radius free. Changing pressure or action separately at the second radius would invalidate the common-material prediction. ### The test that survives a volume-dependent wave pressure

We broadened the hypothesis to any smooth stored wave energy \(E_w(V,L)\) at fixed preparation. Such a wave can push on the side wall as well as the ends. The side pressure it contributes is uniform. On a stationary profile select a normal displacement with zero first-order volume change and keep the axial length fixed. For each finite displacement we also corrected the volume back to its initial value. The pressure and the wave then do zero net work between the two shapes. Only the surface area changes. This gives a direct way to test restoring work without fitting a wave strength.

On the 1 to 1.5 flare, an outward neck displacement of one unit corresponds to an inward exit displacement of 0.499 units to first order. With signed neck movements of 0.005 units, the area changes were \(-0.0000343323\) and \(-0.0000333745\); the relative volume mismatch was below \(5\times10^{-16}\). Both shapes cost *less* energy. The area curvature along this deformation is \(-2.70831\) for the radius-1 waist. The geometrically similar radius-2 comparison gives \(-5.41662\); that comparison was rescaled only to diagnose the mode. Exit-to-waist ratios of 1.25 and 2 at waist radius 1 also admit energy-lowering displacements. An independent calculation integrated the meridian in arclength and rebuilt the perturbed surface with four-dimensional frusta. This is a negative restoring stiffness on the tested profiles. The full coupled material's time evolution remains unmeasured. **Decision for 0PN.** Neither route has supplied a stable physical depth, flare, total intake, translational inertia or \(D/M\) at both calibration radii. The reference-mouth depth exponents near −2 are local sensitivities of held geometries. The evidence selects neither a continuous family nor one discrete elementary radius. The next test is to give the occupied throat the *same* signed neck/flare displacement, measure the change in wave frequency at fixed action and integrate the coherent normal momentum flux on both sides, including end reactions. On the radius-1 benchmark, additional fields must supply positive stiffness exceeding \(2.70831\,\sigma\) along this mode **and** preserve first-order force balance there to reverse the measured sign. A true equilibrium at another profile needs its own force and stability tests. Independent wave occupation and exterior traction are still required to test the actual mouth.
