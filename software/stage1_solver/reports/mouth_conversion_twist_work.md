# Can converting rim material power one unit of exterior flux?

**An inward stream can distinguish a mouth from its mirror. When all sites share the same mean level energy, every state carrying exterior flux still has an energy deficit in the slow limit at the tested coupling and intake.** The existing order potential has equal energies at its two homogeneous endpoints and supplies no signed angular coupling. We therefore tested a proposed law in which a parcel with two internal states crosses three coupled rim sites once. The twist is the phase accumulated around this circuit. Both mouths stay still, with the same material preparation and inward number rate \(D=0.25\). All energies below use the common working unit specified in the [measurement record](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_conversion_twist_work.json). [Existing conversion terms](order_conversion_energy.md)

**Energy leaving the mouth.** On a calibration patch, the mean internal excitation exported per parcel was 0.516990095545 at twist +0.08 and 0.521768124306 at −0.08. The mirror exchanged the readings. The signed half-difference fell from −0.00238901438077 to −0.00119546601617 when the twist was halved. The parcel's loss of forward kinetic energy paid for the excitation. A spatially resolved calculation on two spherical rims also gave mirror-opposite signals after subtracting the ordinary discharge. Independent Python and Wolfram calculations agreed on the calibration patch. [Parcel calculation](../tools/mouth_conversion_twist.py) · [Wolfram check](../tools/mouth_rim_conversion_check.wl)

**Work delivered to the angular field.** We integrated the torque as the twist moved. With the same mean internal level energy at every site, symmetry makes the work zero over every slow closed cycle. Waiting at fixed twist adds zero field energy. The preferred sign received **0.0000292153476142** on the 128-face rim and **0.00000195132002716** on the 512-face rim along a path forming one distributed flux unit. Even allowing all flux profiles and the earlier bound on energy released by separately occupied standing waves, the unpaid energy at the reference radius was at least **0.10443615** on the finer rim. It remained positive on both rims at six sampled radii from \(0.9R_0\) to \(2R_0\), with collar stiffness \(K=R_0/R\) and the stated wave-only axial load. Small oscillations on the tested link lost energy to the stream at all five sampled frequencies. [Combined bound](../tools/mouth_conversion_energy_bound.py) · [Standing-wave assumptions](mouth_standing_wave_budget.md)

**Can deposited energy change this?** Suppose prior deposition shifts both internal levels by the same amount at a site. A slow, externally completed turn can then draw net work from the stream. At unit coupling the largest magnitude over shared links was \(1.19351174335\times10^{-5}\) on the coarse rim and \(5.0445884971\times10^{-7}\) on the fine rim. The quasistatic mean force still cannot pay for the first half-turn starting flat and at rest. [Site-shift test](../tools/mouth_conversion_site_shift.py)

We tested this shift independently on an isolated patch by comparing both transit phases against a coherent reference. In the existing resonant receiver model, retaining the receiver from the sixth encounter changed the interference amplitude and gave **zero common phase**. Adding a shift proportional to its stored excitation gave **−0.0172471434289 radians** at transit time 0.33. A separate calibration predicted the half-time and mirror readings. The outstanding material question is whether energy retained at the rim alters the energy of the next parcel at all, and for how long. Formation of one charge and its persistence after release remain open.
