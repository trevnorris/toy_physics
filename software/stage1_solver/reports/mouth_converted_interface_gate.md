# Does the neutral interface determine the force a draining mouth retains?

**Roadmap decision.** The neutral tests distinguish transport through an interface from force on the ordered material. The dense converted candidate has no independently resolved slow shear response or joint measurement of crossing momentum and tangential force on an ordered surface. This blocks calibration of the total holding force in step 2. The constrained-mouth acceleration remains a conditional result. [Free-fall roadmap](../../../docs/zero_pn_free_fall_roadmap.md) · [One-mouth release comparison](zero_pn_free_fall_reassessment.md)

**A continuous-phase candidate.** We put a relaxed order profile between ordered and converted reservoirs on a flat sheet, using the density and potential scale of the radius-one, intake-0.25 reference mouth. The fixture places the half-order point at the sheet. Both model reservoirs have the same compressive equation of state. The converted endpoint is an ideal shear-free fluid in this test. We compared the written constant coefficient for the energy cost of an order gradient with a hypothesis in which half of that cost scales with density. Both laws have the same coefficient at the reference density. They preserve the native current relation and conserve mass. [Normal-current calculation](../tools/mouth_converted_interface_current.py)

| Normal response | Constant gradient coefficient | Partly density-dependent |
|---|---:|---:|
| Current per unit area at phase difference +0.002, reservoirs at ±18 | +0.00000354878 | +0.00000352531 |
| Current per unit excess phase after removing the reservoir contribution | 0.00189596 | 0.00188257 |
| Width inferred from the undriven order slope at the sheet, in mouth radii | 0.04000 | 0.02827 |

Reversing and halving the bias reversed and halved the current. A second reservoir size and independent static energy calculation checked the result. In a separate uniform-density test these two laws predict fractional changes in interface tension per fractional density change of 0 and 0.25 at the reference density. Measuring tension at two nearby densities would distinguish them. [Density and momentum readout](../tools/mouth_converted_interface_readout.py)

**What reaches the ordered surface.** When both coherent reservoirs move at the same tangential speed `U` relative to the held smooth sheet, the signed flux of sideways momentum is `J U`. The contact force along the sheet is exactly zero by translation symmetry. A separate dilute-gas control exposed discrete sites and their lateral average to the same thermal arrivals. The resolved sites received a sideways traction of 2.94% of the gas pressure along the slip (approximate 95% interval 2.81–3.08%) at speed 0.125 times the one-component thermal speed (Maxwell-reweighted estimate). The smooth sheet received zero. Direct opposite-slip launches also gave a positive response at full and half speed in the larger sample. Of 20,480 trajectories, 239 were still in flight at readout. This calculation measures the reaction on a rigid sheet in a gas. Its units and material preparation differ from those of the continuous carrier and the dense four-dimensional sample. [Particle calculation](../tools/mouth_neutral_thermal_slip.py) · [Traction readout](../tools/mouth_neutral_thermal_slip_readout.py)

**Physical roadblock.** In the dense four-dimensional sample at thermal energy 1.024 in bond-depth units, compression and neighbor exchange were resolved. For the ratio of mean late to mean initial shear stiffness, approximate 95% intervals were [−5.97, 1.53] and [−3.86, 2.62] at full and half strain. Each uses three histories from one starting preparation. The cold control resolved roughly 28–30% retention. Counting more contacts from the same histories does not give more independent realizations. [Backing readout](../tools/mouth_backing_response_precision.py) · [Thermal preparations](mouth_bulk_temperature_steps.md)

The distinguishing experiment is **one dense interface with both readings on the same clock**. Locate the carrier boundary and the material sites in the same coordinates. At matched normal load and local order, hold an identifiable ordered material feature. Apply zero, positive, negative and half-strength normal bias and sideways slip independently. Measure the force on the held feature, the mass and sideways momentum crossing in each direction, and momentum stored nearby over one transit. Opposing streams can exchange sideways momentum even when their net mass flow is zero. Use the recovered law or an independent total entrance-force and push measurement on the same draining state to resume. The planar currents alone cannot bound the error a sealed wall would introduce in the whole-mouth load. Free release, radius universality and the blind pair test remain open.
