# Does the companion flow deliver enough material to pull the mouth?

**More material crosses into the throat on the side away from the companion. Capturing enough of that passing material would make the attached material’s contribution pull the mouth toward the companion.** The carrier equations give the crossing imbalance. The probability that a passing constituent binds, and how long it stays, remain independent inputs.

**What the flow supplies.** On the held radius-one mouth at intake 0.25, we drove a weak flow toward a distant companion by tilting the feeding phase. The receiving bath stayed fixed. In a band 0.01 radii wide beside the curved wall, we measured the inward current and the inventory per unit depth. The table gives the fractional coefficient of $U\cos\theta$ for each, with the pole toward the companion. More material was present on the facing side but passed more slowly. Independent integration, three meshes, and halved and doubled widths confirmed both signs. **What must stick.** Each percentage is the share of eligible tracer crossings that must bind in the undriven band for the order force to change sign. A tracer binds at constant rate $k$ in a thin slice of depth $L$, giving probability $1-\exp(-kL/v_w)$. Slow capture favors material that spends longer in the slice. Fast capture counts nearly every arrival. Each depth is tested separately. Residents keep their arrival angle and have angle-independent mean lifetime. These are stationary predictions for the relaxed-host force calibration. The signed checks use reconstructed tangent fields at $U=\pm0.002,\pm0.001$. **Can existing residents rearrange fast enough?** The carrier travels between these cuts in 1.59062 model time units. At the example neutral temperature ($k_BT=0.008$ in microscopic units), passive sideways motion in the effective polar binding potential can produce at most 0.060% of the equilibrium imbalance in one transit. The force reversals require 1.61%–6.41%. For isotropic, independently timed replacement, the necessary root-mean-square resident age is **5.20–10.35 transits**. We also tested exchange with a common dilute thermal bath on independent clamped microscopic patches. Both contact laws give sufficient imbalance at equilibrium. Over one transit, bounds on arrivals and departures give a signed coverage slope below $6.3\times10^{-21}$ per model speed at both tested amplitudes and below $3.25\times10^{-23}$ in the infinitesimal-drive limit. The smallest reversal threshold on this host was 0.0156. Passive thermal exchange from the prepared equilibrium is too slow on this clock.
