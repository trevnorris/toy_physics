# Can bound material turn a companion’s flow into a pull on the mouth?

**The extra order force pointed away from the companion when bound material stayed at its original positions. It can point toward the companion if material spreads around the mouth before it is replaced.** A weak feeding flow lowered the shear order on the companion-facing side. Both trial contact laws bind material more strongly on the other side. Whether enough bound material actually moves there is the open physical question.

**Held-mouth comparison.** We used one radius-one curved mouth with its prescribed intake of 0.25, three brane coordinates and a flared conduit into the fourth coordinate. A static phase tilt in the feeding bath produced a weak flow of speed parameter $U$ toward +x, representing the local flow toward a distant companion. The receiving bath stayed fixed. The dipole drive preserves total intake to first order. We computed the change in the occupied-minus-empty force when switching on the flow. At each test depth the uniformly distributed sites formed a dilute shell with fixed total mass. The carrier drives conversion, and this calculation omits the reciprocal change in carrier momentum from the sites. **What the percentages mean.** They are fractions of the *predicted equilibrium difference in coverage between the two sides* under that same weak drive. The physical fraction of all bound sites that changes sides also depends on the drive amplitude. This is a conditional second test. We let a resident site diffuse on the fixed shell in the cold adsorption energy $m\mu(\langle\chi\rangle)$, using the earlier neutral-patch example temperature $k_BT/m=0.00023633875$ in model units. We assumed uniform replacement at a constant rate $1/\tau$ and no additional sideways entrainment. For shell radius $a$ and surface diffusivity $D_s$, the angular population reaches the fraction $f=\zeta/(1+\zeta)$ of its equilibrium imbalance, where $\zeta=2D_s\tau/a^2$. At the finest mesh, $\zeta=0.01$ left the order force pointing away in all four cases, while $\zeta=0.1$ made it point toward the companion in all four. A conservative angular calculation checked both signs of the drive and half strength. An independent calculation reproduced the reversal thresholds. Giving each site the same lifetime approximately doubled the required diffusivity at a given mean lifetime. The mouth’s temperature, the use of its adsorption energy as a thermal potential, and the replacement dynamics each need independent verification. **0PN stopping point.** Measure the signed difference in bound-site coverage directly at the upstream and half-order depths under opposite weak feeding flows at the same intake. With $x$ the cosine of a site’s angle toward the companion, the direct observable is the population average $3[\langle x\rangle_{+U}-\langle x\rangle_{-U}]/(2U)$. For example, at half order with persistent mean attraction the order force reverses when this quantity falls below −0.019152 per model speed. Following the same residents from attachment to departure would also measure their angular motion and lifetime. This distinguishes frozen sites from migrating or preferentially supplied sites without fitting a gravity coefficient. Momentum delivered to departing material, the carrier and the supports still has to be accounted for before this contribution can predict a free acceleration.
