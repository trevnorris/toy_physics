# Can passing material calibrate the force on the deeper wall?

**The passing material exerted a measurable force on one face of the patch. Its depth profile failed to calibrate the force on the wall of the draining mouth.** We tested one cubic mouth at radius 1 and intake 0.25. We started the same 24-site material patch at depth 1.175 and carried it with the wall-adjacent flow to depth 2. The two layers had a fixed normal separation of one bond length. The lower sites had unit sideways tethers to the held wall. The sites could rearrange in their planes, and all 144 cross-layer bonds contributed. Their well depths weakened in proportion to the local shear order. This is a material hypothesis. These are averages over four thermal histories and over each band, for fixed patch coverage. Normal pull is in patch force units and stiffness is in patch force per bond length. Both virtual displacements hold bond strength fixed. The sideways reading also holds the microscopic configuration fixed. The two layers exert opposite forces on each other. Assigning the one-face reading to the mouth requires a physical path for the reaction from the other face.

**Independent scale test.** Could one force conversion make those bonds account for the missing radial pressure? We fitted uniform backing pressure, uniform wall tension and one signed contact scale using three of eight bands. We then predicted the other five. At depth 1.34–1.42 the carrier pressure was −0.00165659. The prediction was −0.00210899 with constant patch density per wall area and −0.00212080 with a conserved flux of passing patches. Halving the material time step and changing mesh preserved the discrepancy. No sideways release data entered the fit. [Pressure test and balanced-push benchmark](mouth_deep_support_calibration.md)

We also let the bond force vary independently in each band. Each cross bond could contribute up to 3 patch force units before order weakening. Only in the first two bands did we use a lower bound, from the nine original pairs that stayed registered at every sampled step in all four histories. Coverage could range between the fixed-density and conserved-flux prescriptions. Even with these freedoms and an unrestricted constant tension and backing, the smallest possible maximum pressure mismatch was **2.16794 × 10⁻⁵** on mesh 34 for one reaction direction and **2.73683 × 10⁻⁴** for the reverse. The independent volume-work calculation gave **2.16664 × 10⁻⁵** and **2.73734 × 10⁻⁴**. These are numerical bounds for the tested wall, clamp and coverage assumptions. **Free-fall decision.** The contact forces remain a candidate contribution near the entrance to the flare. Their conversion to physical traction and the total dynamic support at depth remain open. The balanced release and later roadmap phases await an independent calibration. A next test would assign the amount of carrier material represented by a neutral microscopic cell, measure its compression response, and compare with the carrier pressure–density law on a second strain. That would test an absolute material stress scale away from the curved mouth. The occupied fraction of the three-dimensional wall and where the other layer delivers its reaction would still have to be established. Baseline checkpoint `1dc45d09`.
