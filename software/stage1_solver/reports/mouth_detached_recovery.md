# What the brane remembers while a shear contact is open

**An attachment can recover through a break at a rate fixed by its intact response, provided the same elastic support and drag persist.** In the flat quarter-order return test, this hypothesis predicted **6.9388%** of the instantaneous exit force. The previously saved simulation measured **6.9358%**. Those are results for a trial material. For the draining brane we identified a possible way to read the remaining shear from the order field, and established when it would be invisible. **Which material property changes the prediction?** Let an attached link of stiffness $k$ pull a feature against a support of stiffness $K$ and drag $\zeta$. The relaxed fraction of its initial force is $p=K/(K+k)$ and the intact relaxation time is $\tau=\zeta/(K+k)$. Once the link breaks, the same support gives $\zeta\dot y=-Ky$, so $\tau_{\rm off}=\tau/p$. The prior stationary-patch calibration gives $\tau_{\rm off}=1.00049$ in model time. Every site engaged at exit through the one-pitch return spends two equal intervals $g=0.1875$ detached, with a brief attachment to a different partner between them. Integrating the attached portions over the exit sites gives a force factor of 0.10094067. Multiplying it by $\exp(-2g/\tau_{\rm off})$ predicts the saved return without fitting the return force. Conversely, the measured force divided by the attached factor gives a single-gap displacement retention of 0.82892 after taking the square root. For persistent site-to-site variation with the same attached response and type independent of phase, that quantity is a root-mean-square retention.

These are new opposite-face force readings. All original links broke. The two-drag mixture was fitted only at the first gap, using the earlier return. Both trial materials have identical intact dynamics and occupancy. With $m(g)$ the measured force divided by its protocol-specific attached factor, $m(2g)-m(g)^2$ is the variance of $e^{-2\lambda g}$ for this persistent distribution of exponential recovery rates $\lambda$. The finest doubled-gap readings gave −0.000194 for the single rate, a numerical residual, and +0.030290 for the mixture. This comparison can falsify the shared-rate law under matched contact timing. **How to read the remaining shear without tracking every feature.** The order energy already includes a shear term. In a small patch, suppose its relevant part is $A(\chi)(e+s)^2/2$ for stored strain $e$ and a weak applied probe $s$ along the same shear component. At the same instant in three matched preparations measure the *material order rates* $D_-,D_0,D_+$ at probe strains $-\delta,0,+\delta$, before the reference moves. For a common passive order mobility, fixed reference and matched conversion sources, the part of the rate that varies with the probe is proportional to $(e+s)^2$. Thus
$$
 e=\frac{\delta}{2}\frac{D_+-D_-}{D_++D_--2D_0}.
$$
The ratio cancels the unknown mobility, the stiffness slope and probe-independent support energy. It requires a resolved nonzero denominator. For an ensemble it can weight strains by the order derivative of stiffness. Absolute shear traction requires a calibrated stiffness and a justified relation between those weights and the load-bearing material.
