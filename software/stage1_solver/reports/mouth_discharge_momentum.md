# Does the sideways load survive discharge?

**The ordinary draining carrier exerts a measurable force on a held reflecting mouth. The earlier 32.18% tagged-stream result cannot be carried through the modeled opening under its original contact law.** Both tests used radius 1 and intake 0.25. The ordinary carrier was driven in the distant brane. The tags received a sideways velocity at depth 1.175.

**Force on the held surface.** On mesh 48 the carrier-on-wall force divided by far-brane speed was +0.250010 in the direction of the stream. The external holder supplied the opposite force. Fifteen closed momentum balances, including flow, density-gradient stress and isotropic stress on the feeding and receiving boundaries, gave 0.249755–0.2500104. Moving the receiving cuts farther into the bulk preserved agreement to about 0.11%. Opposite and half-strength drives gave the expected signs and scaling in the linearized solver. About 99.7% of the direct pressure load had already acted upstream of the tag injection section. Within one microscopic spacing of the wall at injection the calculated current-weighted sideways drift was 0.213% of the far-stream speed. [Pressure and momentum calculation](../tools/mouth_discharge_carrier_ledger.py)

**Where the tags went.** We continued the same smooth normal attraction, with free angular slip and the archived loss of shear order, to the outlet plane at depth 3.05. The signed momentum exported by the unrestricted tags balanced the integrated reaction on the trial potential. Every sampled tag reached that plane outside the opening of radius 1.5. Even the closest tag in the refined positive-stream run using laboratory-velocity quadrature missed its edge by 0.296 mouth radii. The carrier domain has a boundary on that outer annulus. The tag equations specify neither an earlier lateral handoff to the bulk nor a collision there. The formal reaction of about 29.5% is therefore a force on the imposed potential. The mouth’s post-discharge tag force is undetermined. [Trajectory and aperture test](../tools/mouth_discharge_tagged_stream.py) · [Earlier 32.18% result](mouth_upstream_normal_contact.md)

**A control that does discharge.** Requiring tags to stay on a smooth surface one microscopic spacing inside the wall, while allowing angular slip, sent every tag through the opening. The common support received 55.71% of the incident sideways momentum. One third was delivered when the inlet absorbed the tags’ relative radial motion. The guide received a further 22.38% during passage. The signal persisted across three force-free receiver planes. Radial capture, continuous radial confinement, prescribed axial motion and a common support are extra assumptions. [Sliding guide control](../tools/mouth_discharge_sliding_guide.py)

**Ordinary participation and free fall.** The fraction of ordinary current within 0.01 radii of the wall rose from 2.12% at injection to 2.91% at the 1%-order section. The narrow outlet band failed a local conservation check. Proximity does not identify an attached population, and counting the same carrier again as tags would double count it. The next independent gravity input is how much of the pressure at the lip accelerates the mouth and its deeper material together.
