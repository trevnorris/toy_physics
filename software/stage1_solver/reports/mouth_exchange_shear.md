# Does the transverse wave resist exchanging width between the neck and flare?

**The resolved degree-four wave adds positive stiffness. It also exerts a nonzero force at the original profile.** At unit waist radius, the wave energy at fixed action \(J\) contributes \(+1.54070 J\) to stiffness along the measured deformation and \(+1.23150 J\) to the first energy derivative. Positive displacement widens the waist and narrows the exit. The force of the wave is minus the energy slope. With zero carrier contribution, exceeding \(2.70831\sigma\) would require \(J/\sigma>1.75784\) in these radius-one solver units. Neither the material preparation nor the wave eigenproblem determines that absolute action. It could be calibrated from a separately prepared ringdown by measuring the stored strain plus kinetic energy and dividing by the measured frequency. ### What was simulated

The volume-preserving meridian of length 2.76301782 opens flush into an ordered feeding slab of thickness one. The downstream medium loses transverse shear. We used the archived degree-four toroidal curl-energy law, in which transverse stiffness and transverse inertia both follow local order, with unit ordered shear speed. Its natural shear traction is the one used at the free boundary. Symmetric-strain elasticity or a separate bending modulus of the crystalline lip would be a different material law. The transverse-wave inertia here and the crystalline order-amplitude inertia in Route A are independent response coefficients. The side wall and outlet have zero exterior shear traction. The benchmark has a sharp order boundary, with a second preparation fading both coefficients smoothly over the last 0.1 radius of the flare. These are candidate placements of the shear boundary on the reversible-conversion geometry. The reversible planar solution leaves its position free. The rest-frame transverse-wave benchmark does not include advection or leakage through coupling to longitudinal waves. Its squared strain and kinetic forms for mode amplitude \(f(r,w)\), up to the same angular factor, are
\[
 K=\int \chi r^2 [\ell(\ell+1)|f|^2+|2f+r f_r|^2+r^2|f_w|^2]dr\,dw,\quad
 M=\int \chi r^4|f|^2 dr\,dw .
\]
The mouth and feeding slab share displacement and traction. For each transverse depth channel of the uniform slab we matched outgoing spherical Hankel waves (or their decaying evanescent continuation) at the cylindrical numerical cut. Two channels propagate at the measured frequency. The clamped pocket mode supplies a starting field only. The identified free, radiating pole uses the entire coupled domain. The squared modal overlap exceeds 0.999997, using corresponding material nodes and the mass norm. Independent quadrature of the outgoing modal energy flux at the real frequency gives the pole's energy decay rate to within one part per million in the tests. At the widest neck the energy leakage increases by 34.4% over the reference. Over 200 time units this loses 0.0020744% of stored energy at the widest neck, and over one slow cycle of \(400\pi\) it loses 0.0130330% (reference 0.0015436% and 0.0096980%). These fractions use \(1-e^{-2\gamma t}\) for the energy in the prepared resonance; they are fixed-shape readouts. The opening therefore transmits wave energy. The measured loss is small across the reference slow times at the tested shapes. A sufficiently slowly deformed, already prepared mode can use approximately fixed action over times short relative to its decay time. This calculation does not impose infinite lifetime or follow nonadiabatic mode conversion.

At steps ±0.005, the direct stored-wave virtual-work difference gave stiffness \(+1.540689 J\), compared with \(+1.540703 J\) from the frequencies and \(+1.540697 J\) from differentiation of the complex-pole equation. The local work varies both strain and inertia on the moving geometry. Changing to ±0.010 gave \(+1.540685 J\) from frequencies. Reducing mesh density to 16 gave \(+1.541283 J\); moving the open cut from radius 3 to 4 changed the fine-mesh result by less than \(4\times10^{-8}J\). The 0.1-radius outlet layer gave \(+1.504270 J\) and a wave-only threshold of \(1.80041\) for \(J/\sigma\). In that layer control, the widest neck loses 0.0156932% over \(400\pi\). The open exterior has a continuum. The baseline closed-pocket starting frequencies of 4.20802 and 5.00078 describe separation of two localized seed fields only.

Moving material outward in the upper half of the flare and inward in the lower half at fixed waist and exit radii, volume and length gives a separate first energy slope of \(+0.548650 J\) for the specified normalization. Direct work averaged on the two shapes gives \(+0.548606 J\), and mesh 16 gives \(+0.548784 J\) from the frequencies. A physical equilibrium needs both independent first derivatives of total energy to vanish as well as positive stiffness.
