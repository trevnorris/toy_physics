# Which crystal points meet again after melting?

**Use the initially stress-free, permanently bonded core for the exit calculation as a conditional preparation.** The active coherent theory leaves the identity and unloaded spacing of newly ordered lattice points undetermined.

**Order and phase.** In reversible Route A, the carrier current is \(n\mathbf u=n\nabla\theta-\pi\nabla\chi\). The real ordered fraction \(\chi\) evolves with its own momentum \(\pi=I(\partial_t+\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\chi\). An order front can therefore cross the material and change its ability to carry shear. The angle \(\theta\) is the phase of the coherent carrier. A slip of this phase can change carrier winding. It carries no index of a crystal plane on either side of the join. The planar solver follows a positive-density branch. Supersolid hydrodynamics illustrates the missing distinction explicitly by transporting independent lattice coordinates with the normal velocity in its ordered phase. That equation alone specifies no reference for newly formed crystal. **What the elasticity code remembers.** For a given deformation gradient \(F\), the crystal energy is \(\chi W_0(F)\). At zero order its elastic stress vanishes for every admissible \(F\). Raising the order again costs elastic energy according to whichever reference was retained or supplied. The bonded implementation keeps the initial core radius and depth label, follows the same collar surface at each label, and uses that deformation for both static energy and the incremental wave. In the alternative preparation the core takes the identity deformation anew at each shape. Both preparations start with the same wave operator, and under the archived constrained axial variation they predict different frequency shifts. Continuity of oscillatory displacement and traction at the join is compatible with both. It cannot select the slow material correspondence. **Where an explicit choice already appears.** The crystal-connectivity trial has a separate complex density-wave amplitude whose phase locates layers. Its smooth ordered state and imposed dislocation are distinct preparations. The calculation solves the defect profile at given winding and measures normal carrier conductance. It supplies neither a history of crystal slip through melting nor a rule pairing points when shear returns. An earlier material-row experiment *does* pick a partner by nearest pitch inside a prescribed overlap window and starts each new contact unloaded. Both the capture rule and the assumed transport of the rows are additional hypotheses in that experiment.
