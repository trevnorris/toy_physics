# What the signed intake-0.25 runs can calibrate at 0PN

**Same-mouth result.** The feeding field gives one absolute force on the prescribed intake profile. Its amplitude-restoring boundary condition has energy \(E_R=\tfrac12\int g_X(u-1)^2\,d^3x\), where \(u=|\psi|\) and \(g_X=(1-|\mathbf x-X\mathbf e_x|^2)_+^3\). The force on the mechanism translating this profile is \(F_R=-\partial_X E_R\), taking the partial derivative with the instantaneous carrier field fixed. During the push the field itself evolves. This force belongs to one specified coupling in the feed model. [Boundary energy](../../../docs/drain_driven_initial_acceleration.md#what-the-brane-boundary-fixes-about-translation-of-the-opening)

I linearized the full spatially discretized feed equation, including the translated reference density and fixed-current normalization, about the archived radius-one draining state. For \(X=At^2/2\) starting from rest, the signed force contains a term from translating the profile through the original density and a term from the change in density at the opening. At \(A=+0.02,\ t=0.001,\ X=10^{-8}\), in solver force units:

| Reading | Increment |
|---|---:|
| Translate the profile through the original density | \(+2.84877\times10^{-14}\) |
| Change due to the responding carrier density | \(-5.96657\times10^{-14}\) |
| Force on the imposed profile, sum of those two terms | **\(-3.11780\times10^{-14}\)** |
| Total sideways momentum per second entering the aperture, from the archived opposite-drive half-difference | \(-4.68383\times10^{-10}\) |

The new force is a first-order calculation on this finite grid. Reversing the acceleration reverses it and half strength gives half. The carrier carriage and aperture stress from the same tangent were tested against the independent nonlinear histories at all three saved times for both strengths. Mathematica checked the initial force per displacement by directly differencing the surface energy on the archived field. The numerical onset slope was \(+2.84877339\times10^{-6}\). This is a fixed-field displacement derivative, not an inertial coefficient. [Scripts, guard outcomes and readings](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_feed_profile_force.json)

**Accounting at the opening.** In the continuum the conservative profile force can also be written as the integrated mixed density stress of this boundary term when its support and edge are handled consistently. On this grid the stress stencil followed by the aperture mask gives \(-2.15806\times10^{-13}\) for the positive drive. Summing the same stencil over the full periodic plane gives \(-2.98598\times10^{-14}\). The energy derivative defines the reported profile force exactly for the chosen discrete surface energy. Adding it to the incoming flux as a second independent source would double-count the restoring interaction. The integral over the aperture supplies no depth profile for a force on the receiving wall or on its trapped wave. [Feed stress implementation](../tools/mouth_feed_weak_flow.py#L94) · [Moving-region budget](one_mouth_moving_momentum.md)

**Calibration decision.** The prescribed rim trajectory and feeding flux do not fix the force scale of the receiving segment. With no independently known incremental distributed load, multiply its two line masses, two tensions, coupling and terminal impedance matrix by the same positive \(\lambda\). This preserves its displacement histories and multiplies its momenta and tractions by \(\lambda\). The feed run remains identical because its boundary has no receiving-side feedback. A nonzero calibrated traction on the receiving side, paired with its local motion, could break this scale ambiguity if the other shapes and ratios were known. Calibrating the full two-channel terminal law requires the paired wall and wave velocities and forces over sufficiently independent motions of this same drain. The archived physical-input packet still lacks the normalized wave, near-mouth material response, carrier transfer and receiving traces. This ends the calibration available from these feed histories.
