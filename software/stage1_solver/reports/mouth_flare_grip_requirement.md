# How much grip must survive conversion to support the deeper flare?

**The calibrated two-layer coating can supply the weaker trial flare stiffness if its layers keep transmitting sideways force as the brane loses shear order.** Immediate weakening leaves the coating too soft. The stronger trial stiffness exceeds even the value obtained by fixing both layers directly to the wall.

We kept the radius at 1, intake at 0.25 and standing-wave action at one. The entrance and middle stayed fixed. We displaced the flare by ±0.00001 and ±0.000005 radii in the original finite bath. The neutral compression calibration set the elastic force scale. The trial coating covers the wall with one bilayer of fixed thickness 0.02. Both sheets follow the wall normally and can slip tangentially according to the stated constraint. The elastic calculation lets the slips equilibrate on each prepared stiffness profile. The carrier and wave together contribute 0.0641935 to each holding-force slope. Locking the adjacent sheet gives the largest elastic slope attainable by passive grip on that sheet alone for a fixed intersheet law. Locking both gives an upper comparison for the whole coating class. We also let each of the 127 neutral cross-layer bonds sample the current wall order anywhere within its full length, and bounded both signs of its tangential force derivative. At the 1%-order depth, this permits at most 1.843% of fully ordered intersheet grip. The spatial bound freezes each bond’s strength during the slip probe and samples only the wall trace from depth 0.5 to 3.05. **A checkable material hypothesis.** Let the incremental intersheet stiffness fraction \(m\) approach local order \(\chi_B\) over a material time \(\tau\), so \(D_t m=(\chi_B-m)/\tau\). Fresh material enters with the local ordered strength and travels at the measured wall-adjacent speed. To reach the weaker target required \(\tau=1.6191\) model time units with the adjacent sheet locked, or \(1.8121\) with neutral wall grip. At depth 1.42748, where order is 1%, these cases retained **18.91%** and **21.93%** of fully ordered intersheet stiffness. These are requirements inferred from the weaker target. Earlier tagged-pair tests explicitly weakened the bond potential with order. They cannot independently measure this delay. **Decision for 0PN.** In a freshly converted sample at the same intake, displace the two layers slightly relative to each other in opposite directions. Compare their incremental tangential force, per area and per relative displacement, with fully ordered material. Read it during passage through the order transition, including depth 1.42748, using independently evolving contacts. Simply inserting the delay would reproduce the target by construction. The reading would test this proposed source of deep support. Physical wall coverage, renewal of stress-free offsets, stresses that depend on slip rate and the moving mouth’s response remain open. The proposed delay applies to interlayer contacts in the lining. Its optical response is untested.
