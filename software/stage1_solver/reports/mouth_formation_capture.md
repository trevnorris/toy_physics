# Can a mouth acquire its supporting wave as it opens?

**A forming confining region captured part of a passing wave. Starting from a quiet field produced zero trapped energy.** We used the archived radius-one flare, with spherical sections in the three brane coordinates and depth along $w$. Its geometry comes from the intake-0.25 preparation. The new assumption is that the scalar confining well develops smoothly over three model time units. Only the depth of the confining potential changes. The geometry and carrier are held fixed. [Calculation](../tools/mouth_formation_capture.py) · [Archived wave and geometry](mouth_wave_flare_work.md)

We placed one pulse entirely on the feeding side and normalized its initial energy to one. We determined the energy in the final standing wave by projecting both displacement and velocity onto its spatial pattern. All tested meshes resolved one mode below the exterior propagation threshold in this angular sector. The table and energy budget use spacing 0.0625 and time step 0.0025.

| Preparation | Ground-mode energy as a fraction of initial packet energy |
|---|---:|
| Confinement develops during passage, times 3–6 | 0.01823% |
| Same pulse, confinement develops earlier, times 0–3 | 0.000137% |
| Confinement already present, overlap at preparation | 0.000182% |

Halving the pulse amplitude gave one quarter of the stored energy. An independent backward propagation reproduced the timing effect. A separate optimization allowed **any** initial displacement and velocity confined to the feeding side at time zero. For the same times 3–6, the maximum ground-mode fraction was **2.41% and 2.58%** on two meshes. A forward run with the optimized pulse reproduced the finer result. The range describes discretizations of this particular opening history. An initial run of that last check missed its accuracy tolerance. Refining its forward time step passed the same tolerance. **Where the energy went.** Write the scalar stiffness as $L(t)=L_{\rm out}-s(t)\Delta g$, where $g\ge0$ is the final well shape and $s$ rises from zero to one. The work rate on the field due to opening is $-\tfrac12\dot s\,\Delta\langle u,g u\rangle\le0$. In the during-passage run it extracted **2.516%** of the initial energy. By time 48 the remote absorber had removed **96.272%**, and **1.210%** was still on the grid, including **0.01823%** in the ground mode. The energy budget residual was 0.0023% of initial energy. At spacing 0.04 and time step 0.004, capture was 0.01822% and the well extracted 2.529%. Once the well is fixed, the ideal lossless scalar preserves its bound-mode energy. The brane’s transverse material wave can also radiate through the entrance; its lifetime requires a separate measurement. [Material-wave release](mouth_native_shear_ringdown.md)

**What this gives 0PN.** The earlier calculation required ground-wave action above 2.71179 for both tested lateral bends to have positive stiffness with zero independent wall tension. On this mesh that is about **5.8 stored model energy units**. At the measured efficiency the chosen pulse would need about **31,800 incident units**, by linear extrapolation at fixed geometry. Even the optimized feeding-side preparation would need about **225** on the finer optimization mesh. The energy delivered at physical formation is unknown. The two-bend condition also leaves radial equilibrium and the free-mouth acceleration open.
