# Can the two solvers share a mouth in the 0PN holding-force test?

**Their boundary fields disagree at the opening.** The feeding solver allows the carrier phase to vary across the radius-one mouth. The finite receiver imposes one complex inlet value everywhere on that opening. Changing its prescribed intake changes one phase offset. Its in-plane phase gradient, and therefore its sideways carrier velocity on the inlet itself, is always zero. This is a restriction at the mouth in addition to the resting distant bath. [Feed boundary](../tools/mouth_feed_weak_flow.py) · [Receiver boundary](../src/stage1_solver/open_sleeve.py)

I tested the phase over the full three-dimensional opening, weighting each cell by the isolated inward current. The best fit allowed a phase offset and a uniform velocity in any direction. Even with the sideways drive off, the RMS phase mismatch was 0.0235780831353 radians on the 24-cell lateral grid and 0.0238092393909 on the 32-cell grid. For the part that reverses with the drive, the best uniform velocity at $U=0.02$ on the finer grid was 0.020013194794 along the drive. Its residual was $8.9954630183\times10^{-7}$ radians. At half strength the residual was $4.49770376185\times10^{-7}$. Negative drives are reflected partners. A uniform drift approximates the drive-induced phase change closely on these grids. It cannot reproduce the resting field. [Inputs, weighted fits and guarded outputs](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_inlet_trace.json)

**Momentum consequence.** At $U=0.02$, the feed delivered +0.004999999706 in sideways momentum per unit time. Carrier motion supplied +0.00499985009336 of it, about 99.997%. At $U=0.01$, it supplied +0.00249992505059 of +0.00249999985299. The receiver's imposed inlet transmits zero sideways momentum by carriage. Assigning that difference to the mouth would count the effect of the prescribed bath as a physical reaction. [Signed feed balance](../../../docs/_measurements/held_crossflow_budget.txt)

On this flat, constant-density receiver inlet, one stress measurement would determine its remaining flux. Orient the normal $N$ into the receiver. Let $s_1$ be the dipole coefficient along the drive of the normal density slope $\partial_N\log n$ at the perimeter. Integrating the mixed stress by parts gives $T_{x,\rm recv}=-(4\pi/3)a^2 n_b[\hbar^2/(4m)]s_1$. At the unit parameters the slope needed to transmit the **entire** measured feed load would be $-0.004774648012$ at full speed and $-0.002387324006$ at half speed. These are required values under that hypothesis. The receiving runs provide no such measurement for these drives. [Cartesian stress and rim projection check](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_inlet_trace.json) · [Receiver stress readout](../tools/open_sleeve_lateral_balance.py)

**Decision.** Stop the direct join of these two boundary conditions. A physical converting mouth needs an effective interface relation or independent data connecting the inward material flux and its in-plane momentum on the two sides, including the traction exerted on the opening. The model specifies that converted material carries its local brane velocity. It leaves the mechanical load and its partition open. An independent total center-holding reaction would bypass that partition for the force test. The physical holding force and free acceleration remain open. Base fd667ea9, uncommitted pending review.
