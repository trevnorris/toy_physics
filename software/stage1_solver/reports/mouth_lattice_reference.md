# Which crystal carries the load around a draining mouth?

**The implemented throat has a stationary lattice reference and a flowing carrier. The written material laws leave the physical lattice drift undetermined.** Reversible conversion gives carrier current \(j=nu\) and order momentum \(\pi=I(\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla)\chi\). A stationary profile of crystalline order can therefore carry current. The displacement field supplies a shear modulus around its prepared reference. Neither the carrier current nor the order amplitude identifies which crystal plane occupies the lip or what its unloaded spacing was. Supersolid hydrodynamics provides a precedent for transporting crystal coordinates with a velocity distinct from the superfluid velocity. **Stationary crystal.** Suppose one axisymmetric material layer from a flat punctured face follows the whole held wall, from the entrance through the waist to the exit. Let \(S\) be its original radius, decreasing inward, \(b\) the present radius and \(s\) arc length. Its two hoop stretches equal \(b/S\), and its lengthwise stretch is \(ds/|dS|\). If every stretch and reciprocal is at most \(B\), any upstream and downstream pair obeys \(s_v-s_u+b_v\le B^2 b_u\). The saved profiles therefore put a lower bound on distortion **independent of the original hole radius**. The largest stretch or reciprocal stretch anywhere on the wall is **at least 2.0721** at radius 1 and **at least 2.6595** at radius 2. The angular sectional curvature at the two waists is positive, as a second check that a flat layer cannot reach these shapes by bending isometrically. If the entire wall came from the straight bore through a unit-thick slab instead, its mean lengthwise stretches would be **2.1424** and **4.4340**. These are distinct preparations. Exchange of layers, defects or an order front crossing material planes can change the bounds. The observed opening does not specify the original bore, which layers were recruited, or their attachment. Predicting force in either preparation requires a finite-strain stress law beyond the strip test at ±0.002. [Geometry calculation](../tools/mouth_flat_puncture_reference.py)

**Convecting crystal.** We separately locked lattice markers to an ideal 3D spherical feeding flow, \(v_L=-A/r^2\), using the two archived intakes to set \(A=D/(4\pi\rho_3)\) with unit integrated feed density. A shell starting at \(r_0\) reaches \(r^3=r_0^3-3At\). Its two hoop stretches are \(r/r_0\), its radial stretch is \((r_0/r)^2\), and volume is preserved at fixed thickness. Halving the distance to the mouth halves each hoop spacing and quadruples radial spacing even *before* turning into the throat. An infinitely old, perfectly locked flow fed from unbounded radius has unbounded logarithmic strain. A finite formation age limits the strain. The associated stress requires a finite-strain constitutive law. A trial exponential reset of logarithmic strain with time \(\tau\) gives a one-relaxation-time upstream radius \((r^3+3A\tau)^{1/3}\). We checked the memory integral independently. The coherent law supplies no measured reset time or length, and adding relaxation would require accounting for the released elastic energy. **Next independent input.** Track the phase of a crystalline density modulation relative to a fixed point during a carrier current, then measure relaxed hoop and lengthwise spacing of the same tagged layer in a matched unloaded preparation. For a stationary lattice, identify the material that formed the wall and calibrate finite stretches of that material in a neutral specimen. For a drifting lattice, also measure spacing recovery versus time and downstream distance. This test requires crystal labels or density peaks that can move independently of the carrier. Tracking the present scalar order contour cannot supply those readings. With those data a common material law can predict the work on these same shape displacements at both radii.
