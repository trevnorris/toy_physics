# Can the sealed, draining throat support its own depth and flare?

**Neither training mouth reached a supported shape.** With reversible conversion at the downstream disk, both throats lowered their energy by shortening and developing a narrow waist inside the entrance. At the last profiles within the weak-curvature range, independent small changes in depth and interior width could still lower the energy. This rules out using these profiles to infer a free-fall mass. It leaves open a different equilibrium and a different wave preparation. [Shape calculations and numerical controls](mouth_sealed_shape_relax.md)

**What was held fixed.** We used the sealed lateral duct with coherent material and pressure on both sides. At the earlier radius-1 test shape, sealing the wall changed intake from 1.896737 to 0.809122 and carrier stiffness from +0.193441 to −0.500150. Both relaxations used the same absolute action \(J_0=\sigma R_{\rm ref}^4/c_T\) in the same degree-four mode. The reference length was one at both mouths. Its value was chosen as a reproducible state preparation. The previous 12.77% threshold compared wave energy with lateral surface energy along one displacement of the reference shape. That benchmark omitted the carrier contribution. The present calculation includes a terminal disk with the same surface tension as the lateral wall, a fixed entrance radius, free depth and exit radius, and two independent changes of interior width. The sharp joins carry zero extra junction energy by assumption. The positive last column means further shortening lowers energy. The similar final waist radii occur near the same curvature cutoff and do not select an elementary mouth size. Reducing the exit radius also lowered energy at both profiles, even after subtracting the disk’s area contribution from the exit-radius force. The occupied transverse mode remained localized along the sampled controlled profiles. These are quasistatic energy comparisons. [Measured meridians](figures/mouth_sealed_shape_relax.png)

**What supplies bending resistance?** With the wave off, the neutral order interface had zero holding moment when flat. Allowing its order profile to relax lowered its energy under either sign of cylindrical bending. Its small-curvature coefficient was \(B_\chi/(\sigma\delta_B^2)=-5/6\). A finite ordered strip in contact with superfluid on both sides also softened. A crystal that stores ordinary stretching energy through its thickness can add a positive bending moment. Its elastic modulus and the moment carried across the lip are still undetermined. [Signed bending, mixed curvatures and full profile checks](mouth_lip_order_moment.md)

**Where the description breaks down.** As a diagnostic we followed the reduced equations beyond their weak-curvature range. We then held those shapes fixed, switched off the wave and flow, and solved for the order throughout the interior. We pinned half-order on the side and terminal disk and kept an ordered inlet. At the last controlled profiles, order on the axis at the waist was 0.999662 and 0.999698. At the two much tighter extrapolated profiles it fell to **0.501869 and 0.500002**, respectively, with 1 denoting fully ordered and 0.5 the imposed interface value. This identifies a specific assumption that fails in the static control: a fully ordered interior between two thin, distinct sides. The driven throat could behave differently. Neither physical closure nor loss of all shear follows. **Next physical test.** Hold a narrowing waist, allow its order field to vary across and along the neck, and restore the carrier flow and occupied transverse mode together. Measure signed force for slightly tighter and wider shapes and how much wave energy remains in the ordered material. This would determine whether the standing wave can oppose the narrowing when it changes the material that confines it. A physical release and the blind third radius require a supported branch at both training radii.
