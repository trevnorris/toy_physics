# What moves when the held mouth is released in a steady stream?

**Pressure near the lip moved the entrance toward the stream in both trial mouths. The deeper flare lagged.** We kept the round mouth at radius 1, the inward intake at 0.25, the standing-wave action at one and the first-bend area tension at 0.02. Only the deeper bend’s area tension changed. The load came from the draining brane. No gravitational force or bare wall mass was supplied. [Held-mouth pressure and discharge balance](mouth_discharge_momentum.md)

**Release.** We started with the entrance, middle and flare held in their original positions in an established weak sideways stream. The applied holding forces decreased smoothly to zero over four model time units. The far stream stayed on. Subtracting the held equilibrium leaves the released fraction of the original pressure load driving the coupled carrier, standing wave and three lateral coordinates. The mesh-34 surface integral gave a net force of 0.2500112 per unit stream speed in the original bath. Its projections onto independent station displacements gave 0.2478530 at the entrance, 0.0018882 at the middle and 0.0002700 at the flare. These weights specify the work done by pressure on the three displacement shapes. Material momentum stored at each depth requires a separate measurement. Entries are **displacements divided by the imposed stream speed** in the enlarged bath, with feed radius 18 and receiving roof at depth 20. Positive points toward the stream. For the speed used here, multiply an entry by 0.000001 for displacement in mouth radii. By time 16 the flare had moved 18.6% or 21.3% as far as the entrance. The stronger deep support gave the larger entrance excursion at time 32 and the smaller excursion at time 64. The ratios characterize the release transient. They do not measure a constant acceleration or the fraction of inflowing material captured.

**Checks.** The original bath and doubled bath gave the same early flare signs. At the four times tabulated, the largest station difference from doubling the bath was below 0.12%. Spatial refinement, halving the time step and reversing the stream at half amplitude were checked separately. Small deep displacements during the four-unit ramp were sensitive to the time step. In the two time-refined runs the integrated station traces agreed with the continuous Laplace-domain response at rate 0.35 to within 0.045%. An independent full-field calculation reproduced the six coarse-mesh transformed readings and checked the force directly on the wall. “Scoped clear for the finite-bath, three-clamp release.” Formal repository review is pending. **Decision for 0PN.** For either specified wall and release history, linearity gives a displacement proportional to the incident flow at each fixed time. A distant companion with inverse-square incident flow therefore gives that same *leading separation dependence* in this local approximation. The two walls still predict different motion with the same intake, lip pressure and standing wave. The input that would distinguish them is the total restoring force of the deeper bend. Hold the entrance and middle fixed, displace the flare in both directions at the same intake and wave preparation, and determine the support from independently resolved material as it flows past the wall. The earlier same-mouth calibration predicts slopes of 0.2159234 and 0.3676533 in the original bath. Specifying the tension again would only reproduce those trial choices.
