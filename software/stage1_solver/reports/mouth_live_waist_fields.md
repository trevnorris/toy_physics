# How the occupied wave and the draining carrier load the order field

On the held meridians at entrance radii 1 and 2, the order field now changes both the wave eigenproblem and longitudinal carrier transport. The two reciprocal forces were checked by re-solving the fields after opposite order changes in four preparations. On the radius-1 broad test the resulting carrier energy slope was \(+0.000005947518\) and the transverse energy slope was \(-0.1105710920\). Direct variations of the respective operators gave \(+0.000005947518\) and \(-0.1105710905\). These are slopes for an imposed smooth **order** deformation. ### Reversible flow

Eliminating stationary order momentum in the Route A Hamiltonian gives the phase operator

\[
A=n\left[\mathbf1-\frac{I_\chi\nabla\chi\otimes\nabla\chi}
 {n+I_\chi|\nabla\chi|^2}\right].
\]

The carrier contribution is the stationary energy including amplitude-gradient energy, the compressible equation of state and \((\nabla\theta)^T A\nabla\theta/2\). Both baths maintain their saved phase and density. Both sides of the duct carry stress. Its lateral wall is sealed. The terminal disk passes current. Its outer half has the remaining planar phase-delay length \(0.04/12\), since the order gradient on the inner side is now resolved. This retains the earlier thin-front matching only on the unresolved receiving side. Reversing the ports reversed the current and preserved the energy. The implementation differentiates the effective phase operator with respect to the two physical components of the coarse-cell order gradient. A cell-mean transfer and its transpose couple these forces to the finer order grid. For a small material order velocity \(v\eta\), put \(G=I_\chi n/(n+I_\chi|\nabla\chi|^2)\). The local kinetic coefficient is \(\int G\eta^2\). Allowing the phase to respond with both bath phases fixed adds \(h^T A_{\rm free}^{-1}h\) with \(h_i=\int G\eta\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla N_i\). The carrier density in this derivative is held at its value in the static preparation. On a smooth test displacement at the broad radius-1 waist, the total coefficient was 0.000163442306. Direct signed velocity solutions reproduced it to the printed precision. This is inertia for the tested material order displacement. ### Transverse field and the location of its energy

The same local order multiplies stiffness and transverse inertia in the archived degree-four curl law. A uniform rescaling of both over an isolated cavity would leave its frequency unchanged. Their **spatial** distributions distinguish where strain and motion are stored. The sector follows \(J\operatorname{Re}\omega\) on the same open pole. Its order derivative includes the frequency dependence of the outgoing traction on the feeding slab. The wave load is averaged over the sphere in the meridional order equation. The registered exterior and terminal shear-free boundary are retained. Angularly varying order, advection of the transverse mode, and leakage into longitudinal modes lie outside this calculation. [Wave derivative](../tools/mouth_live_waist_wave.py)

In the converged broad radius-1 field, **99.99995283%** of the cavity wave kinetic energy lies more than two interface widths downstream of the narrowest cross section. Only **0.00004571%** lies in the band within two widths on either side. The corresponding broad radius-2 band holds less than \(2\times10^{-12}\) of the cavity kinetic energy at both meshes. The downstream concentration persists on the intermediate and tight preparations at both radii. The radius-1 intermediate band contains less than \(6\times10^{-12}\) of the cavity kinetic energy at both resolutions. The tiniest tail fractions vary with resolution. These are integrations of existing eigenvectors. The shear calculation ends at the half-order wall and cap and omits shear dynamics in the receiving-side half of the conversion front. The feeding slab is its only propagating escape channel. A remote standing wave can still contribute shape work through its pressure and changes in frequency. Kinetic energy distribution alone cannot determine the local wall force. [Cut-cell integration](../tools/mouth_live_waist_wave_location.py)

At \(\chi=0\) both transverse coefficients can vanish. Distinct cells with zero order can share live nodal supports with neighboring material. In the bounded solver we discard only true zero-mass nodal amplitudes and track the occupied component across disconnection. A deliberately empty local patch and a full cross-cut independently reproduced the positive-order frequency limit. The complete cut produced a disconnected cavity. For a mode on an isolated component the ideal model has no outgoing shear power through the fully cut section. Allowing material with no prior shear support to reorder requires a one-sided check on the new degrees of freedom. On the two narrower radius-1 shapes, we tested increasing each free node with exactly zero order while holding all other nodes fixed. The four saved grids contained 1,004 such directions. In 577, the small increase creates wave amplitudes on nodes with no previous shear support. We solved the first-order balance for those amplitudes before evaluating the occupied frequency shift. Every single-node energy derivative was nonnegative at the stated precision. The lowest on the refined intermediate shape was consistent with zero. Direct forward steps at two sizes corroborated the sign at the minimum and at the largest correction on each grid. The result tests individual reopening directions.
