# Can local material connections give a draining mouth a signed unit?

**A cut can reconnect completely and remain neutral. A different local interaction can select a signed unit.** In the second example, neighboring patches of ordered material compare their internal orientations. The comparison around a small triangle has a measurable phase. Around a round mouth the phases sum to one turn on one slab face and minus one on the other. A bound pair connecting the faces gives a microscopic reason that their opposite phase changes can survive together. Interpreting this material count as observed electric charge still requires a three-dimensional material phase and a physical force readout. ### What ordinary bonds do at an opening

At a disk-shaped cut, swap the partners of directed bonds between a finite brane bank and a receiving bank. A bijection of old bond tails to old heads preserves the incoming and outgoing counts at every site. At radii of 1, 2 and 4 lattice spacings the cut intercepted 5, 13 and 49 bonds per bank. In each mirror preparation one unit of prescribed material throughput reached the receiver after repair. The signed change in bond divergence and its flux through the outer brane surface were both zero.

Omitting one reconnection gave opposite endpoints at the port, with zero flux through the outer brane surface. Extending a missing chain beyond the enclosing section produced one brane-surface unit and a compensating remote endpoint. This control tests the distinction between a local imbalance, transfer through the port, and a distant reading. The graph has a prescribed routing and supplies no electrostatic energy. ### A microscopic rule with a different outcome

Give each ordered patch a complex two-component internal state \(z\) with \(z^\dagger z=1\). Its direction \(\mathbf p=z^\dagger\boldsymbol\sigma z\) is defined in a common material frame. A patch prefers \(\mathbf p\) along the projected material normal \(\mathbf n\). A directed bond has a freely variable angle \(a_{ij}\) and prefers the phase of its neighbors' overlap \(v_{ij}=z_i^\dagger z_j\). The assumed site and bond energies are
\[
 J(1-\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf p_i),\qquad
 K\big(|v_{ij}|-\operatorname{Re}[e^{-ia_{ij}}v_{ij}]\big).
\]
The explicit \(|v|\) term makes aligned-bond energy zero for any overlap. Changing it changes the cost of deforming the texture. Local rephasing of the site basis also rephases the incident links and preserves all loop measurements.

For a closed triangulation, sum the oriented principal angles of the physical links around its triangles and divide by \(2\pi\). This gives the signed integer \(n\). At preferred alignment the triangle angle is half the solid angle swept by the three normals, as follows from the complex phase of \(\mathrm{Tr}(P_iP_jP_k)\), \(P_i=(1+\mathbf n_i\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/2\). This is a mismatch in transporting an internal phase around a loop. Every bond can sit at its preferred angle. The ordinary bond-divergence control above counts a different quantity. [Geometric lattice degree](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/055032138190568X) · [Independent projector calculation](../tools/mouth_material_links.wl)

Use round sections of \(w_s(r)=sR^2/r\), with \(s=\pm1\). In a common positive-\(w\) normal convention, the negative slope direction is \(s\hat{\mathbf r}\). With the same prescribed inward rate \(0.25\), the two sections yielded \(+1,-1\) at radii 1 and 2. Doubling the radius reduced the magnitude per unit area from \(0.5\) to \(0.125\). The independent Wolfram construction also obtained one turn at radius 3. All three meshes, with 8, 32 and 128 triangles in that calculation, gave consistent counts. Uniform local orientation or a tilt large enough to remove the wrapping gave zero. Free link configurations with zero and multiple units are available on the same geometry.

**Finite attachment.** Both the directions and link angles were allowed to change in the Python energy bound. Adopt a positive source-free exterior field cost with minimum \(C n^2/R\), realized by the radial field, and identify its flux through each cell with the resolved principal angle. This assumption excludes unobserved full turns of exterior flux per cell. With \(J=150,\ K=20,\ C=1\) in common model units, a wrong sector costs at least \(3/2\) per such face. The unit trial costs 1 at \(R=1\) and \(1/2\) at \(R=2\). For this bound, any site tilt with \(1-\cos\alpha\ge1/100\) already costs \(3/2\). Below that tilt all neighboring overlaps exceed \(3/5\), and the real parts of the three-site projector traces stay positive throughout the deformation from the preferred texture. Changing sector must then move some bond at least \(\pi/6\) from its preferred angle. That costs at least \(12-6\sqrt3>3/2\). The fixed-ray Wolfram control with a weaker bond interaction selects zero at its tested radii. These are finite-parameter examples. The strengths, the common internal frame and its coupling to physical slope need independent material measurements. [Free-site calculation](../tools/mouth_material_links.py)

### Which of the two faces does a probe see?

Use the physical outward normals on two parallel faces displaced by the same mouth profile. Their counts in a common brane coordinate orientation are \((s,-s)\). Counting each protruding face relative to its *own* outward normal gives \(+1\) for either mirror. Simply adding both face counts gives zero. Either result defeats a one-face argument for electric sign.

There is a different face-symmetric coupling. For top and bottom local states, the antisymmetric combination \(v=z_t^T i\sigma_y z_b\) is invariant under a common proper internal rotation. For directions separated by angle \(\theta\), \(|v|^2=(1-\cos\theta)/2\). Thus opposite face directions maximize the local pair amplitude. The ordinary overlap \(z_t^\dagger z_b\) vanishes there. Interchanging the faces changes the sign of \(v\), a constant phase that drops out of its transport energy.

If the pair alone sustains phase stiffness in the exterior, its transport penalizes \(A_t+A_b\) and leaves the opposite combination free at long wavelength. The smallest allowed integer flux vector in the surviving compact field is \((1,-1)\). Exchanging the faces reverses this generator, so opposite mouth orientations give opposite relative signs with one common choice of probe. Taking half the phase of a relative loop would have a square-root ambiguity. Using the primitive group generator avoids it. An equal-sign product of the two face loop phases is insensitive to this field. A comparison of their difference is sensitive, and distinguishing its sign requires a phase reference such as a calibrated bias. These statements concern loop phases. A force on a stationary mouth requires a coupling to their curvature. A common comparison between distant mouths also requires a consistent registration of the two faces.

This result assumes positive, identical exterior curl and kinetic energies on the two faces. The two native radial fields have zero pair strain and together cost \(2C/R\). With independent face energies as above, changing a single face sector costs at least \(3/2\) on that face and \(C/R\) on the retained unit face. Changing both costs at least \(3\). Adding the nonnegative pair strain therefore preserves the strict sector comparison at both Python radii. Pair stiffness alone leaves two transverse polarizations of one gapless field. Ordinary-overlap stiffness alone leaves the same-face field gapless, and the opposite-flux source would require zeros of that overlap on enclosing spheres. Stiffness in both channels gaps both continuous fields. The finite local pair amplitude does not establish any of these macroscopic phases. The identity with the brane shear that carries light is open. [Two-face spectrum and flux conditions](../tools/mouth_material_face_pair.wl)

### Can the pair acquire its transport rule from the constituents?

Let each component move to a neighboring patch with an ordinary directed hopping amplitude. Separating the two costs \(\Delta>0\). Either constituent can hop first. Adding the two virtual paths gives an effective forward pair hop \(-2t_t t_b\exp[i(a_t+a_b)]/\Delta\) to leading order in \(t/\Delta\). A bottom vacancy gives the difference \(a_t-a_b\) instead. This hopping Hamiltonian has no internal spin indices. It establishes how an ordinary bound pair travels and leaves its singlet or triplet selection to the internal interaction. An isolated two-patch spectrum is independent of the link phases. Closing a periodic ring makes their effect observable. [Exact two-patch calculation](../tools/mouth_material_pair_hop.wl)

With one constituent per face, impose total twists \(+\pi/3\) on the two rings and compare with opposite twists of the same magnitude. With no binding the two energy changes agreed. At \(\Delta=16\) and unit elementary hopping, the ratio of opposite-twist cost to common-twist cost was \(0.00368970509\) on six patches and \(2.34093766\times10^{-6}\) on ten. Virtual separations still give a finite response to opposite twists on these finite rings. This tests transport of a single bound pair. It leaves the bulk stiffness at finite pair density undetermined. [Periodic-ring calculation](../tools/mouth_material_face_pair.py)

### What reaches a distant mouth?

In the assumed unscreened point-source exterior, each face has field energy \(\gamma\int |B|^2 d^3x\) and flux \(2\pi n\). Between two matched mouths, the cross term from the top face and the cross term from the bottom face add. Subtracting isolated self-energies gives \(U_{\rm ext}=4\pi\gamma s_1s_2/d\). The corresponding exterior contribution is repulsive for like mouth orientations and attractive for opposite ones. This calculation presumes both mouths carry the flux and compares positions with fixed sources. The stiffness sets its magnitude. The conversion from one material turn to a measured electric unit, physical rim traction, charge transport and gravitational acceleration remain open.

A separate control permits a receiving shell at distance \(L\) to carry compensating flux at energy \(\mu\) per unit, including its reservoir. In a single channel of fixed local count \(s=\pm1\) the spherical Maxwell energy plus that cost is \(C(1/R-1/L)+C n_{\rm far}^2/L+\mu|n_{\rm far}-s|\). The distant count is zero for \(\mu<C/L\) and \(s\) for \(\mu>C/L\), with both possible at equality. This models an available compensating source. It illustrates why the microscopic mouth count alone cannot fix a distant electric reading. [Screening controls](../tools/mouth_material_links.py)

Use independent weak twists of the face phases to measure whether common-phase stiffness survives increasing system size while opposite-phase stiffness vanishes. A paired insulator and a state that orders both individual components are competing outcomes. For any surviving long-range mode, use a mechanical shear drive and a flux-sensitive probe to determine whether they excite the same two polarizations. Neither this test nor the signed-count calculation requires assigning a mass to the mouth.
