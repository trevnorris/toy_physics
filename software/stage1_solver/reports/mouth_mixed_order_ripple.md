# Which material moves with a shear ripple?

**At inward intake 0.25, the saved throat fields locate the material at one-quarter shear order. Its native ripple speed remains undetermined.** A calculation with two passive material laws gives a way to distinguish them. They predict the same displacement, phase speed and attenuation in a uniform quarter-order patch, yet require different shear forces to produce that motion.

**What the existing model fixes.** The carrier has mass density $mn$ and the shear energy has stiffness $\mu_0\chi$. Its displacement is a separate variable. The order count $\chi n$ and the homogeneous brane kinetic term supply no mixed-order momentum measurement. The archived carrier files contain density/phase information through $\psi$, geometry and coordinates. The order files contain $\chi$. The earlier pulse histories evolved an assigned inertia. **A material hypothesis.** Suppose ordered constituents carry the restoring shear, and collisions transfer transverse momentum to the disordered fraction. Give the portions of this one material masses per volume $\rho\chi$ and $\rho(1-\chi)$, and equal and opposite drag $b(v_o-v_d)$. Take $b=\rho\chi(1-\chi)/\tau_s$, the extra assumption that the rate is proportional to both populations. Free, spatially uniform relative velocity then decays as $\exp(-t/\tau_s)$. For a driven patch with time convention $e^{-i\Omega t}$, let $x=\Omega\tau_s/\chi$ and $c_*=\sqrt{\mu_0/\rho}$. In the common background rest frame,

$$
\frac{v_d}{v_o}=\frac{1}{1-ix},\qquad
\frac{\rho_{\rm eff}}{\rho}=\chi+\frac{1-\chi}{1-ix},\qquad
k^2=\frac{\Omega^2\rho_{\rm eff}}{\mu_0\chi}.
$$

A synthetic free-slip release at half order fixed the time unit. Its value predicted the complex wavenumber in separately driven quarter- and three-quarter-order chains. The largest relative wavenumber error across six finest-chain checks was below $1.81\times10^{-5}$. SymPy and Wolfram also agreed on the dispersion and dissipation. At quarter order the phase speed progresses from $0.502c_*$ at $x=0.1$ through $0.608c_*$ at $x=1$ to $0.976c_*$ at $x=10$. At $x=1$, a traveling wave retains 17.55% of its **amplitude** per wavelength. These are predictions of the trial law. The throat's exchange time remains uncalibrated. **Why force distinguishes the mechanisms.** A second material moves its whole mass and relaxes its stress through a spring and dashpot. Over the tested order interval it has dynamic modulus $\mu_{\rm mem}=\mu_0\chi\rho/\rho_{\rm eff}$. This gives precisely the same complex $k$. A positive extra spring fades to zero below quarter order so that both bulk models lose shear at zero order. This continuation is illustrative. In a 180-bond patch driven to the same end displacement, with the other end fixed, the stress-memory material needed **1.372 times the force amplitude** at $\chi=1/4,\ x=1$. Its complex force relative to the slip model had phase $-30.96^\circ$. The displacement fields agreed to within $7.4\times10^{-14}$ of the drive amplitude. The measured quantity is material traction at the driven bond. Extending this comparison to a mouth requires isolating the same traction and subtracting actuator and support loads.
