# Force after a shear contact reconnects near one draining mouth

**The surrounding material can generate a measurable force after a break.** I used the saved near-entrance state of one held-open, flared throat at inward intake 0.25. A localized patch centered at $r=0.27R$, $w=0.62761R$ has quarter order at its center (weighted patch mean 0.26056 on the finer mesh). Three mobile features start at rest. Elastic forces and relative-motion drag between neighbors determine where each feature moves while its contact with the displaced boundary is open. The calculation supplies the contact schedule. It solves for the displacement and the subsequent force. Forces are percentages of the initial intact reaction to the same weak displacement. Times use the model clock. Hold the displacement for 0.5, open the contact once, reconnect it unloaded at the feature’s evolved position, and read both channels another 0.5 later. While detached, the closest feature returned farther toward rest during the long gap. Its displacement at reconnection was 32.24% of the applied step after the short gap and 13.87% after the long one. The support continued to move after reconnection, loading the fresh contact.

**Prediction and control.** The calibration consists of the intact reaction versus time after a small step. For an unchanged linear material, the response to a sequence of steps is their sum. During a break, choose the effective boundary displacement that gives zero force. At reconnection, hold its last value. The predictor receives only the intact reaction, timing and applied step. It reads no gap trajectory or force. As a control I multiplied drag on the first neighbor pair by four only while detached, preserving the entire intact calibration. The table shows where the prediction then fails. The control’s order-inferred forces were 11.46% and 6.02%. The weak-drive comparison includes mesh 34 and 48 checks and opposite-sign, half-strength loading.

**How the two instruments work.** One instrument sums the elastic and viscous force at the fixed far support. In this overdamped serial network it balances the force on the contact. The second makes three copies of the evolved mesh and material state. After opposite and zero small shear increments it reads the change of order at the same place. The energy of each elastic element is proportional to the local order times its squared extension. This gives the three-rate diagnostic a nonzero curvature. The native advected order equation receives the elastic energy, and the evolving order updates the effective spring stiffness. This run evolved both fields throughout loading, release, reconnection and the probe windows. The archived longitudinal flow and throat geometry stayed fixed. The numerical reaction is normalized per effective patch volume. The shared material law links the two measurements. **0PN requirement.** An intact response curve can predict a later force if neighboring material continues responding in the same way across release. The experiment gives a way to detect a change in that response without resolving individual contact lifetimes. The three-node network, elastic order dependence and stress-free reconnection are hypotheses. An independent substrate realization or measurement must establish which response persists during actual conversion, the absolute contact load, and how its directional momentum reaches a free mouth. The test supplies no free-fall acceleration.
