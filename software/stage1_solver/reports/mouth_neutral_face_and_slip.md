# Can a converting surface keep its attraction and grip a passing stream?

At radius 1 and intake 0.25, the earlier release test used the energy of a constituent bound to the surface and, independently, a trial drag rate of 5.5 per model time. We tested the corresponding force measurements separately. A neutral material with intact bonds retains almost all its mean surface attraction as shear order falls. Its constituent recoils after a sideways kick. A weak density disturbance in the cold carrier supplies zero steady drag at the sampled flow speeds. The rate that would act between a bound constituent and the stream in the converting mouth remains open. [Earlier surface work and release](mouth_bound_site_transfer.md)

**Normal force.** We used two periodic layers of sites at fixed projected density. Lowering the uniform shear order from 0.875299 to 0.5 weakens the external normal support. Both microscopic pair potentials retain their strength. The sites can move laterally and normally. A weak test site, sampling those same potentials, measures the upward force required to hold it 1.1 microscopic lengths above the instantaneous mean upper face. We average over lateral position and include both faces. Each warm preparation at patch temperature 0.008 had four transit times to settle and two measurement windows of two transits each. In one alternate-interaction run the half-order mean gap moved from 0.957 to 0.853 between read windows, so the near-unity force ratios describe the sampled histories. Full equilibration remains open. The previous clamped-sheet predictions were 0.571233 for weakening every contact and 1 for preserving mean attraction. **Sideways impulse.** On the registered cold patch we kicked one upper-face constituent and gave the rest equal total counter-momentum. Full-strength and half-strength kicks of both signs agreed with the independently calculated linear vibration modes. For 32 sites with the primary interaction, the constituent’s velocity relative to the center of mass of all other sites first reversed at 0.005336 model time upstream and 0.005348 at half order. The 72-site values were 0.005404 and 0.005416. These times assume equal site masses and the earlier microscopic length and energy conversion. The reversal itself persists under a change of clock. This measures elastic recoil of a member of the sheet. A drag rate between an adsorbed site and flowing carrier requires their relative motion and exchanged momentum. **Can the cold carrier supply that drag?** We tested a weak reciprocal coupling of a probe to the carrier density. We took the local density and tangential velocity from each cut and treated its neighborhood as uniform, cold, and unbounded in four spatial dimensions. The native enthalpy is \(h(n)=(n^4-1)/4\). Its linear density waves obey \(\Omega_k^2=n h'(n)k^2+k^4/4=n^4k^2+k^4/4\). A steadily moving probe can radiate only when \(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf V=\Omega_k\), with \(\mathbf V\) its velocity relative to the stream. The smallest wave phase speed is \(c=n^2\). On mesh 34 a stationary site saw Mach 0.04286 upstream and 0.05352 at half order. Both meshes gave zero steady linear radiation drag for either sign of that slip and for half-strength slip. At Mach 1.25 the probe experienced a finite opposing force for both signs. An independent reconstruction bounded the Mach number below 0.056 across the entire interval on both meshes. A closed-form evaluation reproduced the signed outgoing force and power to within \(1.74\times10^{-18}\) in the test normalization. Finite-time disturbances, thermally populated modes and the actual converting interface can transfer momentum by other mechanisms. **0PN stopping point.** The previous gravity tests established an inverse-square incident flow in the far field. To predict how much of its momentum the deeper mouth retains, the missing measurement is the force *along the surface* on the same bound constituent when carrier and host velocities can be changed independently. At each order, hold the host and site fixed, reverse the weak stream, subtract the zero-flow force and repeat at half speed. Separately reverse the host velocity at fixed carrier and site velocities, comparing the force at the same relative site positions to control for elastic restoring force. Check for equal and opposite momentum transfer and account for outgoing energy before using the result in a free-mouth release. A changing binding energy, a restoring force from neighbors and steady drag have different signatures in this test. The present material and carrier calculations have no independently calibrated interaction at that interface.
