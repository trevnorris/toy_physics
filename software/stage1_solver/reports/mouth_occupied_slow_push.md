# How strongly does a draining mouth accelerate under a sideways push?

**At 0PN the stronger of two candidate wall tensions gave 8.19% more entrance acceleration per force in the slow inertial response.** Moving the outer boundaries twice as far away sharply reduced their restoring force and barely changed the difference. The two-separation companion test depended on finding a common push calibration. That condition remains open.

We used the same round mouth of radius 1, cubic flare, inward intake 0.25, and bound wave action $J=1$. The carrier flows from the feeding brane into the fourth-direction reservoir in the stationary solution. Each remote reservoir stayed at its unperturbed density and phase during the sideways push. Only the entrance received an external force. Both relative bends responded freely under wave and area stresses. The test used a slow harmonic push in linear response with no assigned wall mass. Both trial walls use the prescribed reference shape. Independent selection of the stationary shape, wave action and wall stress remains open. **What moved.** At angular frequency 0.0025 in the largest bath, the weaker wall’s entrance, middle and flare gave in-phase accelerations per force of 0.0592582, 0.0594515 and 0.0594673. The stronger wall gave 0.0642819, 0.0643666 and 0.0643778. Including phase, the entrance–flare differences were 2.4250% and 1.0321%. Halving the push frequency from 0.005 reduced those differences from 5.0545% and 2.1375%. The full coupled solver checked opposite forces and both half-strength forces at 0.0025. This checks the linear equations and their implementation. [Push calculation](../tools/mouth_occupied_slow_push.py)

**Why the gap survives.** For harmonic displacement $X$ the entrance requires $F/X=k_{\rm bath}-\omega^2 M_{\rm eff}+O(\omega^4)$. At fixed bath size and sufficiently low frequency, the bath spring dominates. We extracted the inertia from this coefficient and checked it with direct harmonic solves. The wave calculation enforces translation symmetry for the common displacement. The rigid-translation inertia in the largest bath is 14.72913. Allowing the draining flow and the two bends to respond to sideways velocity adds a term of 2.23415 at the weaker tension and 0.95010 at the stronger one. The relative bend is proportional to sideways velocity at first order. Its time derivative therefore contributes a force proportional to acceleration, even as the motion at each depth approaches common translation. The baseline coarser mesh and coarser wave discretization gave an 8.1889% gap. Enlarged reservoirs retained every original mesh node. These finite-domain results leave the open-bath limit unresolved. [Reservoir construction](../tools/mouth_occupied_push_baths.py)

**Where the momentum went.** At 0.005 in the largest bath, the weaker-wall carrier and wave took up 0.85625 and 0.14585 units of in-phase momentum per unit time and applied force. The feeding and receiving reservoirs supplied another 0.00199 and 0.00012 to the system. At the stronger tension those four readings were 0.84449, 0.15779, 0.00217 and 0.00013. Storage plus net outward flux reproduced the unit push within 0.000033 in complex amplitude across all three fine baths. A separate direct pressure integral on the moving wall had a larger residual, up to 0.00714 on the fine baths. [Storage and flux integrals](../tools/mouth_occupied_push_flux.py)

**Next independent measurement.** At the same intake and wave preparation, fix the entrance and middle, displace the flare slightly sideways, and read the force applied at the flare for both displacement directions. On the original bath the two candidate laws predict force per displacement of **0.2159234** and **0.3676533**. An independently specified material calculation or measured response can distinguish them or reject both.
