# Can a local change in shear order carry a bound site into the mouth?

**A passing stream can pull on a held site through the disturbance it leaves in shear order. In the tested local model that pull is too weak to carry the site through even the first part of conversion.** This is a one-site test at radius 1 and intake 0.25. The force on a released mouth remains open.

**What couples the site to the stream.** The two existing adsorption models give a specific binding energy μ that depends on shear order χ. We hypothesized that the site samples χ over a finite neighborhood, and exerts the reciprocal force on that same field. We used each model’s measured slope s = dμ/dχ, a common Gaussian footprint of width 0.01 mouth radii, and the existing passive relaxation equation for χ. The local tests used a four-dimensional periodic cell of side 0.12 with its mean order held fixed. At half order a freely varying uniform state is unstable. [Adsorption measurements](mouth_bound_site_transfer.md) · [Order dynamics in the curved mouth](mouth_free_order_depth.md) · [Related theory of drag from field relaxation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0132)

**Which motion produces a force?** With the site held still, we reversed the stream and then the sheet, at full and half speed in a homogeneous sample. The two drives represent a comparison at equal site–sheet registry. The calculation holds the binding slope fixed and resolves only the force from shear order. If the stream transports the order disturbance, reversing the stream reverses the site force. Moving only the sheet gives zero force from this coupling. Assigning order transport to the sheet interchanges those readings. The model predicts this difference. Its physical transport velocity still needs measurement. An independent spatial calculation recovered the opposite force on the transporting material and the heat generated by maintaining the flow. At the upstream cut the force doubled to within 0.0001% when the speed doubled. [Signed force and energy calculation](../tools/mouth_order_slip.py)

**How large can the response be?** Let A be the increase in static order sampled by the site relative to the same empty material. Upstream the relaxation rates satisfy λ ≥ ν k², with ν = 0.4. For every regular, nonnegative footprint the steady force per site mass obeys |F|/m ≤ |s| A |U−v|/ν, where U and v are the transporting material and site velocities. The physical range of χ gives A ≤ 1−χ. Even granting that entire range, the respective upstream rate ceilings are approximately 0.01706 and 0.01899 per model time for the two surface laws. The previous illustrative crossing and confinement comparison used 5.5. This ceiling applies to the locally uniform weak-response law. The curved conversion front has a different spectrum.

**First inward passage.** We gave the site its upstream flow speed of 0.042501, or 0.00090317 kinetic energy per unit mass. For the earlier one-coordinate model we allowed the relaxed disturbance to lower the binding energy and added the local steady stream force. On a first forward excursion, A ≤ ε limits the work delivered by the stream to the site to ε |s at entry| Δθ/ν. Here Δθ is the carrier phase rise along the wall. The microscopic energy is linear in χ at fixed site positions. Relaxing positions on the stable adsorption branch makes the binding energy concave in χ, which bounds the slope magnitude by its entry value on this interval. The relaxed order field lowers the specific energy by |s|A/2. The same site mass sets its static distortion and its force at every depth. The upper bound lets the static disturbance reach full order at its peak at entry. Both energy deficits persisted when we changed the carrier mesh, host size, Fourier resolution, cell size and footprint width. This excludes passage under the stated local, instantaneously relaxed force law. The test does not propagate the actual spatially varying front or its stored momentum.

**Implication for free fall.** Increasing the coupling to the local disturbance cannot rescue this first passage within the tested law. A mouth made of many constituents could still receive momentum without carrying this site through conversion. The independent discriminator is the change in the actual conversion profile with and without one bound site, at the same intake and registry. Read the spatial extent, and whether a weak disturbance travels with the stream or the sheet. A collective shift of the conversion layer would require computing the stress it transmits to the held mouth and the momentum carried downstream. The current material tests prescribe the mean order and cannot supply that measurement. [Measurements and independent reviews](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_order_slip.json)
