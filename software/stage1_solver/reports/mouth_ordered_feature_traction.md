# How much sideways force reaches a held feature of the ordered wall?

A candidate lining made of staggered rows exerted a restoring force when we displaced it underneath a fixed stream of arriving material. When we moved the arriving material sideways with the lining, the displacement left the reaction unchanged. When we let a bend change the incoming flow all the way from upstream, even the sign of the load on the deep rows was unresolved. The calculation therefore supplies a way to discriminate between physical attachment laws. The force on a freely moving mouth remains open.

**What we held and measured.** We used the saved radius-one mouth at intake 0.25. We let rows of the compression-calibrated material relax, read the position of their periodic density peak independently of the passing material, and held their staggered arrangement. Cold sites entered at depth 1.175 with uniform transverse positions and zero transverse speed. The interaction with the rows developed their joint distribution of position and speed by depth 1.55. We copied those same arrivals for each downstream test, followed them to depth 1.65 and summed the reciprocal pair forces on every row within reach. The held feature is a proposed microscopic lining. Its normal gap and axial flow were prescribed. On mesh 34, the local shear order fell from 0.00037731 at 1.55 to 0.00002480 at 1.65. Keeping ordered wall sites identifiable there is a material assumption. The archived wave preparation was common to the runs. The wave and the wall were not released together. The slope is the difference in external holding force for opposite steps, divided by their displacement difference and the mass flux of the participating material. Its unit is inverse model time. A positive slope means the incident material pushes back against the imposed displacement. The table uses 32,768 incoming positions, carrier mesh 34 and 512 force-table intervals per pitch. Across the six numerical settings, the full-step fixed-supply slope varied from +0.000267913 to +0.000464984. These are observed numerical differences, so the sign is more reproducible than the magnitude. A separate inlet-velocity perturbation at fixed wall position also changed the reaction. This measures momentum exchange and does not establish dissipation. The external force holding the rows, divided by the participating mass flux, agreed with the difference between the mean outgoing and incoming transverse velocities to within \(2.33\times10^{-16}\) pitches per model time. Reflected runs agreed in normal-force history to within \(1.00\times10^{-15}\) in the readout's normalized units.

**Letting the bend affect the incoming material.** A second experiment displaced each row by the archived bend profile at its own depth, kept the supply fixed at 1.175 and followed each trajectory continuously through 1.55. We summed the external holding forces on the 12 rows centered at or beyond 1.55, including interactions from either side of that section. The slopes per unit participating flux and imposed bend amplitude were +0.001243 and +0.000963 at full and half steps on mesh 34 with the finer force table. On carrier mesh 26 they were −0.000763 and −0.000288. We cannot assign a sign or infer zero from this comparison. This sum gives the net load on the deep rows. A force conjugate to the bend amplitude would weight each row by its own displacement shape. [Continuous bend calculation](../tools/mouth_ordered_bend_transport.py)

**What would advance free fall.** The equations for this trial give the reaction once we specify what moves with the wall. A physical determination still requires a persistent feature attached to the mouth and the flux of material actually interacting with it. The discriminating measurement is to move the bend slightly in both directions at fixed intake, locate a peak in the ordered constituent density upstream without pinning it to the wall, and follow it through conversion. Track its motion and the arriving material relative to the macroscopic bend while reading the reciprocal force. That measures how much of the wall displacement the stream inherits. Only once the attachment and its load through depth are established can we predict an independent push or a free release.
