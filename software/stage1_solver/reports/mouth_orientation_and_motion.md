# Which material moves with a signed mouth?

A spherical mouth that retains its orientable surface material can predict the wave it emits under small prescribed axial motion. The essential input is whether the same material sites stay attached as the aperture moves. Site retention, surface elasticity and the microscopic angular dynamics are hypotheses. The current draining solver records the aperture and carrier flow. Its saved drives contain no measurement of how those orientation sites move or renew. [Earlier moving-source test](moving_mirror_mouths.md) · [Saved material observables](one_mouth_hold_release_requirements.md) · [Guarded measurements and reviews](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_orientation_and_motion.json)

### Does the material have to follow?

Let a sphere of radius \(R\) move by \(X\hat z\). A site stays on that sphere while sliding a distance \(u P_n\hat z\) relative to the geometric surface, where \(P_n\) projects onto the tangent plane. A marked latitude initially at \(\theta\) moves to \(\theta-u\sin\theta/R\). The fractional change in its local area is \(-2u\cos\theta/R\). If sites stay in the layer, their fractional number-density change is therefore \(+2u\cos\theta/R\). A pointwise area-incompressible, locally retained layer must have \(u=0\) for this axial dipole. Toroidal motions are outside this drive. A locally renewing layer can keep the density fixed with a net addition rate \(-2n_s\dot u\cos\theta/R\), where \(n_s\) is the resting site density. That rate averages to zero over the sphere. A reading of total intake cannot tell these cases apart.

For finite area modulus \(K_A>0\) and site mass per physical area \(\Sigma\), the dipole dilation has an uncoupled angular frequency \(\sqrt{2K_A/(\Sigma R^2)}\). Both parameters can be fixed on a patch by weighing its constituents and measuring its area strain under a known load. The calculation varied the site displacement, the angular response of the sites on both faces and the outgoing field together on **one isolated sphere**. The two angular references use the same bond endpoints. Material sliding pulls back their coincident background phases equally at radial equilibrium. The remaining coupling comes from changing the direction of the local preferred orientation over time. The matrix from integrating site velocities and areas agreed with a calculation using displaced latitude rings. With the external angular slip held fixed, the additional torque per unit translation from sliding is proportional to \(\omega^4\) at low frequency. The usual moving-source wave is still present. ### What fixes the angular response?

Each face has an internal two-state orientation. For a conditional microscopic test we gave two such states the local Hamiltonian
\[
 H=J\,\mathbf S_t\cdot\mathbf S_b-h_0\eta\,\hat n\cdot(\mathbf S_t-\mathbf S_b).
\]
Here \(h_0>0\) aligns the faces in opposite directions, \(J\ge0\) favors opposite orientations, and the prepared mouth sign is \(\eta=\pm1\). The bond senses the dominant eigenray of each face's reduced density matrix. For an entangled pair that is a separate material assumption, undefined when the local polarization vanishes. The ground-state staggered polarization is \(p=h_0/\sqrt{(J/2)^2+h_0^2}\), and the transverse transition energy is \(\Delta=J/2+\sqrt{(J/2)^2+h_0^2}\). Both can be checked on a held patch. A four-state density-matrix calculation and an exact symbolic reduction gave the transverse susceptibility
\[
 \chi_\perp(\omega)=\frac{p}{h_0}\frac{\Delta^2}{\Delta^2-(\hbar\omega)^2}.
\]
For one pair per unit physical area this gives a link restoring coefficient \(h=4R^2p h_0\) and angular inertia \(m=h(\hbar/\Delta)^2\). A second restoring coefficient \(B\) is the independently specified bond stiffness. The induced common transverse spin need not vanish. For this axial drive its common link shift is a surface gradient removable by the assumed freely equilibrating site rolls. The tested frequencies are off the isolated transition and the model has no material damping. [Four-state and two-mouth solver](../tools/mouth_pair_constituent_response.py) · [Linear-response method](https://doi.org/10.1143/JPSJ.12.570)

A separate classical calculation assigned equal first-order phase actions to the two orientable faces. Allowing a small departure from antiparallel alignment produced positive orientational inertia. Constraining exact antipodality canceled the first-order forms. For one tested preparation the extra angular slip per unit translation was +0.039148504 or −0.039148504 on ambient mirror textures and zero on carried sites. All three gave the same held angular response of 0.187255924. A distinct mechanical control put known constituent masses at offsets from marked pivots. Its translation–rotation coefficient was the difference of the two signed first mass moments. Swapping the attachments reversed that coefficient while preserving the masses and angular inertia. Neither a precessing internal state nor a bearing with a particular face assignment follows from the carrier equation. ### What distinguishes the alternatives on a held mouth?

In a single-mouth calibration, impose a weak dipolar angular displacement \(Y\sin\theta\) with the geometric center held still. Let \(\tau\) be the dipole amplitude of the bond torque per area and \(s\) the corresponding reference slip. If a fixture transmits site displacements equal to a prescribed fraction \(f\) of the local tangential center displacement, the applied holding force in excess of a matched fully carried fixture is
\[
 \Delta F_{\rm hold}=\eta\frac{4\pi}{3}(1-f)(\tau-hs).
\]
The comparison uses the same signed background, spin spectrum, exterior drive and force path. Reversing the drive alone also reverses the ordinary exterior reaction. The formula vanishes for a static twist. Finite virtual displacements of the marked sites gave the same result. For a fixture specified to carry 40% of the displacement, the held response at 0.65 of the transition frequency recovered a lag fraction of 0.6. It then predicted the mirror and half-amplitude checks at other frequencies. This is a synthetic test with controlled attachments. A real force reading would need the matched subtraction or an independently characterized exterior and fixture. [Held force and sign controls](../tools/mouth_held_orientation_reaction.py)

A separate pair experiment prescribed the material registration at **both** mouths. Two mirror spheres at separation \(4R\) moved oppositely by \(0.01R\) each. At \(R=5\) and 1.15 of the transition frequency the ambient registration gave **4.11165758** times the carried equatorial angular amplitude, with **3.14159215 radians** relative phase. The integrated power ratio was **16.9129165**. The phase refers to the outgoing angular displacement after removing propagation phase, with the positive mouth's motion as reference. The contact law from local spectroscopy and the joint four-state response agreed on complex amplitudes using the same exterior solver. This pair test uses an imposed local rule relaying the tagged orientations to the geometric cut. Its linear contact coefficients are evaluated about radial orientations. The induced static field is included, but advection and changes of stiffness from a nonuniform equilibrium tilt are outside that approximation. The largest sampled static tilt at \(R=5\) was 0.00825930971 radians at degree 10. It supplies no nonlinear error bound near a cancellation. Pair radiation for a freely sliding or renewing layer remains open. [Pair calculation](../tools/mouth_pair_constituent_response.py)

### What does this supply for 0PN?

There is a calculable contribution to the inertia of a **single isolated prepared mouth**. Let \(q\) be the signed material flux, \(C\) the angular field modulus, and \(c\) its transverse wave speed. The radial static field is \(D_r=q/(4\pi r^2)\). A slow translation changes the exterior azimuthal angle by \(X D_r\sin\theta\). Integrating its kinetic energy and independently expanding the outgoing force about rigid translation gave
\[
 M_{\rm angular,ext}=\frac{C}{c^2}\int_{r>R}D_r^2\sin^2\theta\,d^3x
 =\frac{Cq^2}{6\pi c^2 R}.
\]
The slow-force check includes the in-phase prestress required for zero force under static translation. This coefficient describes slow harmonic forcing in the zero-frequency limit. The initial acceleration after a sudden force step is a distinct observable. This exterior term is even in the sign of the mouth and halves when the radius doubles at fixed flux. A locally retained layer separately contributes its actual mass \(4\pi R^2\Sigma\) in the regular slow limit. The components depend differently on radius. For a candidate intended to reproduce universal free fall, both belong in the same force and inertia accounting, with any compensating material terms counted once. The calculation contains no companion drain load, throat-interior momentum, or identification of the angular flux unit with physical electric charge.
