# Do many cross-face pairs sustain a long-range field that brane shear can carry?

**The required phase stiffness can occur at finite density in three spatial dimensions. A separate light test exposes a symmetry conflict.** Several specified material models resist a common twist of the two face phases while their resistance to opposite twists vanishes in large boxes. With physical face exchange as an exact symmetry, a wave that moves the two faces together has zero linear response in the signed flux of the proposed electric field. Both statements concern the resting medium. The calculations supply no measured constants for a physical brane. ### What the twist experiment measures

Give a periodic cube of side \(L\) a total boundary phase twist \(\Theta\) along one brane direction. The top and bottom constituents experience twists \((\Theta,\Theta)\) in the common test and \((\Theta,-\Theta)\) in the opposite test. A cross-face pair picks up the sum. The stiffness \(Y_\pm=L^{-1}\partial_\Theta^2 F|_0\) uses the curvature of the **total** equilibrium free energy at each finite \(L\), followed by the large-box limit. Lattice spacing and Boltzmann's constant are one. A nonzero stiffness means a weak twist costs energy on macroscopic length scales. This experiment keeps the probe phases externally set. It does not directly measure propagating shear, a transverse wave speed or the force on a mouth. Paired phases of two-component lattice matter have independent precedents. [Kuklov, Prokof’ev and Svistunov](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.92.030403)

### Fixed constituent number with mobile pairs and free constituents

Use an ideal Bose mixture with one mobile species on each face and a mobile bound pair. The constituent hopping is 1, pair hopping is \(1/8\), and the pair band minimum is \(1/4\) below two free constituents. Each face contains exactly \(L^3/8\) constituents, counting those inside pairs. These are illustrative band parameters. The binding relative to the band minima is distinct from the earlier two-patch separation energy. Pairs are explicit particles of this effective model. Assuming chemical equilibrium, sum the canonical partition functions over every allowed pair count \(p\), with \(N-p\) unbound constituents on each face. This includes unbound constituents that can respond to opposite twists.

With condensate densities \(n_{a0}\) per face and \(n_{p0}\) for pairs, \(Y_-=4t_a n_{a0}\) and \(Y_+=Y_-+8t_p n_{p0}\). At the chosen density the pair transition is at \(T=0.179719634873\). At \(T=0.12\) the pair condensate density is \(0.0617784069625\). At \(T=0.20\), the original expectation of common stiffness failed. The bulk unpaired density is only \(0.00139705579555\) per face per site, so around 98.9% of constituents are still paired. Both stiffnesses vanish. Favoring mobile unbound constituents by changing the pair band minimum instead gives \(Y_+=Y_-=0.490105874551\) at \(T=0.12\). These ideal-gas calculations give an equilibrium twist response. ### Independent interacting models

* **Constituent attraction.** Two mobile fermionic species on a cubic lattice have hopping \(t\), on-site attraction \(4t\), and fixed mean total filling \(0.8\). A homogeneous Hartree–BCS calculation solves both the gap and the density equation. At \(T/t=0.15\), it gives \(Y_+/t=0.563348632583\) on \(64^3\) sites. The opposite result is within roundoff of zero there and vanishes exactly in the bulk saddle by periodic momentum translation. At \(T/t=1\) both vanish. Re-solving the twisted finite-size free energy checked the curvatures. Immobile constituents provide a separate control with strong local pairing and exactly zero response to either twist. The model uses different statistics and parameters from the ideal gas, and its many-body result is approximate. [Constituent calculation](../tools/mouth_pair_bulk_bcs.py)
* **Exact interacting example.** A fixed-number hard-core pair model at a tuned nearest-neighbor exchange point gives \(Y_+=8t_p\nu(1-\nu)\), \(Y_-=0\) at zero temperature and filling \(0<\nu<1\). The pair-only Hilbert space already enforces blindness to opposite twists. The positive common stiffness follows from the ground state. The tuning requires extra attraction beyond elementary pair hopping, the ground energy is linear in pair number, and more attraction favors phase separation. An insulator of disconnected, pinned dimers at finite filling has both stiffnesses zero. Independently mobile ordered faces give positive responses to both twists. These controls separate pair mobility, pair coherence and uniform stability. [Exact calculation](../tools/mouth_pair_bulk_exact.wl)

The three calculations give different kinds of evidence for a constitutive possibility. They neither measure the brane's equilibrium state nor establish that the microscopic throat chooses this sector. A positive \(Y_+\) and vanishing \(Y_-\) supply the proposed phase-stiffness pattern. A massless field further requires the exterior gauge and curl dynamics assumed in the mouth test.

### Can ordinary transverse shear be that same electric wave?

Reflect \(w\to-w\) while using the same \(x,y,z\) frame for both faces. The ordinary long-wavelength transverse displacement of the flat slab is even: at any given brane position the faces move together. The proposed primitive electric connection is the opposite-face combination. Ordinary face exchange reverses it, its local signed in-brane curl, and the calibrated flux of a mouth. A travelling neutral wave is read with a small oriented patch. A mouth's total charge is read through a closed brane sphere. The shared fact is how the signed readings transform. [Flat-slab displacement and its first odd mode](../../em_charge_attribute/reports/flat_mixed_shear.md)

At fixed in-brane wavevector, frequency and reflection-invariant material state, the linear response matrix commutes with reflection. Its even-to-odd block is zero. A whole-slab shear drive therefore has exactly zero transfer into signed electric flux, even if two independent waves happen to have the same speed. A separate check allowed face exchange to conjugate the connection as well. The relative field then becomes even and a shear response is allowed, but the mirror mouth retains the **same** signed flux. The two cases exhaust invertible sign actions on a single compact flux generator with fixed calibration. This excludes simultaneous identification of ordinary even shear with the signed electric wave and reversal of mouth charge in this symmetric realization. Flipping the detector or applying a drive to only one face changes the experiment. ### Does motion in opposite directions across the slab carry the energy?

An odd displacement \(v\) can respond to an odd field in the same symmetric material. Test a thickness mode with inertia \(\rho\), in-plane shear modulus \(G\), and a positive spring energy \(\kappa|v-\lambda B|^2/2\). Here \(B=\nabla\times A\) is the signed curl of the surviving connection in the dual description, with energy \((b|\dot A|^2+K|B|^2)/2\). All coefficients are fixed and positive, and both quantities are measured in the same in-brane frame. On the long-wave, gapless branch, \(v/B\to\lambda\). A calibrated face-sensitive displacement sensor could therefore track the field. But the **fraction of in-plane wave power transported by the ordinary shear stress of \(v\)** is \(G\lambda^2 k^2/K+O(k^4)\). It tends to zero. Counting the spring energy once gives the same quadratic suppression for the fraction of energy in the ordinary kinetic and in-plane strain terms of \(v\). The conserved total current divided by the total energy agrees with the group velocity. Matching the bare speeds still gives vanishing mechanical fractions. The calculation leaves the microscopic origin of the energy in \(A\) open. [Energy and power calculation](../tools/mouth_pair_odd_shear_energy.py)

**What this means for the common material.** Keep cross-face pairing as a candidate for the electric source. The finite-density test supplies a possible equilibrium phase. The stronger identification with ordinary through-thickness-uniform optical shear fails in a reflection-symmetric resting slab. The next question is whether a physically specified internal motion reverses under face exchange and itself carries the long-wave energy. A test must identify which constituent coordinates move, what restores them, and which stress transports the power. If instead the material state distinguishes its two faces, recompute both the signed mouth reading and the optical spectrum in that state. The symmetry argument assumes a reflection-invariant state. None of the twist or wave measurements calibrates a free mouth's 0PN acceleration.
