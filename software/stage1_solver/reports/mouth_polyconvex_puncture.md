# Can a shallow draining puncture support itself?

**Neither tested mouth is in full force balance.** Both can balance the depth, waist and one order variation under an assumed permanent bond. Narrowing the downstream exit still lowers the energy. Radius 1 also tends to reorder locally. Its fate as an open mouth remains unresolved. Radius 3 stays blind.

**Neutral material.** The stationary crystal and frictionless carrier share a four-dimensional material domain. For its elastic deformation \(F\), the proposed energy per reference volume is
\[
 W=\chi\left[\frac{\mu}{2}(\operatorname{tr}F^TF-4)-\mu\log J+
             \frac{K_0-\mu/2}{2}(J-1)^2\right],\qquad J=\det F>0,\quad \mu=K_0=1 .
\]

Convexity in the independent arguments \((F,J)\) gives a rank-one stiffness of at least \(\chi\mu|a|^2|b|^2\). At each order \(\chi=1,0.01,0.0001\), six hydrostatic holds spanning volume ratios \(10^{-4}\) to 300 gave positive tangents. Localized shear tests on two mixed deformations also passed. Shear vanishes at zero order. This nonlinear completion of the two existing moduli is a testable hypothesis. [Neutral material measurements](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_polyconvex_neutral.json)

**Wave and material attachment.** At unit depth the strained-crystal wave frequency fell from 7.003 to 4.141 at radius 1 and from 4.826 to 2.538 at radius 2. Beyond the waist band lay 69.05% and 82.62% of the cavity kinetic energy, respectively. The same weakly radiating mode has prepared action one. The lateral wall is sealed, with carrier and order loads on both sides, an open terminal exit and fixed reservoir phase bias. [Wave measurements](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_polycrystal_wave.json)

The pierced flat reference ends at a positive radius inside the collar. For the central region we compared two separate preparations sharing identical starting optics. One continually reforms stress-free. The other starts stress-free and subsequently stays bonded to the same collar points. Under an axial stretch with order and scaled radial profile held fixed, the predicted frequency slopes per log depth are +0.07225 and +0.05755 for reformation. With bonding they vanish at both radii. **Relaxation from one thickness.** The bonded core and collar first moved together, varying depth, exit radius, interior shape, spacing and order. Both meshes found a lower energy. We then varied depth, middle width and the amplitude of the spatial order change independently. The mouth kept draining and retained its wave. The table gives the refined outcomes in benchmark units. The last column tests a further signed variation, with positive work slope favoring a smaller exit.

| Entrance | Balanced depth / slab thickness | Minimum waist radius | Wave kinetic energy inside cavity | Energy slope per log exit radius |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

The positive curvature in these three coordinates coexists with an exit that can still lower the energy at both radii. At the initial radius-1 puncture the derivative for restoring order was negative at 6 of 321 melted sites on the finer mesh and 3 of 154 on the coarser mesh. A finite increase lowered the energy at the strongest site of each mesh. Those sites differ. The opening itself stayed finite. **Next physical test.** Mark material on both sides of the join and slowly stretch a neutral pocket axially. Measure its frequency shift and the local displacement and traction. This distinguishes slow attachment laws without gravity tuning. A local equilibrium calculation must release the remaining material and shape constraints. Deciding whether the puncture heals also requires a geometry that permits closure. Until then these shapes supply no freely measured inertia for 0PN. The independent exit variation lowers the energy toward a smaller exit on both meshes. Free equilibrium, healing, a limiting radius and 0PN inertia remain unresolved.”
