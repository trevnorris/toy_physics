# Can the ordered brane and its receiving material share a neutral interface?

The disordered receiver relaxed weak sideways shear in a homogeneous sample. To measure how much force returns to an ordered mouth, the two materials must share an opening where sites can move. Joining their saved periodic cells directly would introduce mechanical work before any flow is applied. [Full-transit shear and pressure measurements](mouth_receiver_transit.md)

**Geometry and load before joining.** We used two pairs of saved, undriven samples. Each receiver was assigned the three in-plane periods of its crystalline partner at fixed initial density, with the chosen periodic face reoriented normal to the fourth direction. The crystalline halves underwent rigid rotations. The table gives last-quarter *pre-fit* stresses on each parent's own periodic face and the change in receiver potential energy if the fit is made instantaneously. The pressure is positive in compression. The tangential column gives the magnitude of all three tangential traction components.

**Heat during preparation.** As the fitted receivers settled, the thermostat removed 247.97–295.19 bond energies from each 128-site sample of receiver A and 1,182.63–1,191.16 from each sample of receiver 41. The cell also exchanged energy as its height and tilt relaxed. The table gives the initial change in potential energy per site, while these heat totals cover the subsequent settling interval for the whole sample. The measurements establish how disruptive the abrupt fit was. They leave open what caused the temporary density dip and how much heat a converting throat would release. [Energy measurements and independent check](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_receiver_common_face.json)

**How to locate the boundary.** In the parent cells we measured the angles between bonds to neighbors within 1.30 bond lengths and averaged their degree-six angular correlation. A threshold of 0.03334, fixed using receiver A and crystal A, separated ordered and disordered material in all 768 one-bond-thick windows tested, including windows from the second pair. The windows overlap and contain 32–50 sites each. Individual site scores overlap substantially. At a junction the regional score can locate candidate ordered and disordered regions; a constituent crossing that boundary must be followed long enough to distinguish passage from a return. [Independent measurements and scope](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_receiver_common_face.json)

**Separate-material test.** Each pair had three thermal realizations per material, with matching random seeds across partners. After the abrupt fit, the height and three cell tilts could relax under zero imposed face traction. Each realization had 48.88171 native units to settle and 48.88171 for measurement. All twelve retained their expected order and connectivity. Eleven met every material criterion. In the second thermal realization of receiver 41, five successive late blocks fell below the density floor of 1.2. The lowest block averaged 1.16556. The final measurement block averaged 1.27828. The receiver stayed connected. At the end, 89.85% of its original neighbors were absent and its sites had moved 4.65 bond lengths RMS after subtracting box deformation and center motion. At zero applied pressure a finite sample can expand without bound, so this measurement tests persistence over a finite window.

Both crystals and receiver A met the full criteria in all three realizations each. Receiver 41 met them in two of three. At all 96 late observations (eight per trajectory), the largest connected group contained all 128 sites. The whole-sample ordering test and all eight spatial windows agreed with the starting phase at every one of those observations. The five low-density blocks therefore establish a temporary failure of the dense-receiver criterion. There was no observed loss of the disordered phase to reverse with a gradual fit. Those five consecutive blocks belong to a single trajectory. A future rate comparison should measure the continuous density and preparation work across independent thermal histories before drawing a conclusion about how often density falls below the threshold.

All four paired component intervals in both pairs overlapped the tolerance band and extended outside it. In the independent pair one sideways component averaged +0.14366 [+0.03322, +0.25409]. These are marginal intervals over three thermal realizations of one geometry per pair. The two normal intervals span both signs and permit close matching as well as appreciable imbalance. The data leave compatibility unresolved. [Force and order readout](../tools/mouth_receiver_junction_preflight.py) · [Local momentum and energy checks](../tools/mouth_receiver_junction_balance.py)

**Decision for 0PN.** We stopped before joining the phases. A force on the mouth requires a measurement of momentum transferred as material crosses a mobile boundary. These separate samples establish which preparation needs attention first. Compare gradual and abrupt fitting of the independent receiver to the same final in-plane periods at the same temperature and total preparation time, with height and tilt free. Measure fit work, density and all four face stresses. If gradual preparation preserves density, use independent thermal histories and longer held measurements to resolve the traction differences before attempting a join. **Verification.** The independent audit covered 12 native histories, 192 measurement blocks, 6,144 local momentum balances and 204 stress readings reconstructed from site coordinates and velocities. A reporting assertion rejected eight completed histories because the integrator rounded the step to fit the interval. Their saved outputs were validated in place. Four histories initially refused admission then ran once. The independent verdict was “Scoped clear for the measured neutral preflight and stop before a joined-interface force claim. The material and/or vector-traction conditions failed or remain unresolved.” [Measurement and review record](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_receiver_common_face.json)
