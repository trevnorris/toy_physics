# Can a replenished flare transmit a known sideways force?

**Step 1 of the 0PN free-fall roadmap stopped at the wall-to-material interaction.** We drove the wall of the radius-one cubic flare and let both material sheets slip independently along it. Two walls with the same aligned normal binding, neutral material calibration, and jointly clamped membrane stiffness gave different forces after fresh material crossed the entire conduit. Both sheets stayed in normal contact with the wall. The result identifies a missing interface property. It leaves the companion force and free acceleration open. **Why the physical roadmap stops here.** A smooth potential can reproduce the aligned lattice’s normal energy and force at every gap. Shifting a site tangentially at fixed gap distinguishes them. At the reference gap the two local slopes were zero and 5.12780765 per wall three-area per unit slip. Earlier neutral bonds fix interactions within and between mobile sheets. The earlier moving-patch calculation assigns wall anchors and an incoming reference explicitly. Neither supplies the actual lateral wall interaction under replacement. To proceed, independently resolve or measure the traction on a wall-facing material patch versus its lateral offset and slip speed, including whether arriving material binds at its present offset or retains an earlier reference. A steady holding force alone confounds contact and drag. This is a local material calibration at the same conversion state and intake, with no gravity input. Step 2 needs that calibration before using a held-flow and push response; steps 3 and 4 need it for a two-mouth release and radius universality.
