# Can one draining parcel change what the next parcel feels?

**Yes, if the rim has a localized material excitation that can exchange energy with a passing parcel.** We tested an attached site with two internal levels and an open bulk channel. One parcel loaded the site and left. After a delay, a second parcel crossed the same patch. We measured its two internal phases against a bypass and subtracted the empty-site reading. Their mean is the common phase. All parameters below are illustrative, in units of the bulk coupling and its inverse, with \(\hbar=1\). The existing conversion law leaves the material spectrum open. [Prior resonant control](mouth_conversion_twist_work.md) · [Equations, measurements and review](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_rim_retained_excitation.json)

**What persists.** With a bulk band from 2 to 6 and a rim level at 4, the excitation dispersed and the phase shift vanished at long delay. Raising the rim level to 5.5 produced a joint rim–bulk bound mode at \(37/6\). After the same loading pulse, its mean occupation was **0.0842521740041**, of which **0.0468067633356** was on the original rim site. The next parcel had a common phase shift of **−0.00154756895319 radians** at transit time 0.33. The matched mirror has the same local transitions and gives the same response. The loading energy came from the parcel and the work of making and breaking contact. Well-separated repeated arrivals approached a mean bound occupation of 0.779882280139 at the tested loading. [Simultaneous loading, storage and readout](../tools/mouth_rim_delayed_transit.py) · [Binding spectrum](../tools/mouth_rim_receiver_binding.py)

**What the next parcel feels.** A gentle passage with a frequency separated from the bulk band gave the same conditional phase for both parcel states while leaving almost all the stored excitation in place. The sign followed the difference between the stored and parcel transition energies. For an isolated receiver with detuning \(\Delta\), exchange amplitude \(G\), and second excitation costing \(U\) more than the first, the weak mean energy shift is \(G^2 U/[\Delta(\Delta+U)]\), away from resonance and with the specified oscillator matrix elements. Equal excitation spacings cancel this leading term. Measuring both spacings tests whether a proposed rim material can have the effect. [Slow-transit test](../tools/mouth_rim_stored_level.py) · [Capacity comparison](../tools/mouth_rim_capacity_spectrum.py)

**How long it must last.** Opening another bulk channel at the bound frequency removed permanent localization for the tested couplings. For one excitation prepared on the site, sampled at the next random arrival at either of two earlier face rates, a second-channel coupling of 0.05 left **48.85% or 52.90%** of the corresponding one-channel reading. These sampling rates come from distributing the earlier intake of 0.25 across the mouth. The actual rim leakage and arrival statistics still need a material law. [Arrival-rate test](../tools/mouth_rim_revisit.py)

The next material input is a rim mode's first two transition energies and its decay into the available bulk channels. The effect found here survives reflection with the same sign. Connecting it to a mirror-opposite exterior flux still requires a spatial coupling and an energy balance across the whole mouth.
