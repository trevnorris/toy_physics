# Independent review of the held radius-four exit traction

**Verdict.** “Scoped clear for the fixed-wall radius-four diagnostic. The terminal face-force gate failed on both meshes.”

I checked the archived field hashes, four nodal recovery outcomes, two independent conservative face reconstructions, both strip-source calculations and the separate same-trace control. The controls use the same physical wall displacement on the duct or receiving face, extending it four or eight reference cells into the surrounding material. An axial boundary traction must do identical work for both extensions. On the rim-refined mesh the signed works differ by −0.001420393 on the duct and +0.000219561 on the receiving side. A face-only force must therefore miss at least one measurement by 0.000710197 and 0.000109780, respectively. Halving the signed displacement changes the energy-derived values by less than 0.000000010. The independently assembled nodal forces reproduce the volume work for those motions. Using just the wall nodes leaves four-cell errors of +0.00134127 and +0.00438023 on the rim-refined grid. Sharing the terminal endpoint once and retaining the measured exterior flux gives near/far sums of −0.02303795/−0.02344811 on the doubled grid and −0.04042704/−0.04078059 on the rim-refined grid. The element integration-by-parts check identifies the largest terminal contribution beyond pointwise wall stress in the carrier phase. The compensating terms come from both the cell interiors and the jumps between cells. [Strip decomposition](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_terminal_element_sources.json)

Three finite-volume face reconstructions conserve the supplied local momentum to within 4 × 10⁻¹⁶ per cell. On the rim-refined grid their face forces miss the separate four-cell signed works by +0.03011 to +0.03388 on the duct and +0.02656 to +0.02861 on the receiving side. As a control, an explicit divergence-free change to the discrete flux adds one unit to the duct bank and zero to the receiving bank while preserving all six localized wall-test moments and the end fluxes. It changes both enclosing-surface fluxes by −1. Independent enclosing-surface measurements would detect that change. This construction tests identifiability from conservation alone. The physical question that remains is how much axial reaction belongs to the held wall after accounting independently for the work done in the adjacent material. The present comparison supports keeping the wall fixed while testing that distinction. It leaves the corner-singularity interpretation and the free-release test open.
