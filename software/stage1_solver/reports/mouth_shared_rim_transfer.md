# Does the held exit force depend on how the carrier mesh moves?

At radius four, moving the bonded disk, its rim and the adjoining wall together gives an exterior momentum prediction of **+0.50016167** on the doubled grid and **+0.49895823** on the rim-refined grid. Keeping the physical displacement identical across grids was essential. The earlier one-node wall measurement narrowed its support by half during refinement. With its original physical width restored, the near residual changes by +0.00292530, versus −0.01739082 when the width also changed. The complete bonded disk motion and the one-node wall measurement probe different reactions. [Independent surface calculation and far-enclosure correction](mouth_shared_rim_surface_review.md)

The held shape has one circulation quantum, an ordered interior and the same bonded crystal on both grids. The new transfer represents conversion order on the crystal's material coordinates. Moving only carrier cells then does zero direct work at fixed crystal sites. The carrier amplitude and phase still respond to how their elements move. Their coupling to the conversion order also changes where their cells cross a material-element boundary. Omitting the thin strips swept across those boundaries misses a first-order force. We integrated their contribution separately for each sign and included it in both the energy and its field derivatives. Two-point quadrature over the finite strips on the saved fields agreed with the limiting correction within 0.00000565 at half step for all six grid/motion combinations. The signed solver uses that first-order correction. The check samples intersections between strips, so a fully subdivided finite-cell integral may differ.
