# Can trapped standing waves pay for one exterior flux unit?

**A finite mouth can trap an angular dipole. In the passive rim model tested here, even excited modes fall short of the energy needed for one exterior unit.** Trapping, occupation and coupling to flux are three separate measurements. [Previous rim test](mouth_collar_charge_transmission.md) · [Equations, assumptions, guarded outputs and review](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_standing_wave_budget.json)

**What fits in the throat.** At the archived radius \(R_0=1.892410005412\), the hard-wall dipole frequency is 2.53562 against a bulk cutoff of 2.5. Replacing the hard side with a finite gap gives 2.11445968 with the same reflecting end. Opening the receiving end as well gives a squared dipole frequency at most 5.63081762, below the squared continuum threshold 6.25. An independent bound excludes additional families at both \(0.9R_0\) and \(R_0\) in this open finite well. Each has exactly one fundamental and one threefold dipole below the continuum. [Archived hard-wall result](../../em_charge_attribute/reports/undriven_trapped_bow.md) · [Cavity spectra](../tools/mouth_cavity_occupation.py) · [Open-end variational bound](../tools/mouth_cavity_open_end.py)

**What fixes the occupation.** Each occupied mode contributes \(-I_b\,\partial_R\omega_b\) and \(-I_b\,\partial_L\omega_b\) to the wave's radial and axial forces, with action \(I_b=E_b/\omega_b\). When only the fundamental and dipole families are present and their force coefficients are independent, those two readings recover each family's total action. In the finite-side, reflecting-end example, synthetic actions 0.7 and 0.3 were recovered. Dipole lobes rotating in either direction or oscillating along a fixed axis gave identical loads. Two phase-resolved readings distinguish these spatial patterns. With more mode families, the same two loads can also hide different total actions per family.

**What those modes can supply.** We gave the rim four real displacement coordinates per patch, positive alignment and neighbor springs, and a phase acquired in crossing each link. A three-patch spectrum detects the proposed signed coupling even before its modes are populated. For the whole rim we used the axial load that would balance the archived wall and volume terms at each held radius. We bounded the energy release for every free link configuration and every nonnegative mixture of the initially bound modes whose actions stay fixed by frequency rank. On both tested lattices and both mirrors, the exterior alone costs more than the maximum release at \(R/R_0=0.9,1,1.125,1.25,1.5\). At \(R_0\) the release is at most **0.05549243** against an exterior cost of at least **0.09817477**, in common model units. At \(2R_0\) the exterior-only comparison is inconclusive. Including the seven-layer field energy with spring scale \(K=R_0/R\) gives at least **0.08282135** against at most **0.07297099** across the two lattices. The native scalar wave has no coupling to this angular link. The rim springs, exterior coefficient and axial balance used here are hypotheses. A different law must identify measurable work that favors one flux sign at the mouth and the opposite sign at its mirror. A specific test is whether the material leaving a converting rim carries a different energy under opposite weak angular twists, after subtracting ordinary throughput at the same intake. No electric normalization or new free-fall force follows from the mode count.
