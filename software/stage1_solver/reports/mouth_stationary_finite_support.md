# Can a stationary crystal support a draining mouth?

**The finite-strain crystal pushes both deep waists outward, and it pulls both throats shorter. Neither entrance reaches a verified self-supporting shape.** We adopt zero mean lattice drift in the held mouth’s frame while coherent material drains through it. A released mouth can still translate, and its crystal can deform or oscillate. Radius 3 remains reserved. Supersolid hydrodynamics distinguishes lattice transport from superfluid transport. The stationary background here is a model choice. [Kinematic audit](mouth_lattice_reference.md) · [Yoo and Dorsey, Eqs. 6–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0621)

**Material preparation.** For the four-dimensional deformation gradient \(F\), write \(J_v=\det F>0\). The trial energy per unit reference four-volume is
\[
W=\chi_0\left[\frac{\mu_0}{2}\left(J_v^{-1/2}\operatorname{tr} F^TF-4\right)+\frac{K_0}{2}(\log J_v)^2\right].
\]
The existing normalized moduli are \(\mu_0=K_0=1\). A neutral small-strain calculation recovered the previous strip tangent of 2.4. This finite-strain continuation introduces no extra modulus. We chose an unloaded straight bore of radius equal to its entrance radius, a collar one slab thickness wide centered on the bore, and a common material attachment for both radii. Each material tag retains the shear-order value sampled from the deep held-state chart. The bore follows the geometric wall, axial material planes keep their fractional depth, and radial spacing relaxes within a positive, exponentially varying family on each side. These choices prescribe which material formed the wall and how it stays attached. A less constrained calculation failed orientation or convergence checks on refinement. In the accepted map the Jacobian stays positive, but releasing an omitted axial displacement does nonzero work. The forces therefore belong to a constrained specimen. Shortening from 3.533 to one thickness at entrance 2 lowered it from 748.916 to 357.977. At radius 2, allowing the cavity order to readjust at unit depth left field forces of **+5.815 radial and +8.509 axial**. The wave remained concentrated downstream. **Can the load-bearing lattice end at the lip?** We held the entrance radius and its tilt exactly fixed while changing depth and waist together. A lip energy depending only on these two quantities does zero work along that motion, for any hinge stiffness. The two-sided fields and separately ordered wave pocket still did **+0.2271** and **+5.2855** work per unit length at the deep entrances, both positive on the coarser grid. The shallow radius-2 result was **−0.8070** about the readjusted center. Radius 1’s shallow order failed the stability check above. A finite-width attachment sensitive to the actual material strain or curvature across the lip remains possible. Retaining a downstream wave in the lip-only test requires a pocket that still carries transverse shear. [Fixed-angle calculation](../tools/mouth_lip_angle_work.py)

**Where the support test stops.** Small localized strain perturbations had negative elastic stiffness at some weakly ordered points on the receiving side of both deep collars on both numerical resolutions. The resolved ordered-side points passed that local check. At shallow radius 2, the small receiving-side negative region appeared only on the finer approximation. This diagnoses the elastic energy at the prepared stretches. The complete coupled spectrum remains open. Moreover, **99.615% and 99.955%** of the original *cavity wave kinetic energy* overlaps the strained collar at the deep entrances. The field calculation follows the same outgoing wave branch as the geometry changes, but uses the wave operator on unstressed material. A physical equilibrium requires computing the wave about the stressed state and allowing its stress, crystalline order and attachment to readjust together. [Local tangent and overlap check](../tools/mouth_finite_collar_tangent.py)

**Next material test.** On a neutral coherent sample, measure pressure and incremental stiffness over finite volume changes at fixed shear order. This can discriminate between volumetric energies with the same small-strain \(K_0\) before investing in another throat shape. Then use an admissible, freely loaded material attachment and its stressed wave operator at the two training entrances. No force coefficient was fitted to gravity.
