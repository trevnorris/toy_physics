# Can the material locate the reflecting wall of the draining mouth?

The half-order surface can turn a constituent around. Inferring a wall height from its travel time gives a different answer at each incident speed. Applying the same mean surface potential to a continuous carrier also allows steady current through the sheet. Together these tests rule out identifying the geometric wall of the radius-one, intake-0.25 mouth from the site potential alone in these preparations. [Earlier attachment test](mouth_attachment_impact.md)

**Single constituent.** We held the triangular sheet fixed and launched a neutral tag perpendicular to it. Directly above one site, the wall heights inferred from the round trip were 1.55093, 1.35353 and 1.22854 microscopic lengths above the host-site plane at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of the flux-weighted incident thermal speed distribution. Above a triangular hollow they were 1.54106, 1.33810 and 1.20811. Attraction speeds the tag up before it turns. The actual closest approaches across all six preparations ranged from 0.76100 to 0.84313. Changing the observation height and checking the three-dimensional trajectories independently reproduced the travel-time result. [Probe calculation](../tools/mouth_surface_reflection_probe.py)

**Continuous carrier.** We tested one additional law on a flat neutral sample. Each unit of carrier mass feels the analytic plane average of the Morse interaction, with the inherited length and energy scales. This candidate keeps the far tail. The discrete tag interaction tapers between 4.5 and 5 microscopic lengths. We placed identical carrier at the measured local density on both sides of a fixed sheet and allowed a single phase across it. The undriven, positive-density solution left a density at the sheet of 0.00004154 times the far density. A weak longitudinal wave incident normally gave the following energy fractions. Wavenumber is in inverse mouth radii. These wavelengths describe an infinite flat material sample.

| Wavenumber | Reflected | Transmitted |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.05 | 99.416% | 0.584% |
| 0.00625 | 72.734% | 27.266% |
| 0.001 | 6.393% | 93.607% |

At equal reservoir densities, a steady phase difference of +0.002 drove a normal current per unit sheet area of +0.00000354878 in carrier units for baths at ±18 radii. Reversing the phase reversed the current. Halving it halved the current, and zero bias gave zero. The steady prediction follows from integrating the current relation across the undriven density profile, \(\Delta\theta=J\int dz/n(z)+O(J^3)\). The mouth solver prescribes zero normal current through the curved side wall. The surface law with this backing cannot by itself select the position of that boundary. The calculation concerns material crossing a side wall. Intake along the throat and transverse light are separate observables. **Requirement for 0PN.** Measure normal current under a weak phase bias with the physical material on the far side of the surface. If normal current is negligible on the gravity timescale, locate the effective boundary and the material sites in the same coordinates. A background-subtracted weak-probe force scan gives a second check. For the cold retained-mean surface, the predicted holding forces at the continuum wall for offsets 1.5 and 2.0 differ by a factor of 111.1 at known coupling. With an unknown common amplitude, moving the probe out by 0.25 microscopic lengths gives respective force ratios of 0.09593 and 0.09235. These predictions require a known probe origin and force shape. Until a backing law or a shared position measurement selects the surface, the signed residence and the force on a released mouth remain open.
