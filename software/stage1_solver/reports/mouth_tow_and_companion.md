# Can towing one mouth predict the pull from another drain at 0PN?

**One towing force measures a sum.** Let $U_{\rm br}$ and $U_{\rm bulk}$ be the incident in-plane speeds of brane material and receiving bulk in the lab, and $V$ the mouth speed on the same axis. Consider a steady linear response to these two relative streams alone, at the same radius, intake and material preparation. The force exerted on the mouth is then

$$
F_x=A(U_{\rm br}-V)+B(U_{\rm bulk}-V).
$$

Here $A$ and $B$ are slopes of the **total** reaction with independent changes of the incident streams. Each can include induced stresses on both sides. Towing at constant $V$ through two resting reservoirs takes external force $(A+B)V$. Holding the mouth fixed and sending brane material past it with the bulk at rest measures $A$ from the negative holder reaction per incident speed. These two controls determine both slopes. Equating a common stream past a fixed mouth to towing assumes the full preparation transforms with the frame, including supports and remote boundaries. **Checked case.** In the ideal potential-flow calculation the brane absorbs and the bulk emits the same mass per second. From the archived SymPy full-strength readings in both directions, $A=1/4$ and $A+B=0$ in benchmark units. The inferred $B=-1/4$ predicted all eight reserved half-strength or mixed-stream readings. With the target held, the companion draws brane material toward itself and expels bulk material away from itself. The two responses therefore add. Using the ideal source-only speeds without fitting to either pair, the predicted pulls were $6.1376358054964655\times10^{-5}$ at $d=8$ and $1.4957579871550849\times10^{-5}$ at $d=16$. They agree to the stored precision with the independent Mathematica surface integrals at radii 1 and 3. The brane contribution fell by four on doubling separation and the ideal 4D bulk contribution by eight. This checks an ideal exterior with a single external holder. Attaching its flat potential receiver to the saved carrier opening failed the phase-matching test. **Physical inputs.** In the isolated spherically symmetric preparation, an inward **material** mass rate $D_s(d)$ measured across the radius-$d$ sphere fixes the speed there through $U_{\rm br}=D_s(d)/(4\pi d^2\rho_{\rm br}(d))$ on the ledger's coordinate measure. One can use the rate from a smaller sphere if the intervening shell has zero net material exchange. The saved carrier number rate does not determine this material rate or density. The ledger also leaves the bulk load and physical support response open.
