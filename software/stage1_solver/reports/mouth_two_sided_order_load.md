# Can an intact supersolid–superfluid interface carry a normal load while material crosses it?

**Yes, if the interface is held and order can relax as material crosses.** A pressure difference supported by the holder and a current through the interface can coexist. The measured surface tension alone gives neither the current nor a physical attachment to the mouth. We tested a planar patch with the same coherent material on both sides, at unit resting density, with a weak load applied from the bulk side. The coordinate increases from ordered brane to disordered bulk. Forces, currents and powers below are per unit three-dimensional interface area in model units. This local test excites no transverse shear or trapped wave. **What keeps the flat brane in place?** At equal pressure and chemical potential, uniform coherent phase and a stationary planar order transition give zero normal current. A free planar transition also admits common drift with the material. Its material crossing rate is $j_{\rm rel}=n(u_n-V_\Sigma)$, where $V_\Sigma$ is the speed of the half-order surface. On that branch a laboratory plane records current while $j_{\rm rel}=0$. The single-front calculation leaves the thickness of a finite slab and the localization of sustained drainage at a throat undetermined. The projected replenishment term $S_{\rm leak}$ accounts for exchange once present. [Order transport, finite-slab caveat and replenishment](../../../research/pde_ledger_v2/notes/stages/ledger_stage006_two_phase_chiB_ontology.md)

There is a stronger result under the passive conversion hypothesis. For a steadily translating free front with equal-depth order wells, no extra conversion drive and vanishing far order gradients, $j_{\rm rel}$ is constant. Integrating the order equation against $\chi'$ across the two sides gives

\[
0=\left[W-\tfrac12\kappa_B(\chi')^2\right]_{\rm brane}^{\rm bulk}
=-j_{\rm rel}\int\frac{(\chi')^2}{M_\chi n}\,ds .
\]

The integral is positive, so steady material crossing must vanish even at variable positive density. A fixture supplies the extra force in the held test. At an actual mouth the candidate attachment must supply its own reaction.

**Step 2 decision.** The physical input is how strongly the mouth anchors the order front. A discriminating test would displace the front relative to the rim at fixed trapped-wave action and measure the restoring force on release, with the unoccupied mouth as a control. Common translation of the front, rim and trapped wave supplies no relative displacement. Track the half-order surface, carrier current and reaction on the rim under the same weak normal pressure. Calibrate the conversion rate independently by the decay of a small order disturbance. The existing flare has no outer fluid beside the eight wall bands.
