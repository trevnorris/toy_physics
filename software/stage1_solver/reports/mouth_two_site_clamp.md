# Holding two ends of one trial attachment at a measured offset

**At 0PN, both directions of a weak sideways step increased the force needed to hold one trial attachment off center. Both directions decreased it for a wider attachment made of the same strands.** The current brane fields supply a local shear order. Resolving and joining two distinct material sites requires a microscopic hypothesis. This experiment establishes what two force gauges could distinguish.

We used the archived radius-one mouth at nominal intake 0.25, taking the local order of 0.5000000000000019 as uniform and frozen across a small test patch. Two parallel rigid pads represented the two sites, separated normally by one material pitch. Each pad had two anchors separated by a span \(b\). Two independent tensile strands crossed between opposite anchors. Both used unit axial stiffness and unloaded length 0.95 pitch. The assumed geometry could be checked from site and anchor positions, and the strand properties by axial stretching. The 0PN solution supplies neither. [Archived state and shear control](mouth_offset_brane_readout.md)

We clamped the midpoint and held the measured difference in pad positions at \(q=1/8\) pitch along the original offset. We stepped their relative position perpendicular to it, on independent copies of the same state. Each pad had its own finite-stiffness gauge. Its calibrated deflection gave the applied force \(G_i\). At fixed midpoint, the holding force along the offset is \(H=(G_{B,x}-G_{A,x})/2\). We repeated each reading with both strands removed and the same pads clamped at the same measured positions. The brane contribution came from the frozen shear energy on three successive gaps between grounded planes and pads (1, 1 and 1.5 pitches), with unit area and a central preload strain of 0.001. This control gave \(H_{\rm brane}=0.0007083333333333360\) for all three sideways steps. [Acquisition](../tools/mouth_two_site_clamp.py) · [Gauge reduction](../tools/mouth_two_site_meter.py)

Forces are in model units for one unit-area patch. Moving sideways stretches both strands and changes how much of their tension points along the held offset. Their signed contributions depend on the anchor spacing. Both layouts remained under tension and had different zero-step holding forces. The opposite-step difference vanished to the recorded precision. Each corrected pad reaction matched the force from the strand energy to within \(1.3210\times10^{-34}\) model force across 14 connected/control pairs. A separate offset calibration showed that a differential drift of only \(3.88\times10^{-13}\) pitch would imitate the smaller wide-attachment signal. This is a numerical experiment with fixed pad orientations, prescribed elastic properties and no conversion during the reading. [Measurements and independent checks](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_two_site_clamp.json)

**What this gives the 0PN calculation.** The protocol isolates the contribution of a single postulated attachment. To select a physical response, we need an independently resolved attachment geometry and force law, or two-site position and reaction data from the material. The zero-step force is already a stronger discriminator between these two particular layouts and can be used before testing their small sideways predictions. How such forces sum over a moving mouth remains open.
