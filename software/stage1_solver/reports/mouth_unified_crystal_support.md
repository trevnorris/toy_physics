# Can one shear modulus carry light and support a draining mouth?

**Using the same shear modulus for transverse waves and lasting crystalline strain gives the flat strip positive bending stiffness in the solver’s benchmark units. It does not determine the force on the walls of a puncture.** At the same measured opening and the same modulus, the crystal pushes outward if its unloaded opening is larger and pulls inward if its unloaded opening is smaller. The missing input is which stress-free geometry the material carries into the throat and how it changes during conversion. The tests below fix all constitutive choices before consulting the throat forces. Radius 3 remains blind.

## What the wave fixes

The previous wave-off calculation had stress on both interfaces and a small stress change inside the strip. Its crystalline *stretch* contribution was zero. The old displacement law penalizes curl and can carry a static load through an attachment. Uniform axial stretch has zero curl. We used the prior signed strip as the neutral control and did the sustained-stretch runs on the new law. [Previous stress profile](mouth_ordered_strip_bend.md) · [Static attachment control](mouth_bend_static_shear.md)

We adopted one registered, isotropic crystal for this test. Write \(e=(\nabla u+\nabla u^T)/2\) in the four spatial dimensions. Its energy density, incremental stress and transverse wave speed are
\[
 W=\tfrac12 K(\mathrm{tr}\,e)^2+\mu\,\mathrm{dev}(e):\mathrm{dev}(e),\qquad
 T=2\mu e+\lambda\,\mathrm{tr}(e)\,\mathbf1,\qquad
 \lambda=K-\mu/2,\qquad c_T^2=\mu/\rho_T .
\]
The new strain energy **replaces** the curl energy of that same displacement. Both \(\rho_T=\rho_{T0}\chi\) and \(\mu=\mu_0\chi\) retain the old wave’s order weights. The conditional preparation takes \(\rho_{T0}=c_T=\mu_0=1\), \(K=K_0\chi\), and persistent material registration. Thus the same shear normalization enters the flat optical mode and the slow stress. The axial tangent follows from the specified material and fixtures. A rescaling of both transverse inertia and shear modulus would preserve the transverse speed. An independently known oscillatory material inertia or traction-to-velocity impedance would calibrate that normalization. The coherent carrier’s density and compressibility are already defined. Identifying them with the elastic lattice’s participating inertia or relaxed compression requires an extra constitutive assumption. [Transverse operator](../tools/mouth_exchange_shear.py) · [Earlier constitutive audit](pathA_23_stage2_constitutive_form.md)

For a uniaxial specimen with all three lateral tractions released, \(Y=2\mu(1+\nu)\) holds and \(\nu=\lambda/(2\mu+3\lambda)\). The strip fixture holds its two other in-brane strains and releases the incremental crystal traction normal to the slab. Its effective tangent is
\[
 Y_{\rm strip}=\lambda+2\mu-\frac{\lambda^2}{\lambda+2\mu}
     =4\mu\frac{K+\mu/2}{K+3\mu/2}.
\]
We froze \(K_0=1\) as a comparison with the carrier compression scale. This gives \(\nu=1/7\), \(Y=16/7\) and \(Y_{\rm strip}=2.4\). The shear wave alone selects no numerical \(K_0\) or Poisson ratio. Other positive compression moduli with identical wave speed gave different tangents. For this particular strip fixture, every \(K_0>0\) gives \(Y_{\rm strip}>4\mu_0/3\) in the fully ordered material. With all transverse stresses released that lower bound no longer applies. At \(K_0/\mu_0=0.001\), for example, the ordinary uniaxial modulus is 0.01590. ## Sustained load and thickness

We imposed axial strains of ±0.002 and ±0.001 on the same flat, wave-empty profile, ramping over six transverse crossing times and holding for twenty. The normal displacement and inertia were resolved through both diffuse faces, with free incremental crystal traction, two in-plane transverse strains held and equal resting baths. This is a small-strain elastic response on the prepared material profile. The late mean axial load per imposed strain was **2.399993**, compared with 2.400000 in equilibrium. In the last ten crossing times the maximum oscillation relative to equilibrium was 0.0248%. A faster one-crossing-time ramp left a 1.90% oscillation. The conservative calculation assumes lasting registration. The hold measures the resulting response over this time window. [Guarded hold, stress and control histories](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/mouth_unified_strip_hold.json)

Dividing the static integrated axial load per strain by the specified strip modulus gives \(\int\chi\,ds=1.000000H\). The middle **0.92701H** carries 90% of that load. Bending weights distance from the mid-surface twice, giving \(\int s^2\chi\,ds=0.0885972H^3\), equivalent to a uniform bending thickness \(1.02063H\) when the diffuse tails are included. The previous 0.03911444 threshold used a different modulus profile proportional to \(\chi^2\). With the wave’s linear order weighting, the corresponding weak-curvature threshold is **0.0300988** in units of \(\sigma/H\). The benchmark clears both numbers. Its total bending stiffness is \(+0.209967\,\sigma H^2\), including the measured neutral front contribution once. Even the lower bound at this normalized shear strength gives positive stiffness. These are flat specimen thicknesses. The depth that participates in a bent, converting wall depends on the local material placement. ## Direct checks at entrances 1 and 2

We recalculated the degree-four trapped mode on each broad saved profile with the symmetric-strain operator and its outgoing traction. In homogeneous material the transverse speed is unchanged, while curved free boundaries transmit different traction. At fixed action \(J=1\), the stored wave still favors widening and lengthening. More than 99.9997% of its cavity kinetic energy lay more than two interface widths downstream of the waist at either entrance. These resolved modes use the archived cavity ending at the half-order surface. Shear propagation into the receiving-side half of the diffuse front and coupling to other polarizations remain open. A homogeneous Cauchy solid also supports a longitudinal material mode. Compatibility with the light sector requires testing its mixing and visibility at the front. **Does knowing the stiffness determine the initial crystalline force?** A flat, finite-thickness slab with a spherical through-hole gives a test with an exact internal equilibrium. Give the stress-free hole a radius larger by \(d=0.001H\) than the held radius. The relaxed radial displacement varies as \(1/r^2\), the broad slab faces are traction-free, and the integrated crystal force favoring a wider hole is \(16\pi R d\int\mu(s)\,ds\) to first order. It is +0.0502655 at radius 1 and +0.1005310 at radius 2. Reversing the offset reverses each force. All four specimens use the same bulk and shear moduli.

We also tested an attached annular piece of each **curved** throat from half to one and a half times its local radius. The calculation integrates the full four-dimensional volume with radial measure \(4\pi r^2dr\,dw\). It uses the archived interior order, newly sampled receiving-side order and one continuous displacement across the dividing surface. The interior displacement and both sides of the join were free to equilibrate while the specimen’s cuts and ends were held. A smooth compatible change in the stress-free material labels preserves four-volume and dies away at both longitudinal ends. With its positive offset of 0.001, the crystal reactions on the cuts (widening, lengthening) were (+0.00401817, −0.0000348993) and (+0.00662927, −0.000109626) at the two radii. Reversing that offset reversed both reactions. Receiving-side stress contributed to both, and the two internal interface tractions canceled to numerical precision. Under prescribed widening, the normal displacement of the material join at the waist was about 27% of the motion prescribed there in the geometric wall test. These cut reactions cannot be added to the held-geometry force table, which displaces the dividing surface itself. This tests dependence on the unloaded lattice directly in a volume. A complete shape relaxation still needs the material geometry, its attachment and its strain history. **Return to the free-fall roadmap.** Specify or measure the unloaded circumference **and axial spacing** of a tagged piece of coherent crystal as it turns around the lip. An unloading test on that piece can distinguish retained strain from crystal that takes on a new stress-free spacing at the bend. The same rule must determine what is inherited from the flat brane, what is lost as order disappears, and how the material is attached at the entrance and exit. A nonlinear finite-volume relaxation can then use that reference on both sides, update order, carrier and wave together, and test the resulting equilibrium at both training radii. No free-mouth mass or acceleration follows from the flat rigidity alone.
