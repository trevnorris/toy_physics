# Does a steady drain select the energy of its trapped wave?

**Both weak waves gave energy to the material flowing through the mouth. Each recovered some in the second cycle, and each finished the two cycles with a net transfer to the material.** This is a perturbative calculation for the same isolated, held, cubic mouth of radius 1 and depth scale 1. Its unperturbed intake was 0.25. The far reservoirs retained their stationary phases while flow and density inside responded. No sideways force or free-fall measurement entered the calculation. [Resting-mouth pressure test](mouth_occupied_radial_balance.md)

**What allows energy exchange.** The existing real scalar wave oscillates in a fixed confining well and its energy is conserved at any amplitude. We tested one additional material law. If a density change shifts the local squared wave frequency by $g\,\delta n$, the corresponding interaction energy density is $g\,\delta n\,\Phi^2/2$. The same term lets the wave push on the material. We took the original wave shape, drove the linearized carrier with that reciprocal force, and integrated the work for two full cycles. The wave displacement and interaction energy vanish at the endpoints. The work gives the leading change in wave energy from this channel. The magnitude of $g$ is a trial input. A frequency-shift measurement under a small density change on a uniform material sample would calibrate it. [Power calculation](../tools/mouth_wave_drain_power.py)

| 0.0025 | $+5.1668\times10^{-9}$ | $-5.2011\times10^{-10}$ | $+4.6467\times10^{-9}$ |
| 0.0100 | $+8.2669\times10^{-8}$ | $-8.3218\times10^{-9}$ | $+7.4347\times10^{-8}$ |

These are model energy units at $g=1$ on carrier mesh 34. The same mouth geometry with resting material took $7.3789\times10^{-8}$ at the larger seed. Reversing $g$ preserved the work and setting $g=0$ removed it. Work scales as $g^2 E^2$ in this approximation. Refining the carrier and wave grids and halving the time step preserved the sign of each cycle. Independently, the quadratic carrier energy was positive on three meshes. Starting from an unperturbed carrier with these fixed bath data, any weak density-potential pulse that starts and ends at zero leaves nonnegative energy in the carrier.

**Where the energy went.** For the larger seed, the net measured carrier energy crossing the waist toward the feeding brane over two cycles was $4.6116\times10^{-9}$. The net crossing the outlet toward the receiving bulk was $4.9924\times10^{-8}$. Independent balances on each side agree after subtracting work done there directly by the wave. These are internal crossings in finite reservoirs. The calculation leaves the long-time escape rate and the lifetime of a freely evolving wave open. **Decision for 0PN.** This reversible density coupling provides a path for wave energy to enter the draining material. It supplies no preferred nonzero occupation. A sufficiently isolated wave could retain energy set when the throat formed. For a wave maintained by ongoing conversion, the next independent input is the available energy per converted constituent. On a material patch, measure that energy, the work delivered in phase with the wave velocity, and the competing losses at two seed energies. Positive net wave work at low energy and negative work at higher energy would bracket a candidate stable occupation if the net work varies continuously. The existing passive conversion calculation already accounts for energy spent changing material order. [Conversion-energy budget](mouth_bend_conversion_power.md)
