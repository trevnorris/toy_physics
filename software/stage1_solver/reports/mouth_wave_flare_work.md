# Can the trapped wave make a draining mouth fall together?

**An occupied wave can transmit the sideways pull from the entrance to the flare.** At radius 1 the two parts moved together under a sufficiently slow load with the restoring force and added inertia calculated from the same wave equation. Universal free fall remains open. The tested wave loses binding at smaller radii, and two simple prescriptions for its stored energy miss the axial force needed across sizes.

The test uses three brane coordinates and the depth coordinate \(w\). A smooth well follows the existing curved collar and flare, from the waist to the exit. It represents one hypothesis for how the material confines a neutral wave. Occupying or emptying it leaves the carrier and its intake fixed within each comparison. Across radii the carrier has a common weak reservoir phase difference. The axial depths come from the earlier requirement of equal conductance per unit **carrier** inertia. **Axial force and size.** Slowly stretching the well gives the extra force \(-J\,\partial_H\omega\). Per unit action it pushes toward greater depth by 0.3331, 0.4858 and 0.6447 at radii 1, 2 and 4. Keeping the same action across sizes gives force multipliers 1.46 and 1.94. After recalibration at radius 1, even the smallest restoring-force multipliers from positive pressure and tension are 3.55 and 10.91. We also tried preparing each mouth with the same stored wave energy per throat volume. Three positive coefficients fit radii 1, 2 and 4. At two unused radii the net axial restoring force was 3.46% too low at radius 1.5 and 7.99% too high at radius 3. Refining the carrier mesh at the unused radii gave 3.50% low and 7.97% high. These discrepancies measure axial balance at the carrier-only target depths. The volume preparation and the sideways \(J=1\) experiment have different occupations.
