# Measuring equilibrium of the radius-four mouth

The experiment asks whether the draining mouth can support its shape once the bonded crystal can rearrange throughout its interior. The held disk and lip audit supplies the accuracy of the force measurement. Free fall requires a separate translation experiment after the shape has settled. **What moves.** The entrance radius is four. The six free shape coordinates control depth, exit radius, three variations of the profile along the depth, and the polar–equatorial radius difference. On the coarse and fine solid grids, 10,074 and 30,783 independent material coordinates allow radial, axial and polar displacement. Crystal on both sides of the core–collar bond shares material nodes. The calculation uses rotational symmetry about the vortex and reflection symmetry across its equator. It retains the ordered shaft interior, one quantum of circulation on both sides, the original lip construction, and the two-sided mobile conversion band. The ambient baths and the entrance preparation are fixed. [Relaxation equations](../tools/mouth_whole_r4_relax.py)

**Which force is minimized.** At each proposed wall shape, the receiver background adjusts and the lattice deposits a force on the conversion field at the current material positions. The reciprocal force comes from differentiating the same crystal energy. In the notation of the solver the total energy is $E_{\rm static}+E_{\rm solid}(q,0)+E_{\rm field}(u;L(q))$, where the last term includes the coupling of the deposited load to the mobile crystalline order. The resting and unwound controls serve to measure momentum and retained intake on the resulting geometry. The fine grid tracks the original last free ring at 97.7569% of initial physical depth. Thirty-six physical sample points cover the separate compression zone just inside the disk edge. Check material orientation throughout every cell, strain and its sensitivity to resolution, and signed inward current with total speed including swirl. Compare the two enclosing momentum surfaces using the same physical displacements and include the work between them.
