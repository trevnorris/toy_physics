# What crosses the resolved conversion layer at the wound exit?

**Near the held force crossing, the layer carries inward current through a depleted vortex. Every sampled part of the exit exceeds the local sound speed even in the normal direction.** Widening the exit to 2.45 makes most normal flow subsonic. Only 8.59% of the intake there crosses material that is dense and subsonic when the azimuthal motion is included. The outward radial force on a mouth and the axial stress transmitted by this patch are different observables.

The exit has no assigned phase jump, normal current or pinned order at its center. Density one is the bath reference; dense here means \(n>0.5\), and the long-wave speed is \(c=n^2\). Both rows are for entrance radius 2. The receiving-side reading averages the first axial cell. **Stress.** For \(f=\sqrt n\), relative velocity \(\boldsymbol u\), order \(\chi\), energy density \(\mathcal H\), and order-gradient coefficient \(\kappa_\chi\), the stationary flux of axial momentum is
\[
T_{wj}=n u_w u_j+\partial_w f\,\partial_j f+\kappa_\chi\,\partial_w\chi\,\partial_j\chi-\delta_{wj}\mathcal H .
\]
It includes angular energy, pressure, density gradients and order. With the same vortex, boundary density and order, subtracting a control with uniform meridional phase isolates how drainage changes the stress. The receiving-side change integrated over the disk is +0.012535 at 1.04, and +0.005773 at 2.45. At the wider exit the density-above-half and total-subsonic region contributes −0.009793 and the rest contributes positive stress. That dense, slow region accounts for 17.70% of the integral of the absolute local stress change. The portion within the unperturbed vortex’s half-density radius, \(s<1.547951\), carries 47.37% of the wider exit’s inward current. This reference radius does not track a material boundary.

**Controls and limits.** At matching half-distance 0.12, three grids gave intakes 0.139767, 0.139210 and 0.138891 near the force crossing, and 1.394079, 1.402285 and 1.405015 at the wider exit. Feed, upper receiver and lateral receiver currents closed within \(10^{-9}\) in both finest runs. Interior virtual displacements independently test conservation of axial momentum including lateral stress. For the matched flow-minus-rest flux, the residual divided by integrated absolute test contributions was 0.551% and 0.0201%. Moving the cuts from ±0.12 to ±0.18 gave intakes 0.134985 and 1.379228 and receiving stress changes +0.014769 and +0.005706. The latter cuts gave 9.23% dense, total-subsonic current at the wider opening. The near-crossing integrated stress is sensitive to resolution and to the finite ports; its sign survived both checks.

**Radius-1 check.** At exit 0.455 the sharp preparation carries 0.00321903. The resolved patch on the two grids carries 0.00699706 and 0.00704648. On the finer grid the largest receiving density is 0.0538582, the smallest normal Mach number is 464.51, and the matched integrated stress change is +0.005737. The finite patch and full sharp model differ by more than a factor of two in intake here. A feedback solve would be needed for a new physical intake, while both descriptions exclude a dense subsonic annulus on these sampled shapes.

The feeding and receiving phase and amplitude at the outer cuts are interpolated from the held sharp-front solution. The inner lateral wall is sealed and holds its material rim at half order. Beyond the lip an artificial matching cylinder uses the receiving carrier trace and a decaying order profile. There is no free collar motion or exterior stationary-wave condition in this patch. The measurements establish conditional transmission over a resolved layer. To obtain fully self-consistent stress on a freely moving exit, the altered matching fields must feed back into the exterior and the collar. The held conditions already distinguish a depleted near-crossing exit from a wider one whose dense, total-subsonic annulus carries little of the total current.
