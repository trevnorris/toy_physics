# Does a pair of moving mirror mouths send a detectable wave?

**Yes, for a specified contact between each mouth and the ordered brane.** Two spherical mouths carrying opposite prepared material turns were displaced toward and away from each other. The curl-elastic exterior carried energy outward. A distant neutral constituent loop responded with alternating, oppositely directed currents on its two faces. The motion was prescribed. The contact stiffness, its frequency dependence and the mapping from material turn to physical electric charge remain model inputs. ### Where the signed current goes

Translate the earlier radial internal states and form normalized neighbor overlaps in space **and time**. The pair preparation uses the product of two independent overlap phases. The oriented sum of principal plaquette angles on a spatial cube gives its integer count in turns. Applying the same construction to cubes containing time gives the signed current across each face. Cancellation of common faces enforces discrete continuity. These are observables of a prescribed texture. The ordinary constituent intake is absent from the calculation. Moving the positive texture one cell to the right and its mirror one cell to the left gave two positive crossings on different planes and a dipole change of two turn–cell units. Moving both right gave one crossing of each sign, with zero dipole change. Stationary mouths gave zero current. The enclosing cuts retained opposite units. Moving each center by 0.11 of a cell changed spatial and temporal plaquette signals outside the cores while producing zero integer crossings. Two ways of filling the excluded core agreed on the exterior field and the total transported dipole but gave different crossing times. At a separation of 0.30 cell the composed trial ceased to resolve the separate counts. These tests establish a possible local transport of signed geometry. Actual time registration and charge transport through a converting throat remain open.

### How radiation was calculated from moving surfaces

The independent wave calculation uses two disjoint spherical cuts in a homogeneous three-dimensional brane. Each retains a signed primitive flux of magnitude \(q=2\pi\). This fixes the sector of each prepared mouth. Capture of that sector during motion has not been derived from the single stationary mouth’s energy bound. The two experiments also have different domains. The prescribed lattice texture establishes continuity. It is not evolved as a radiating solution or used as numerical input to the spherical solver. [Stationary angular contacts and wave energy](mouth_torsional_light.md)

In each smooth patch of the exterior let \(D=\nabla\times a\), where \(a\) is the internal angular displacement. Nonzero flux on each cut requires patches for the static potential. The fixed-sector perturbation has zero net flux on each cut and admits a global perturbation potential. Its energy density is \((b|\dot a|^2+C|D|^2)/2\), its outgoing energy current is \(CD\times\dot a\), and its wave speed is \(c=\sqrt{C/b}\). Both counterrotating faces together give \(C=4,b=8\) in the chosen units. The per-face primitive flux is counted once. For a linear spherical contact, angular slip at the moving rim costs \(G|y|^2/2\) per unit area. Site rolls equilibrate and the radial reference texture follows the sphere. For each axisymmetric harmonic of degree \(l\ge1\) the surface equations are
\[
 y_l=\frac{R(D_r-D_r^*)_l}{l(l+1)},\qquad C D_{\theta,l}+G y_l=0.
\]
Here \(D_r^*\) is uniform on the moving sphere. The projections use \(P_l(\cos\theta)\) radially and \(\sin\theta\,P_l'(\cos\theta)\) tangentially. The second equation follows by varying the exterior curl energy and surface spring together. On displacing mouth \(i\) by \(X_i\hat z\), the required perturbation at its original surface is \(\delta D+X_i\partial_z D_0\). The static background \(D_0\) includes both mouths’ induced multipoles. Both displaced surfaces then drive one outgoing partial-wave solution. No point current, prescribed dipole power or force law is used to drive this solve. The construction supplies a smooth, angularly uniform contact law, neglects flow corrections and assumes no independent rim torque source. For separation \(d=4R\), dimensionless contact stiffness \(GR/C=8\), peak motion \(0.01R\) per mouth and \(kR=0.008\), common translation radiated **0.0000544417479** times the power of opposed motion. The pair still radiates in higher multipoles during common motion. An isolated point-dipole benchmark follows independently by computing the work carried by the angular field over a sphere:
\[
 \overline P=\frac{C\omega^4|p_{\rm peak}|^2}{12\pi c^3}.
\]
With the dipole inferred by slowly displacing the two equilibrated finite mouths, this predicted the opposed-motion power to within 0.012% for that preparation. The induced angular distribution matters. At \(4R\) the static dipole change per unit of relative displacement was \(0.979775041q\); it became \(0.998668934q\) at \(10R\) with the same spring. An independent static calculation reproduced the six tested dipole derivatives. For \(GR/C=2\) the induced static dipole of each sphere vanishes in any regular incident harmonic field, giving precisely \(q\) in the static derivative. Power computed on separate spheres around both mouths, on a larger common sphere and from the outgoing amplitude agreed. The benchmark also passed an independent finite-source work test. The familiar dipole angular dependence and power law here refer to the material wave. ### What a distant probe would measure

Place a weak square loop of side \(0.1R\) in the equatorial plane, with one bound constituent on each face. Use equal hopping energies \(t\), separation energy \(16t\), and \(\hbar\omega/t=0.02\). On each face the hopping phase follows the corresponding angular link. Subtract the equilibrium current at the actual static background flux. Integrating the outgoing \(D\) through the finite square gave the same alternating flux as integrating the angle around its edges. Because the detector is cyclically symmetric, its spatially averaged difference current responds equally to a change on any of its four directed links. Local site detunings have zero linear response in this average. The result concerns a weak reversible probe below its absorption line. ### Which property of the mouth controls the power?

Two passive contacts can have the same resting stiffness and exactly the same static pair. Compare the direct spring with a movable internal angular reference \(s\) on the co-moving support. Give it two positive springs, with energies \(G_1|y-s|^2/2\) and \(h|s|^2/2\), inertia \(m\) and drag \(D_s\). For peak phasors proportional to \(e^{-i\omega t}\) its effective stiffness is
\[
 G(\omega)=G_1\frac{h-m\omega^2-i D_s\omega}{G_1+h-m\omega^2-iD_s\omega}.
\]
Taking \(G_1R/C=hR/C=16\) gives the original static stiffness 8. At \(\omega/\sqrt{h/m}=\sqrt{18/17}\) a single lossless cut has an exact radiation zero. The interacting lossless pair radiated **0.7905409742** times the direct-spring pair’s power. Giving the reference a dimensionless drag \(D_s\sqrt{h/m}/h=0.02\) at that frequency gave **0.9807662324** times the direct-spring radiation. Only **0.0169382087** of the angular drive work then reached the wave, and the internal drag accounted for the rest. The radiation comparison holds the imposed motion fixed. The second fraction uses a different denominator, the total work supplied to the modeled angular system. The force needed to move a complete draining mouth includes other material and flow terms. [Actuator, stored and dissipated work](../tools/mouth_pair_contact_motion.py)

A separate synthetic calibration held one mouth stationary, applied a known regular oscillating twist and measured the scattered amplitude and phase at \(1.55R\). The ratio of inferred surface torque to slip recovered the complex stiffness. With that calibration and the known static geometry, the two-mouth outgoing power was predicted within \(5\times10^{-9}\) relative error across the eight tested settings. The local, isotropic contact in these tests gave the same inferred stiffness for a dipole and a quadrupole drive. Real contacts could also depend on the angular pattern. This is a noiseless identifiability test within the chosen contact family. The brane model has yet to fix the reference inertia, drag and attachment strength from its own constituents. A stationary-mouth torque-and-phase measurement tests this part of the model. Motion can excite terms invisible to that measurement. **A translation-sensitive reading distinguishes a wider passive family.** Give the same internal reference a kinetic energy per unit area \(m|\dot s-\alpha(\dot{\mathbf X}\times D_r^*\hat n)|^2/2\). This is an example of internal angular inertia responding to translation relative to the resting medium. The dimensionless coefficient \(\alpha\) drops out of held-mouth angular scattering and static preparation. It changes the work required to accelerate the rim. A clamp could also register its reaction during a held angular drive. At half the reference frequency scale, the pair with \(\alpha=1\) radiated **0.499014274** of the power predicted from the held measurement alone with \(\alpha=0\). Both had identical held-mouth scattering. Reading the complex outgoing dipole from a *single translating* mouth as well supplied the missing torque. Those two one-mouth measurements then predicted the pair power at all nine tested settings to within \(5\times10^{-13}\) relative error, using noiseless data, a known static background and the specified local, isotropic translation coupling. This accuracy checks the inference and implementation inside the candidate model. It gives no experimental accuracy or evidence that the brane chooses the example's parameters. **Disposition.** A moving signed pair can source and register a traveling wave through the material equations in this specified realization. Identifying the photon requires explaining which material displacement actual optical transitions couple to and its behavior across the conversion layer. Identifying a free-mouth acceleration would also require the translational momentum budget. Angular drive work supplies neither the missing inertia nor an independent Newtonian acceleration.
