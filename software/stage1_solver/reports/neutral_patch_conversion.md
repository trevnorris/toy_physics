# Following the same neutral pairs through a draining conversion layer

**Both previously tagged pairs kept their original partners from 95% to 1% shear order. A held mouth rim received about 4–5% of a weak imposed sideways impulse during that passage.** This is a local 0PN material test at inward intake 0.25. The carrier supplies the flow and order profile. The bond weakening and the mechanical connection to the rim are explicit hypotheses. [Neutral preparation and force calibration](neutral_patch_sites.md) · [Archived wall transit](mouth_contact_tracking.md)

We recovered the nine continuously registered pairs for each material from the same 24-particle neutral preparation. We started from its saved relaxed coordinates, with the original site labels and no new selection. The patch traveled with the carrier along the flared wall, including its changing angular spacing. Four thermal histories shared the same clock. At each time step we checked whether each original pair remained mutual nearest neighbors across the two layers and bound more deeply than 60% of its **current** well depth. Each first failure ended that pair’s continuous-survival record. In the primary model the cross-layer well depth followed \(\chi/0.95\) times its entry value; same-layer cohesion remained fixed.

**Force at the rim.** We applied constant opposite sideways forces of magnitude 0.02 to the upper tagged site, with matched thermal noise and a zero-force copy. Twelve initially unloaded unit springs connected the lower sites to the patch’s current station on a stationary elastic strip. The strip follows the wall’s arclength to the held model waist at \(w=0.5R\). Its tension coefficient follows local shear order, its mass per unit length is one, and a matched dashpot terminates the deep end. We read stress at the waist and took half the difference between the opposite drives. The table gives the cumulative rim impulse divided by the applied impulse up to that order. Over 5.29667 model time units, the mean rim forces were **0.00097505** (central) and **0.00080977** (layered), in patch force units. These include all paths through the patch. The momentum balance accounts for the rim, momentum in the strip, the far termination and drag on the surrounding material. Halving the drive gave the same percentages to the precision shown. **Sensitivity and scope.** On the coarser grid the final readings were 4.91% and 4.08%, with one fewer continuously registered layered pair on the first path. Holding the cross-layer bonds at their entry strength gave 6.19% and 5.59% on that grid. Quadratic softening gave 3.90% and 3.14%. Reducing the wall grip stiffness to one quarter gave 4.08% and 3.38% with linear softening. Disconnecting the grip gave zero incremental rim force in the tested central case. The integrated load is better resolved than the instantaneous endpoint force. The patch clock, grip and wall propagation scale relative to the throat require independent calibration. The carrier was prescribed without backreaction. A gravity test would need to establish what lateral load a companion’s incident brane flow supplies to these sites and compare the rim reaction with the same drain alone.
