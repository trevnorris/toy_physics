# Slip and drag on the two layers of a held 0PN rim patch

The wall-facing layer gives a signed test of how deeply a companion’s lateral flow penetrates the patch. When only the brane-facing layer received the moving flow, its bonds pulled the wall-facing layer toward the companion against resistance from its resting surroundings. When both layers received the flow, those surroundings drove the wall-facing layer toward the companion. The direction of its drag distinguished the two cases in every conversion interval and every thermal history at eight mouth radii. [Layer measurements and checks](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/neutral_patch_layer_slip.json)

| Binding | Sites exposed to the moving flow | Brane-facing lag | Wall-facing lag | Drag on brane-facing layer | Drag on wall-facing layer |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Central | Brane-facing only | 30.72% | 73.83% | +0.002928 | −0.002494 |
| Layered | Brane-facing only | 27.82% | 76.37% | +0.002652 | −0.002252 |
| Central | Both layers | 3.44% | 9.81% | +0.000328 | +0.000935 |
| Layered | Both layers | 2.99% | 10.22% | +0.000285 | +0.000974 |

Each reading is paired minus isolated on the same equatorial patch at intake 0.25, with the held rim, its twelve unit grips and the conversion history fixed. The average covers one transit from 95% to 1% order and four matched thermal histories. Lag is the fraction of the integrated companion-induced displacement by which a layer falls behind the **same** carrier. We subtract local patch expansion and compare identical thermal impulses. The mean carrier speed at 8R was 7.94371 × 10⁻⁶ mouth radii per model time unit. Drag is summed over twelve sites per layer in patch force units, positive toward the companion. At 16R the drag signs persisted and their magnitudes were about a quarter as large. [Preparation and units](neutral_patch_companion_rim.md)

**Two readings of the force.** Let $B$ be the force exerted by the cross-layer bonds on the wall-facing layer and $G$ its outgoing grip load. Overdamped balance gives bath forces $B$ on the brane-facing layer and $G-B$ on the wall-facing layer. The velocity reading gives $12\gamma(eU-V)$, where $e$ is the layer’s exposure, $U$ is mean carrier velocity and $V$ is the layer’s mean incremental velocity. The calculation used unit drag per site. The two readings agreed within 4 × 10⁻¹⁴ patch force units across the fine runs. The grip transmits momentum into the elastic strip, which can store some during the transit. [Layer-force instrument](../tools/neutral_patch_layer_slip.py)

**What can be identified.** A fit using velocities and mechanical forces from three conversion intervals at 8R recovered the assigned drag and exposures and predicted the other two intervals and the 16R force readings. Nearly comoving layers make the separate coefficients sensitive to measurement error. The force direction is a simpler discriminator for these two trials. Refining the timestep and strip changed transit lags by less than 0.20 percentage points. On one archived isolated history, independent differentiation of the bond forces reproduced all eight local 8R drag readings within 0.013%. [Verified runs and independent review](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/_measurements/neutral_patch_layer_slip.json)

The native carrier supplies one velocity field. The physical exposure of each material layer remains open. The next independent input is a tagged wall-facing velocity and signed traction in a calculation where the resolved carrier exchanges momentum with the sites. The current test predicts what that instrument should read under each exposure. Converting a patch force to free acceleration also requires coverage, the other momentum channels and mouth inertia.
