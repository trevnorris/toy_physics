# Shear response through conversion at one draining mouth

**The order-weighted shear energy fixes how the reversible shear stiffness fades. It leaves open how much sideways force persists after a pulse.** We followed the saved wall parcel on the radius-one curved mouth at inward intake 0.24999999999821823. Between depths $w/R=1.24879$ and 1.54536, its shear order fell from 0.95 to 0.01 in 5.29667 carrier time units. The projected elastic energy for a local sideways displacement $X$ with one transverse gradient is $G_1\chi_B(\partial_z X)^2/2$. With the material reference held fixed, the same incremental strain at the exit produces $1/95$ of the entry traction. The shear contribution to the order energy is quadratic about the unstrained state, so this pulse has no direct first-order shear forcing of the order on that background. The depth projection and $G_1$ in the gauge units remain material inputs. **The two gauges distinguish strain from strain rate.** Prescribing both recorded velocities removes the unknown rear boundary from a second calculation on the four cells between the gauges. The supplied viscous law reproduced both recorded tractions for all 36 signed drives to within $8.24\times10^{-14}$ relative RMS. In the positive pulse with a free backing, the interval still had positive mean strain $8.94\times10^{-5}$ at exit. Its reconstructed mean traction was negative, and both raw gauges also read negative. A positive, spatially uniform elastic stiffness with a fixed material reference gives positive mean traction for that strain at every possible force scale. This distinguishes the two laws on synthetic data generated with viscosity.
