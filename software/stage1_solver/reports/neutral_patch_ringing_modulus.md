# Does a prestrained neutral brane stiffen the wave that reaches the first bend?

**The written wave law gave zero additional stiffening.** The previous radius-one mouth test found that the ringing wave pushed the held flare in the direction of its bend. For the smoothly weighted mean over times 24–48 (Hann window), the leading-order wave force would reverse above a dimensionless coefficient of about 0.03197 if its shear modulus varied as $\mu(s)/\mu(0)=1+\lambda s^2$ at fixed inertia. Here $s$ is slow shear, the sideways shift per unit depth. We tested a flat patch at the archived wall orders 0.99999991, 0.99992886 and 0.97411339, taken at depths 0.60, 0.85 and 1.10 of the same intake-0.25 mouth. The existing quadratic curl energy kept the same incremental modulus at positive and negative shears up to 1/32, the peak shear of the previous bend. This checks what the written law predicts. The physical coefficient remains open. [Mouth force and threshold](mouth_ringing_bend.md) · [Patch calculation](../tools/neutral_patch_ringing_modulus.py)

**Two microscopic candidates softened when their layers slid.** We used the existing central and layered bond laws in cold, periodic triangular sheets at the previously measured pitch and fixed gap. At each prestrain we probed along and across the slide and repeated at half probe strength. The central law settled with opposing sites aligned. The layered law settled with each site above a hollow. Aligned layers were unstable for that law. Independent direct energy derivatives gave these limiting coefficients of $s^2$ in the normalized, sign-averaged tangent stiffness.

| Neutral sample | Along the slide | Across the slide |
|---|---:|---:|
| Central bonds, aligned | −1.20003 | −0.40001 |
| Layered bonds, hollow | −35.62631 | −11.87544 |

Changing order multiplies the interlayer bond energy in these candidates, so its factor cancels from each normalized ratio. A single hollow also couples the two probe directions linearly in signed strain, with normalized coefficient −3.60291 for the tested hollow and axes. Finite-difference tractions verified this coupling. These static coefficients describe sliding in the tested crystals. **Why the sliding coefficients cannot calibrate the mouth wave.** Starting from zero shear, we allowed each sheet and its normal to turn together while holding pitch and gap fixed. Moving the assembly as a rigid body cost zero bond energy in both samples. A symmetric shear that leaves the wave’s curl zero cost four times the reference single-shear energy to quadratic order. For the written curl energy evaluated on the same displacement gradients in a fixed reference frame, those ratios were four and zero, respectively. The two single-gradient probes agreed after normalization. This rules out directly identifying the wave displacement with ordinary site movement in either material under these conditions. A transverse wave sampling just one displacement gradient could still have the same propagation speed in both descriptions.
