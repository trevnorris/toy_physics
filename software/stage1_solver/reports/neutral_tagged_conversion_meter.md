# Do the material contacts amplify a small prestrain during conversion?

**Neither of the two tested materials supplies the contact amplification or the energy release that the proposed wave source needs.** We reused the previously identified pair in each 24-site patch and the order history of the planar intake-0.25 test. Shear order fell from 0.85 to 0.45 in 1.29703 model time units. The sites stayed in their two planes at the original separation. As in the earlier microscopic model, loss of order reduced the strength of the attractions between planes. We prescribed no change in a bond’s relaxed length. [Tagged materials](neutral_patch_conversion.md) · [Wave-source test](mouth_conversion_wave_patch.md)

**What the contacts did.** We held the tagged ends at fixed separation after opposite sideways steps of 0.002 bond lengths and repeated at 0.001. On copies at nine ages we moved the ends equally and oppositely to locate the sideways force zero. The measured extension is the held offset minus that zero. Taking the difference between opposite prestrains removes drift common to both copies. One preparation centered the single bond. A separate preparation started at zero total clamp force and let the other 22 sites move throughout the passage. The neighbors were frozen only during the instantaneous force readings. Ratios compare the exit to the entrance. Changing only the depth of a single attractive well leaves its force zero in place. The neighboring material could move that zero. In the converting cold patches it relaxed about 94% and 96% of the imposed differential extension. The tags kept their partners at every checked step. Halving the displacement and the time step preserved the result. In two matched thermal histories per material the whole-clamp ratios were 0.05787–0.05932 and 0.03644–0.03770. An independent linear-response calculation reproduced the cold readings. For the previously tested conversion-driven contact law, the weak-wave sign crossing at the mouth frequency corresponded to **51.99** in the tagged-extension test.

**What conversion cost.** At zero added prestrain in the cold total-clamp preparation, reducing the bond strengths required **+0.083621** and **+0.146187** patch energy units per site for the central and layered laws. An independent calculation let all sites relax in their planes at each order level and found costs of +0.083621 and +0.146114. In the specified energy convention each interplane binding energy is negative relative to dissociated sites. Weakening those attractions raises their energy. The additional internal energy per constituent assumed in the earlier wave-source test has no microscopic counterpart in these two laws. Their patch-unit costs do not determine the full brane-to-bulk energy difference. **0PN decision.** Set aside these two materials as realizations of the proposed conversion-driven contact mechanism. The question of how much wave energy a mouth retains remains open. A further test of conversion as its source needs a distinct receiving-material state, with energy independently referenced to the incoming material, and a way for the difference to do coherent work. A wave that acquires its energy at throat formation is also consistent with this result. Working base `1dc45d09`.
