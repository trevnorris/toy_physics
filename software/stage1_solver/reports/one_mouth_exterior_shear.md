# Shear just outside one accelerating mouth at 0PN

The Stage 1 probe now evolves the brane's in-plane transverse displacement \(\mathbf u(r,\theta,t)\) for a prescribed mouth motion. I repeated the four commands \(X=At^2/2\) and the stationary control in this field, using the archived radius-one drain with inward number rate \(0.25\) and zero sideways reservoir velocity. The carrier solution is reused one-way. Only the opening's motion drives the new field. The contact strength and material properties in this test are illustrative. [Field solver](../src/stage1_solver/mouth_shear.py) · [Probe](../tools/mouth_shear_field_probe.py) · [Archived carrier benchmark](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_prescribed_acceleration.txt)

The exterior is homogeneous, initially at rest, and supports outgoing rotational shear. I used density and transverse wave speed equal to one. The test contact transmits tangential traction per area \(\gamma(\mathbf V_{\rm mouth}-\dot{\mathbf u})_\parallel\). This allows the tangential material displacement to differ from the motion of the opening. No bare mouth mass or normal attachment is used. For the dipole \(u_r=p\cos\theta,\ u_\theta=-d\sin\theta\), the integrated force along the drive axis on material **outside** a spherical cut is

\[
F_{\rm shear}(r,t)=\frac{8\pi}{3}\mu_R r\,[p-\partial_r(r d)] .
\]

The probe evaluates this from displacement samples at neighboring radii. Both cuts are in the brane, at \(r=1.000025\) and \(1.0001\). The retarded radial solution resolves the near-rim wave inside a single carrier grid cell. A force at either cut excludes the momentum changing between the rim and that cut. ### Can the four runs calibrate the contact?

The original four accelerations and the stationary control saved the center position, inward rate, carried momentum and scalar-carrier stress at the opening. Those runs evolve the carrier with no dependence on \(\gamma\). The material theory allows the brane and carrier to slip tangentially. Tracking carrier density or phase therefore supplies no independent brane marker. All 60 later shell readings came from the solver with a specified contact strength. Mathematica took that same strength as input. There are zero independent near-rim displacement or shear-traction histories for these four accelerations in the records examined. [Carrier archive inspection](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_material_readout.txt) · [Material and carrier velocities](pathA_35_G0_freeze.md#L38) · [Shear run inputs](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_shear_field_probe.txt)

For a direct calibration of the trial slip law, an independent interface calculation or observation must supply the tangential traction **on the brane** and its velocity at the same rim location and time. At the equator, with both components signed along the drive, \(\gamma=t_\parallel/(\dot X-v_{\parallel,\rm brane})\) wherever slip is resolved. An independent displacement history at the two exterior radii is an alternative if the draining material's density and shear response are known independently. In that case fit the histories through the exterior propagation law, which accounts for travel time and momentum stored between rim and shell. Use the full-strength pair for the fit and the half-strength pair for prediction, with the stationary run as baseline. The physical coefficient remains open. ### What the interface equations determine at one rim point

At the equator the translation axis is tangent to the spherical rim. For the homogeneous rotational shear field above, the brane velocity along that axis is \(v=\partial_t d(a^+,t)\), and the local traction **on the exterior brane** is \(t_\parallel=(\mu_R/a)[p-\partial_r(rd)]_{a^+}\). These give both observables at the same point if the material field is solved. The interface variation retains a separate mouth-work term \(t_A^i\,\delta u_i\); it leaves the tangential loading by the moving mouth open. The prescribed command gives \(V=\dot X\). The feeding-side stress reported by the driver is integrated across the aperture and supplies no pointwise value of \(t_A^i\). [Rotational boundary variation](pathA_23_stage3_noleak_closure.md#L64) · [Mouth work](pathA_23_stage1_kinematic_leak_audit.md#L146) · [Carrier readout](../tools/mouth_prescribed_acceleration.py#L60)

Even granting a known homogeneous exterior, write \(\widehat t_\parallel=Z(s)\widehat v\) for its causal response from rest. Hats denote Laplace transforms and \(Z\) is the local shear traction per unit brane velocity. Combining this equation with the trial slip law gives \(\widehat v=\gamma\widehat V/(Z+\gamma)\) and \(\widehat t_\parallel=Z\gamma\widehat V/(Z+\gamma)\). Every positive \(\gamma\) yields a pair consistent with the same prescribed motion. Reversing or halving \(V\) reverses or halves both outputs for every such coefficient. An independently known \(Z\) and **one** independently determined, nondegenerate material velocity or traction history at the rim would suffice within this model. The separate E5 wall equation uses the **bulk** tangential velocity trace on the wall, takes its contact coefficient as an open input, and locks the normal velocity to the wall. Extending that normal condition across the aperture would suppress relative inward drainage. The E4 constraint instead locks a collar shear velocity to the prescribed motion. Neither supplies independent rim data for this preparation. The full-strength contact fit and the half-strength physical test are both undetermined.
