## The equation the solver needs

Use the geometric center $X$ along the test axis. Define $L_x$ as sideways momentum per unit time arriving through the three-dimensional opening, including carried momentum relative to the cut and stress. Let $P_X$ be the momentum assigned to the translating mouth. Let $R_x$ account for the momentum passed on, storage outside $P_X$, and any separately counted rim reaction with its signed contribution. Use one partition throughout. Define the external force required by the physical equations as

$$
\mathcal F_x=\dot P_X+R_x-L_x=f+H .
$$

Here $f$ is the known actuator force and $H$ is the force the holder exerts on the mouth. Positive force does positive work for positive displacement. The implementation needs a mouth-level equation for $\dot P_X+R_x$, or an independently calibrated equivalent. For the held measurement it must determine the steady momentum passed on as the incident flow changes. For release it must also determine how the required force changes when the center accelerates from the *same* prepared state. These are independent inputs. They can be represented at the opening without resolving the throat interior. An inertia inferred from a force that was itself inferred from an assumed inertia would give a circular calibration. ## How to separate momentum passing through from momentum moving with the mouth

**What passes through.** Take two traces immediately on either side of the same three-dimensional opening, both oriented from the brane into the drain. Call their signed sideways momentum rates $T_-$ and $T_+$, respectively. For either trace, integrate the lab-frame current $(\Pi_{xj}-g_x w_j)N_j$ over the moving opening, where $g_x$ is momentum density, $w$ is cut velocity, $N$ is the common normal and $\Pi$ includes carriage and stress. For a stationary flat opening on a single neutral carrier with equal pointwise inward number current and a common differentiable phase, the tangential velocities coincide. All the *carried* sideways momentum then passes through. The mixed density stress depends on the receiving-side normal density slope and its tangential variation. The feed equations leave that slope open. [Moving-cut balance](../../../docs/drain_driven_initial_acceleration.md#momentum-arriving-and-momentum-leaving) · [Two admissible local traces](../../../docs/held_drain_pair.md#what-passes-on-if-the-carrier-phase-is-continuous-at-the-mouth)

For the stationary radius-1 benchmark at $U=+0.01$, the feeding field gives carriage $+0.00249992505059$ and total $T_-=+0.00249999985299$ in solver force units. A smooth local continuation gives $T_+=T_-$. The alternative local match with zero normal density slope on the receiving side gives zero mixed stress there and $T_+=+0.00249992505059$ on the same boundary-law readout. Both use the same feeding field and inward current. These are examples of possible boundary data, with no global receiving solution or physical selection. **What moves with it.** Fix the material, field and any surface momentum assigned to the mouth, and use the same moving boundary and lab axis in every run. For example, on the fine periodic grid with the restoring density law, the slope of carrier momentum with respect to prescribed steady mouth speed is $+0.00052348043409$ for the full feeding patch and $-0.000300120219104$ for the portion over the mouth footprint. **Measurement at release.** From the same inward-draining state in resting brane, apply the calibrated $+f,-f$ steps and their half-strength versions, retaining a zero-force control. Over the same interval $[0,t]$, use differences from that control. Write $J_{\rm in}$ for the applied impulse plus the change in feeding-side impulse, $I_+$ for the change in impulse passing the receiving trace, and $I_{\rm other}$ for the remaining signed terms in $R_x$. The latter includes other outward flux and change in excluded stored momentum, less any separately acting rim impulse, counted once. Then

$$
\Delta P_X+I_++I_{\rm other}=J_{\rm in}.
$$

Here $\Delta P_X$ is the change in assigned momentum from the start relative to the control. For a constant force step $f$ and a smooth start with bounded acceleration, the archived finite-grid exterior gives $J_{\rm in}=ft+O(t^3)$, since the change in the incoming load starts at order $t^2$. Track $X(t)$ as well. Even when the velocity change $\Delta V$ is measured, adding $k\Delta V$ to one of $\Delta P_X,I_+$ and subtracting it from the other leaves the measured sum unchanged. The fixed inventory and physical mouth law would select one split. In a steady held state $\dot P_X=0$, so $H+T_-$ gives the entire net rate passed on, including the other signed terms. The receiving-face rate alone still requires those terms to be accounted for. Record the receiving-side relative number current, tangential velocity and total mixed stress across the opening at each time, or measure their integrated total $T_+$ directly.
