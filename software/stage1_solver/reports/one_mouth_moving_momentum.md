# Sideways momentum conservation at a moving mouth, 0PN

**Result.** Momentum conservation relates the two sides of the opening to the force on the rim. The saved radius-one drain, with zero incident sideways speed and prescribed inward number rate \(J=0.25\), supplies only the feeding side. SymPy and Mathematica give the same balance and distinguish two receiving-side traces at the same pointwise current. The physical acceleration remains open. [Guarded runs and outputs](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_moving_momentum.txt)

**Moving balance.** Take the same normal \(N\) from the brane into the drain on both faces. At each face the sideways momentum transmitted per unit time is \(T=\int_A (\Pi_{xN}-g_x W_N)\,d^3r\), where \(W_N\) is the face's normal speed, \(g_x\) is lab-frame momentum density and \(\Pi\) is the full momentum current. For the neutral carrier this is \(\int_A(mjv_x-\sigma_{xN})\,d^3r\), where \(j=n(v_N-W_N)\), \(v_x\) is the lab velocity and \(\sigma\) is the tensile-positive Cauchy stress. For sideways translation of the flat opening \(W_N=0\) on its broad faces. Its perimeter still moves. Integrating the balance over a small region following the opening gives

\[
\dot P_{\rm rim}= f_{\rm push}+T_{\rm feed}-T_{\rm recv}-E_x-\dot P_{\rm other}.
\]

Here \(E_x\) counts the remaining net outward momentum per time at the edge and other cuts, including traction. \(P_{\rm other}\) is the momentum in that region excluded from the chosen rim inventory. This form assumes that the push is the only separately applied external force. If the region includes other fields, their momentum and stresses belong in the same budget exactly once. In a frame momentarily moving at speed \(V\), the carried flux on **each** side changes by \(-mJV\) for equal inward rates. It cancels in the difference. In a frame accelerating at \(a\), the carrier momentum equation acquires \(-mN_c a\) for its enclosed constituent count \(N_c\). This is the same balance in different coordinates. It supplies no independent relation between the geometric center and the stored momentum. **Test on the saved three-dimensional opening.** Share the density, differentiable phase trace and relative normal number current pointwise on both sides. The same tangential velocity then carries the same momentum through both faces. Let \(b=\partial_N\log n\). The mixed density contribution to the momentum flux is \(-\beta n\partial_x b\), with \(\beta=1/4\) in solver units. Smooth continuation gives zero face difference. As a second local continuation set \(b_{\rm recv}=b_{\rm feed}+\epsilon h\), with \(h=x\max(1-4r^2,0)^3\) in centered mouth coordinates. This changes the receiving density slope only in the inner half-radius and preserves the pointwise current and the trace at the perimeter. The feed-minus-receiving flux changes by \(\epsilon\beta\int_A n\,\partial_x h\,d^3r\). On the archived field with \(\epsilon=+0.01\), both engines give **\(-8.9281041\times10^{-8}\)** in solver force units. Reversing the trial slope reverses the sign. Halving it halves the difference. These are possible local stresses under a chosen slope variation. Their amplitudes do not measure the physical response to a rim push.
