# Measuring the 0PN holding force outside a draining mouth

## Does the bulk calculation match the saved draining mouth?

**The receiving-side force is still unmeasured.** The stationary radius-one feeding fields stop at the opening. The archived finite receiver has a spatially uniform inlet phase, different bath and wall conditions, and uses rates 0 and 0.125 in its lateral-response runs. Those runs impose frame acceleration. Their outlet stresses do not belong to the rate-0.25 held-mouth experiment. [Receiver inputs and measurements](../../../docs/_measurements/open_sleeve_lateral_balance.txt) · [Held-mouth input and measurements](../../../docs/_measurements/held_crossflow_budget.txt)

**Direct matching test.** I used the full saved normal carrier current \(j(\mathbf y)\) at zero and opposite sideways speeds to drive an ideal 4D bulk in \(w>0\). The test assumes constant receiver number density \(n_b>0\), a flat boundary with zero normal flow outside the opening, no other sources in the receiver, and irrotational, steady flow. The archived 3D opening is a single aperture for the receiver calculation. In solver units \(m=\hbar=1\). The decaying modes of the half-space Laplace equation with \(\partial_w\phi=j/n_b\) give the boundary velocity potential

\[
\phi(\mathbf x,0)=c+\mathbf W\!\cdot\!\mathbf x-
\frac{1}{2\pi^2 n_b}\int_{\rm mouth}\frac{j(\mathbf y)}{|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|^2}\,d^3y.
\]

For a direct continuation of a single neutral carrier, its phase differences on the opening must agree on both sides. At each \(x=\pm1/2\), I compared the phase at \(y=0\) with the mean at \(y=\pm1/2\), all at \(z=0\) in the brane coordinates. This difference cancels the arbitrary phase origin and **any** uniform sideways bulk speed separately in each run. The measured feed phase includes the imposed phase gradient. Negative speeds are reflected partners of the measured positive-speed fields. [Numerical calculation](../tools/mouth_bulk_phase_match.py) · [Fourier and radial check](../tools/mouth_bulk_phase_match.wl)

At rest the mean of the two contrasts on the fine grid was **+0.0272607125057** on the feeding side and **−0.0278545760267** in the receiver at \(n_b=1\), in radians. The coarser grid also gave opposite signs. The continuum radial receiver gives **−0.02830834579470561**. Every positive constant \(n_b\) preserves that sign. Taking half the difference between the contrasts at positive and negative \(x\) isolates the sideways response. At \(U=+0.02\) it was \(+2.13765019876\times10^{-6}\) on the feeding side and \(-6.93940615337\times10^{-6}\) in the receiver. At \(U=+0.01\) the readings were \(+1.06881244585\times10^{-6}\) and \(-3.46962189583\times10^{-6}\). The negative-speed partners reversed these signs. Every run kept inward number rate 0.25. The largest reported difference between the two cell-integration methods was \(6.75\times10^{-14}\) for these contrasts. **Decision for gravity.** The previous ideal holding-force formula below remains conditional. Directly attaching this flat receiver to the saved feeding trace fails a necessary matching condition, even with its far sideways velocity free. The check concerns the neutral carrier phase. It places no force bound on a curved, converting mouth and tests no photon or shear-wave trajectory. The physical calculation needs either a matched receiving solution with an effective mouth condition specifying sideways momentum transfer (including stress), or an independent reading of total outward momentum in the bulk under these same signed drives. A measured mechanical support reaction could replace that budget. No holder force or release acceleration was inferred. Working result at base fd667ea9, pending review.

## Conditional exterior force calculation

**Exterior force measurement.** Enclose one held mouth and the material exchanging momentum with it. Take \(+x\) along the imposed sideways brane flow. With outward flux positive, the force \(H_x\) exerted by the holder obeys \(H_x=\dot P_x+\Phi_x-F_{{\rm other},x}\). Here \(\Phi_x\) is the flux of sideways momentum on the complete external surface, including carriage and stress. \(F_{{\rm other},x}\) counts separately applied forces inside the boundary. Take the difference from zero sideways flow at the same intake. If both states are steady and the holder supplies the only changing external force inside, the change of the total outward flux gives the holding force directly. The internal brane-to-bulk cut cancels when both sides of the transfer and its reactions are included. A change in local rim stress at fixed total outer flux must be balanced elsewhere in the steady budget. Forces from any separately pinned wall, feed or return controller still require accounting. [Conservation and cut conventions](../../../docs/held_drain_pair.md#what-the-holder-would-measure)

**Calculated benchmark.** Two distinct ideal exterior domains share a localized transfer at the held mouth. The 3D brane absorbs and the 4D bulk emits the same mass per second \(\dot M>0\). Each exterior has its own constant density, steady inviscid potential flow and a spherically spreading background. The perturbed far velocities along \(+x\) are \(U\) on the brane and \(W\) in the bulk. These are independent boundary data. For a sphere of radius \(R\) in \(D\) dimensions, let \(q\) be its signed outward mass rate. Write the dipole velocity potential as \(f(R)\cos\theta\). Integrating the first variation of \(\rho v_x v_R+p\cos\theta\), with Bernoulli pressure, gives \(q[f'+(D-1)f/R]/D\). The uniform-flow potential \(f=VR\) contributes \(qV\). The decaying dipole \(f=B/R^{D-1}\) contributes zero after pressure and carriage are added, even when the local velocity perturbation is nonzero. Summing the cuts gives

\[
H_x=\dot M(W-U)
\]

to first order, under the single-holder and stationary conditions above. For \(\dot M=1/4\) in solver mass units and \(U=+0.02\), the calculated forces are \(-0.005\), \(-0.0025\) and zero for \(W=0,U/2,U\), respectively. Reversing \(U\) and \(W\) reverses each force, and halving both halves it. SymPy integrated in polar components. Mathematica formed the Cartesian stress from the potentials and checked the odd flux at two different pairs of cut radii. [Force calculations and literal outputs](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_outer_holding_force.txt)

**Companion check.** Place a source mouth a distance \(d\) along \(+x\). Give each mouth equal intake and discharge mass rates of its own, with \(\dot M_t,\dot M_s>0\) for target and source respectively. Take both brane intakes and both bulk discharges to be isotropic point flows in their respective unbounded domains. At the target the companion drives \(U=+\dot M_s/(4\pi\rho_3d^2)\) and \(W=-\dot M_s/(2\pi^2\rho_4d^3)\). These densities are per 3D and 4D volume. The paired-minus-isolated net flux is independent of enclosing radius in each source-free shell by steady momentum conservation. Shrinking the cut around the point target leaves the companion's regular velocity at its center. The companion-only stress is smooth at the target and gives zero in that limit. Thus the holder pulls away from the companion with

\[
\Delta H_x=-\dot M_t\dot M_s
 \left(\frac{1}{4\pi\rho_3d^2}+\frac{1}{2\pi^2\rho_4d^3}\right).
\]

A direct Mathematica integration kept the companion's velocity varying across the whole sphere. Doubling separation from 8 to 16 reduced the brane and bulk terms by factors of four and eight, respectively, at both cut radii 1 and 3. The inverse-cube term belongs to this chosen 4D discharge geometry. **What the physical test still needs.** The radius-one archive at prescribed carrier number intake 0.25 supplies the feeding-side momentum, with no matched exterior bulk field. Even a bulk pressure approaching a constant can leave a finite sideways flux. For example, an odd pressure contribution \(p_1\cos\theta/R^3\) alone integrates to \(\pi^2p_1/2\) on a 4D sphere. This is a sensitivity of the measurement, not an asserted solution. The discriminating observable is the signed change in **total** external momentum flux, including the actual bulk pressure and carriage, all brane stresses, and any other applied loads under the same holding protocol. The physical model has yet to supply these fields or an independently measured holder reaction. A free-fall prediction also requires the independent push response \(\chi\) of that same draining mouth. The benchmark has no assigned bare mass and yields no measured acceleration.
