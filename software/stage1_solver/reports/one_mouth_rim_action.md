# Can the 0PN action drive the rim of the saved draining mouth?

**Working result.** The confinement energy gives a reciprocal force between the wall and carrier. A responding field can contribute inertia without assigning mass to the center. The existing action and boundary data do not determine that response for the saved radius-one mouth with prescribed inward number rate $J=0.25$. Both algebra engines and both saved-field readouts agree. The four physical pushes remain unrun. [Run record and literal outputs](../../../docs/_measurements/mouth_rim_action.txt)

**Coupling obtained from the action.** Write $V(r-R_0)$ for the confinement profile with the wall thickness absorbed in its argument and $\nu=\cos\theta$ along the push. Moving a wall section by $q\nu$ changes the potential seen by the carrier by $-q\nu\,\partial_r V$. From $E_{\rm conf}=\int nV\,d^4x$, a density perturbation $n_1(r,w)\nu$ exerts the opposite reaction on the section,

$$
\delta F_q(w)=\frac{4\pi}{3}\int r^2 n_1(r,w)\,\partial_r V\,dr .
$$

This is force per unit axial length. A rim push does endpoint work $f\,\delta q(0)$. Taking outward rim displacement as positive, the reaction above has a plus sign from $-\partial_q E_{\rm conf}$. The parent paper prints a minus in its schematic density-source equation; the calculation here follows its printed energy and displacement convention. **What inertia could emerge.** Suppose the actual translation dipole and its density response give a coupling $C X y$ in the Lagrangian to a stable field mode $y$ of frequency $\Omega>0$, normalized to kinetic energy $\dot y^2/2$. With no direct $\dot X^2$ term and static coefficient $K$, eliminating $y$ gives force per displacement $K-C^2/(\Omega^2-\omega^2)$. The coefficient of $-\omega^2$ at low frequency is **$C^2/\Omega^4$**. This establishes a mechanism for slow added inertia. Its magnitude requires the normalized dipole mode, its coupling to the rim, and its frequency for this draining state. The paper develops this reduction for other angular modes. Its low-frequency coefficient alone does not give the initial response to a sudden push. [Parent mode reduction](../../../research/pde/paper/pde.tex#L1249) · [Required branch inputs](../../../research/pde/paper/pde.tex#L2517)

**Where the action stops on the actual opening.** At fixed center let $u=|\psi|$ and $\theta$ be the carrier phase. In solver units the restoring energy $\tfrac12\int g_X(u-1)^2 dA$ supplies the amplitude derivative $g_X(u-1)$. The imposed inward current requires the phase derivative $-j[u]$, where $j[u]=J s u^2/\int s u^2dA$ and $s$ is the saved normal-speed shape. Mixed variations of a twice differentiable instantaneous energy of these two traces must agree. Choose $\delta u=u h$ and $\delta\theta=h$, with $h=xg_X$ over the three-dimensional opening. The amplitude derivative is phase independent. The phase derivative changes with amplitude by the signed mixed work $-2\int j(h-\bar h)^2 dA$, with $\bar h=\int jh\,dA/J$. On the saved zero-sideways-flow field this was **$-0.00578340356927687$** in solver normalization. Both full- and half-width variations approach that value; a uniform variation gives zero to roundoff. This excludes an instantaneous energy of the current amplitude and phase alone that generates **both** saved boundary laws. An open interface with additional responding fields or boundary work can evade that restriction.
