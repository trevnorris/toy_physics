# Can a normal rim traction distinguish attraction from drag at 0PN?

Take a spherical opening of radius $a$ with unchanged inward intake. Let $+x$ point toward the companion, $\mu=\mathbf n\cdot\hat{\mathbf x}$, and $\mathbf n$ point outward from the rim within the brane. Measure all changes from the isolated resting drain. The proposed law has $\dot e+e/\tau=\delta v_{\rm br,n}-V\mu$ and exerts $ke\,\mathbf n$ per rim area on the opening. Here $V$ is the geometric center velocity, $k>0$ and $\tau>0$ are uniform over the rim, and $\delta v_{\rm br,n}$ is the **material** velocity perturbation. This proposal concerns the in-plane edge force. Inward drainage through the opening continues.

**Conditional prediction.** Define $u_1=(3/4\pi)\int \mu\,\delta v_{\rm br,n}\,d\Omega$. Angular integration gives $\tau\dot F_x+F_x=\Gamma(u_1-V)$, where $\Gamma=4\pi a^2k\tau/3$. With the same slip history and initial strain, a held opening with $u_1=+u$ has the same rim force as an opening moving at $V=-u$ with $u_1=0$. At steady positive $V$ in stationary material this term removes energy at rate $\Gamma V^2$. If the two full preparations are related by a uniform change of inertial frame, any deformation that accompanies the moving opening transforms with it. Entrainment alone gives no independent suppression of drag in that comparison. [Two-engine calculation](../../../docs/_measurements/rim_slip_comparison.json)

**Finite companion distance.** For an undisturbed point-drain potential $Q_s/(4\pi|\mathbf x-d\hat{\mathbf x}|)$ with $d>a$, projecting its radial velocity on the *whole* rim gives $u_1=Q_s/(4\pi d^2)$ exactly. The faster flow at the near edge and the slower flow at the far edge leave the same dipole as uniform motion at the center speed. The calculation assumes the incident material follows that potential. A responding brane and stresses from other channels remain open. [Direct velocity and independent potential projections](../../../docs/_measurements/rim_slip_comparison.json)

**Saved runs.** The radius-one carrier archive prescribes $J=0.25$. At $U=+0.01$ its feeding-side sideways momentum flux, including stress, is $+0.00249999985299$. In the corresponding $V=-0.01$ resting-feed laboratory frame it is $-1.47005441614\times10^{-10}$. The difference is $+0.0025=JU$ in solver units. At twice the speed it is $+0.005$. Both readings use the same solved carrier envelope. On two cuts with equal intake the boost shifts both carried fluxes equally and cancels from their difference. The small one-face reading supplies no measurement of mechanical drag. **Decision.** The proposed term alone ties steady pull to drift resistance whenever the local material slips are matched. To establish a separate effect, compare an actual held companion with a uniform-flow control of the same center incident speed and an isolated translating mouth, with the same target preparation and specified reservoir and bulk conditions. For each, record $u_1-V$ over the whole rim and the signed total holding or driving reaction. A difference at matched slip would require physics beyond this term to explain the total. The saved packets contain neither the brane-material rim velocity nor the actuator reaction. Physical attraction, drag and orbital energy loss remain unmeasured here.
