# Which material detects the torsional wave, and where does its power travel?

**The candidate carries an observable wave. Its identification with the existing optical displacement remains open.** A mechanical instrument can send and receive the wave by moving the two faces oppositely. The earlier bound pair of constituents has a stricter selection rule. At strong binding it can sense a weak, slowly varying twist through a circulating constituent current. Absorbing energy in a single ground-state transition driven by that twist requires access to a face-odd, separated state. A bulk that relaxes static twist can still carry a substantial part of a traveling wave's energy. ### Emission and detection in the same neutral resting medium

Equip a square of the angular lattice with two equal movable pistons, one on each face. Moving a piston changes the four contact springs' preferred angles equally. A spring in the common housing references each piston to the extension difference of two diagonal braces, which measures ordinary shear on that square. Another spring links the pistons. For face sign \(s=\pm1\), angular circulation \(F\), strain \(e\), and piston travel \(q_s\), the summed static energy is
\[
V=\tfrac12\mu e^2+
\sum_s\left[\tfrac K8(sF+4\ell q_s)^2+\tfrac h2(q_s-be)^2\right]
+\tfrac{h_b}{2}(q_+-q_-)^2 .
\]
This is proposed hardware. The levers \(\ell,b\), three spring constants, piston inertia and drag could each be measured by applying a force or torque to one element. The cross term with the angular wave also holds dynamically because summing four spring extensions eliminates the corner angles. The ordinary field couples to the sum of piston travels and the signed field to their difference. Crossed-diagonal strain measures symmetric deformation. Its identification with the existing shear observable here is limited to an axis-aligned transverse plane wave. [Original angular lattice](mouth_torsional_light.md)

Drive one instrumented plane and dissipate the signal in another 12 lattice spacings away. The outgoing leads and the dampers at both instruments enter the power balance. Both leads have the same bare long-wave speed. At angular frequency 0.38 in the chosen units the following fractions of the total input power were dissipated at the remote instrument.

| Driven pistons | Common-motion detector | Opposite-motion detector |
|---|---:|---:|
| Both pistons together | 0.0304817479442 | Zero by symmetry |
| Pistons oppositely | Zero by symmetry | 0.173723117193 |
| Force on one piston, partner free | 0.016432626519 | 0.0800694622985 |

Swapping the drive and readout gave the reciprocal complex response. This calculation resolves one axial transverse polarization per plane. Identical apparatus in the orthogonal plane tests the other by cubic permutation. If every such square is instrumented, relaxing the pistons reduces its two-face curl modulus from 4 to 2.439024390243903 and raises its ordinary shear modulus from 2 to 2.696774193548387. Even if the bare housing loses its static shear stiffness, keeping these measuring contacts engaged leaves a contribution of 0.696774193548 in that strain channel. ### A detector already present in the model

Place one constituent on each material face. The earlier Hamiltonian gives a hopping energy \(t\) and costs \(\Delta\) when the particles occupy different sites. Test its ground state against a real change in the bond hopping and against opposite changes of its two directed bond phases. For a two-site link let \(Q=n_{t,R}-n_{b,R}\). Then \(H(a)=e^{iaQ}H(0)e^{-iaQ}\). Static link measurements cannot distinguish two time-dependent preparations. In the bond frame the drive is \((v+\hbar\dot a)Q\), where \(vQ\) is the differential site energy. Clocks that follow the link with \(v=-\hbar\dot a\) cancel the drive exactly. With fixed site energies, at \(\Delta/t=16\) the low transition at \(0.246211251t\) is dark. The signed drive reaches the face-odd separated state at \(16.246211251t\). Real hopping modulation reaches a face-even state at \(16.492422502t\). These are model binding energies. No optical frequency in physical units has been assigned. A microscopic clock example projects a constituent onto a slowly changing, prescribed two-component state \(z_i\). Its projected rightward hop is proportional to \(z_j^\dagger z_i\), and its site energy includes \(-i\hbar z_i^\dagger\dot z_i\). Rephasing the two local bases cancels from the measurable bond drive. Tilting one site of an initially uniform patch gives zero to first order in this preparation. With a neighboring normal already turned through 0.4 radians in the orthogonal plane, the same tilt gives opposite coefficients of magnitude 0.101355017755 per unit tilt rate on the two mirrors, in units \(\hbar=1\). This tests a textured contact with specified state transport. How an incident wave drives those site states is a separate input. [Clock and mirror control](../tools/mouth_pair_optical_transition.py)

A closed square removes the single-link ambiguity about static circulation. For any face-matched graph with this separation energy, a face-antisymmetric wavefunction vanishes whenever both particles occupy the same site. Its spectrum is exactly \(\Delta+\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j\), \(i<j\), where the \(\epsilon\)'s are the single-face hopping energies. A linear face-odd drive from the even ground state can only reach this sector. On the uniform square at \(\Delta/t=16\), the four low states included the ground state. Their signed-flux transition strengths from the ground state vanish, while a common local bond strain has nonzero weight there. The first active signed-loop transition cost \(16.499541679t\). Unequal bonds shared by both faces preserved the low-band zero and moved that first line to \(14.363349154t\). Thus the signed perturbation has a binding-scale absorption line and zero oscillator strength for transitions between the paired low states from the ground state. **Detection below the absorption line.** A weak signal can polarize the bound pair through virtual excursions to the separated states. In the open dimer at \(\Delta/t=16\), the slow change of differential occupancy per unit of \(v+\hbar\dot a\) was −0.001837813099 per unit of hopping energy (in units of inverse \(t\)). On a square, an actual closed-loop turn \(F\) generates opposite circulating constituent currents on the two faces. The conjugate current \(I_F=-\partial E_0/\partial F\) reverses when the turn reverses. With \(t=1,\Delta=16\) its linear slope was −0.007111516715 from spectral response. Direct ground-energy and current differences agreed within the expected finite-step error. A common real bond strain gave zero signed current. Extending the loop to six sites reduced the slope magnitude to 0.000108049792. This predicts a face-resolved, reversible readout for a wave that threads the ordered loop with signed flux. Its size depends on the detector. The lossless ground-state response absorbs no net energy over a slow cycle. [Polarization and current controls](../tools/mouth_pair_optical_polarization.py)

### Loss of order and optical power

The previous three-dimensional springs act along the brane. As a separate hypothesis, route four such contacts in series between neighboring depths. Identify the ordered fraction \(\chi_B\) with the fraction of complete contact units whose angular references are locked. The remaining references can slide, with inertia \(H\) and passive drag \(D\) for their combined slip. Their restoring modulus for a harmonic twist is
\[
M(\omega)=\chi_B C+(1-\chi_B)C\frac{-H\omega^2-iD\omega}{C-H\omega^2-iD\omega},
\qquad C=K/4 .
\]
This construction reproduces the supplied static modulus \(\chi_B C\). Whether the references survive conversion, their resonance \(\omega_0=\sqrt{C/H}\), and the normal coupling are independent material questions. For a wave incident **head-on**, the lossless, unlocked receiver reflects all power at \(0.5\omega_0\) and transmits 0.994845224 at \(2\omega_0\). With drag \(D=32\), at \(0.5\omega_0\) it converts 0.430383592 into heat. If the contact physically disconnects, all incident torsional power reflects in the lossless, single-channel control. **A wave traveling along the slab is a different experiment.** Use the same sliding-contact law for both depth and in-plane gradients in a welded, translation-invariant slab of thickness 64. For one transverse internal angular polarization, the in-plane wave number is shared across either face. It is real in the lossless bound-mode calculation and complex when the mode attenuates. In the lossless positive-modulus regime a bound mode requires \(k_b<k_\parallel<k_s\), where \(k_b=\omega\sqrt{\rho_b/M}\) and \(k_s=\omega\sqrt{\rho_s/C}\). Matching the actual torques fixes the wave number and energy distribution. The even depth envelope here refers to the amplitude of the internally counterrotating wave. With equal angular densities of 4 this gives limiting speed 0.455089860562 versus 0.707106781187 for the bare core. Keeping drag positive changes the limit. **Disposition.** Keep opposite-face torsion as a candidate signed field. The ordinary optical branch remains even under face exchange. The constituent loop can read the signed field below its absorption threshold through a reversible current. These two material probes distinguish the wave species. The sliding-reference bulk also fails a strict requirement that light energy stay in the ordered material for the example above. It passes a weaker test of lossless guiding at selected frequencies. Curved mouths, normal acoustic loading and radiation fed by through-flow require separate boundary data. The next candidate optical source is a neutral pair of oppositely oriented mouths whose *relative position* can change. Its existing signed flux supplies a concrete test: follow a small prescribed relative displacement, locate the compensating current and measure the outgoing wave work. The timing relation between material motion and flux is the missing input if the static source law permits multiple currents.
