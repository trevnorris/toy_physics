# Does stretching the brane between two drains pull them together at 0PN?

**Local stretch persists, but its net tensile force is zero in the uniform rate-dependent benchmark.** Use a steady, constant-density, three-dimensional brane exterior with two fixed point intakes at separation $d$. Each supplies $\mathbf v_i=\nabla[Q_i/(4\pi r_i)]$ with positive volume rate $Q_i$. For this test the brane material follows that velocity, the two fields add without further mouth disturbance, and the shear viscosity $\eta>0$ is spatially constant. The deviatoric Cauchy stress is $\tau=\eta(\nabla\mathbf v+\nabla\mathbf v^{T})$ since the flow is divergence-free outside the intakes. These are conditional choices in the projected S15 stress law. The live model permits relaxation and leaves the evolving material reference and stress open. [S15 stress law](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/directives/S15_decision_list.md) · [Material premises](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/steps/O2_steady_brane_balance.md)

**Between equal drains.** At the midpoint the velocity vanishes. Along the line of centres the material stretches at $8Q/(\pi d^3)$, and in each perpendicular direction it contracts at $4Q/(\pi d^3)$. Here $Q$ is the intake of each drain. The positive axial deviatoric stress persists for as long as the flow and the assumed stress law persist. The isotropic pressure is a separate contribution.

**Around the target.** Enclose only the target with a fixed sphere of radius $0<b<d$ outside its core. Subtract the isolated target with the same intake. Take $+x$ toward the companion and the normal outward from the sphere. By linearity the stress change is just the companion's stress; its divergence vanishes everywhere inside this sphere because the companion potential is harmonic there. Integrating the axial traction $(\Delta\tau\,\mathbf n)_x$ gives

$$
F_{\rm facing}=+\frac{\eta Q_s b^2}{(d^2+b^2)^{3/2}},\qquad
F_{\rm opposite}=-\frac{\eta Q_s b^2}{(d^2+b^2)^{3/2}},\qquad
F_{\rm whole}=0 .
$$

SymPy integrated over each hemisphere. Mathematica derived the Cartesian stress, closed each hemisphere with an equatorial disk and checked the curved surfaces directly at two radii and two separations. Cancellation holds at every allowed separation. The previously calculated inverse-square feeding-side influx from carriage and pressure remains a separate term in the same budget. **What could give an inverse-square tug.** At large separation the companion supplies a nearly uniform incident speed $U_s=Q_s/(4\pi d^2)$ at the target. The decisive observable is the signed change of the integrated material traction per unit incident speed after the brane relaxes. For example, S15 leaves $\eta$ live. If on the sphere its paired-minus-alone response contains $\delta\eta=\beta(b) U_s\cos\theta$, multiplying this change by the isolated target's radial strain contributes $4Q_t\beta(b)U_s/(3b)$ toward the companion for $\beta>0$. This is one sensitivity of the stress term. The model supplies no measured $\beta$, and perturbations of the flow, other stresses or transfer to the bulk may change the total. Distortion of the material flow near the mouth is another possible source of a stress response.

**Physical test.** Compare the same held, inward-draining mouth at zero and opposite weak sideways brane speeds after settling. Read the axial material traction over the complete enclosing surface, including tangential components, and repeat at half speed. To infer a pull on the opening, also account for carried momentum, the other external cuts and supports, or read the total holder reaction directly. The saved carrier fields have no independent draining-material stress response. The physical tension contribution and the free-mouth acceleration remain open.
