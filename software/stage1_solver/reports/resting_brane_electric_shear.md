# Can the brane shear that carries light also transmit electric stress?

**Working result.** A polarizable elastic medium can preserve an inverse-square electric force while its shear and its light travel on different branches. There is also a positive exterior construction. One curl-elastic field can carry the two light polarizations and the Coulomb stress of a *supplied* signed flux. Identifying a measured light wave as material shear requires access to the material motion or stress as well as the electric field. ### 1. Couple an electric field to material displacement

Use the uniform, isotropic three-dimensional effective brane at rest, with no interfaces or background current. Supply a Maxwell field with permittivity \(\epsilon>0\) and inverse permeability \(\nu>0\). A displacement \(u\) of inertia density \(\rho>0\) has transverse stiffness \(s+\mu k^2\) and longitudinal stiffness \(s+Kk^2\) at wave number \(k\). The proposed polarization is \(P=(g_0+fk^2)u\) with interaction \(P\cdot E\) in the Lagrangian. A charge probe measures the potential \(\phi\), an electric current drives the transverse vector potential \(A\), and an independent material force drives \(u\). All coefficients are fixed across the probes. This is a trial constitutive law. Strain-gradient polarization has a physical precedent in insulating solids. [Hong and Vanderbilt](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.88.174107)

| Assumption | Weak static charge | Waves and independent mechanical drive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free displacement, \(s=f=0,\ g_0\ne0,\ K>0\) | Screened beyond \(\sqrt{\epsilon K}/\lvert g_0\rvert\) | The lowest mode has \(\omega\propto k^2\). |
| Only gradients polarize, \(s=g_0=0,\ f\ne0,\ K>0\) | The long-range Coulomb force survives. | Two waves per transverse polarization. Even tuning their bare speeds equal gives two distinct frequencies at finite \(k\), with half the limiting normalized weight in each for either drive. |
| An internal displacement feels a restoring force \(s>0,\ f=0,\ g_0\ne0\) | Coulomb survives with \(\epsilon_{\rm stat}=\epsilon+g_0^2/s\). | The low wave obeys \(c^2=\nu/\epsilon_{\rm stat}\). Material polarization follows the electric field, \(u/E\to g_0/s\). The ratio of elastic shear power to electromagnetic power tends to zero as \(k^2\). |
| Let the neutral reference material move too | In the transverse control the restoring force acts only on relative motion. | The common displacement supports acoustic shear. It is electrically dark when the two elastic coordinates decouple. Elastic mixing can make it visible. Equal limiting speeds still leave two waves when the mixing is nonzero. |

The curl-only light action used in S9 has \(K=0\). With \(s=K=0\), a free longitudinal material displacement cancels the static electric field at each finite \(k\) with nonzero coupling. A true incompressibility constraint would be a different premise. The unconstrained material also has a longitudinal mode with squared frequency \((s+Kk^2+(g_0+fk^2)^2/\epsilon)/\rho\). It must be counted when assessing other radiation channels. The positive dielectric case offers a prediction. Static electric and magnetostatic measurements fix \(\epsilon_{\rm stat}\) and \(\nu\) before measuring the light speed. The independent material drive determines whether those light waves actually transport brane shear. [S9 light action](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/steps/S9_light_requires_shear.md)

### 2. Let shear carry electric flux directly

Try a time-even **axial** coordinate \(W\) in the effective brane. This changes the physical interpretation of S9's displacement. In the exterior let \(D=\nabla\times W,\ H=\dot W,\ E=D/\epsilon,\ B=bH\), with kinetic energy density \(b|\dot W|^2/2\) and elastic energy density \(|\nabla\times W|^2/(2\epsilon)\), \(b,\epsilon>0\). Variation gives Faraday's law, and differentiating \(D\) gives the source-free Ampère law. The transverse waves have \(c^2=1/(b\epsilon)\). The longitudinal coordinate has zero restoring force. Initial data must satisfy \(\nabla\cdot\dot W=0\) to represent the magnetic Gauss law. This construction supplies the dictionary between material variables and the measured fields.

On an intact sphere enclosing the mouth, a single smooth \(W\) gives **zero net electric flux** by Stokes' theorem, even though the mouth is excised. Allow two local descriptions, one regular at each pole. For the spherically symmetric monopole, if their difference has circulation \(q\) around the equator, both yield \(D_r=q/(4\pi r^2)\). The difference is locally a gradient, with a nonzero period around the full overlap when the flux is nonzero. Other multipoles can be superposed at the same total flux. The exterior energy of the monopole between radii \(R\) and \(L\) is \(q^2(1/R-1/L)/(8\pi\epsilon)\). In a uniform applied field \(E_0\) the cross term of the electric stress integrates to \(qE_0\). Reversing the assigned flux reverses the exterior load. The flux calculation leaves \(q\) continuous. This uses the familiar two-patch monopole construction for electric displacement. [Wu and Yang](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.12.3845)

**Material test.** If the two descriptions are literally the angle of the same small material rotor, with fixed conversion \(\theta=\lambda W\), their angular difference on the equator is \(\lambda q/(2\pi r)\) about the same axis. Equivalent rotations require an integer multiple of \(2\pi\). The difference varies continuously with \(r\), so a nonzero \(q\) fails that interpretation on an interval. A physical identification of the two descriptions, or a defect whose stress and energy account for the mismatch, is an additional law. The static flux sector supplies neither a unit bound to a mouth nor its current during unwinding.

### 3. Give the material an internal angle

Suppose two local descriptions are related by a phase defined modulo \(2\pi\). On the overlap let its gradient be \(a(W_N-W_S)\), with one material calibration \(a\ne0\) for all radii. Single-valuedness of the *transition function* around the equator then requires \(aq=2\pi n\). The flux spacing is independent of mouth size. A smooth, nowhere-zero material phase on both entire hemispheres would give zero difference between their boundary windings. A nonzero sector therefore requires actual defect structure if this transition is to be realized by such matter. As a kinematic control, a phase can wind around a ring with \(W=0\) and zero enclosing electric flux. Holding that configuration can require an external reaction when the phase has stiffness.

There is a cost to making the phase physical. Give the neutral homogeneous brane the positive energy density \(\kappa_\alpha|\nabla\alpha-aW|^2/2\). At nonzero wave number a phase gradient is longitudinal. Both transverse polarizations acquire the dispersion
\[
\omega_T^2=\frac{k^2}{b\epsilon}+\frac{\kappa_\alpha a^2}{b}.
\]
Any uniform positive \(\kappa_\alpha\) gives a nonzero minimum frequency in this lossless model. Restricting the stiffness to the vicinity of a mouth preserves the massless *far-zone operator*. The boundary-value problem still owes the transition and its flux. Reflection within the three brane directions preserves enclosed flux. To account for opposite charges, the core of a mouth pointing into the other side must reverse \(n\), and the mouth must select and retain \(\lvert n\rvert=1\). None of these follow from the periodicity alone.

### 4. Can the trapped transverse wave supply that phase?

Make one assumption specific to the saved throat. A circular polarization follows the plane orthogonal to the unit projected face normal \(\hat n\) on a closed mouth section. Carrying a polarization around that section can change its phase even at fixed wave amplitude. With \(e_h=(e_\theta+ih e_\phi)/\sqrt{2}\), \(h=\pm1\), and the convention \({\cal A}_h=i e_h^\dagger d e_h\), the curvature on the sphere of directions is \(-h\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\phi\). Its integral divided by \(2\pi\) is the signed integer called the first Chern number. The two opposite integers for photon polarizations on a direction sphere are established physics. Our hypothesis ties that sphere to the material geometry of the throat. [Palmerduca and Qin](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.109.085005) · [Saved mouth geometry](../../em_charge_attribute/reports/mouth_count_unwinding.md) · [Degenerate trapped-wave modes](../../em_charge_attribute/reports/trapped_wave_charge.md)

For the saved tilt \(\beta\ge0\), write \(t=r/R\). The unit normal points along \(t\hat r-\beta\hat z\). Direct integration gives \(C_h=-2h\) for \(\beta<t\) and \(C_h=0\) for \(\beta>t\). At \(\beta=t\) the projection vanishes at one point and the polarization line is undefined there. Mirroring the normal while keeping \(h\) reverses the integer. Mirroring the normal and reversing \(h\) leaves it unchanged. The full pair has zero trace Chern number. The earlier mechanical unwinding calculation leaves the energy and current of an electrically coupled slip open.

A candidate **local law at the mouth** adds the energy density \(\kappa|\Psi|^2|v|^2/2\) on a closed, ordered material section. Here \(v=d\eta-a W_\parallel+{\cal A}_h\), \(W_\parallel\) is the exterior flux potential pulled back to that section, \(\eta\) is the relative phase and \(\Psi\) its coherence amplitude. A change of local polarization basis or flux potential changes \(\eta\) so that \(v\) describes the same material strain. The ability of the material to sustain this coherence is a postulate. Use the positive loop orientation to define the signed winding \(m_j\) around each vortex core. Assume the two connections are regular in local coordinates there and that the material section and the measuring sphere enclose the same charge with consistent orientation. Stokes' theorem gives
\[
\frac{a q}{2\pi}=C_h+\sum_j m_j .
\]
A globally nonzero polarization vector on a line with nonzero Chern number is impossible. The proposed coherence lives in the *difference* of the material and polarization bundles and can be nonzero when their integers match. This result allows finite stiffness and nonzero local strain. Smooth nonzero coherence with no vortices gives **two exterior flux spacings** in magnitude for a single circular branch on an untilted section. One spacing could occur if the relative phase supplied an appropriate odd vortex winding. Whether it is favorable to have any such vortex requires an energy calculation. Identifying the observed elementary charge with two spacings would instead require an independent reason why one-spacing carriers are absent. The throat still owes helicity selection, its transformation under a mouth mirror, and transport of the charge when a section becomes disordered.

**Consequence for the joint requirements.** Given the proposed identification of electric displacement with curl strain, an electric calibration also fixes the normalization of that elastic energy. For gravity the electric surface load still needs a law for how the mouth stores and transmits momentum before it gives a center acceleration. The model continues to owe the gravitational source and target ratios across radii. The next material discriminator is a stationary mouth, its mirror and a neutral control with one set of coefficients. Measure electric flux through an intact enclosing sphere and the circulation difference between its two patches while varying radius and allowing the core to relax. A local rim state that winds while the sphere reads zero fails the charge test.
