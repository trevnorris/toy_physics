# What crosses the first downstream cut and what stays near the rim at 0PN?

The draining-mouth solver can prescribe opposite accelerations of the same radius-one rim at fixed inward number rate \(0.25\). Its four archived trajectories, including the half-strength pair, end at the **feeding face**. The receiving cut and the volume between that cut and the rim are outside this grid. The separate open receiver evolves a neutral carrier against reflecting walls. Its wall has no momentum field, and it contains no trapped shear mode. The exterior shear probe and the reduced two-coordinate wave calculation do not supply those fields on this draining state. There are **zero signed measurements of the complete physical budget**. **Carrier measurement.** I used the same saved inward state at rate \(0.125\) for each sign in the separate receiver. The straight entrance, receiver and both baths translate together. The baths have uniform phase in that frame. I drove the linear carrier equations with \(A(t)=A_0 e^{st}\) from rest in the remote past, sampled at \(t=0\), and read the cut at \(w=L/4\) and the volume \(0<w<L/4\). The scalar intake has zero first-order change because the driven perturbation is a dipole. The table shows baseline-subtracted lab-frame quantities in solver units at \(s=0.01\), with 128 cells per mouth radius. A positive cut reading exports positive sideways momentum downstream. The rig velocity at the reading is \(A_0/s=\pm 0.01\). Ordinary transport at that common velocity accounts for \(\pm 0.00125\) of the cut reading. In the instantaneous rest frame of the rig the remaining cut flux is \(\pm 0.000016769\), predominantly density-gradient stress. Likewise, most of the stored lab momentum is the enclosed carrier translating with the rig. We obtained the inventory from the carrier field inside the volume and checked its rate against feed minus cut minus side flux. The absolute imbalance fell from \(2.543\times10^{-6}\) at 64 cells per radius to \(1.342\times10^{-6}\) at 128, about \(0.69\%\) of the fine storage rate. At \(s=0.02\) the fine storage-rate magnitude was \(0.000193712\). Reversal is a consequence of solving a linear response for the two signs. These figures measure a driven receiver, and its support absorbs momentum. [Signed results and independent angular and frame checks](../../../docs/_measurements/rim_cut_inventory.json)

**What would complete the measurement.** On the same inward-draining mouth, record synchronized histories of the full downstream momentum current and of the total lab momentum in the moving rim-to-cut volume. Add the wall traction where the cut crosses the wall and any trapped-wave stress across the cut. Include wall and wave momentum inside the volume. Carrier traction on the *side* of the rigid support is a different surface reading. When wall and carrier are included together, their mutual force is internal. If a rapid standing wave is present, compare the same phase or average all channels over the same cycles. Subtract the unforced state and take the half-difference of opposite drives. Over a common time interval the net impulse from applied forces and the feeding side equals downstream and side impulse plus the change in enclosed momentum. This gives an independent balance check and a way to infer the combined reaction if all exterior loads are measured. Separating export and storage requires an independent reading of at least one of them in addition to that balance. A locally calibrated wall and wave response at the cut and in this short volume would suffice without reconstructing the rest of the throat.
