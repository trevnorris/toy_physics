# Does carrying brane material along with a draining mouth suppress drag?

**Result.** In a relaxed viscous brane with passive normal rim contact, solving for the material velocity gives the same force coefficient for a steady incident stream and for steady motion through resting material. This survives the first correction from inward drainage. Strong contact can make material move almost exactly with the rim while the drag remains finite. [SymPy field solution and Mathematica reciprocal-force calculation](../../../docs/_measurements/rim_material_entrainment.json)

**Physical assumptions.** Use a spherical mouth of radius $a$ in an unbounded, isotropic, incompressible three-dimensional brane of density $\rho$ and constant shear viscosity $\eta$. The exterior material has resting-drain velocity $-Q_t/(4\pi r^2)$ radially, where $Q_t$ is a volume intake. Hold this preparation fixed. Linearize in a weak steady sideways drive; keep the first correction in $\epsilon=\rho Q_t/(4\pi\eta a)\ll1$. Set incremental tangential traction to zero. At the in-plane rim assign the incremental normal mechanical traction to the proposed relaxed contact law. Material may cross the rim; the sideways dipole integrates to zero change in intake. No throat interior, bare mass or numerical material coefficients enter. The ledger allows relaxation and leaves the actual draining-state response open. [Project material premises](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/directives/O2_SHARED_PHYSICS.md#L89) · [Rim law](one_mouth_rim_slip_comparison.md) · [Exterior Stokes equations and radial modes](https://www.web.mit.edu/2.21/www/Lec-notes/chap2_slow/2-5Stokes.pdf)

Let $U$ be incident **material** speed, $V$ mouth speed, and $W=U-V$. The normal velocity perturbation relative to the rim is $b\cos\theta$. Write $Z=4\pi\eta a$ and $\Gamma=4\pi a^2 k\tau/3$ for the exterior and contact resistances. The signed axial force exerted by the material on the opening follows from two equations for the *same* traction:

$$
F_{\rm rim}=\Gamma b
= Z(W-b)+\rho Q_t(W/6-b/2)+O(Z\epsilon^2 W).
$$

At leading order $b=ZW/(Z+\Gamma)$ and $F_{\rm rim}=Z\Gamma W/(Z+\Gamma)$. At fixed exterior resistance, increasing the contact strength drives $b$ to zero. The product $\Gamma b$ approaches $(Z+\rho Q_t/6)W$ through first order. The pressure and viscous stress needed to move the surrounding material persist. SymPy solved the perturbed velocity and pressure profiles. Mathematica integrated the convective momentum terms against a unit normal rim motion. Both gave the same $\rho Q_t$ term. Mathematica discarded an assumption on its limit variable at positive infinity; the resulting limit also agrees with SymPy. [Exact profiles, second derivation and limit checks](../../../docs/_measurements/rim_material_entrainment.json)

**Decision.** Retire passive normal entrainment as a stand-alone explanation for large steady attraction with independently small steady drag at matched material speeds. The calculation covers one mechanical stress channel. Momentum leaving with converted material and other stresses still enter the total reaction. A direct experimental discriminator would calibrate $C_{\rm drift}$ from the signed external drive required to translate a single drain in resting brane. With an actual companion held far away, measure its incident material speed $U_s$ and the target's change in holder force $\Delta H$. If the same steady mechanism accounts for both loads, $-\Delta H=C_{\rm drift}U_s$ at leading order. A resolved residual scaling as $d^{-2}$ would identify a contribution this model misses. The carrier archive supplies neither independent drive nor incident material velocity. Physical free fall remains open. Working on base fd667ea9, uncommitted pending review.
