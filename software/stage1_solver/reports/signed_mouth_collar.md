# Signed 0PN wall and wave measurement on a short receiving segment

**Result.** The solver now evolves the wall displacement \(q(z,t)\) and the sideways center \(Y(z,t)\) of a standing shear pattern independently, from the rim to a point beyond the measuring cut. It saves both motions as functions of depth and time, both stored momenta, the two cut tractions, and their time integrals. The archived radius-one, intake-0.25 mouth supplies the rim drive. Its receiving-side material, wave normalization, coupling and terminal load are unmeasured. The numbers below use a separate benchmark for those inputs. The carrier load is not added to this calculation. [Signed mouth data](../../../docs/_measurements/signed_mouth_wall_wave_coverage.txt) · [Fields and run manifest](../../../docs/_measurements/signed_mouth_collar.json)

**Local equations.** For \(u=(q,Y)\) the current along the intrinsic depth is \(\Phi_i=-T_i\partial_z u_i\), and the momentum up to the cut \(b\) is \(P_i=\int_0^b m_i\partial_tu_i\,dz\). The two line equations are \(\partial_t(m_i\dot u_i)+\partial_z\Phi_i=S_i\). The benchmark uses equal and opposite distributed forces from \(k(q-Y)^2/2\), prescribes \(q(0,t)=At^2/2\), and gives the wave zero rim traction. Its wave line mass and stiffness are the independently specified shear coefficients times \(I f(z)^2/2\), where \(I\) is the transverse gradient norm and \(f\) is the standing amplitude. This projects onto slow translation of one normalized wave shape. A deforming or multimode wave would require additional fields. At the outer edge both tractions obey \(\Phi=Z\dot u\). I fitted the passive, reciprocal matrix \(Z\) to independent velocity and traction histories from two outgoing reference waves. The relative prediction error on different reference drives was \(4.97\times10^{-11}\). A physical continuation may have memory beyond this instantaneous law. **Timing and readings.** I used the five saved drives, \(A=0,\ \pm0.01,\ \pm0.02\), all initially at rest. The benchmark cut is at \(b=0.4625\), the outer edge at \(0.578125\), and the fastest characteristic speed is 1. The original record ends at \(t=0.001\), before the rim signal can reach the cut in this benchmark. To exercise the readout after arrival, I extended the same imposed accelerations to \(t=1\). For \(A=+0.02\), the wall and wave center at the cut have moved \(0.00275419\) and \(0.000180779\), respectively. The table is in solver units, with positive transmission downstream.

| At \(t=1\) | Wall | Trapped-wave envelope |
|---|---:|---:|
| Momentum stored between rim and cut | 0.00675579 | 0.00118932 |
| Momentum transmitted across cut since onset | 0.00266690 | 0.000155510 |
| Momentum crossing per unit time at that instant | 0.00958894 | 0.00102688 |

The opposite drive reversed all readings, the half drives scaled within numerical tolerance, and the undriven fields stayed at rest. Reversal and scaling check the implementation of the linear model. A separate Mathematica finite-element calculation agreed to within 0.030% on all six table entries. An uncoupled outgoing-wall solution checked the absolute sign and normalization. **Using a measured state.** The guarded driver accepts \(\texttt{--material}\) with a JSON packet and \(\texttt{--output}\) with a new directory. The packet identifies the baseline by hash and intake, supplies depths, cut index, material coefficients, standing amplitude, normalization and coupling, and supplies training and held-out terminal velocities and forces. An explicit null distributed force models zero additional drive; signed force histories can supply measured carrier or other distributed loads. The executable input test constructs an example packet. A physical application needs these inputs from the same drain and evidence that the wave projection and terminal law apply on the drive timescale.
