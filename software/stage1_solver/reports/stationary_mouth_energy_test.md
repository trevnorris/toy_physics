# Energy crossing a stationary mouth's enclosing surface at 0PN

Hold mouth 1 at rest. At a chosen separation $d=X_2-X_1>0$, prescribe mouth 2 a signed approach speed $s=-\dot d$. Each mouth draws material from the three-dimensional brane at its fixed rate $D(a_i)>0$ and releases it into the four-dimensional receiving bulk in this benchmark. With outward rates defined in each exterior, $q_i=-D(a_i)$ on the brane and $q_i=+D(a_i)$ in the receiving bulk. In each domain use two disjoint spheres, each centered on its own mouth. Measure the interaction energy per second crossing the sphere around stationary mouth 1 into its exterior.

**Prediction.** In the unbounded constant-density potential-flow benchmark with radial monopoles, matched far pressures, and the entire static load assigned to each mouth, let $A(d)>0$ be the attraction magnitude. To first order in speed, the outward interaction fluxes at the two mouths are $J_1=A(d)s$ and $J_2=0$. The center work rates of the medium on the two mouths are $0$ and $A(d)s$, respectively. The exterior interaction kinetic energy grows at $A(d)s$ on approach. Together with the work on the moving center, this requires an incremental power of $2A(d)s$ relative to the isolated controls. The fixed surface measures the part that enters exterior storage. With prescribed motion the translation actuator can absorb the second contribution. On recession the leading energy flux and mechanical power reverse sign. [Two-drain energy derivation](two_drain_energy.md)

**Controls and sign.** Use the fixed-mouth rest frame and a normal pointing from its enclosed region into the exterior. At the same separation with fixed radii and the same intake law, run pair, mouth 1 alone, moving mouth 2 alone, and background. Keep the cut even where the enclosed mouth is absent. Match imposed trajectories, reservoir heads, energy zero and current convention, and driving histories within each subtraction. Apply $\delta Q=Q_{12}-Q_1-Q_2+Q_0$ to the surface integral in each channel. Sum the channels. For a fixed spherical cut use lab energy current through it. A moving cut elsewhere needs the relative energy flux $(\mathbf J_E-\mathcal E\mathbf W)\cdot\mathbf n$. Include carried energy and pressure or stress work once. Record separately any work by feed, drain and translation controls.

**Independent comparison.** In the same preparation at rest, measure the interaction holding force along the line of centers, subtracting the isolated reaction. The attraction magnitude is the opposite of the signed holder force on mouth 1 along $+x$. Measure it with a force readout independent of the energy flux. At $s=0,+s,-s,+s/2,-s/2$, read the fixed-surface energy flux separately. Compare the odd slope $[\delta J_1(+s)-\delta J_1(-s)]/(2s)$ with that force. The benchmark also predicts zero stationary intercept and opposite signed fluxes at full strength. Check both signs at half strength, where the denominator of the odd slope is $s$. Repeat with a second radius around the same geometric center, keeping the companion outside both surfaces. Resolve the sign and size against numerical and force-readout uncertainty, and check convergence under spatial and time refinement. If the two speed pairs give different slopes, reduce the speed or bound the finite-speed error. A resolved mismatch that persists in the zero-speed limit is a failed benchmark comparison. An unresolved sign or error larger than the signal leaves the test inconclusive.

**Physical readout.** The held carrier solver supplies the feeding side. Applying the test to a physical mouth also needs the matched receiving-side energy current, including unsteady pressure, other live stores and fluxes on the chosen cuts, and an independent total holding reaction. For a changing annulus account for its energy storage and any additional volume sources before comparing its two radii. Identifying a conversion energy source further requires the enclosed energy change and other outputs relative to the same controls.
