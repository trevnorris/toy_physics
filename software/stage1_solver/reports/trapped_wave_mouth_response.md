# Can a trapped shear wave help an open mouth fall at 0PN?

A bound shear pattern could carry inertia, transmit exterior momentum to the mouth, or do both. The existing body definition includes that pattern in the translating body. The step plan leaves its profile, confinement and radial support open. The following calculation uses a cycle-averaged mouth response and a translating trial field. No throat interior or physical coupling coefficient has been fitted. [Body definition](../../../docs/em_u1_body_definition.md#L20) · [Candidate support mechanisms](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/V3_STEP_PLAN.md#L917)

**Radial support and translation.** Assume an isolated, in-brane isotropic draining state. A candidate wave must remain localized, or be replenished with a stated power budget, over the measurement. Its mean outward load must balance tension and backpressure with a stable radial response. Missing bulk transverse shear and a narrow neck motivate confinement. Leakage through the neck and conversion to other allowed modes still enter the lifetime. Translating the whole isolated configuration in a uniform brane leaves its energy unchanged. A wave can therefore support the radius and exert zero net sideways force at rest. [Trapping hypothesis](../../../docs/conceptual_foundation.md#L472) · [Open escape channels](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/SUBSTRATE_REQUIREMENTS.md#L920)

**One possible transfer mechanism.** Use a round effective stress surface of radius \(a\) in the three brane directions. Choose positive \(x\) toward the companion and \(\mu=\cos\theta\). Model the signed outward normal load on the receiving structure as \(p=\kappa\langle s^2\rangle\), with \(\kappa>0\) and shear strain amplitude \(s\). For weak incident brane-material speed \(U\), take

\[
s=S\cos\omega t+U\mu\,[b_c\cos\omega t+b_s\sin\omega t].
\]

The first term supplies mean pressure \(\kappa S^2/2\). Integrating \(p\mu\,dA\) gives the sideways load per speed \(C_{\rm surf}=4\pi a^2\kappa S b_c/3\). The out-of-phase response \(b_s\) gives zero linear load. For a constructive example, let the dipole strain per unit speed obey \(\ddot y+2\gamma\dot y+\omega_1^2y=hS\cos\omega t\). Here \(h\) describes flow-induced mixing on the prepared inward branch. The periodic solution has \(b_c=hS\Delta/[\Delta^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2]\), with \(\Delta=\omega_1^2-\omega^2\). For \(\gamma>0\), detuning can give either sign and exact resonance gives zero mean linear load despite a driven dipole. This example assumes the mixing, stress law and spherical projection. The physical shape requires the work of its full traction under translation. [Guarded SymPy and Mathematica checks](../../../docs/_measurements/trapped_wave_mouth_response.json)

**Whose force is it?** The wave's force on an inner wall and the wall's force on the wave cancel for the composite body. The calculated surface load can transmit a pull **if an external medium supplies the corresponding momentum** and the rest of the boundary does not cancel it. The ideal single-holder exterior already supplies \(D_tU\) at leading order for material intake \(D_t\). If the wave-bearing structure transmits all of it, the two readings must agree. Adding them would count it twice. A wave could instead move with the mouth and contribute inertia while a different stress transmits the pull. [Exterior result and its assumptions](held_force_angular_falloff.md) · [Total holder balance](one_mouth_outer_holding_force.md)

**Inertia without a bare mass.** On a slow translating branch, take a localized shear displacement \(\boldsymbol u=\boldsymbol f_a(x-X,\ldots)\cos\omega t\) with kinetic density \(\rho_{\rm sh}|\partial_t\boldsymbol u|^2/2\). The second derivative of the cycle-averaged kinetic energy with respect to \(\dot X\) gives \(M_{\rm wave}=\tfrac12\int \rho_{\rm sh}|\partial_x\boldsymbol f_a|^2\,dV\). It is finite and positive for a normalizable translation with nonzero norm. Count it once in the full effective inertia, including material motion, wall response and cross terms. The rigidly translated profile is a trial branch. Slip or delayed adjustment calls for a frequency-dependent push response. A slow inertia does not fix the instantaneous acceleration after a force step. [Collective-coordinate prescription](../../../docs/em_u1_body_definition.md#L98) · [Release versus slow response](one_mouth_translation_equation.md)

**Conditional free fall.** With one external holder, full transmission of the ideal exterior load, and a freely translating wave-bearing body of total slow inertia \(M_t>0\), the source flow gives \(a_{t,x}=D_tD_s/(4\pi\rho_3 M_t d^2)\) at leading order. Here \(D_s\) is the mass rate supplied by a spherical far brane flow of density \(\rho_3\). No Newtonian potential enters. If both mouths belong to one family, common \(D(a)/M(a)\) gives target-independent fall and a single source-mass normalization on this branch. Trapping alone fixes neither full transmission nor this ratio. [Source and target scaling](../../../research/pde_ledger_v3/SUBSTRATE_REQUIREMENTS.md#L2153)

**Discriminator.** On the *same* inward branch \(D(a)\), measure cycle-averaged facing-minus-away stress and the independent total center-holding force at \(0,\pm U,\pm U/2\), with the remote bulk at rest. Compare the dipole's phase against the standing oscillation. In resting brane, push the same mouth and independently track both the mode envelope and the geometric center. These readings distinguish radial support alone, transmitted pull, and inertia moving with the mouth. For a regular scalar response on the same timescale, \(a_x=C_a U/M_{\rm eff}\), with \(C_a=-H'_a(0)\) the measured total slope. The ratio must be positive and common across target radii for the velocity-driven Newtonian limit. A physical result also needs the independently determined source strength per mass and the two-drain power budget. Working calculation pending independent review.
