# Sideways momentum in the wall and trapped wave at 0PN

Consider the same open mouth and inward state for each sign of the rim acceleration. Take positive flux to mean positive sideways momentum traveling downstream across the cut. Subtract the undriven history and average any fast standing oscillation over matching cycles. For the previous linear histories \(A(t)=A_0e^{st}\) starting from rest in the remote past, the total signed impulse across the cut up to the reading is \(\Phi(0)/s\) for a flux perturbation with the same time dependence. The fixed intake gives a carrier number rate. Neither the signed mouth histories at rate \(0.25\) nor the separate accelerating receiver at \(0.125\) supplies a wall or bound-wave history at the cut. [Signed measurements and domains](rim_cut_inventory.md)

**Wall.** Write the sideways displacement of the wall as \(q(w,t)\cos\theta\), absorbing the angular and area factors in the line inertia \(\mu\) and tension \(T\). At a cut \(w=b\), the projected wall action gives
\[
 P_{\rm wall}^{<}=\int_0^b\mu\,\dot q\,dw,\qquad
 \Phi_{\rm wall}(b)=-T(b)\,\partial_wq(b).
\]
The equation gives \(\dot P_{\rm wall}^{<}=\Phi_{\rm wall}(0)-\Phi_{\rm wall}(b)+\int_0^b(S-Hq)\,dw\), including the distributed drive and restoring term. For a uniform segment with these terms zero, the known rim displacement and one additional displacement history at the cut suffice. Two example terminations show why the rim alone is insufficient. A traction-free cut gives zero export. A cut loaded by an equivalent reflecting field segment of length \(b\) gives \(\Phi_{\rm wall}(b)/A\to\mu b\). In both examples \(\dot P_{\rm wall}^{<}/A\to\mu b\) at slow drive, with no bare mass. The nonzero linear responses reverse with \(A\). These examples use identical near-rim properties and different downstream loads. **Trapped wave.** In a local straight patch of the *ordered brane*, choose intrinsic coordinate \(z\) along the bend through the cut. For one transverse polarization take \(u_y=f(z)h(x-Y(z,t))\cos\omega t\), where \(Y\) is the slowly moving center of the wave. With uniform transverse weights and a centered or localized profile satisfying \(\int h\,\partial_xh\,dx=0\), put \(I=\int(\partial_x h)^2dx\). Per unit remaining transverse measure, the cycle-averaged kinetic and rotational shear terms give the momentum conjugate to translating the wave pattern and its downstream current
\[
 P_{\rm wave}^{<}=\tfrac12 I\int_0^b \rho_{\rm sh} f^2 \dot Y\,dz,\qquad
 \Phi_{\rm wave}(b)=-\tfrac12 I\,\mu_{\rm sh} f(b)^2\,\partial_zY(b).
\]
These are projected field contributions for this polarization and geometry. A sideways acceleration can build stored momentum while the center remains aligned along the patch and this shear current vanishes. Maintaining that motion then requires a drive through the wall or another boundary. A gradient in the sideways shift transmits stress even when the fast mode is standing. For illustration, use the paper's node-at-entrance, reflecting-end *support* profiles as trial shapes, with uniform inertia weight and the same sideways speed along the mode. For the lowest shape \(f\propto\sin(\pi z/2L)\), the fraction of the wave's translation momentum in the first quarter is \(1/4-\sqrt2/(2\pi)\), about **2.49%**. For the first overtone it is \(1/4-\sqrt2/(6\pi)\), about **17.50%**. For the slower signed drive, if the wave follows the rim exactly, its first-quarter momentum at the sample would be \(\pm 0.01\,F M_{\rm wave}\), and the rate of change would be \(\pm 0.0001\,F M_{\rm wave}\), where \(F\) is the chosen fraction and \(M_{\rm wave}\) is the full field translation inertia. The complement is momentum located farther along the mode. A spatial fraction alone gives no export rate. The trial shapes, normalization of wave inertia, and mapping of intrinsic length to the receiving cut have not been measured on this mouth. **Physical result.** To use the same opposite drives, determine the wall's velocity through the short region and its tension times slope at the cut. For the wave, determine the normalized standing field and the translation or deformation it develops there, including its phase and cut traction. Sampling on either side of the cut gives the spatial slopes in the projected equations. Calibrated local responses for these observables would also suffice. Wall–wave exchange internal to this volume counts once in the combined budget. The present data leave both the physical export and the stored momentum undetermined.
