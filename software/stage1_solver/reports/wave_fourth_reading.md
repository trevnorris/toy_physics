The radius-one drain with prescribed inward number rate \(0.25\) has a calibrated sideways rim-force probe. Its saved draining state fixes the aperture. **Fourth-reading control.** I reserved the real Laplace rate \(3s\), with \(s>0\), and fitted the single-mode law to the slow limit \(m(0)\), \(m(s)\) and \(m(2s)\) of the archived reduced-model push. Here \(m(s)=\widetilde F(s)/(s^2\widetilde X(s))\) for initial rest and baseline-subtracted displacement. Let \(\alpha=M_w/(M_r+M_w)\) be the wave fraction of the reduced field inertia, \(\Omega\) the relative oscillation rate, and \(u=s/\Omega\). For \(0<\alpha<1\), the three readings gave \(\omega_f^2/\Omega^2=1-\alpha\). They predicted

\[
\frac{m(3s)}{M_r}=\frac{1+9u^2}{1-\alpha+9u^2}.
\]

After the fit, direct evaluation at \(3s\) gave residual **zero** for all \(u>0\). Mathematica independently integrated the saved time-domain center trajectory at the reserved rate. The no-wave control gives two zero calibration differences and an undefined fit. This checks the method on an exact single-mode force history with symbolic field inertias. The calculation provides no bound in physical time units and no independent validation of that law for a throat.
