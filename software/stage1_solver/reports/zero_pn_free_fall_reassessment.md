# What would finish Newtonian free fall for two draining mouths?

**The flow supplies a mechanism for attraction. The remaining question is what moves together as a particle.** With the entrance and flare constrained to translate together, the curved carrier model predicts positive inertia and acceleration along an incident brane stream with zero assigned bare mass. At radius 1 and intake 0.25, the predicted acceleration per incident speed was 0.0203915. A separate slow oscillating solve gave 0.0204083 in phase. In the weak two-mouth calculation, doubling separation left about a quarter of the feeding-side force. Both results depend on the stated geometry and reservoir preparation. [Carrier force, free response and pair results](mouth_carrier_0pn.md)

**The physical ambiguity is measurable.** Most of the pull acts near the entrance, and much of the inertia is deeper in the flare. Two model mouths with zero trapped-wave occupation, the same inflow, geometry and first-bend holding force had almost the same steady pull. Changing only the deeper support changed the in-phase free entrance acceleration by 1.85922% at the tested frequency on mesh 34. Homogeneous patch compression constrains a material pressure law. It gives neither the sideways momentum stored with the translating mouth nor the total stress returned from converting material. The physical law could be specified through these net effects at the opening. [Flexible release](mouth_entrance_tag_release.md) · [Zero-load patch test](neutral_patch_zero_load.md)

**The physical mouth is internally supported.** During free fall its center can translate while the occupied wave, wall tension and surrounding material act on its shape. The model proposes a balance between the closing tension and the opening stress from the wave and flow. A conservative fixed-action wave model had a restoring force for small changes in radius and depth about its stationary shape. Supporting an opening of a given area and transmitting a sideways load from the entrance to the flare probe different motions. In the radius-one model with an assumed confining well and trial wave action $J=1$, the wave supplied restoring force and added 2.4681 units of inertia alongside the carrier’s 12.2859. At angular frequency 0.005 the entrance–flare mismatch was 4.60% or 0.374% of common displacement for the two prescribed bend profiles. In a separate calculation with the brane’s torsional waves, the degree-one wave restored a restricted deep bend and none of the tested waves stabilized both independent bends by itself. The physical wave state and its slow response remain inputs to establish. **Area and mass.** The older lepton calculation imposed a constant cross-sectional area while one axis lengthened and the other shortened. The standing wave’s spatially uniform pressure did zero linear work on that distortion. A separate oscillating sideways force of 0.001 produced about 0.00000568 displacement at two tested frequencies in a reduced wave-supported bend with trial mass 12 and stiffness 6. These are distinct loading tests. The later 0PN bends translate round sections sideways through depth and can preserve every section’s area even when the centers move relative to each other. For the reference particle with its supporting wave occupied use fixed opening area as a working comparison. Whether the physical area changes under the weak drive, and whether extra trapped energy changes translational inertia at the same area, are separate observables. **Occupied-mouth follow-up.** The two bends have now been released together under a weak, slow feeding-brane stream. The stronger occupied-wave example transmits the load with zero independent wall tension. The entrance acceleration differs measurably from rigid translation. The current task is to select the occupation and material tension on the same independently prepared geometry. [Matched occupied and rigid responses](mouth_occupied_companion.md)

**The shortest one-mouth test.** Hold only the geometric entrance in weak incident flow \(U\) and measure the signed holder force \(H\), with the standing wave and material support present and the inner sections allowed to respond. In otherwise resting reservoirs, apply a known push to the same entrance and measure its free acceleration per force \(\chi_a\). Use the same prepared wave state, intake, frequency and outer conditions. Track the opening area and entrance–flare relative displacement to check the common-motion approximation on the slow drive. Linear superposition then predicts \(a_{\rm free}/U=-\chi_a H/U\). Predict a separate release at half amplitude or opposite sign. A different time profile requires the complex responses over its frequency band. The force and displacement must come from an independently calibrated material model or data. Balanced bend loads help locate the reaction; a net push also probes the momentum carried in common translation. **Where another sector can help.** Target the material that carries light as it loses shear order. An independently normalized shear force per area together with material velocity or strain can discriminate them. Test whether one lower-level law predicts both ripple propagation and the slow load transmitted during material replacement, using forces and momentum on both sides. For the physical supported state, the standing-wave pattern, its occupation and its force on a bend require independent evidence. Electric charge supplies a later orientation test. Each source needs the same far-field volume intake per inertial mass. Fix the same ambient and material laws, independently select the intake at each radius, swap unequal sources and targets, and check that \(d^2 a_\parallel/M_s\) approaches one positive constant with subleading net drag.
